# What language actually moves a Philippine office

> Doctrine note, 16 September 2026. Built from the MWK-001 correspondence corpus — ~16 months of
> outbound instruments and the replies they did or did not produce. **Measured, not assumed:** every
> claim below points at a document in the corpus. LandTek work product; not a legal opinion.

## The finding, in one paragraph

We have been writing **advocacy**. Philippine offices do not run on advocacy — they run on
**indorsement**. An officer who agrees with you cannot act on your agreement; he can only act by
**forwarding a described item to a named officer with a period attached**, over his own signature,
with copies to his superiors. That is the unit of work. A letter that cannot be converted into that
unit dies on the desk of someone who may well be sympathetic. The question is therefore not
"is my letter persuasive?" but **"can the receiving officer lift an item out of it, paste it into his
own indorsement, and be safe?"**

## The proof is in our own file

Our letter to MLGOO Guerrero of **18 August 2026** — 2,547 words — **worked**. Six days later he
issued two 2nd Indorsements, received-stamped by the Mayor and by the Sangguniang Bayan on
25 August, each carrying a five-working-day clock [docs 6703, 6704, 6705].

Now look at **what survived the trip.**

| Our 18 Aug letter | What Guerrero did with it |
|---|---|
| §1 — "The premise of the close-out is mistaken" (≈500 words of argument) | **Discarded** |
| §2 — "A material fact was omitted… the Mayor presented half of each document" | **Discarded** |
| §3 — items (a) through (h): duty · statutory cite · duty-holder · documented conduct | **Lifted.** Three items became his indorsement to the Mayor, three became his indorsement to the SB |
| §3(g) — the Municipal Hall parcel, argued at length with three numbered proofs | **Carved out** as *sub judice* — the most argued item was the one he refused to carry |

He even kept our own numbering as a citation: *"[Item 3(h) Letter of Jonathan Zschoche]"*,
*"[Item 3(d) from Letter of Jonathan Zschoche]"*, *"[Item 3(f) from Letter of Jonathan Zschoche]"*.

**Read that table again.** Two thousand words of our best argument were ballast he had to strip
before he could act. The eight hundred words that were already in indorsable shape are the entire
reason anything happened. And the single most heavily argued item is the one he dropped.

That is the whole doctrine: **argument is what an officer must remove before he can move. Give him
less to remove.**

## The native instrument, anatomy

From the Guerrero 2nd Indorsement to Mayor Pajarillo, 25 August 2026 [doc 6704] — this is the form
to imitate, because it is the form that got received-stamped and started a clock:

| Part | What it does | Verbatim from doc 6704 |
|---|---|---|
| **"Basis:"** — 5 enumerated prior papers | Makes the letter a *continuation of an existing record*, not a fresh demand. An officer acting on item 6 of a chain is safe; an officer acting on a stranger's new idea is exposed | *"FB Message… dated 13 July 2026"* / *"Memorandum (1st Indorsement)… dated 17 July 2026"* / *"Response of Mayor Alex Pajarillo… dated July 24, 2026"* … |
| **"2nd INDORSEMENT"** + date | Names the instrument and its position in the chain | — |
| **The referral sentence** | Transfers the matter to a named officer, by title, with the attachment identified by date and author | *"Respectfully referred to the HON. ALEXANDER PAJARILLO, Municipal Mayor… the herein attached letter-request dated 18 August 2026 from MR. JONATHAN ZSCHOCHE…"* |
| **The itemiser** | Announces a short, closed list | *"Attention is respectfully drawn to the following specific issues requiring LGU action, clarification, and response:"* |
| **The items** | Bold label → one sentence → bracketed source reference. Three of them. No argument | *"**Special Power of Attorney (SPA) Requirement:** Legal and statutory basis for requiring a 'signed by all heirs' Special Power of Attorney… [Item 3(d) from Letter of Jonathan Zschoche]"* |
| **The clock sentence** | A period, **with a reason that serves the officer** | *"In view whereof, this Office respectfully requests a written comment/action taken report on the abovementioned matters **within five (5) working days from receipt hereof to facilitate appropriate feedback to the requesting party and submission to DILG higher levels.**"* |
| **The assignment** | Four words that place the duty | *"For your appropriate action."* |
| **"Copy Furnished:"** ladder | The actual enforcement mechanism | Requester · **Provincial Director** · **Regional Director** |

One page. Zero adjectives. Zero grievance. Zero law-review prose.

## Seven rules that fall out of it

**1. Lead with the chain, not the injury.** Open with an enumerated "Basis" of prior paper — dates,
authors, reference numbers. The officer's first question is not "is this right?" but *"what is this,
and is it already in the system?"* Answer that in the first eight lines.

**2. One item = one duty + one cite + one duty-holder + one observable fact.** No item may need a
paragraph of setup. If it does, it is not yet an item.

**3. Cap the items at three to five, and pre-sort them by addressee.** Guerrero had to split our
eight items across two offices himself. Do that work for him: a letter to a supervising office should
arrive already separated into the indorsements it will become.

**4. The deadline must give the officer a reason that serves *him*.** Guerrero's clock runs *"to
facilitate appropriate feedback to the requesting party and submission to DILG higher levels"* — the
period exists because **he** must report upward. A deadline framed as our entitlement is a demand he
can ignore; a deadline framed as his own reporting requirement is a thing he must protect.

**5. Give him the words.** Include a short, fully drafted paragraph he can lift. He will lift it —
the bracketed *"[Item 3(d)]"* citations prove he lifts rather than rewrites. A named, numbered item
is easier to carry than an argument he must first reduce.

**6. The copy-furnish ladder is the engine, not a courtesy.** Every instrument that worked in this
file carried copies upward: PD Relucio, RD Escober. The letters that went to one office with no
ladder are the ones still unanswered. The ladder is not aggression; it is how an office is *given
cover* to act.

**7. Never argue with the office you need to act.** "Respectfully, that premise is mistaken" is a
sentence that makes a correct point and costs an indorsement. Where a premise really is wrong, do not
rebut it — **restate the item so the wrong premise no longer reaches it.** The Municipal Hall item is
the standing example: argued hardest, carved out, still dead.

## The phrase-book

These are the load-bearing formulas, taken from instruments in this corpus that produced action.
They are not ornament — an officer scanning a letter is looking for them, and their absence reads as
"this is not an official communication."

| Function | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Transmitting | *"Respectfully referred to…"* · *"Respectfully forwarded to…"* · *"Respectfully returned to…"* | "I am writing to you about…" |
| Pointing at the item | *"Attention is respectfully drawn to…"* · *"Attention is specifically invited to the provisions of…"* | "You should note that…" |
| Pivoting to the ask | *"In view whereof…"* · *"Relative thereto…"* · *"In this connection…"* | "Therefore, it follows that…" |
| Asking | *"…respectfully requests a written comment/action taken report…"* · *"A written status report or comment relative to these matters within five (5) working days upon receipt will be highly appreciated."* | "I demand…" · "You are required to…" |
| Assigning | *"For your appropriate action."* · *"For information and appropriate action."* | "Please handle this." |
| Closing | *"Very respectfully,"* | "Sincerely yours," (fine, but *Very respectfully* is the register) |

Two notes on this table. First, **"will be highly appreciated" is not weakness** — in this register it
is how a peer-level office sets a deadline without asserting a command it lacks the power to assert.
Guerrero used exactly that phrasing on the Sangguniang Bayan, a body he cannot direct, and got a
3:16 pm received stamp the same day. Second, **"For your appropriate action" is the operative
sentence of the entire instrument.** It is what converts a document into an assignment.

## What our letters have been doing wrong

Measured against the above, the recurring defects in this file:

| Defect | Where it shows | Cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Argument before item** | Nearly every demand letter opens with 300–600 words of framing before the first actionable request | The reader must construct the item himself, and most will not |
| **Correcting the office** | *"Respectfully, that premise is mistaken"* (DILG PD Relucio, 26 Aug) | Converts a potential ally into a defendant |
| **Moral register** | *"the citizen is left standing in the gap between two deferrals"*; *"it is a misrepresentation to the supervising Department"* | Reads as accusation; accusation triggers legal-office routing, and legal offices close things |
| **Item count** | Eight items in one letter, unsorted by addressee | Forces the officer to do the splitting; he may split none |
| **Length** | 2,547 words; the Provincial Oversight demand runs far longer | Only the last third is ever used |
| **Novelty** | Instruments that open a new theory rather than continuing a numbered chain | Nothing to indorse; there is no prior paper to attach |

None of this means the argument was wrong. The 18 August letter was *right*, and it worked. The
point is narrower and more useful: **it worked at about one-third efficiency, and the two-thirds that
was discarded is exactly where we spend most of our drafting time.**

## The one place the long form still belongs

Adjudicative bodies — ARTA Litigation Division, the Office of the President, the Ombudsman, the CSC,
and the courts — **do** read argument, because their output is a resolution, not an indorsement. The
ARTA 1378 Manifestation is correctly written in the long form. Keep the two registers strictly
separate, and never send the adjudicative register to an executive office:

- **Executive / supervisory office** (DILG, DPWH, BLGF, Assessor, Engineer, Mayor, Governor) → **indorsement register.** One page. Basis, items, clock, ladder.
- **Adjudicative body** (ARTA, OP, OMB, CSC, courts) → **pleading register.** Argument, authority, verified facts, prayer.

Most of our misfires are a pleading sent to an executive office.

## Applying it: the DPWH letter

The draft at `case_work/MWK-001/DPWH_NBC_ENFORCEMENT_REQUEST_2026-09.md` is a pleading sent to an
executive office — nine argued paragraphs, block quotations of five statutes, a disclosure section.

## Addendum, same day — two refinements found in application

### 1. Office register vs. private-party register

The indorsement formulas are **not all available to a private requester.** *"Respectfully referred
to…"* and *"For your appropriate action."* are what one office says to another when transferring a
matter — a citizen using them is claiming an authority he does not have, and it reads wrong. The
private-party equivalents carry the same structure without the false claim:

| Function | Office → office | Private party → office |
|---|---|---|
| Opening the chain | *"Basis:"* | *"Reference is respectfully made to the following:"* |
| Transmitting | *"Respectfully referred to…"* | *"The undersigned respectfully submits for… consideration"* |
| Pointing at the item | *"Attention is respectfully drawn to…"* | *"Attention is respectfully invited to…"* (both work) |
| Pivoting | *"In view whereof…"* | *"In view of the foregoing…"* (both work) |
| Closing the ask | *"For your appropriate action."* | *"Your favorable action on this request will be highly appreciated."* |
| Sign-off | *"Very respectfully,"* | *"Very respectfully yours,"* |

The rev1→rev2 DPWH draft made exactly this error and was corrected
(`.bak-register` holds the pre-fix text).

### 2. The strongest form of indorsable payload is a table with blank columns

An officer avoids **drafting**, not work. A request that requires him to compose a reply competes
with everything else on his desk; a request that requires him to **fill in three columns of a table
someone else built** does not. The Provincial Assessor request
(`case_work/MWK-001/PROVINCIAL_ASSESSOR_IMPROVEMENTS_VERIFICATION_2026-09.md`) is built this way:
29 rows, four columns pre-filled from the office's own records, three columns blank.

This also disposes of the standing objection in this cluster. A blank-column table is not a request
for information — it is a **verification of the office's own assessment record**, which is the
Provincial Assessor's duty under §472(b)(1) to *"ensure that all laws and policies governing the
appraisal and assessment of real properties… are properly executed."* Same documents, a duty instead
of a favour, and no §4(f) wall in front of it.
