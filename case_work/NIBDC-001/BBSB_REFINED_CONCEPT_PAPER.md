## 1. The model in one paragraph

A "Big Brother / Little Brother" (BBSB) structure pairs a large-scale tenement holder (**Big Brother — NIBDC**, holder of Exploration Permit Application EXPA-000250-V) with an organized small-scale mining cooperative (**Little Brother** — the cooperative holding a Small-Scale Mining Contract inside a declared **Minahang Bayan**). The Little Brother mines selectively in a defined carve-out under the Big Brother's technical and safety supervision; ore flows to a designated processing route; geological data from the rehabilitated historical workings feeds the Big Brother's resource model; and the community gains formal, mercury-free livelihood. The model follows established Philippine coexistence practice and the national policy of formalizing artisanal mining.

It descends from MPSA application APSA-000322-V (2004), converted in 2022 under EO 130 / DAO 2021-25. By Memorandum **MTES-2025-411 dated 10 July 2025**, MGB Region V transmitted the complete application to the MGB Central Office *"for further evaluation and issuance of Exploration Permit."* Region V's evaluation records: financial capability accepted (paid-up capital ₱28,368,500 per the 2024 GIS; Amanah Islamic Bank credit guarantee of 23 April 2025); the sole protest (Yinlu Bicol Mining Corp., 2009) dismissed in 2010; NCIP FPIC compliance certificate; area status and clearance issued; every concerned barangay and municipality favorably endorsed. **Central Office action after July 2025 is to be confirmed — this is the file's single open question.**

## 3. The legal architecture — two sequences, one structure

The relationship between a Minahang Bayan and the EXPA depends entirely on **which issues first**.

**Sequence A — Minahang Bayan declared while the EXPA is pending.**
- NIBDC, as an applicant, holds no "existing mining right" (RA 7076 §3). Its **consent is not required** for the declaration; it receives notice and a 15-day comment window (DAO 2015-03 §9(d)) and may file an adverse claim (§9(g)).
- The declarable basis for an area under a same-mineral pending application is **§8(a) — occupation and active small-scale mining before 1 August 1987**. Paracale's artisanal history and the small-scale workings recorded in NIBDC's own Environmental Work Program support this; the petition must carry that evidence.
- Once declared, the area is **closed to mining applications**, including the Exploration Permit, *"unless with prior consent of the small-scale miners,"* any royalty forming a community trust fund (RA 7942 §19(e)). **The consent lever sits with the Little Brother.**

**Sequence B — Exploration Permit issues first.**
- NIBDC becomes the holder of an existing mining right. A Minahang Bayan carve-out then requires **NIBDC's consent**, is capped at **25% of the area above 500 hectares** (≈465 of 2,360 ha), minimum 20 hectares, and carries a **royalty to NIBDC** of at least 1.5% of gross gold value (DAO 2015-03 §8(c); RA 7076 §14). **The consent lever sits with the Big Brother.**

**Why the structure works in either sequence:** with Allan Villafría Inocalla holding a shareholding and board/officer role in NIBDC **and** standing as principal of the Little Brother cooperative, both consent levers are in the same hands. Boundary, royalty, offtake and data questions become internal coordination rather than adversarial negotiation. A NIBDC letter of non-objection to the Minahang Bayan petition — procurable because the Investors act for President Villarreal — shortens Sequence A's comment step and eliminates the only procedural friction.

**Timing note.** MTES-2025-411 means the EP could issue at any time. Whichever sequence is preferred, the Minahang Bayan petition and the EP file must be managed **together**, not raced against each other.

## 4. The coexistence terms (heads of agreement, to be papered)

1. **Area allocation** — a discrete, surveyed carve-out (tens of hectares; in Sequence B, within the ~465-ha ceiling), boundaries monumented and jointly monitored; the remaining ~1,900 ha reserved for formal exploration.
2. **Coexistence MOA** — boundaries; safety and environmental standards; production reporting; dispute resolution; term and review. Executed between NIBDC and the cooperative **at arm's-length, market-linked terms** — both sides being under common control makes clean papers more important, not less, for PMRB, NCIP, Sanggunian and DENR review.
3. **Ore offtake** — the Little Brother's ore is processed through the **MFPS plant** (mercury-free, planetGOLD-aligned) on transparent, market-linked pricing less agreed treatment charges; secondary processing station handles small batches on a toll basis (Deal Memorandum §5).
4. **Technical and safety support** — Big Brother supplies training, PPE, ground-support and ventilation standards, staged rehabilitation of historical workings, and supervision.
5. **Data sharing** — mapping, channel sampling and surveys from the rehabilitated shafts flow to NIBDC's resource database under agreed protocols; the cooperative receives the operational data it needs. Historical-workings data materially de-risks and cheapens the later drilling program.
6. **Community and SDMP alignment** — local employment preference; once the project advances, Social Development and Management Program commitments institutionalize the benefits.

## 5. The money, stated honestly

NIBDC's 2024 audited statements show **cash of ₱451,850**, a small operating deficit, stockholder advances funding costs, and ₱27.2M of property and equipment comprising nearly the whole balance sheet. The Amanah Islamic Bank instrument is a **credit guarantee lodged with MGB as proof of financial capability — not demonstrated working capital**. Accordingly, and contrary to the 22 August draft, Phase 1–2 activities are **not** fundable "within existing credit facilities": they require external financing. That financing is the Investors' function, and a documented funding commitment for the exploration program is itself stronger financial-capability evidence than a comfort letter — structure per counsel (foreign-equity rules apply: an EP may be foreign-held, MPSA conversion requires 60% Filipino, and the small-scale side is 100% Filipino).
