# Does a Minahang Bayan over EXPA-000250-V need NIBDC's consent — and how the structure gives Allan control
*Working note 2026-08-22. Sources: RA 7076 §§3, 5, 6, 14 (lawphil); DAO 2015-03 §§4, 8, 9 (Revised IRR, via legaldex/e-library). Not legal advice — for Philippine mining counsel to confirm.*

## 1. The short answer
NIBDC's **consent is not a legal prerequisite** — an EXPA is an application, not an "existing mining right." But NIBDC's **non-opposition is what makes the petition move**, because the IRR gives a pending applicant notice, a comment period, and an adverse-claim right, and MGB-V's own control map will flag the overlap. Consent is the cheap way to convert a guaranteed objection window into a clean file.

## 2. What the law actually says
- **RA 7076 §3** — "existing mining right" = a *perfected and subsisting* claim, lease, license or permit. An EXPA is none of these. So §6(b) (MB over lands under existing mining rights that are not active mining areas) and §14 (claimowner royalty) do not, strictly, reach a mere applicant.
- **DAO 2015-03 §8 — areas that may be declared Minahang Bayan:**
  - **(a)** areas already occupied and actively mined by small-scale miners **before 1 Aug 1987** — the statute gives these *priority*. No consent from anyone. Paracale's artisanal workings are centuries old, and NIBDC's own Environmental Work Program (doc 1165) records "present and former small-scale mine workings" in the area. **This is the strongest no-consent route, if the candidate area can be shown to have been mined pre-1987.**
  - **(b)** areas under **pending mining applications** — declarable only if (1) the applicant's declared minerals are *different*, or (2) the application was denied and is on appeal; royalty held in escrow for the applicant. NIBDC's EXPA is for gold/copper; the MB would be gold. **Same mineral, not denied → §8(b) does not open the door.**
  - **(c)** areas under existing **permits/contracts** — declarable up to 25% of the area above 500 ha (for 2,360 ha: max ≈ 465 ha), minimum 20 ha, royalty to the holder (min 1.5% gross for gold), **and only if the holder consents** (§8(c)(2)(iii)). This is the route that applies *once NIBDC holds an EP or MPSA*.
- **DAO 2015-03 §9 — procedure:** (b) MGB Regional Office checks the **control map for prior rights** — EXPA-000250-V will show; (d) where the area conflicts with existing permits **or applications**, the Board **formally notifies** them to comment within 15 days; (g) adverse claims may be filed within 10 days of publication, the Board resolves, appeal lies to the Secretary; (f) NCIP certificate and Sanggunian endorsement required; the whole declaration is subject to DENR Secretary review (RA 7076 §5).

## 3. So where is Jonathan right, and where mistaken
- **Right:** NIBDC cannot veto. An applicant's consent is not on the §8 list the way a permit holder's is. Nobody can tell the PMRB "NIBDC must sign first."
- **Mistaken if it becomes "the EXPA is irrelevant":** (i) with the same mineral and no denial, the only no-consent category that fits is §8(a) (pre-1987 occupation) — the petition must be built on that evidence; (ii) §9(d) guarantees NIBDC a formal notice-and-comment, and §9(g) an adverse claim — a twenty-year applicant objecting at the same regional office that is evaluating its EXPA is the delay mechanism; (iii) the Secretary reviews every declaration.
- **Practical translation:** what the petition needs from NIBDC is a **letter of non-objection / coexistence** to put in the file against §9(d). That is exactly memo §10's "remove opposition to the Minahang Bayan request," and Stephen's "NIBDC consent + coexistence MOA" is the regulator-facing name for the same thing. Because Jonathan and Allan act for Maribel, this is the cheapest document in the whole deal.
- **Check first:** whether Allan's pending MB petition area actually overlaps EXPA-000250-V. If it's in Casalugan (SMBC's already-declared MB), none of this applies. MGB-V's control map / area-status certification answers it.

## 4. How the structure gives Allan controlling rights over the tenement
The control is real, and it comes from Allan being Filipino and sitting on **both sides** of the coexistence:
1. **Control of NIBDC = control of the EXPA.** NIBDC is a Philippine corporation; Allan may hold any percentage with no foreign-equity issue. A controlling shareholding + board seat + officer role (memo §8, Maribel's written sign-off on the nature of the stake) puts the tenement holder under his control. That is the only durable form of "control of the tenement" — not a MOA, which is only as good as the EP that hasn't issued yet.
2. **Big Brother and Little Brother under one hand.** As NIBDC's controlling shareholder Allan gives the non-objection/consent (§9(d) now; §8(c)(2)(iii) later); as leader of the small-scale cooperative he holds the Small-Scale Mining Contract inside the declared area. The carve-out, the offtake terms, and the data-sharing all sit between two entities he controls.
3. **The end-state economics run through him.** Once NIBDC holds an EP/MPSA and consents to the MB carve-out under §8(c), NIBDC receives the §14 royalty (min 1.5% gross gold) from the cooperative — money flowing from one Allan entity to another, with the larger 1,900+ ha retained for formal exploration.
4. **Constraints that shape the design.** (i) The SSMC side must be 100% Filipino — Allan qualifies; Jonathan cannot hold it. (ii) Jonathan's stake in NIBDC: an Exploration Permit may be held by a foreign-owned corporation, but **MPSA conversion requires 60% Filipino** and small-scale contracts exclude foreigners entirely — design NIBDC's cap table for the end state (Allan ≥ 60%), not the EP stage. (iii) Self-dealing optics: the PMRB, NCIP, Sanggunian and Secretary all see the same name on the consent and the contract — the coexistence MOA should be at arm's-length terms with transparent, market-linked offtake pricing, which is precisely what Stephen's §5 describes. (iv) Control of NIBDC also means owning NIBDC's **financial-capability problem** (see AFS/Amanah note) — the EXPA is only worth controlling if it survives MGB-V's evaluation.

## 7. CORRECTION (Jonathan, 2026-08-22) — the Mining Act side of the question
Jonathan's point: a Minahang Bayan declared over part of the EXPA *takes that area away from NIBDC*, so no consent and no Big Brother / Little Brother is required. Verified against the statute:

- **RA 7942 §19(e)** (Areas Closed to Mining Applications): applications "shall not be allowed … in areas covered by small-scale miners as defined by law unless with prior consent of the small-scale miners, in which case a royalty payment … shall be agreed upon by the parties, said royalty forming a trust fund for the socioeconomic development of the community concerned."
- Consequence: while EXPA-000250-V is **pending**, a declared Minahang Bayan is an area closed to that application; at EP issuance MGB will exclude it (or require the small-scale miners' consent + community-trust-fund royalty — the *mirror image* of BBSB, with the lever in the small-scale side's hand). NIBDC's consent is not needed; it gets DAO 2015-03 §9(d) notice and comment, nothing more.
- **What §§2–3 above got wrong:** they treated the §9(d) objection window as the decisive constraint. It is only a delay lever; the substantive outcome (area out of the EP) is governed by RA 7942 §19(e). Sections 2–3 stand as the *procedural* picture; this section is the *substantive* one.
- **What still holds:** (i) timing — MTES-2025-411 (10 Jul 2025) means the EP could issue first, after which NIBDC is a rights-holder and DAO 2015-03 §8(c) consent applies; (ii) the declarable category for a same-mineral pending-application area remains §8(a) (pre-1987 occupation) — the petition must carry that evidence; (iii) Allan holding NIBDC shares makes the sequencing question academic: he controls the consenting party and the cooperative, and under §19(e) NIBDC's later EP over the MB area would proceed on the cooperative's consent with the royalty trust fund — both his.
- **Memo Rev 4** now states the structure this way (Sections 7, 8, 10; compliance notes 6(g)–(i) and 8).
