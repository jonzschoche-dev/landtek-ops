## C. What the corpus says that the study leaves out

1. **NIBDC is illiquid.** 2024 AFS (doc 1166): cash **₱451,850**; total current assets ₱451,850; liabilities ₱389,150; deficit **₱1,078,300** (OCR reads "4,078,300" but 28,368,500 − 27,290,200 = 1,078,300), up from ₱835,000 in 2023. The ₱27.2M is PP&E and is the *entire* balance sheet. The Amanah ₱5M is a credit *guarantee* lodged for MGB financial-capability purposes — the corpus does not show it as drawable working capital. Phases 1–2 are **not** fundable from "existing facilities" on these numbers; they are fundable only with outside money — i.e., this study quietly assumes the kind of financing the deal memo is about, without saying so.
2. **Adverse-claim history.** Astrolabe Mining & Development Corp. filed a letter-complaint against APSA-000322-V (19 Jul 2006, doc 1163 item 28) questioning NIBDC's financial eligibility; NIBDC later obtained a no-protest certification (2009–2010, items 54–55). The study's "regulatory delay" risk ignores that NIBDC's financial capability has been attacked before — and the 2024 AFS invites the same attack.
3. **The APSA lineage.** The tenement is a 2004 MPSA application (APSA-000322-V, 2,360.0466 ha) refiled 26 Aug 2022 as EXPA-000250-V under DAO 2021-25 / EO 130. "Related controlled claims" in the study is unexplained; the Amanah guarantee still cites the APSA docket.
4. **No NIBDC authority.** Nothing in the corpus authorizes Stephen to write in NIBDC's name or to address MGB-V. The only current NIBDC-authored items are Maribel's April 2026 requests for certified copies of the case folder (docs 461, 798) and the 15 Jun 2026 CTC fee receipt (doc 1162) — NIBDC is mid-way through its own records reconciliation. **Stephen Lloyd appears in zero NIBDC-001 documents.**
5. **Who the "Little Brother" is** — unnamed. SMBC is cited only as a precedent. Whether the intended cooperative is SMBC, Allan's pending Minahang Bayan group, or a new entity is the question that decides everything downstream.
