Seven reflexes

  1. Fact, then stop. If you hear yourself start "which shows…", "which proves…" or "the evidence points to…", stop talking.
  2. Minds are quicksand. Never testify to what Cesar, the heirs, your mother or Gloria thought or knew. Say: "I can't speak to what anyone thought. The document says…"
  3. How do you know? → the document. Not 1996, not your mother, not a tip.
  4. Fewer facts, not more. When you're rattled, concede the small truth, then go back to the class.
  5. Concede what's true and known. For what you don't know, say "I can't speak to that." Don't adopt their premise, and don't invent a counter.
  6. Read the terms, defer the law. "What the wording means legally is for counsel and the court."
  7. Which exhibit says this? If you can't name it, don't say it. "I can't speak to that," or "I'd point the court to the document."
  8. Wide question → small answer. "Nothing further, Your Honor. I'll let the record and my counsel present the rest."
