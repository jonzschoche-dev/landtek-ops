Cross-examination checklist: their own witnesses prove our facts

Civil Case No. 26-360 · defense dates 13 January to 10 March 2027 · for counsel · replaces the request for admission and the motion for production, which are held in reserve and not filed.

Why this route. Discovery after the trial has begun is uncommon and may be refused as late. Cross-examination of the defense witnesses, on what they swore and the exhibits they sponsor, is what the court expects. Every answer becomes their own evidence. Ask short, closed questions, each tied to a document, and stop when the answer is on the record. Show only their own exhibits and our marked exhibits. Never show a record held for rebuttal: ask the question that commits the witness, and let the record answer it later. A question that a witness cannot answer truthfully in their favor also opens a door for Patricia's rebuttal.

When a witness mentions a paper we have not seen (a Llamanzares contract, a receipt book, a rent ledger, a permit): ask whether it exists, who holds it and where it is, and ask the court in open court that it be produced at the next setting. Ask once, politely; the record of a refusal is enough.

5. What this leaves to documents alone

  • Already in evidence: the deed naming no title (Exh. H), the survey date (Exh. C p. 2), the registration entries (Exh. B). Held for rebuttal: Cesar's confirmation instrument, the "my property" SPA, the notarial registers, the DENR file, the Primary Entry Book (see the trap map, Annex A).
  • Counsel's note on one trade-off. Rule 26 §5 says a party who never served a request for admission on facts "within the personal knowledge" of the other side may be barred from proving them, "unless otherwise allowed by the court for good cause." Our proof rests on public records and on the defendants' own testimony, so the risk is small, but counsel should weigh it. The drafted request for admission is held in reserve if counsel prefers to serve it.
