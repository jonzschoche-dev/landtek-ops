## 1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY AND THE WINNING THEORY

### The goal
One judgment, in this court, that:
1. declares the Deed of Absolute Sale of **29 September 2016** (Doc. No. 304, Page 61, Book CXVI, Notary Donald E. Asis) **null and void**;
2. **cancels TCT No. 079-2021002126** and **ARP No. GR-2023-II-07-001-00256** and restores Lot 2-X-6-I-4-C-1 (2,587 sqm) to the three registered owners;
3. orders the defendants and everyone admitted by them to **vacate**;
4. declares the post-2016 improvements **forfeited without indemnity** (Art. 449) and denies any right of retention;
5. orders an **accounting of fruits** from **22 August 2025** (receipt of the demand);
6. awards **exemplary damages (₱200,000.00) and attorney's fees (₱100,000.00)**, the figures the En Banc awarded in *Duenas*; and
7. **dismisses the counterclaim**.

### The question the court must answer
Was Gloria H. Balane a purchaser in good faith and for value when she bought, from an agent, a 2,587-square-meter portion of land registered to three sisters who were never consulted and never paid?

### The silver bullet: the class limit in the agent's own authority
The defendants' entire case rests on the **Special Power of Attorney of 16 March 1992** (plaintiff's Exh. E; defense Exh. 7; Annex B). They call it "sufficient written authority to sell." Read its paragraph 7. Cesar M. de la Fuente was appointed:

> "to negotiate for the sale of the above-described real estate property **in favor of third persons in possession of CONTRACTS TO SELL executed by the previous administrator, MR. BEN LLAMANZARES and/or to those who have paid their downpayments or have made partial payments therefor to the latter** … Said sale shall be upon such terms and conditions … **after prior consultation with us**, and to sign and execute the necessary documents … as well as to receive the proceeds."

Two limits, both on the face of a public instrument that the Registry annotated on **TCT T-52540 on 21 June 1996** (Exh. B; Annex C, p. 2):

- **A class of buyers:** holders of a Llamanzares contract to sell, or persons who had paid Llamanzares a down payment or partial payment. The Balanes, by their own verified Answer, were **tenants** paying monthly rent (Answer ¶¶40, 43, 44[b]; Annex A, pp. 3–4). They produced no Llamanzares contract and no Llamanzares receipt. Their earliest receipt is **October 2003**, to Cesar's side (Annex D, p. 2).
- **A condition:** prior consultation with the owners. None was ever made. Their own collector swears the owners "never came home" and that Cesar's contact was the owners' Manila counsel (Salvador JA S31; Annex Q, p. 4).

A sale outside the written authority is **void** (Civil Code Arts. 1874, 1878 ¶5, 1898); "the sale ipso jure is consequently void" (*Cosmic Lumber Corp. v. CA*, G.R. No. 114311, 29 Nov 1996). Because the limits were annotated on the title from 1996, the buyer is charged with them (PD 1529 §52). **The revocation of 2005 is not needed to win; it is the backup.** Lead with the class, and the defendants' best fact (the SPA on the title since 1996, no revocation annotated) becomes ours: the title sent every buyer to the instrument, and the instrument excludes her.

### Why we win on this court's method
Judge Cu decides by **statute, section and exhibit**, in short English paragraphs, and writes operative orders with exact particulars. She has cited no case law in seven writings. Put **Art. 1874 beside Exh. E ¶7 and Exh. B page 3**, **Art. 496 beside Entry No. 2021003236**, and **Art. 449 beside the deed's date and the demand's receipt**, and the judgment writes itself in her register. Everything in this playbook is built to give her exactly that, and nothing that requires her to characterize anyone.

### The one sentence that carries the case
*Cesar sold to a buyer outside the class his authority allowed, without the consultation it required, and the buyer knew the owners, paid them rent for decades, never asked them, and finished the paperwork years after the agent was dead and his authority had been published as revoked.*

## 3. THE THREE PILLARS OF OUR CASE

### Pillar A — The Void Agency

**The rule.** Art. 1874: a sale of land through an agent without written authority "shall be void." Art. 1878 ¶5: a special power is required "to enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted." Art. 1898: a contract by an agent "exceeding the scope of his authority," not ratified, "shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted." A power of attorney "must be strictly construed, and courts will not infer or presume broad powers from deeds which do not sufficiently include property or subject under which the agent is to deal" (*Bautista-Spille v. NICORP*, G.R. No. 214057, 19 Oct 2015). "For the principal to confer the right upon an agent to sell real estate, a power of attorney must so express the powers of the agent in clear and unmistakable language" (*Cosmic Lumber*). An unauthorized agent's land conveyance is void (*Alcantara v. Nido*, G.R. No. 165133, 19 Apr 2010).

**The fact.** Paragraph 7 of Exh. E (Annex B, p. 3) names the class and the condition. "In possession" governs **contracts to sell**, not the land, so an occupant is not in the class by occupying. The Balanes were tenants (Answer ¶¶40, 43; rent receipts to 2014, Annex D), with no Llamanzares contract and no Llamanzares payment; their first payment is October 2003, on a receipt signed "Von dela Fuente" (Def. Exh. 5, p. 2; Annex D, p. 2); the receipt itself does not state the signer's relationship to Cesar, though Salvador's own affidavit identifies him as Cesar's son. Nobody consulted the owners: Salvador S31 (Annex Q, p. 4). The SPA describes only the undivided T-4497 parcel (Annex B, p. 2): use this for strict construction, not as a stand-alone "never covered this lot," because the Registry carried the SPA onto T-52540 in 1996 and the 2005 Era sale was registered under it.

**The class clause, word by word.** "In favor of third persons in possession of CONTRACTS TO SELL executed by the previous administrator, MR. BEN LLAMANZARES and/or to those who have paid their downpayments or have made partial payments therefor to the latter."

| Words | What they do | The defense's reading | Why the defense's reading fails |
|---|---|---|---|
| "third persons in possession of CONTRACTS TO SELL" | Sub-class 1: holders of a written contract to sell | "in possession" means in possession of the land, so any occupant qualifies | The object of "in possession of" is "CONTRACTS TO SELL," and contracts are what the next words say Llamanzares "executed." Reading "possession" as possession of land requires deleting the words that follow it. |
| "executed by the previous administrator, MR. BEN LLAMANZARES" | Ties sub-class 1 to one named man | — | The clause names a person, not a description. No contract to sell from Llamanzares to any Balane has ever been produced or pleaded. |
| "and/or to those who have paid their downpayments or have made partial payments therefor" | Sub-class 2: payors | A tenant who paid rent "paid" | "Therefor" means for the sale. Rent is paid for occupancy, not for a purchase. Their own receipts bill "Payment for the month of." |
| "to the latter" | Payments must have been made to Llamanzares, the last person named | "the latter" is loose; payments to Cesar count | The only person "the latter" can refer to is Llamanzares. Every receipt the Balanes hold is signed by Cesar's son, the earliest in **October 2003**. |
| "have paid … have made" (present perfect, 16 Mar 1992) | The class was closed on the date of the SPA | Later payments brought them in | Payments after 1992, and all payments to Cesar, cannot create membership in a class defined by payments already made to Llamanzares. |
| ¶6, "interested third parties" | The purpose clause | "Interested third parties" is broad, so ¶7 is illustrative | ¶7 begins "Accordingly" and is the operative grant; the specific controls the general. |
| ¶7, "after prior consultation with us" | A condition on every sale | A private arrangement between owners and agent | See the next box. |

**A "downpayment" needs an object and a price; a bare payment to Llamanzares is neither.** Sub-class two is not a separate, looser door. A down payment is a payment on a sale, and "there is no contract unless … consent … object certain … [and] cause" concur (Art. 1318). A sale requires "a determinate thing" and "a price certain" (Art. 1458), a thing "particularly designated or physically segregated from all others of the same class" (Art. 1460), and is perfected only on "a meeting of minds upon the thing … and upon the price" (Art. 1475). Earnest money is "part of the price and … proof of the perfection of the contract" (Art. 1482): it presupposes a perfected sale to be part of. So a member of sub-class two is someone who had agreed with Llamanzares on an identified portion and a price and had started paying for it: the same people as sub-class one, caught at an earlier documentary stage. On the defendants' own evidence no object or price existed in Llamanzares's time: the portion was first "segregated" by a survey they paid for in **December 2006** (Annex D, p. 1); "Lot Area: 2,587" first appears on a receipt dated **7 August 2016** (Annex D, p. 29); and the price of ₱250,000 was fixed by Cesar (Gloria S33 [COND]), not by Llamanzares. A ₱3,000 payment in the 1980s with no lot, no price and no receipt is, at most, money handed to the administrator, which on their own Answer is what rent was. It is not a down payment "therefor."

**Who must prove membership.** The defense pleads it as its fourth affirmative defense (Answer ¶40) and as the first of its ten good-faith circumstances (¶44[a] to [c]). An affirmative allegation is proved by the party who makes it (Rule 131 §1), and the buyer who claims good faith carries that burden anyway (*Bucton*; *Duenas*). They must produce a Llamanzares contract or a payment to Llamanzares. They have produced neither.

Their collector's 2025 letter says the same from the other side (Annex Y).

**A crack in our own Reply to manage before the memorandum.** Our Reply of April 2026 (¶14) answered the Art. 1921 defense by saying the 1992 agency "was not created for the purpose of dealing with the defendants specifically **or with any other specified persons**." The second clause overshoots: the SPA does name specified persons, the Llamanzares class, and our theory depends on it. The memorandum should restate the point correctly: Art. 1921 protects only the persons the agency was entrusted to deal with; the SPA names that class; the defendants are outside it; and on either reading Art. 1921 gives them nothing. Do not let Ramos read ¶14 aloud before we have said this ourselves.

**What "prior consultation with us" means, and why it is not a "secret order."** The clause does not define consultation; its plain meaning is that before the agent fixed the terms of any sale he had to refer it to the three owners. The defense will reach for Art. 1902 ("Private or secret orders and instructions of the principal do not prejudice third persons who have relied upon the power of attorney") and Art. 1900 (acts "within the terms of the power of attorney as written" bind the principal "even if the agent has in fact exceeded the limits of his authority according to an understanding between the principal and the agent"). Both articles protect a buyer against limits she could not see. This limit is in the instrument itself, annotated on the title since 1996 (PD 1529 §52), and therefore one of the "terms … as written," not an "understanding" outside them. A buyer "put upon inquiry" must "discover upon his peril the authority of the agent" (*San Pedro v. Ong*) and ascertain "the nature and extent of the agent's authority" (*Yoshizaki*); reading paragraph 7 and asking Cesar for proof that the owners had been consulted, or writing to the owners at the California address on their own tax declaration, was the inquiry the law demanded, and none was made (Salvador S31; Gloria S43 [COND]). **Weight it correctly:** the class is the stronger limit because it is objective and the buyer could test it against her own papers; consultation is the corroborating limit, proved through their own collector (no owner ever spoke to Cesar; his only contact was the Agcaoili office) and through the agent's duty to "act in accordance with the instructions of the principal" (Art. 1887). The Answer never alleges that any consultation occurred. Lead with the class; use consultation to show both that the sale was outside the written terms and that the buyer never asked.

**Their own witness reads it our way.** Salvador's letter (Annex Y): the instruction reached "the people … who possesses CONTRACT OF SALE issued by Benjamin Llamanzares"; everyone else was "appease[d]" with paper.

**Concede the right thing.** Our affidavit says the SPA was "limited to negotiation" (Q26, Q53). Paragraph 7 also lets Cesar "sign and execute the necessary documents" and "receive the proceeds," so a court will find a power to complete a sale **for the class**. Concede that wording on cross and hold the class. The class needs no construction against the grant; it is the grant.

**The revocation, in its place.** On **15 Aug 2005** the owners disowned every SPA but the new one (Exh. F, Annex H); it was published **25 Sep–9 Oct 2020** (Exh. G); it was never annotated. Order of argument: (1) the sale was outside the agency, so revocation is irrelevant to her; (2) in the alternative, the publication is a fact bearing on her good faith before registration. **Never** argue that the 2020 publication was Art. 1922 "sufficient warning" while also arguing Art. 1922 does not apply; Cesar's powers were special and class-limited, not general (*Central Surety v. Hodges*, G.R. No. L-28633, 30 Mar 1971: Art. 1922 governs an agent authorized to deal "not with a particular person, but with the public in general"). Art. 1921 protects only the "specified persons"; she was never one. PD 1529 §64 (registration of a revoking instrument) is a notice device, not a condition of validity (*Republic v. Ciruelas*, G.R. No. 239505, 17 Feb 2021), and only matters for a sale **within** the power.

**Salvador is not Cesar.** Cesar's 29 Feb 2012 paper to Salvador (Annex Z) passed administration only, from an agent revoked in 2005: nothing to delegate, and a substitute never holds more than the agent had. **Do not argue that substitution was forbidden**: Art. 1892 permits a substitute unless prohibited, and *Escueta v. Lim* (G.R. No. 137162, 24 Jan 2007) upheld a substitute's sale under an SPA silent on substitution. Argue "nothing left to substitute." **Consequences.** A void contract "produces no effect either against or in favor of anyone and cannot be ratified … laches will not set in against a void transaction" (*MCIAA v. Unchuan*, G.R. No. 182537, 1 Jun 2016); the action does not prescribe (Art. 1410); "registration does not vest title" (*Heirs of Gonzales v. Basas*, G.R. No. 206847, 15 Jun 2022). The estate receipts went to a revoked agent and no owner saw a peso; the agent's own Deed of Confirmation (Annex G, p. 1) is not the principals' ratification.

### Pillar B — The Bad Faith Buyer

**Burden and standard.** "The burden of proving the status of a purchaser in good faith and for value lies upon one who asserts that status. This onus probandi cannot be discharged by mere invocation of the ordinary presumption of good faith" (*Heirs of Bucton v. Spouses Go*, G.R. No. 188395, 20 Nov 2013; *Duenas v. Metrobank*, G.R. No. 209463, En Banc, 29 Nov 2022; *Vallido v. Pono*, G.R. No. 200173, 15 Apr 2013). Art. 527's presumption governs possessors; IPV status is an affirmative claim.

**A buyer from an agent must inquire.** "Every person dealing with an agent is put upon inquiry, and must discover upon his peril the authority of the agent … If a person makes no inquiry, he is chargeable with knowledge of the agent's authority" (*San Pedro v. Ong*, G.R. No. 177598, 17 Oct 2008). "In case the buyer does not deal with the registered owner … the law requires that a higher degree of prudence be exercised" (*Bucton*). "Despite the presence of an alleged SPA from the registered owners, respondent should have inquired with the registered owners as to the extent of the authority of the attorney-in-fact, especially when the property is co-owned" (*Spouses Yabut v. Nachbaur*, G.R. No. 243470, 12 Jan 2021). The presumption applies only if the seller is the registered owner, in possession, and the buyer unaware of any defect in his capacity; "absent one or two of the foregoing conditions, then the law itself puts the buyer on notice" (*Duenas*; *Chua v. Republic*, G.R. No. 253305, 2 Aug 2023).

**All three conditions fail.** The seller was not the registered owner; he was not in possession (she was, as a tenant; and he swore under oath he was: Annex F, p. 14); two days before the deed he swore the duplicate was lost (Annex C, p. 3), the *Vallido* warning sign. She knew the owners (rent for decades), held their California address on a tax declaration she herself cited in 2020 (Annex M, p. 4), and knew their Manila counsel was the contact (Salvador S31). She never asked. *Bucton* found it "inconceivable" that buyers who knew the owners made no inquiry. The defense's *Sarili* line (face inspection of a notarized SPA suffices; G.R. No. 193517, 15 Jan 2014) defeats her: the face of this SPA contains the class and the consultation clause. *Tolentino v. Latagan* (G.R. No. 179874, 2015) is **adverse** (buyer upheld as IPV) and distinguishable (buyer dealt with the title-holder, not an agent).

**Good faith had to last until 23 November 2021.** "Buyers of registered land must be continuing purchasers for value and in good faith until the registration of the conveyance. In the event a buyer … is made aware of any claim or interest of some other person in the property, or of any defect or restriction in the title of the seller or in his or her capacity to convey title, the buyer shall no longer be considered to be in good faith even if he or she subsequently registers the conveyance" (*Duenas*, En Banc). PD 1529 §51: registration is "the operative act."

Manantan*, G.R. No. 158328, 23 Feb 2007); "a person whose occupation of realty is by sheer tolerance of the owner is not a possessor in good faith" (*NHA v. Manila Seedling Bank*, G.R. ### Pillar C — The Impossible Partition

**The rule.** Art. 1877: "An agency couched in general terms comprises only acts of administration." Art. 1878 requires a special power "(12) To create or convey real rights over immovable property" and "(15) Any other act of strict dominion." Art. 496: "Partition may be made by agreement between the parties or by judicial proceedings." The parties to a partition of co-owned land are the co-owners.

**The fact.** Neither SPA grants any power to partition or subdivide; the 1992 SPA's general clause (¶8, "any and every act … requisite, necessary or proper") is exactly what Art. 1877 confines to administration, and it was revoked in 2005 anyway. No co-owner signed or knew of the Partition-Subdivision Agreement of **15 Mar 2017** (Doc. 402/Pg. 81/Bk. CXXII). Its chronology is impossible on its face: it says the land was subdivided "in accordance with subdivision plan (LRA) PSD-E2020005406," a plan **surveyed 8 Sep 2020 and approved 6 Apr 2021** — dates the **defendants' own Exh. 1, page 2** recites. In Asis's Book CXXII the Deed of Confirmation of **28 Mar 2017 is Doc. 115** while this agreement of **15 Mar 2017 is Doc. 402**: the later act sits 287 entries earlier. Cesar was alive on 15 March 2017 and died 21 June 2017; he is the only person who could have purported to sign for the owners, and if anyone else did so after his death the instrument is worse than unauthorized. The survey of 8 Sep 2020 was certified "with the full knowledge and consent of the owner(s)" when no owner knew and the agent was three years dead (Annex L, p. 2).

**Their best answer on the survey, and ours.** The defense will say the field survey was done in **2006** (Princess's Villafuerte receipt of 29 Dec 2006, Annex D, p. 1), while Cesar was alive and "under his management," and that the 2020 plan merely formalized it for LRA approval on the strength of that earlier consent. Four answers, in order of strength. (1) Consent from Cesar in 2006 was consent from nobody: his authority had been revoked on **15 Aug 2005**, and even the 1992 SPA never contained a power to subdivide or partition. (2) The 2020 plan certifies a survey "**SURVEYED: September 8, 2020**," not 2006; an LRA-approved plan certifies the survey it describes, and the DENR file will show whether it was a re-survey, who requested it, and whether any 2006 plan was ever submitted. (3) The partition's annotation says the land was subdivided "in accordance with subdivision plan number (LRA) PSD-E2020005406," a number that did not exist until the EPEB entry of **17 Sep 2020**; if the instrument itself names that plan, it could not have been written on 15 Mar 2017 whatever happened in the field in 2006. That is why the instrument must be read, not inferred. (4) On 8 Sep 2020 the registered owners were the three sisters; the engineer certified their "full knowledge and consent" when none of them knew, the only agent was dead, and the Balanes were not owners. On the defense's best version, the certification is still false as to the owners. *Until the DENR file and the instrument are in hand, the antedating is an inference, marked as such; the chronology, the revocation and the absence of any partition power are facts.*

**An independent ground to cancel the title.** The Balane title rests on two instruments: the deed and the partition. Even if the court upheld the deed, a buyer of a portion of co-owned registered land holds at most an interest in the co-ownership; a title to a specific bounded lot can issue only on a partition made by the co-owners or ordered by the court. None exists. **TCT No. 079-2021002126 falls on any view of the deed.** Plead it in the alternative so the court has a second, narrower path to the same order.

**What not to argue.** Never say Cesar lacked authority for the 1993–2000 subdivisions that produced T-52540 itself. Those titles issued to the owners, the owners hold and sue on T-52540, and that is ratification (Art. 1910); the argument would attack our own root of title and reopen every lot in the 2-X-6 tree. The 1993 Subdivision Agreement (Annex AA) has one use: it names Cesar's actual transferees, and no Balane is among them. Aim "no power to partition" at the 2017 instrument only.

**Special Proceeding 2156** is used for what Cesar said and hid, not to annul the order. *Strait Times v. CA* (G.R. No. 126673, 28 Aug 1998) voids a replacement duplicate only if the original was not in fact lost, and that attack belongs in the Court of Appeals, not here. We cannot yet prove the duplicate was not lost; we can prove the petition concealed the sale, listed a dead title, and reached the owners by posting only.

### 5.4 The Proposed Judgment (the WHEREFORE she can adopt)

> Considering that the Special Power of Attorney dated March 16, 1992 (Exhibit "E"), annotated on Transfer Certificate of Title No. T-52540 on June 21, 1996 (Exhibit "B"), authorized Cesar M. de la Fuente to sell only to third persons holding contracts to sell executed by the previous administrator or who had paid him down payments or partial payments, and only after prior consultation with the registered owners; considering that defendant Gloria H. Balane, by her own Answer, occupied the property as a lessee and produced no contract to sell and no payment to the previous administrator; considering that no consultation with the registered owners was made; and considering that under Section 52 of Presidential Decree No. 1529 the annotation was constructive notice of its limits to all persons from June 21, 1996, the Deed of Absolute Sale dated September 29, 2016 (Exhibit "H") was executed beyond the scope of the written authority and is void.
