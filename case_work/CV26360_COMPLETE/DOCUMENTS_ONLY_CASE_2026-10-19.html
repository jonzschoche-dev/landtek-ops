The answer. On paper alone, every element of Article 1898 is proved, and two of the three by the defendants' own papers. Article 1898: if the agent contracts "exceeding the scope of his authority, and the principal does not ratify the contract, it shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal."

  1. Outside the scope. Paragraph 7 allows sale only to holders of Llamanzares contracts to sell or those who paid Llamanzares. The defendants' own receipt exhibit has 111 slips and not one from Llamanzares or Violeta. The first is dated 2003. The only slip marked "Down payment" is dated 6 January 2005 and signed for Cesar.
  2. Not ratified. The principals disowned the power in 2005, eleven years before the deed. No paper signed by any principal approves the sale. The "confirmation" is signed by Cesar alone.
  3. Aware of the limits. The power, paragraph 7 included, has been on the title since 21 June 1996. Their verified Answer says "Gloria checked the SPA."

The one gap. Their Answer pleads a ₱3,000 down payment to Llamanzares "in the 1980s." No paper supports it, and their own receipt exhibit contradicts it. It is their defence, and "burden of proof never shifts" (Rule 131, §1). Part 3 lists three moves that turn the gap into a nail, all by paper.

3. The gap, and three paper moves to close it

The gap. Answer ¶40: "in the 1980s they were told they could buy the area they occupied … they paid a downpayment of Php3,000.00 in installments." No contract to sell, no Llamanzares receipt, no Violeta receipt. Their own best paper, Exhibit 5, puts the down payment in 2005, paid to Cesar's side. It is their affirmative defence; the burden to prove it is theirs and "never shifts" (Rule 131, §1).

Where the gap could reopen. If the court admits Gloria's excluded judicial affidavit (the motion to admit is pending), her account is first-hand, not hearsay: the ₱3,000 was paid to Manuel Violeta in three instalments of ₱1,000, with no receipt, "itinala niya ang mga bayad sa kaniyang brown columnar book" (S21 to S22). Testimony of payment needs no receipt to be admissible; the Original Document Rule does not apply to it. The court weighs it against her own papers: her verified Answer calls what she paid through Violeta "monthly rent" (¶40); her own receipt exhibit puts the down payment in 2005, ₱20,000, to the agent's side; and the book she describes is a collector's rent ledger. Two consequences: (a) keeping her affidavit excluded matters, so the opposition to the motion to admit should be pressed [COUNSEL]; (b) the book, on her own account, went to Cesar; the Agcaoili office knows nothing of it (Jonathan, 19 Oct 2026). If it exists it is on the agent's side, and Salvador is the one to produce it.

  1. Ask for the Llamanzares paper. Salvador swears that in about 1992 he and Cesar went to Llamanzares's house "para kunin ang mga papeles ng lupain" (S15). If any contract to sell or Llamanzares receipt for the Balanes exists, it came into the agent's side's hands then. A subpoena for Salvador to bring the Llamanzares papers when he testifies puts it to the test.
