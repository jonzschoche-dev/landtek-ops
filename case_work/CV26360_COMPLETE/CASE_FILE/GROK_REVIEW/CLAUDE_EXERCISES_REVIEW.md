## Verdict

This is serious, mostly sound trial planning, and in places it is better than ours. In particular, it is the first document to face the demurrer problem head-on: Jonathan has no first-hand knowledge of anything before 2025. It also follows the locked positions. It refuses "negotiate ≠ sell", refuses to argue the SPA is void on its face, leads with Tumibay and the ¶7 class, and treats the revocation as backup. It keeps the excluded topics out of the case-in-chief.

But its central promise does not hold up against its own tables. It says "every element proved twice without Jonathan by 9 Dec". Element 4 (no consultation, no ratification) and parts of elements 6, 7 and 10 are sourced to defense-stage cross or to records it deliberately holds back for rebuttal. The class leg, which it says "carries the demurrer", is also the defense's best counter-fact: an alleged 1980s Llamanzares down payment, pleaded in Answer ¶40. The "quiet gathering / spring on rebuttal" trap is clever. It is also the plan's biggest procedural risk before a judge who has written "No further liberality shall be accorded." If the court says the records belonged in our case-in-chief, or the defense simply demurs, the trap never springs. **Adopt the structure, fix the four discrepancies below, and do not rest on 9 Dec until the element matrix is honestly filled from pre-marked exhibits and admissions alone.**

## Findings by severity

### Critical

**C1. "Every element proved twice without Jonathan" is contradicted by the plan's own matrix.**
- *Claim:* Overwhelm Plan Part 2 and Cover Memo Priority 2 say every element is in the 9 Dec offer at least twice.
- *Source checked:* Overwhelm Part 2 rows 4, 6, 7 and 10; Trap Map Part 4.
- *Status:* **DISCREPANCY.**
  - Row 4 (no consultation) lists cross of Salvador, Princess and Erwin (January 2027), notarial registers "offered on rebuttal", and Patricia "on rebuttal only". The only case-in-chief item is Exh. G, and the plan itself says Exh. G is not proof of 2016.
  - Row 6 relies on Registry file p. 20 and the CC 8563 record, both "held for rebuttal".
  - Row 10 admits it has no peso figure.
  - The Cover Memo also says consultation "rests on the notarial registers" in the case-in-chief, while the Trap Map holds those registers back. The two documents contradict each other.
- *Fix:* Re-label the matrix honestly, splitting **case-in-chief (pre-marked + Answer)** from **defense stage / rebuttal**. For the demurrer, element 4 must be carried by the burden rule, not by our proof. Persons dealing with an agent must ascertain the agent's authority, and the buyer asserting good faith carries that burden. *Heirs of Sarili v. Lagrosa*, G.R. 193517 (15 Jan 2014): "the buyer also has the duty to ascertain … the latter's legal authority to convey" — VERIFIED in our lawphil copy. *Bucton*, G.R. 188395 (20 Nov 2013) — VERIFIED. Combine that with the deed reciting no consultation and the Answer's silence. Do not leave Rule 131 §1 as the plan's only burden statement.

**C2. The class leg is weaker than "it carries the demurrer" suggests.**
- *Claim:* Overwhelm Part 0 says the class limit, proved by the SPA, the Answer and pre-marked exhibits, carries the demurrer.
- *Source checked:* Balane Trajectory Part 2 row 1 (Answer ¶40: a Llamanzares payment "recognized and later credited"); ¶7 text in SPA_CLAUSE_DISSECTION, row ¶7.
- *Status:* **DISCREPANCY (internal).** The trajectory itself calls this "their best card". ¶7 covers anyone who "paid their down-payments or have made partial payments … to the latter". So the Answer does not admit we are right on the class. It pleads the opposite.
- *Fix:* In the case-in-chief, the class leg must rest on documents:
  - the first receipt (Oct 2003), on the dela Fuente side;
  - the only "Down payment" receipt (6 Jan 2005);
  - monthly "Occupant" receipts running to 2014;
  - Answer ¶¶40/43 (tenancy).

  Add the consultation leg by burden (C1) and the revocation as a third, independent leg. Never present the class as self-proving.

### High

**H1. The trap / hold-for-rebuttal strategy carries real exclusion risk.**
- *Claim:* Trap Map Part 1 says rebuttal and impeachment evidence fall outside the pre-marking bar (A.M. 03-1-09-SC).
- *Source checked:* Rule 30 §5(f), as quoted. The A.M. 03-1-09-SC text was taken from secondary reproductions, which the plan itself flags.
- *Status:* **UNVERIFIED** for A.M. 03-1-09-SC. The rule is right in principle. But a record proving our own element (for example, no owner on the notarial register) is original-case evidence. A court can exclude it on rebuttal as evidence that "should have been presented in chief". The plan names this risk but leaves it unsolved.
- *Fix:* Split the held records:
  1. Records that prove our elements: offer in chief, with good-cause leave sought early, which costs little before the defense has testified.
  2. Records that only contradict a defense witness's sworn claim (Gloria's "checked the Registry", Princess's "no dispute before 2025"): hold for impeachment and rebuttal.

  Read A.M. 03-1-09-SC in full before relying on it.

**H2. A wrong article for voidness.**
- *Claim:* Trajectory row 5 says "Art. 1874 makes it void."
- *Source checked:* the Art. 1874 text quoted in *Sarili*: "authority … shall be in writing; otherwise, the sale shall be void."
- *Status:* **DISCREPANCY.** Here the authority *is* in writing, so Art. 1874 does not void the sale. Our theory is an act beyond the written limits: Art. 1898 (void if the buyer was aware of the limits) plus *Tumibay*. Ramos will answer with Art. 1403(1) (unenforceable, and so ratifiable). That makes the ratification defense live, and "void, Art. 1409" no longer closes it.
- *Fix:* Cite Arts. 1881, 1898 and *Tumibay*. Show the buyer's awareness of the limits through the deed's recital of the SPA and the 1996 annotation (PD 1529 §52). Treat non-ratification as a fact point, using Exh. G and the 2023 adverse claim (Exh. I), not as automatic. The Cover Memo's "void (Arts. 1874, 1898, 1409)" ordering needs the same fix.
