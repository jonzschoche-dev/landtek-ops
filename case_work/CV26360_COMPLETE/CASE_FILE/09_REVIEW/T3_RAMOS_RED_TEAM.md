# TASK 3: RED TEAM. The best memorandum Atty. Ramos could write on this record, and our answer to each point
Second-chair review, 14 Oct 2026 (PHT). Internal work product for Jonathan and Atty. Barandon. Not a pleading. It uses only articles and exhibits in the record. Cases are cited only where verified in T1.

## Verdict
Ramos's strongest ground is not "negotiate only." It is this chain:
1. The SPA, annotated since 1996, gives Cesar power to sign, execute and receive the proceeds.
2. The class clause is a limit **between principal and agent** (Arts. 1900, 1902): "prior consultation with us" reads as an internal instruction, and the annotation summarized the power as "to sign, negotiate for the sale," without the class.
3. The revocation was never registered (§64) and was published only in 2020, after the 2016 deed (Art. 1922; *Central Surety*).
4. Any excess makes the sale unenforceable, not void (Art. 1403(1)). It was ratified by years of the estate's receipts, by Patricia's 2023 affidavit of loss on T-2021002127, and by the owners' silence.

Our answers hold on (1) and (3). They are **weakest on (2), the consultation clause as an internal instruction, and on (4), the 2023 affidavit**. The record also hands Ramos three gifts we should neutralize now:
- the pre-revocation 2005 "Down payment" and "payment for lot" receipts;
- the 2006 survey receipt ("survey done in 2006");
- our own Exh. F ¶4 and JA Q26 wording.

## PART ONE: RAMOS'S MEMORANDUM (as he would write it)

**R1. The SPA is sufficient written authority (Arts. 1874, 1878 ¶5).** Exh. E ¶7 empowers Cesar "to sign and execute the necessary documents … as well as to receive the proceeds." The SPA was annotated on T-52540 on 21 June 1996 as authority "to sign, negotiate for the sale" (Exh. B p. 3), and re-entered in 2021 "with power to sign and negotiate for the sale of the land in this title" (Exh. B p. 5). Plaintiff's own JA Q25 and Q53 and Complaint ¶25 say the SPA was "only to negotiate." Her witness has now abandoned that position, and the court should weigh the shift.

**R2. Gloria was in the class (Answer ¶40).** The family paid rent to Llamanzares through Manuel Violeta, was told in the 1980s it could buy, and paid ₱3,000. The 6 Jan 2005 receipt says "Down payment" (Annex D p. 3). ¶7 covers persons "who have paid their down-payments or have made partial payments." Plaintiff produced no Llamanzares ledger to show who paid what. The absence of a 40-year-old receipt is not proof of non-payment, and the only living witness on the point (Salvador) says the family was a buyer.

**R3. Cesar's camp sold openly with the owners' knowledge (Arts. 1900, 1911).** Pre-revocation receipts "payment for lot" run 2003–2005 (Annex D pp. 2–3). Cesar sold to Era in April 2005 under the same SPA, registered without objection (Exh. B p. 3); Hansol in 1999 (Def. Exh. 3/4). The owners "allowed" the agent to act as though with full powers (Art. 1911).

**R4. "Prior consultation" is an internal instruction (Art. 1902).** A third person may rely on the power of attorney "and not on private or secret orders." Whether Cesar telephoned Los Angeles is a matter between principal and agent. A buyer cannot audit it, and plaintiff offers no witness who was in a position to know that consultation did **not** happen. Jonathan was not attorney-in-fact until 2025.

**R5. The revocation does not bind Gloria (Arts. 1921, 1922; PD 1529 §§52, 64).** Exh. F does not name Cesar. It was never registered although §64 requires it (Exh. B: none). It was published in September–October 2020, four years after the deed (Exh. G). Under Art. 1922, publication is "sufficient warning" only from then on. *Central Surety v. Hodges* holds the principal to the consequences of failing to publish. The Complaint ¶50 claim that the SPA was "annotated only in 2021" is false (Exh. B p. 3, 1996).

**R6. At most unenforceable, and ratified (Arts. 1317, 1403(1), 1910).** The estate's receipt books ("HEIRS OF MARY W. KEESEY ESTATE," TIN 160-902-582) took the price (Annex D pp. 29–32). Cesar's Deed of Confirmation (Doc. 115, 28 Mar 2017) confirmed the sale. Patricia's 22 Feb 2023 affidavit of loss was entered on T-2021002127, the title the partition created (Annex G p. 4). That is acceptance of the partition. Reply ¶14 [VERIFY text] concedes payments to the estate.

**R7. Registration and indefeasibility (PD 1529 §§48, 51, 53).** TCT 079-2021002126 issued on a deed and partition presented with the court-issued owner's duplicate (Entry 2017000693), "conclusive authority" to register under §53. The attack on the partition, Spec. Proc. 2156 and T-2021002127 is collateral (§48). The estate and heirs of Cesar, and the RD, are indispensable (Answer ¶38).

**R8. Death of the agent is irrelevant (Arts. 1919(3), 1931).** The sale was executed in 2016 while Cesar lived; registration is a ministerial act of the RD, not of the agent.

**R9. The partition is regular.** The survey was done in 2006: Princess's Villafuerte receipt of 29 Dec 2006, "subdivision survey … segregating a portion thereof in favor of the above-named" (Annex D p. 1). The 2020 plan merely formalized it. The plan number was supplied at registration. The instrument is notarized and presumed regular.

**R10. Laches and estoppel (Answer ¶43).** Fifty years of open possession; owners abroad who left everything to Cesar; no adverse claim until after the 2021 title; no demand until August 2025.

**R11. Good faith (Answer ¶44; Art. 527; *Sarili*).** Cesar showed the notarized SPA and the mother title. Under *Heirs of Sarili v. Lagrosa*, "mere inspection of the face of such public document already constitutes sufficient inquiry." The title showed the SPA annotated and no revocation. Good faith is presumed (Art. 527).

**R12. Plaintiff's documents are incomplete and inconsistent.**
- Exh. E (pp. 2–3 of 4) and Exh. H (p. 1 of 2) are incomplete.
- The JA says the sale was "September 26, 2019" and the area "28,981."
- Complaint ¶25 says "September 24."
- The witness introduced himself as "Jayjay Moreno" (Princess S14).
- Exh. F ¶4 itself speaks of properties "fraudulently sold."

**R13. Plaintiff's witnesses are interested and impeached.** Donata King was prosecuted for perjury on a complaint arising from this land. She and Francia de los Reyes were the defendants in Civil Case 8563 against Juntilla and Torralba (Annex N p. 1), so they are adverse parties in a related suit. Shirley De Leon says her own father "bought their spot," which proves Cesar's camp sold to occupants.

**R14. Builders in good faith (Arts. 448, 546, 548; *Macasaet*; *DepEd v. Casibang*).** The family built with the knowledge of the owners' administrators over decades. Under *Casibang*, a possessor by tolerance who builds with the owner's permission is a builder in good faith. Reimbursement and retention follow.

**R15. The families on the lot show the owners' tolerance.** About ten families live on the land and never paid the owners (playbook F39). The owners' own inaction let them settle, so the court cannot single out the Balanes.

## PART TWO: OUR ANSWER, POINT BY POINT (strength rated)

**A1 (to R1): STRONG.**
- We concede the power to sign and receive: for the class, after consultation. Strict construction applies (*Bautista-Spille*, G.R. 214057, 19 Oct 2015). §52 makes the **registered instrument**, not the Registry's summary of it, constructive notice; the 1996 entry refers to the SPA acknowledged before Consul Munda on 16 Mar 1992 (Exh. B p. 3). *Sarili* says inspect the face of the SPA, and the face of Exh. E contains ¶7.
- The JA wording is corrected on the record. **Weak spot:** our own SJ motion (doc 393 ¶¶5–6) and JA Q53 led with "negotiate ≠ sell." Concede the change once, then never argue it again.

**A2 (to R2): STRONG on the record, with a burden caveat.**
- ¶7's down-payment holders are those who paid "to the latter," meaning Llamanzares (Exh. E ¶7). The only "Down payment" receipt is to "Von dela Fuente," 6 Jan 2005 (Annex D p. 3). That is payment to Cesar's camp, not to Llamanzares. The Answer's own ¶40 calls the 1975–1980s payments "monthly rent," and the receipts 2007–2014 are monthly "Occupant" bills [VERIFY Annex D pp. 9–26].
- The burden to prove IPV status, including class membership, is theirs (*Bucton*, G.R. 188395, 20 Nov 2013). **Caveat:** the burden on *class membership* as an element of plaintiff's nullity claim may be held to be plaintiff's (Rule 131 §1). Jonathan's and the owners' "no Llamanzares contract was ever shown" is negative evidence. Strengthen it with Salvador's own letter (Annex Y), foundation laid on cross (Rule 132 §13), and with the 1993 Subdivision Agreement's list of Llamanzares-era buyers that omits Balane (Annex AA [OFFER]).

**A3 (to R3): MEDIUM.**
- Era (28 Apr 2005) and Hansol (1999, on the 1991 SPA per the playbook [VERIFY Def. Exh. 3/4]) predate the revocation and say nothing about class membership. Art. 1911 needs the principal to "allow" the agent to act beyond his powers. From 15 Aug 2005 the principals' act was a written disowning (Exh. F), then publication (Exh. G).
- **Weakness:** the 2003–2005 "payment for lot" receipts (Annex D pp. 2–3) show Cesar's camp treating the Balanes as buyers before the revocation, and the owners cannot show they objected then. Answer: receipts to the agent's son are not acts of the principals. Art. 1911 looks at the **principal's** conduct, and none is shown. Don't argue the receipts were rent: they say "payment for lot."

**A4 (to R4): WEAKEST POINT; handle with care.**
- Art. 1902 protects reliance on the power of attorney against "private or secret orders." The consultation clause is not a private order: it is written in ¶7 of the instrument registered in 1996 (Exh. E; Exh. B p. 3), so the buyer is charged with it (§52; *San Pedro v. Ong*, G.R. 177598: one dealing with an agent "must discover upon his peril the authority of the agent"; *Yabut v. Nachbaur*, G.R. 243470: inquire with the registered owners "especially when the property is co-owned").
- **Proof of non-consultation** cannot come from Jonathan's personal knowledge before 2025. Sources:
  - (a) Patricia, by her reserved affidavit (Ex. O). This is the best witness. Decide now; it must be in by 9 Dec.
  - (b) Salvador's S31, that the owners never came home and dealt through Manila counsel (Annex Q p. 4 [VERIFY]).
  - (c) Agcaoili's 15 Aug 2019 letter (Annex J [OFFER]).
- **Recommendation:** make the class, not consultation, the lead ground. Consultation is the second limb.

**A5 (to R5): STRONG if sequenced.**
- First: the sale was outside the agency, so Arts. 1921–1922 never engage (Art. 1921 protects only "specified persons"; Gloria was not one).
- Second: non-registration of a revocation does not make it ineffective. *Republic v. Ciruelas*, G.R. 239505, 17 Feb 2021: §64 "does not state that the registration of an SPA is a prerequisite to its validity or conversely, its non-registration makes the agent's authority ineffective."
- Third: Art. 1922 governs agents with **general** powers dealing with the public (*Central Surety*). Cesar's were special and class-limited.
- Never argue the 2020 publication as Art. 1922 notice of the 2016 sale. Use it only for good faith before registration (Art. 528; *Duenas*: good faith "until registration of the sale on the title").
- **Fix our own error:** Complaint ¶50 ("annotated only in 2021"). Correct it by manifestation before Ramos offers it.

**A6 (to R6): MEDIUM; the 2023 affidavit is the soft spot.**
- A sale beyond the written authority of an agent selling land, to a buyer charged with the limits, is **void** (Art. 1898 "void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits"; Art. 1874; *Cosmic Lumber*, G.R. 114311). Void contracts "cannot be ratified" (*MCIAA v. Unchuan*, G.R. 182537; Art. 1409).
- The Deed of Confirmation is the **agent's** own act (Exh. B p. 6: "EXECUTED BY CESAR M. DE LA FUENTE"). It is not the principals' ratification. Keep it Barandon-only.
- Receipts went to a revoked agent and his collector, and no principal received anything (Jonathan; Patricia's Ex. O).
- **Weak:**
  - Art. 1898's "void" depends on awareness. If the court holds Gloria unaware, Art. 1403(1) "unenforceable" becomes available, and ratification becomes a live question.
  - Patricia's 2023 affidavit of loss on T-2021002127 [VERIFY Annex G p. 4 text]. Our answer: an owner who swears loss of a duplicate she never held protects whatever the Registry shows. Ratification of an excess of authority must be express or tacit (Art. 1910). Tacit ratification needs "knowledge of the reason" and "an act which necessarily implies an intention to waive" (Art. 1393, written for voidable contracts; cite by analogy only). She must say this herself in Ex. O, in one paragraph.
  - Reply ¶14: read it before Ramos does [UNVERIFIED].

**A7 (to R7): STRONG.**
- The caption the court itself recites prays for "Cancellation of Transfer Certificate of Title No. 079-2021002126" (Annex AB p. 4). This is the direct proceeding §48 requires.
- §53's conclusive authority runs "in favor of every purchaser for value and in good faith," which is the contested status.
- "Registration does not vest title" (*Heirs of Gonzales v. Basas*, G.R. 206847).
- **Weak:** the partition and T-2021002127. If we ask to undo them, Ramos says collateral attack, and that the co-owners Geraldine and Marcia are not parties. Ask only for cancellation of 079-2021002126 and restoration of Lot C-1 to the registered co-owners. Treat the partition as void only insofar as it carved out C-1 for Gloria. Art. 487 says only "Any one of the co-owners may bring an action in ejectment." Whether it extends to accion reivindicatoria for the benefit of all co-owners is a jurisprudence point for counsel [UNVERIFIED: no case fetched].

Exh. 1 p. 2), and on 8 Sep 2020 the engineer certified owner consent when the only agent was dead [Annex L, VERIFY wording].
