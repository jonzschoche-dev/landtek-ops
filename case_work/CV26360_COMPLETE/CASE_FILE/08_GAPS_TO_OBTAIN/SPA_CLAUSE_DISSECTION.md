## Verdict

**Jonathan's claim, that no Filipino lawyer would treat this SPA as a valid instrument to sell, does not survive the document.** Tested against Arts. 1874 and 1878 and the *Cosmic Lumber* test, the SPA is adequate in form:

- It is in writing.
- It identifies the property by title number and technical description.
- It expressly addresses "the sale" and adds "to sign and execute the necessary documents … as well as to receive the proceeds."
- All three registered owners signed it.
- It was acknowledged before a Los Angeles notary and authenticated by the Philippine Consul, which is the route Act No. 2103 §2 provides.

The closest Supreme Court decision, *Tumibay v. Lopez* (2013), examined an SPA built the same way: "offer for sale … subject to our approval … sign all papers." The Court said there "would have been no obstacle to its use … had the ensuing sale been consummated according to its terms." **What defeats the 2016 sale is not the drafting. It is the SPA's own limits and its end:**

- the ¶7 buyer class;
- "after prior consultation with us";
- the ¶6 tax purpose;
- no power to subdivide or partition;
- revocation on 15 Aug 2005;
- Cesar's death on 21 Jun 2017 before registration.

*Tumibay* voided a sale made outside the SPA's stated condition under Art. 1898. That is the decision that actually supports Jonathan's bottom line. **I found no Supreme Court decision holding an SPA drafted like this one facially insufficient to authorize a sale.**

## 3. Defects, ranked (strongest first)

1. **Revocation, 15 Aug 2005 (Arts. 1919(1), 1920).** It ends the power. It is outside the four corners of the SPA, but it is decisive.
2. **The ¶7 buyer class.** The sale is "in favor of" only Llamanzares contract-to-sell holders or payors to Llamanzares (Art. 1881; Art. 1898; *Tumibay*). The deed recites the SPA by number, which charges the buyer with its terms (*San Pedro v. Ong*).
3. **"After prior consultation with us."** It is a condition in the writing, and none was sought (Annex Q p. 4). This is *Tumibay*'s condition of principal approval, in different words.
4. **No power to subdivide, segregate or partition (Art. 1878(15); Art. 1877 for ¶8).** This defeats the 2017 Partition-Subdivision Agreement and the 2021 registration (PD 1529 §58).
5. **¶6 purpose.** The authority is tied to settling pre-1992 real estate taxes (Art. 1881). Medium strength; Ramos calls it a recital.
6. **Property description is the T-4497 whole.** A sale of an unsegregated 2,587 sq m portion of a derivative lot is not described (*Woodchild*: "legally identical"). Medium; the Registry carried the SPA to T-52540 (PD 1529 §59).
7. **No price, minimum or terms.** This cuts both ways. Terms were left to "consultation," which reinforces defect 3. Standing alone it is not a defect: *Veloso* upheld a power "upon such terms and conditions … as my said attorney shall deem fit."

**Oddities that are not defects:**

- the operative verb is "negotiate";
- the deed and annotation misdescribe Munda's role;
- the "known/satisfactory evidence" box on the California form;
- the "Municipality of Daet" reading;
- no instrumental witnesses or marginal signatures on the copy;
- the PE-number variance;
- the ¶1 death date;
- no duration or substitution clause (Art. 1892 permits substitution unless prohibited; *Escueta v. Lim*, T1 audit).

**Where the SPA is adequate (concede):**

- it is in writing (Art. 1874);
- it is special as to the act of sale (Art. 1878(5), *Cosmic*);
- it identifies the property by title and technical description;
- all three co-owners signed;
- it was acknowledged before a Los Angeles notary and authenticated by the Philippine Consul (Act 2103 §2);
- it carries a power to sign and receive proceeds.

**Do not argue the SPA is void on its face.** Our own JA (Q23) concedes Cesar could sign, and the 2005 Era sale under it is unchallenged.

## 4. Cases on an SPA that mentions sale but still fails

| Case | SPA wording examined (exact) | Holding (exact) | Bearing |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tumibay v. Lopez**, G.R. No. 171692, 3 Jun 2013 (lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2013/jun2013/gr_171692_2013.html) | "To administer our real property … To offer for sale said properties, the selling price of which will be subject to our approval. x x x To sign all papers and documents on our behalf in a contract of sale x x x." | "...there would have been no obstacle to its use by Reynalda had the ensuing sale been consummated according to its terms." "...acted beyond the scope of her authority … at a price … not approved by [the principals] … Under Article 1898 of the Civil Code, the sale is void and petitioners are, thus, entitled to the reconveyance of the subject land." | **Best support for Jonathan's bottom line.** A conditioned SPA, a sale outside the condition, and a buyer aware of the limits made the sale void, with the title cancelled and the old title reinstated. **Against his premise:** the SPA form itself was adequate. |
| **Bautista-Spille v. NICORP**, G.R.
