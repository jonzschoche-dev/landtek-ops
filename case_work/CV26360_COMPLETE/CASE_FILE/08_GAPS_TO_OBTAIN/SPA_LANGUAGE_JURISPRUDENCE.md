## Verdict

**The Supreme Court reads an SPA's words strictly. Our best uses of that rule are the ¶7 buyer class, the 2005 revocation, Cesar's death, and his own confirmation deed. "Negotiate only" is not one of them.**

**Negotiate vs. sell (test c).** I found **no decision that holds that the words "negotiate for the sale" in an SPA, standing alone, withhold a power to sell.** The closest holding is *Litonjua v. Eternit* (2006): "an authority to find a purchaser of real property does not include an authority to sell." That case involved a broker with no written authority at all. Our SPA ¶7 adds "to sign and execute the necessary documents … as well as to receive the proceeds." Under *Cosmic Lumber*'s test ("expressly mentions a sale or … includes a sale as a necessary ingredient"), a court will likely read ¶7 as a power to complete a sale **for the ¶7 class**. Two other readings are also against us:

- The registry annotation reads "to sign, negotiate for the sale."
- The 1991 Agcaoili SPA (whose text we do not hold) reads "power to sign for and in behalf of."

**Run "negotiate-only" as a minor point at most.** The rows that follow are rated by strength.

**What the cases strongly support:**

1. **Strict construction tied to the property and the act.** *Woodchild* (2004): authority to sell one titled lot did not authorize burdening or selling a portion of a different titled lot. "The act done must be legally identical with that authorized to be done." See also *Bautista-Spille* and *Cosmic Lumber*.
2. **Buyer's duty to read the agent's authority.** See *San Pedro v. Ong*, and *Yabut* ("especially when the property is co-owned").
3. **Acts after the agency ended are void.** *Rallos* (1978): an agent's sale of a co-owner's undivided share after the principal's death is "void ab initio." This applies to anything Cesar is said to have done after 21 Jun 2017, and by parity to acts after revocation (Art. 1919).
4. **A void sale cannot be ratified.** See *AF Realty*, *MCIAA v. Unchuan* and *Aggabao*.

**Main risks:**

- *Sarili*: a notarized SPA's face is "sufficient inquiry." Ramos will say the face says "sign, negotiate for the sale."
- *Pahud*: the Court upheld a co-heir's sale without written authority **by estoppel**, because the other co-heirs later admitted it.

## Mapping to Jonathan's six questions

| Question | Best authority | Bottom line |
|---|---|---|
| (a) Which property / derivative lot / portion | *Woodchild*; *Bautista-Spille*; *Cosmic* | Strict construction is supported. No case found voids a sale because the SPA named the mother title rather than the derivative. **Do not plead that as a stand-alone ground.** |
| (b) Limits on whom, price, consent | *Woodchild* ("legally identical"); *San Pedro*; *Yabut* | Strong for the ¶7 class and the consultation requirement. |
| (c) "Negotiate" vs. "sell/sign" | *Litonjua* (broker); *AF Realty* (facts) | **Not established for an SPA that also says "sign and execute."** Weak. |
| (d) Co-owners' SPA over undivided land | *Yabut*; *MCIAA*; *Rallos* | Inquiry is "especially" required. Cesar had no share of his own, so Art. 493 saves nothing for the buyer. |
| (e) After revocation or death | *Rallos* (death: void ab initio); *De Joya* (revocation context) | Strong as to death on 21 Jun 2017. Revocation is governed by Arts. 1919–1922. |
| (f) Agent's self-confirmation / ratification | *AF Realty*; *MCIAA*; *Woodchild* ("absent estoppel or ratification") | Only the principal ratifies (Arts. 1317, 1910). Cesar's 28 Mar 2017 Deed of Confirmation (Annex K p. 7) is the agent confirming his own act. **No SC case found on an agent's self-confirmation specifically**; that point rests on statute [INFERRED]. |

**UNVERIFIED, not cited:** any case squarely holding that (i) "negotiate for the sale" in an SPA excludes the power to sell, (ii) an SPA over a mother title does not reach a derivative title, or (iii) an agent's self-confirmation is ineffective. None was found and read in full.
