**P8 text (verified):**
- **Art. 1898:** "If the agent contracts in the name of the principal, exceeding the scope of his authority, and the principal does not ratify the contract, it shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal."
- ***Heirs of Bucton v. Spouses Go*, G.R. No. 188395, 20 Nov 2013:** "The burden of proving the status of a purchaser in good faith and for value lies upon one who asserts that status. This onus probandi cannot be discharged by mere invocation of the ordinary presumption of good faith."

**Reading:** *Bucton* puts the buyer's good faith on the buyer. Rule 131 §1 leaves the elements of our own claim on us. So non-consultation must be **proved** by P1 or P2 with P4, P6 and P7. The burden argument only supports that proof.

## 4 · If Patricia is abroad: how she could testify

- **Deposition, Rule 23 (1997 text as on lawphil; the 2019 amendments did not reach Rule 23 [VERIFY]):**
  - **§1:** "By leave of court after jurisdiction has been obtained … or without such leave after an answer has been served, the testimony of any person, whether a party or not, may be taken, at the instance of any party, by deposition upon oral examination or written interrogatories."
  - **§4(c):** the deposition "may be used by any party for any purpose if the court finds: … (2) that the witness resides at a distance more than one hundred (100) kilometers from the place of trial or hearing, or is out of the Philippines, unless it appears that his absence was procured by the party offering the deposition."
  - **§11:** "In a foreign state or country, depositions may be taken (a) on notice before a secretary of embassy or legation, consul general, consul, vice-consul, or consular agent of the Republic of the Philippines, (b) before such person or officer as may be appointed by commission or under letters rogatory; or (c) the person referred to in section 14 hereof."
