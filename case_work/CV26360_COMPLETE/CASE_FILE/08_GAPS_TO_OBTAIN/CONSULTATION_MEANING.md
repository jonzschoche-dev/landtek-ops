# What "prior consultation" means in SPA ¶7

*Civil Case 26-360, Zschoche v. Balane, MTC Mercedes · 16 Oct 2026, 10:40 PM PHT · internal research for counsel*

## Verdict

"After prior consultation with us" is a **written limit on Cesar's power to sell**. Under the SPA's own words, the terms of any sale were to be fixed only after the principals had been consulted.

The Supreme Court has treated "prior consultation" in an authority to sell as a **condition** of that authority. In *Esguerra v. CA* (G.R. 119310, 1997), it refused to read the condition in because the instrument did not contain it. Ours does contain it, so the same reasoning cuts the other way. Because the limit is in the power "as written," it binds the buyer (Art. 1900; Art. 1902). Read with "us," and with three principals who are co-owners, it means **each sister, before the terms were set and before the 29 Sep 2016 deed**.

**Where Ramos has a point.** On its plain meaning, consultation is *not* consent. The LGC cases list "prior consultation" and "prior approval" as two separate requirements. Read for itself, ¶7 leaves the final call on terms to the agent ("which said Attorney-in-Fact may deem fit"). So we should not argue that ¶7 required the sisters to approve the sale. Our argument is narrower and stronger: **no consultation of any kind took place**, so the condition never happened and the power to sell on any terms never arose (Arts. 1181, 1881, 1898).

The open risk is proof, not meaning. The burden of that proof is dealt with in the CONSULTATION_GAP_PLAN.

## 2. Rules, quoted from the Civil Code (lawphil.net, RA 386)

Source: https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1949/ra_386_1949.html. Text read in full.

**Interpretation**
- **Art. 1370.** "If the terms of a contract are clear and leave no doubt upon the intention of the contracting parties, the literal meaning of its stipulations shall control. If the words appear to be contrary to the evident intention of the parties, the latter shall prevail over the former."
- **Art. 1371.** "In order to judge the intention of the contracting parties, their contemporaneous and subsequent acts shall be principally considered."
- **Art. 1372.** "However general the terms of a contract may be, they shall not be understood to comprehend things that are distinct and cases that are different from those upon which the parties intended to agree."
- **Art. 1373.** "If some stipulation of any contract should admit of several meanings, it shall be understood as bearing that import which is most adequate to render it effectual."
- **Art. 1374.** "The various stipulations of a contract shall be interpreted together, attributing to the doubtful ones that sense which may result from all of them taken jointly."
- **Art. 1375.** "Words which may have different significations shall be understood in that which is most in keeping with the nature and object of the contract."
- **Art. 1376.** "The usage or custom of the place shall be borne in mind in the interpretation of the ambiguities of a contract, and shall fill the omission of stipulations which are ordinarily established."
- **Art. 1377.** "The interpretation of obscure words or stipulations in a contract shall not favor the party who caused the obscurity."
- **Art. 1378** (excerpt). "If the contract is onerous, the doubt shall be settled in favor of the greatest reciprocity of interests. If the doubts are cast upon the principal object of the contract in such a way that it cannot be known what may have been the intention or will of the parties, the contract shall be null and void."
- **Art. 1379.** "The principles of interpretation stated in Rule 123 of the Rules of Court shall likewise be observed in the construction of contracts."

**Agency**
- **Art. 1878(5).** Special powers of attorney are necessary "To enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted or acquired either gratuitously or for a valuable consideration."
- **Art. 1881.** "The agent must act within the scope of his authority. He may do such acts as may be conducive to the accomplishment of the purpose of the agency."
- **Art. 1887.** "In the execution of the agency, the agent shall act in accordance with the instructions of the principal. In default thereof, he shall do all that a good father of a family would do, as required by the nature of the business."
- **Art. 1898.** "If the agent contracts in the name of the principal, exceeding the scope of his authority, and the principal does not ratify the contract, it shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal. …"
- **Art. 1900.** "So far as third persons are concerned, an act is deemed to have been performed within the scope of the agent's authority, if such act is within the terms of the power of attorney, as written, even if the agent has in fact exceeded the limits of his authority according to an understanding between the principal and the agent."
- **Art. 1902.** "A third person with whom the agent wishes to contract on behalf of the principal may require the presentation of the power of attorney, or the instructions as regards the agency. Private or secret orders and instructions of the principal do not prejudice third persons who have relied upon the power of attorney or instructions shown them."

**Conditions**
- **Art. 1181.** "In conditional obligations, the acquisition of rights, as well as the extinguishment or loss of those already acquired, shall depend upon the happening of the event which constitutes the condition."
- **Art. 1182.** "When the fulfillment of the condition depends upon the sole will of the debtor, the conditional obligation shall be void. If it depends upon chance or upon the will of a third person, the obligation shall take effect in conformity with the provisions of this Code."
- **Art. 1184.** "The condition that some event happen at a determinate time shall extinguish the obligation as soon as the time expires or if it has become indubitable that the event will not take place."
- **Art. 1186.** "The condition shall be deemed fulfilled when the obligor voluntarily prevents its fulfillment."
- Arts. 1183 and 1185 were read and are not material here.

**Notice to co-owners**
- **Art. 1623.** "The right of legal pre-emption or redemption shall not be exercised except within thirty days from the notice in writing by the prospective vendor, or by the vendor, as the case may be. The deed of sale shall not be recorded in the Registry of Property, unless accompanied by an affidavit of the vendor that he has given written notice thereof to all possible redemptioners. …"

**Codal point to note.** The Arts. 1181–1186 provisions govern *conditional obligations*. A condition on an agent's authority is not strictly an "obligation." Use them by analogy: until the event happens, the right (here, the power to sell on terms) does not arise. The direct rules are Arts. 1881, 1887 and 1898. [Counsel's call on how to frame it.]

## 3. Supreme Court decisions, read in full on lawphil

**A. *Esguerra v. Court of Appeals*, G.R. No. 119310, 3 Feb 1997 (3rd Div., Panganiban, J.).** VERIFIED, read in full. https://www.lawphil.net/judjuris/juri1997/feb1997/gr_119310_1997.html

This is the only SC case found on "prior consultation" as a limit on a power to sell. A co-owner attacked a sale because she "has not been notified nor consulted as to the terms and conditions of the sale." The trial court voided half the sale on that ground. The SC reversed, because the authority "specified no condition that the latter shall first consult with the former prior to selling":

> "the trial court's decision … is tainted with grave abuse of discretion for having injudiciously added 'prior consultation' to VECCI's authority to sell the properties, **a condition not contained** in the judicially-approved compromise agreement."

It also applied Art. 1900: third persons may rely on the power "as written."

- **For us:** the SC calls prior consultation "a condition" of authority to sell, about "the terms and conditions of the sale." Here it *is* written, so Art. 1900 puts it in the buyer's face.
- **For Ramos:** the SC also says one earlier consultation "did not affect nor vary the terms of the authority". Course of dealing does not add or drop terms.

**B. *Lina v. Paño*, G.R. No. 129093, 30 Aug 2001.** VERIFIED, read in full. https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2001/aug2001/gr_129093_2001.html

The Court quotes LGC §26, which states what a consultation consists of: the duty "to consult with the local government units, nongovernmental organizations, and other sectors concerned **and explain the goals and objectives of the project or program, its impact upon the people and the community** …". §27 requires "consultations … and prior approval" as separate items. The holding is that §27 is limited to the listed ecological projects; it does not define consultation beyond this.

**C. *Bangus Fry Fisherfolk v. Lanzanas*, G.R. No. 131442, 10 Jul 2003.** VERIFIED, read in full. https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2003/jul2003/gr_131442_2003.html

> §§26–27 "require every national government agency … **to hold prior consultations** with the local government unit concerned **and to secure the prior approval** of its sanggunian before implementing …"

Consultation and approval are again two distinct steps. The case also holds that the missing paper proof of consultations in an ECC file does not by itself void the ECC. That is a point about proof in a regulatory setting, not a definition.

**D. *Province of Rizal v. Executive Secretary*, G.R. No. 129546, 13 Dec 2005 (En Banc).** VERIFIED, read in full. https://lawphil.net/judjuris/juri2005/dec2005/gr_129546_2005.html

> "two requisites must be met …: **prior consultation with the affected local communities, and prior approval** of the project by the appropriate sanggunian. **Absent either of these mandatory requirements, the project's implementation is illegal.**"

The government official's claim that "they had conducted the required consultations," qualified by "the officials we may have been talking with … may no longer be incumbent," was called an "ambivalent reply." The Court treated it as not establishing consultation.

- **For us:** where consultation is required beforehand, its absence alone defeats the act. A vague claim of past talks is not proof.

**E. IPRA, RA 8371 §3(g)** (statute, not a decision). https://lawphil.net/statutes/repacts/ra1997/ra_8371_1997.html

> FPIC "shall mean the **consensus of all members** … obtained after **fully disclosing the intent and scope of the activity** …"

This shows the legislature uses "consent" when it means consent. Consultation is the lesser term. Its useful content here is full disclosure *before* the act.

**Not found or not read:**
- No SC decision defining consultation in a labor or CBA setting was read in full for this memo. UNVERIFIED; do not cite.
- No other agency case defining "consultation" was found.
- *Tumibay v. Lopez* (G.R. 171692, 2013; our verified file) concerns an SPA requiring the principals' *approval* of price. It is the consent analogue, not a consultation case.

## 5. Application: rule, application, disposition

**Rule.** Plain words control (Art. 1370). The clauses are read together and in the sense that makes them work (Arts. 1373, 1374). An agent must keep within the written power and the principal's instructions (Arts. 1881, 1887). A sale beyond those limits, with a buyer aware of them, is void unless ratified (Art. 1898; *Tumibay*). A limit written in the power binds third persons (Arts. 1900, 1902; *Esguerra*). A required prior consultation that did not happen defeats the act (*Province of Rizal*). Consultation means seeking the views of the persons concerned, with disclosure of what is proposed, before acting (M-W; LGC §26 as quoted in *Lina*).

**Application.**

1. **What it requires.** "Said sale shall be upon such terms … which said Attorney-in-Fact may deem fit … **after prior consultation with us**." The agent's discretion over terms is real, but it is timed and conditioned: it begins only *after* consultation. At a minimum, the principals had to be told of the proposed buyer and terms, and asked for their views, before the terms were set.
2. **Who.** "Us" are the three signatories, Geraldine, Patricia and Marcia, who are also co-owners. The same "us" and "our" run through ¶7 ("our true and lawful attorney-in-fact," "in our collective behalf"). Read together (Art. 1374), consulting one sister is not consulting "us." Each co-owner's own share is at stake (Art. 493 context), which supports this under Art. 1375.
3. **When.** "Prior" fixes the time: before the terms and before the deed of 29 Sep 2016. Anything after the deed is ratification, not consultation, and the owners' 2020 publication repudiates ratification (Exh. G).
4. **Form.** Not specified. Oral consultation would satisfy the text. But someone has to prove it happened, with whom and when.
5. **Our facts.**
   - No letter, email or acknowledgment of any consultation exists in either side's exhibits.
   - The deed itself recites only the SPA and that "the provision of Article 1623 … had been complied with." It does **not** recite any consultation with the principals.
   - The Art. 1623 recital is a statement about *written notice to possible redemptioners*. In a sale made by the co-owners themselves through their agent, the "redemptioners" would be the co-owner sisters. If the defense relies on that recital, it amounts to a claim that written notice was sent. They must produce it, or the recital is shown to be unsupported. Use it on cross and at rebuttal (Patricia: no notice received). It is not proof of consultation.
6. **Purpose.** The power was conditioned for an evident reason. The principals lived abroad, so terms were to be shaped with them (Art. 1375). A reading that lets the agent skip the consultation makes the clause meaningless. Art. 1373 favors the reading that gives it effect.

**Ramos's best reading, and our answer**

| Ratified later by accepting the arrangement or by Patricia's 2023 affidavit. | No proceeds reached any principal. The owners published a repudiation in 2020 (Exh. G). On the same day as the 2023 affidavit, Patricia filed an adverse claim calling the sale void (Exh. I). |

**Disposition.** Present the consultation clause as a **condition on the power to set terms and sell, owed to each of the three principals before 29 Sep 2016**. Its non-occurrence makes the sale exceed the authority, and it is void under Art. 1898 as against a buyer charged with the SPA's terms. Do not argue that consultation meant approval. Pair the clause with the ¶7 buyer class (lead) and the 2005 revocation (backup).
