## 1. Bottom line

1. **A real test exists, and it can answer the question only one way.** GC-MS solvent-loss analysis can show that an entry is **young**, meaning written within roughly the last months to two years. It cannot give a calendar date. It cannot prove an entry is old: a "not young" result is equally consistent with 2005 or with 2023.
2. **The window is short and closing.** Published thresholds range from under 3 months (some inks, per a 2023+ ASQDE study) to under 18 months (Gaudreau & Aginsky 2010). Riley Welch LaPorte states "up to 2 years." **If the entries were written in early 2026, a test in late 2027 may show nothing.** Urgency is the main argument for filing early.
3. **Two complementary tests do not depend on the window:**
   - **Ink identification.** This compares the ink against a library for its first date of manufacture. An ink first made after a receipt's stated date is decisive.
   - **Ink comparison.** This tests whether entries dated years apart share one ink formulation and batch.
4. **Destructive sampling needs court leave.** That is now prayer (c-1) and ¶17-A in the draft motion. In the Philippines, admissibility should not be the obstacle: courts weigh expert opinion freely (*Jimenez*; *Tamani*). The fight will be over weight.
5. **"Riley Welch" is real.** The firm is **Riley, Welch, LaPorte & Associates**, Frankenmuth, Michigan, USA (rileywelch.com). It does GC-MS ink dating (2-phenoxyethanol) and lists international work.

## 2. What the tests can and cannot show

### 2.1 Static methods (no time window)

- **Ink comparison** (ASTM E1422; SWGDOC "Test Methods for Forensic Writing Ink Comparison"). Uses optical examination (UV, IR, VSC) and thin-layer chromatography (TLC). It answers "same or different ink formulation."
  - By its own scope, "performing ink dating examinations [is] beyond the scope of this standard" (SWGDOC §1.2, read today).
  - **Use here:** Do the 2013–2017 estate-form entries (Nos. 0106–0228) share one formulation with each other, and with the post-deed cluster? Does the body ink match the signature ink?
- **Ink identification** (ASTM E1789; SWGDOC "Standard for Writing Ink Identification"). Matches the ink against a reference library to identify the manufacturer and the formula's first production date. Some inks carry manufacturer date tags.
  - **Use here:** if a 2005 receipt is written in an ink first sold in 2018, the entry cannot be from 2005.
  - **Limit:** libraries are US-centred. Philippine pen brands may not be covered [VERIFY with the lab].

### 2.2 Dynamic (ink-age) methods

- **Principle.** Ballpoint inks contain volatile solvents, chiefly **2-phenoxyethanol (2-PE)**. These evaporate quickly at first, then slowly, and then level off as the resin hardens.
  - The 2023 PMC study on black BIC ink, read today, found "a substantial decline between the 1st and the 33rd day … followed by a stabilisation of the signal," with 2-PE still detectable up to 3 years.
  - Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10302093/
- **Solvent Loss Ratio Method (SLRM)** (Aginsky 1996; Gaudreau & Brazeau 2002; Gaudreau & Aginsky 2010). Paired micro-plugs are taken from the same line. One is heated (about 70 °C, 1–2 h). The lab measures R% = 100 × (P − P_T)/P by GC-MS.
  - Thresholds reported in 2002: "R% larger than 50% … less than 5 months"; ">25% … less than 10 months."
  - The 25% threshold "was abandoned (as it had shown false-positive results)." It was revised in 2010 to "R% value larger than 35% indicating the age of the ink to be less than 18 months."
  - Source: *J. Am. Soc. Questioned Doc. Examiners*, https://journal.asqde.org/articles/10.69525/jasqde.210, read today.
- **Reliability caveats from that same article** (expect the defense to use them):
  - "neither the experimental data … nor the statistical evaluation relating it to any of the claimed SLRM thresholds have been published to date in a peer-reviewed article"
  - SLRM "has yet to be subjected to outside proficiency testing to determine its error rate"
  - in that study, R% "correlates with the age of the inks within a rather short timeframe which proved to be less than 3 months" for 15 common inks
- **Sequential Extraction Technique (SET)** and dye-ratio or percent-extraction methods (Aginsky; Speckin). These are relative-age comparisons against known-dated entries of the **same ink on the same paper** (Speckin: "it is imperative that the same ink formulation from the same type of paper comprise both samples").
- **Practice example (Riley Welch LaPorte report, US federal court, S.D.N.Y., read today).** The firm found "significantly high levels of 2-phenoxyethanol" with an average SLR of 31% on entries dated 2016. It concluded it was "highly probable" they were written "within two (2) years before I performed my testing" and "consistent with … the past six (6) months."
  - That is the form of opinion to expect: probabilistic, about recency, with no calendar date.
  - Source: https://storage.courtlistener.com/recap/gov.uscourts.nysd.500584/gov.uscourts.nysd.500584.128.2.pdf

### 2.3 What no method can do

- Give an exact date of writing.
- Date gel, felt-tip or many water-based inks by 2-PE. The ink type must be confirmed first.
- Prove an entry is old.
- Survive a poor sample: plugs too close to other ink lines or crossings, uneven pressure, or storage in heat. Speckin's Michael Sinke (2013 talk) said some protocols need about **30 ink plugs**, which can "obliterate much of the signature." Plan sampling from body text, not signatures.

## 3. The realistic "fresh ink" window and urgency

| Source | Window in which "recent" can be shown |
|---|---|
| Gaudreau & Aginsky 2010 (as reported in JASQDE) | R% > 35% means under **18 months** |
| Gaudreau & Brazeau 2002 | R% > 50% means under **5 months** |
| JASQDE study (15 inks) | SLRM correlates only within **under 3 months** for those inks |
| Riley Welch LaPorte website | drying "can last up to **2 years**"; GC/MS shows "if a written entry was created in the past two years" |
| Speckin (Sinke, 2013) | ink "has a **three to three and one-half year** drying time" (relative-age method) |
| PMC 2023 (BIC black) | fast change to about **33 days**; detectable to 3 years; time frames 0–33 d / 34–109 d / >109 d |

**Working assumption: about 6–24 months, depending on ink and method.**

- If the entries were written in late 2025 or early 2026, sampling in Q4 2026 to Q1 2027 is likely still inside the window for the more sensitive methods. Every month of delay erodes the result.
- **Argue urgency in the motion** (done, ¶17-A). Evidence that degrades with time is good cause for prompt production and sampling, separate from the rebuttal timing.

## 4. Sample design

1. **Non-destructive triage first** (PNP or the chosen lab): ink type (ballpoint or gel), VSC/UV/IR grouping, and an ESDA indentation scan. Then choose the plugs.
2. **Questioned entries: 6–10, spread across the years and across visual ink groups.**

   | # | Receipt | Why |
   |---|---|---|
   | Q1 | 25 Oct 2003, "V" form (p.2) | Earliest |
   | Q2 | 6 Jan 2005, "Down payment" (p.3) | Key purpose wording |
   | Q3 | 2006 red-ink receipt (p.6) | A distinct ink group |
   | Q4 | No. 0019 or 0061, 28 Mar 2007 (p.9) | First estate form |
   | Q5 | 2009–2011 violet group (pp.15–21) | Mid-period |
   | Q6 | No. 0106, 31 Jul 2013 (p.23) | First receipt after the 30 Jun 2013 ATP cutoff |
   | Q7 | No. 0129 or 0133, 2016 (p.29) | Near-consecutive pair |
   | Q8 | No. 0217, Aug 2016 (p.30) | After the serial jump |
   | Q9 | No. 0222, 30 Sep 2016 (p.31) | The day after the deed |
   | Q10 | No. 0228, Feb 2017 "Full payment" (p.33) | Last receipt |

3. **Controls:**
   - (a) **Paper blanks** from each receipt sampled, for background solvent.
   - (b) **Known-age references on the same paper stock.** If unused blank estate forms or stubs are produced, write fresh entries with candidate pens, and keep a set aged under a documented protocol.
   - (c) **Known-dated writing by the same hand, if any exists:** Salvador's notarized or filed documents of known date, written in ballpoint.
   - (d) **Duplicate plugs** for each test, and **split samples** offered to the defense.
4. **Sampling protocol** (per the JASQDE description):
   - Plugs come from adjacent, uniform stretches of the same line.
   - No plugs near crossings or other lines.
   - Each plug's location is mapped and photographed.
   - Plugs are sealed in glass vials immediately.
   - Body-text strokes (amounts, dates) are sampled rather than signatures, to preserve the signatures for handwriting comparison.

Only the vials travel; the originals stay. Speckin's published Hong Kong example used this model: plugs were taken locally and analysed in the US lab.
- **Shipping.** Ink plugs are not hazardous. Use a tracked courier with tamper-evident seals, and record temperature and time in transit (heat ages ink).

## 6. Admissibility and weight

- **United States.** Ink-age testimony has been challenged under *Daubert*, with mixed results.
  - Speckin reports a S.D.N.Y. judge in *Rezende v. Citigroup* found its accelerated-aging and extraction methods "scientifically reliable," with Aginsky as the unsuccessful opposing expert. That is from Speckin's own website, so it is **UNVERIFIED** as to the opinion text.
  - Speckin also reports admission in *Torok v. Summix Mines* (B.C. Supreme Court, 1998), also **UNVERIFIED**.
  - The JASQDE article shows that SLRM thresholds lack published validation data and error rates. Expect a challenge on that.
- **Philippines.** There is no *Daubert* gatekeeping rule. Rule 130 §52 admits opinion on "special knowledge, skill, experience, training or education, which he or she is shown to possess."
  - Courts weigh expert opinion freely and are "not bound" by it (*Jimenez v. Commission on Ecumenical Mission*, G.R. 140472, verified).
  - Value lies in "pointing out distinguishing marks, characteristics and discrepancies" (*Tamani v. Salvador*, G.R. 171497, verified).
  - A PNP examiner may not be excluded merely because the examination lacked prior court authority (*Navarro Marcos*, G.R. 198240, verified). A court order still avoids that fight.
  - **No Philippine Supreme Court decision on ink-age (solvent-loss) evidence was found or read. UNVERIFIED.**
- **How to maximise weight:**
  - use a lab with published methods and peer-reviewed analysts
  - follow the ASTM/SWGDOC methods for comparison and identification
  - test in duplicate
  - use controls on the same paper
  - give a candid statement of limits
  - corroborate with the non-chemical anomalies (serial reversals, ATP validity, receipts dated after the deed)
