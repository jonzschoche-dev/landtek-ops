## 1. Bottom line

1. **The burden is on the defense.** Payment is their defense, so they must prove it "with legal certainty" (*Bognot*, *Joseph*). Receipts are the best evidence of payment but not the only evidence, and they are not conclusive (*Multi-International*). Once the defense offers receipts, the burden of going forward shifts to us, so we need some evidence of non-payment or of irregularity. The burden of proof itself does not shift (Rule 131 §1).
2. **Our strongest law point is Art. 1240 plus *Culaba*.** A payor must "ascertain not only the fact of agency but also the nature and extent of authority," and if that authority is disputed, "the burden of proof is upon them to establish it." No receipt names Cesar or any appointed collector.
3. **Our strongest new documentary point is BIR RR 18-2012 §5.** All unused receipts printed before the regulation "shall be valid until June 30, 2013." The estate forms carry a 1998 printer authority. **All 34 estate-form receipts dated 31 Jul 2013 to Feb 2017 are on forms that, by that rule, were no longer valid when they were dated.** This is a regulatory and credibility point, not an automatic bar to proving payment. It is cheap to prove through BIR records, and it does not tip off the defense.
4. **The handwriting law favors a court comparison.** The judge may compare handwriting personally, and an expert is "not mandatory or indispensable" (*Jimenez*, *Pontaoe*, *Gamido*). The comparison has to be against writings "admitted or treated as genuine" by the party against whom the evidence is offered, or "proved to be genuine to the satisfaction of the judge" (Rule 132 §22). **We hold no Salvador exemplars yet.** That is the gap to close.
5. **Rebuttal is a valid route, with limits.** Under Rule 30 §5(f), the parties "may then respectively adduce rebutting evidence only," unless the court allows otherwise. *Abalos* holds that a "new matter" introduced by the defense may be met with "positive evidence" on rebuttal. The receipts attack qualifies because the receipts are the defense's own exhibit. The authority, consultation and revocation case must still stand in chief.
6. **Two presumptions cut against us and have to be answered.** Rule 131 §3(i) presumes "prior rents or installments had been paid when a receipt for the later one is produced," and §3(u) presumes "a writing is truly dated." The serial-order and date anomalies are what rebut §3(u). The authority gap (Art. 1240) defeats §3(i), because the presumption only helps if the later receipt was itself a valid payment to an authorized person.

## 2. Rules (exact text, VERIFIED)

Source for the rules text: the official 2019 text as reproduced verbatim at vivasandnobleslawoffice.com/codes/rules-of-court/… (each page states it was checked against the SC text). **Barandon should confirm against the SC-published A.M. 19-08-15-SC and 19-10-20-SC before quoting in a filing.**

### 2.1 Authentication of private documents

**Rule 132 §20 (Proof of private documents).** "Before any private document offered as authentic is received in evidence, its due execution and authenticity must be proved by any of the following means: (a) By anyone who saw the document executed or written; (b) By evidence of the genuineness of the signature or handwriting of the maker[;] or (c) By other evidence showing its due execution and authenticity."

- **Application.** The defense must authenticate each receipt. Salvador's judicial affidavit (S18–S22) says only that he "nag-iissue" (issued) receipts. Under §20(a) and (b), the questions are: who wrote each receipt, and who signed the "V" and "S" forms?
- **Disposition.** Object at the formal offer to any receipt not specifically identified by its writer or signer. Preserve the objection even if the exhibit is admitted, because weight remains open.

**Rule 132 §22 (How genuineness of handwriting proved).** "The handwriting of a person may be proved by any witness who believes it to be the handwriting of such person because he or she has seen the person write, or has seen writing purporting to be his or hers upon which the witness has acted or been charged, and has thus acquired knowledge of the handwriting of such person. Evidence respecting the handwriting may also be given by a comparison, made by the witness or the court, with writings admitted or treated as genuine by the party against whom the evidence is offered, or proved to be genuine to the satisfaction of the judge."

- **Application.** Standards for Salvador must come from writings the defense admits or treats as genuine. Examples: his signed judicial affidavit, his ID, notarized instruments, or a signature he admits through a Rule 26 request.
- **Disposition.** Get Salvador's signed affidavit and jurat page into the record as a standard. Serve an RFA asking him to admit specific exemplars.

**Rule 132 §31 (Alteration in document, how to explain).** "The party producing a document as genuine which has been altered and appears to have been altered after its execution, in a part material to the question in dispute, must account for the alteration. … If he or she fails to do that, the document shall not be admissible in evidence."

- **Application.** The record shows "August" struck on 31 Jul 2009, an impossible "April 31, 2010," the overwritten year on No. 0104, and month-only dates on Nos. 0062–0063 (Exam §7 #12). Dates are material because the question is payment before the deed and before the revocation.
- **Disposition.** Object to those receipts specifically under §31, and make the defense explain each one.

### 2.2 Original document rule

**Rule 130 §3.** "When the subject of inquiry is the contents of a document … no evidence is admissible other than the original document itself, except … (b) When the original is in the custody or under the control of the party against whom the evidence is offered, and the latter fails to produce it after reasonable notice …"

- **Application.** Our copy is a phone scan. The defense must produce the originals at the offer. If they offer photocopies, object.
- **Disposition.** Demand that the originals be produced at the offer for comparison. The scan cannot settle questions of ink, pressure or indentation.

### 2.3 Opinion evidence

- **Rule 130 §52 (Opinion of expert witness).** "The opinion of a witness on a matter requiring special knowledge, skill, experience, training or education, which he or she is shown to possess, may be received in evidence."
- **Rule 130 §53 (Opinion of ordinary witnesses).** "The opinion of a witness, for which proper basis is given, may be received in evidence regarding - (a) [T]he identity of a person about whom he or she has adequate knowledge; (b) A handwriting with which he or she has sufficient familiarity; …"
- **Note.** The 2019 rules number these §§52–53. Older cases cite them as §§49–50. The task brief's "§§49–52" refers to the 1989 numbering.
- **Application.** Patricia, or any witness who has dealt with Cesar's writing, may give a §53(b) opinion on Cesar's hand: that the receipts are not his. That does not need an expert.

### 2.4 Burden and presumptions

- **Rule 131 §1.** "Burden of proof is the duty of a party to present evidence on the facts in issue necessary to establish his or her claim or defense … Burden of proof never shifts. Burden of evidence … may shift from one party to the other …"
- **Rule 131 §3.** Disputable presumptions, "satisfactory if uncontradicted, but may be contradicted and overcome by other evidence":
  - "(e) That evidence willfully suppressed would be adverse if produced"
  - "(f) That money paid by one to another was due to the latter"
  - "(i) That prior rents or installments had been paid when a receipt for the later one is produced"
  - "(p) That private transactions have been fair and regular"
  - "(q) That the ordinary course of business has been followed"
  - "(u) That a writing is truly dated"
- **Application.** Use (e) if the defense refuses to produce the full booklets, stubs or duplicates after a Rule 27 order or a subpoena. Rebut (u) with the six serial reversals. Rebut (q) with the no-ATP point and the near-consecutive serials. Rebut (i) by showing the receipts were not issued by an authorized person.

### 2.5 Civil Code (VERIFIED text)

- **Art. 1233.** "A debt shall not be understood to have been paid unless the thing or service in which the obligation consists has been completely delivered or rendered, as the case may be."
  - The task brief describes Art. 1233 as "burden on debtor." The article does not say that in its words. The burden comes from case law construing it (*Culaba* cites Art. 1233 for it; see also *Bognot* and *Joseph*).
- **Art. 1240.** "Payment shall be made to the person in whose favor the obligation has been constituted, or his successor in interest, or any person authorized to receive it."
- **Art. 1241, ¶2.** "Payment made to a third person shall also be valid insofar as it has redounded to the benefit of the creditor. Such benefit to the creditor need not be proved in the following cases: (1) If after the payment, the third person acquires the creditor's rights; (2) If the creditor ratifies the payment to the third person; (3) If by the creditor's conduct, the debtor has been led to believe that the third person had authority to receive the payment."
  - **Risk.** The defense will argue (2) or (3). Answer: the sisters never ratified; the 2005 revocation; no conduct by the sisters pointing to Salvador; and "redounded to the benefit" requires proof that money reached the sisters. Per the case file, none did [VERIFY with Patricia].
- **Art. 1892.** "The agent may appoint a substitute if the principal has not prohibited him from doing so; but he shall be responsible for the acts of the substitute: (1) When he was not given the power to appoint one; …" A substitute's authority cannot exceed the agent's. After the 15 Aug 2005 revocation, there was nothing left to delegate.
- **Art. 1900.** "So far as third persons are concerned, an act is deemed to have been performed within the scope of the agent's authority, if such act is within the terms of the power of attorney, as written, even if the agent has in fact exceeded the limits of his authority according to an understanding between the principal and the agent."
  - **Risk.** The defense may invoke this. Answer: the SPA "as written" (¶7) limits the buyer class and requires prior consultation. Receipts issued by Salvador are not "within the terms of the power of attorney, as written," which names Cesar.

## 4. Practice: examination of the originals

1. **Court order first.** Seek it under Rule 27 §1, with "terms and conditions" covering:
   - the examiner (PNP Crime Laboratory, Questioned Documents section; or a court-approved private examiner)
   - the place, a sealed transfer, a custody log, and photographing at every hand-off
   - non-destructive tests first (magnification, UV/IR and video spectral comparison, side-lighting/ESDA for indentations)
   - leave of court before any sampling (TLC ink comparison; solvent-loss dating)
   - notice to both parties

   *Navarro Marcos* lets an examiner testify even without an order, but an order removes the due-process objection.
2. **Standards and exemplars.** These must be "admitted or treated as genuine" (Rule 132 §22), and close in time to the questioned writing (*Tamani*). For each candidate writer (Salvador; Cesar for exclusion), collect:
   - **Collected standards:** signed public filings, IDs, notarized deeds, bank cards, and Salvador's judicial-affidavit signature. Ideally several from 2003–2005, 2007, 2013–2017.
   - **Requested standards:** dictated writing taken in court, if ordered.
   - **RFA** on each standard's genuineness.
3. **Questions for the examiner.**
   - Same ink, or a different ink, across receipts dated years apart (especially Nos. 0106–0136 and 0217–0228)?
   - Is the body and the signature the same ink and the same hand?
   - Indented impressions of other receipts' entries, which would show writing in sequence on a stack?
   - Do the originals and duplicates or stubs match?
   - Is the paper or the numbering-machine font consistent with the 1998 print run?
   - Is relative ink age consistent with the dates? (Only if the court allows sampling. Results are typically "fresh within about 2 years or not." **No SC authority on admissibility was read.**)
4. **Forensic accounting of the books.**
   - Rebuild the full serial ledger for 0001–1250 from the stubs.
   - Mark each number as issued (to whom), voided, blank or missing.
   - Test date order against serial order.
   - Reconcile collections against what was remitted to the principals.
   - Separate rent from price.
   - Compare against the deed price and any installment plan.
   - Output a schedule usable under Rule 130 §3(c) (a summary of voluminous documents) through the witness who prepared it.
5. **Cost.** See RECEIPTS_EXAMINATION §11. No new figures were verified here.
