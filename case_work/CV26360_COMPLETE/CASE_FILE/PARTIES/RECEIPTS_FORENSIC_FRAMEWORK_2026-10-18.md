## 0. Summary

1. A genuine installment sale leaves a chain of records. It runs from the terms, through receipts, stubs, a ledger, remittances and tax filings, to the deed recital and registration. Each link has a holder who can be asked for it.
2. Def. Exh. 5 supplies only the receipts. Every other link is missing from the record, or contradicts the receipts.
3. Four hypotheses are tested neutrally:
   - H1: genuine sale installments
   - H2: rent relabeled as price
   - H3: payments to Salvador on his own account, never remitted
   - H4: receipts written after the fact
4. On the present record H1 is the weakest fit. H2, H3 and H4 are not mutually exclusive, and any of them defeats payment to the principals.
5. The discriminating evidence is: stubs and duplicates; remittance to the sisters; the BIR records on the authority to print (ATP) and the TIN; ink age; and the CGT/DST filing date.
6. **New anomaly:** capital gains tax (CGT) and documentary stamp tax (DST) on a deed dated 29 Sep 2016 were paid on **15 Jun 2020**, and the BIR's certificate authorizing registration (eCAR) issued **18 Nov 2021** (DOS l.216, l.250).
   - That is about 3 years 8 months after the deed. The CGT return is normally due within 30 days of the sale [VERIFY].
   - The eCAR declares the ₱250,000 price against a fair market value of ₱2,509,390.
7. The receipts total about ₱602,950, against a ₱250,000 price. The defense's own offer of Exh. 5 is "to prove rents paid and the installment or partial payments" (ANS p.10 ¶61). So by the defense's own account, the set mixes rent and price.
8. The best quiet first moves are certified records from the BIR (RDO 64), the Register of Deeds, the Assessor and the Treasurer, plus the PSA. Court process comes next (stubs, ledger, remittances); bank records come last and only within RA 1405.

## 2. Competing hypotheses: evidence matrix (ACH)

- **H1:** genuine, contemporaneous payments of the ₱250,000 price, received for the principals.
- **H2:** genuine payments, but rent or occupancy fees, later treated as price.
- **H3:** genuine payments to Salvador on his own account, not remitted to the principals.
- **H4:** receipts written after the fact, in one or a few sittings.

**Reading:**
- H1 is inconsistent with every factual row and consistent only with untested rows.
- To survive, H1 needs (a) stubs showing other payors and (b) remittance to the principals. The defense has produced neither.
- H4 turns on the ink and the stubs.
- H3 turns on remittance.
- H2 is supported by the defense's own ¶61 offer.

   - **Tensions:**
     - Receipts dated before the deed do not reach ₱250,000 in Gloria's or Golly's name unless the 2005–2014 monthly ₱2,000 entries are counted as price.
     - If they are counted, the total overshoots the price.
     - S50 (paid in full before the deed) conflicts with ₱219,000 receipted after it.
3. **Rent and price allocation.** Classify each receipt by its wording: "Payment for the month of" or a monthly cadence suggests rent; "Down payment", "partial payment" or "Full payment" suggests price. Run three scenarios:
   - (a) all price
   - (b) only receipts worded as price
   - (c) only post-2014 large amounts

   Show that no scenario produces ₱250,000 paid by 29 Sep 2016 on receipts worded as price.
4. **Round-number and digit tests.** These are **low weight**: 106 of 110 are multiples of ₱1,000, which is natural for rent. Benford analysis is not meaningful on about 110 round, fixed-amount entries. Note it and do not argue it.
5. **Source of funds.** The jump to ₱10,000–₱60,000 payments from Aug 2016 (₱287,650 in the final cluster) raises capacity questions. Pursue them only through lawful process:
   - cross-examination and requests for admission on where the money came from
   - documents the defendants voluntarily produce
   - bank records only within RA 1405 (§5)

   No private investigation.
6. **Output.** A summary schedule, offered under Rule 130 §3(c), through the witness who prepared it.

## 4. RA 1405 (bank deposits): limit on bank records

RA 1405 §2 (lawphil, VERIFIED): "All deposits of whatever nature with banks or banking institutions in the Philippines … are hereby considered as of an absolutely confidential nature and may not be examined, inquired or looked into by any person, government official, bureau or office, except upon written permission of the depositor, or in cases of impeachment, or upon order of a competent court in cases of bribery or dereliction of duty of public officials, or in cases where the money deposited or invested is the subject matter of the litigation."

- **Application.** The subject of 26-360 is land and a deed, not deposited money. The "subject matter of the litigation" exception is unlikely to reach the Balanes' or Salvador's accounts [VERIFY case law on that exception].
- **Remaining routes:**
  - (a) the depositor's written consent, e.g. the sisters' own accounts, to show **non-receipt**
  - (b) the defendants voluntarily producing proof of withdrawals
  - (c) cross-examination and requests for admission
- Other laws (AMLA; tax-code inquiry powers) are not available to a private litigant. Do not pursue.

## 6. New anomalies (not in EXAM's anomaly register)

1. **Late tax on the deed.** CGT/DST receipts are dated 15 Jun 2020 and the eCAR 18 Nov 2021, for a deed dated 29 Sep 2016 (DX6 p.2; DOS l.216, l.250). The 30-day CGT filing rule needs [VERIFY].
   - Questions: who filed, what date of sale was declared, and were surcharges paid?
   - A deed actually executed on its date would ordinarily be taxed in 2016.
2. **eCAR number series.** The number reads "2019…" (201900675224) but the eCAR is dated 2021 [VERIFY whether eCAR numbers encode the year of application]. If they do, it may show an application in 2019. That would be a third date, between the 2016 deed and the 2020 tax payment.
3. **The defense's own purpose statement splits the set.** ANS ¶61 offers Exh. 5 "to prove rents paid and the installment or partial payments." Exh. 5 therefore cannot be summed as price by the defense's own account, yet the receipts do not mark which is which.
4. **Two down payments.** The Answer and Gloria describe a ₱3,000 down payment in the 1980s, unreceipted and later "credited" (ANS ¶40; JA-G S21–S23). Exh. 5 has a ₱20,000 "Down payment" dated 6 Jan 2005. The record does not reconcile them.
5. **The loss affidavit cluster.** The loss affidavit for an owner's duplicate is dated 27 Sep 2016 (Belen Doc. 560), Cesar's affidavit is also dated 27 Sep 2016, and the deed is dated 29 Sep 2016 (DOS l.914).
