# MOTION FOR PRODUCTION OF THE ORIGINALS OF DEFENDANTS' EXHIBIT "5" AND FOR LEAVE TO HAVE THEM EXAMINED BY A QUALIFIED QUESTIONED-DOCUMENTS EXAMINER

Plaintiff, through counsel, respectfully states:

1. Defendants have marked as Exhibit "5" a set of receipts: 33 pages of photocopies or scans showing 111 receipts dated 25 October 2003 to February 2017 (Def. Exh. 5 pp.1–33).
2. Of these, 43 are printed forms headed "HEIRS OF MARY W. KEESEY ESTATE … TIN-160-902-582-Non Vat". They are serially numbered, from No. 0019 to No. 0228, and printed "Authorized Collector" (Def. 4. The receipts are offered "[t]o prove the identification of the receipts for partial payments and subdivision survey" (Judicial Affidavit of Salvador Osum dela Fuente, statement of purposes, item 6 [VERIFY numbering]). The deed in issue recites a price of ₱250,000.00 "in hand paid" on its date, 29 September 2016 (Def. Exh. 6 p.1).

## II. The rules

5. **Rule 27, Section 1** provides [VERIFY 2019 text]:

> "Upon motion of any party showing good cause therefor, the court in which an action is pending may (a) order any party to produce and permit the inspection and copying or photographing, by or on behalf of the moving party, of any designated documents, papers, books, accounts, letters, photographs, objects or tangible things, not privileged, which constitute or contain evidence material to any matter involved in the action and which are in his possession, custody or control … The order shall specify the time, place and manner of making the inspection and taking copies and photographs, and may prescribe such terms and conditions as are just."

6. **Rule 132, Section 22** provides [VERIFY 2019 text]:

> "The handwriting of a person may be proved by any witness who believes it to be the handwriting of such person because he has seen the person write, or has seen writing purporting to be his upon which the witness has acted or been charged, and has thus acquired knowledge of the handwriting of such person. Evidence respecting the handwriting may also be given by a comparison, made by the witness or the court, with writings admitted or treated as genuine by the party against whom the evidence is offered, or proved to be genuine to the satisfaction of the judge."

7. **Rule 130 on expert opinion** provides that "[t]he opinion of a witness on a matter requiring special knowledge, skill, experience or training which he is shown to possess, may be received in evidence" (Rule 130, Sec. 49 of the 1989 Rules [VERIFY 2019 section number and text]).

8. **Rule 29, Section 3** provides that if a party refuses to obey an order under Rule 27, "the court may make such orders in regard to the refusal as are just" [VERIFY 2019 text].

## III. Application: good cause

9. **Materiality.** The receipts are defendants' proof of payment, so their authenticity, dates and authorship are matters "involved in the action" (Rule 27, Sec. 1).

10. **The originals are needed.** The copies on record cannot show ink, pressure, stroke order, alterations, paper or printing. They cannot show whether the serially numbered forms were filled in on the dates they bear. Specifically, on the face of the copies:
    - (a) the serial numbers do not follow the dates in several places. Examples: No. 0122 is dated 30 June 2014 and No. 0121 is dated 31 July 2014. No. 0130 is dated 9 December 2014 and No. 0129 is dated 31 March 2016. No. 0220 is dated September 2016, and Nos. 0219 and 0218 are dated November 2016. No. 0227 is dated 7 January 2017 and No. 0226 is dated 30 January 2017 (Def. Exh. 5 pp.26–33);
    - (b) No. 0136 is dated May 2016 and the next receipt, No. 0217, is dated August 2016 (p.30);
    - (c) nine receipts totalling ₱219,000.00 bear dates after 29 September 2016. One of them, No. 0228, is marked "Full payment" (pp.31–33);
    - (d) several dates show strike-throughs or overwriting (pp.16, 18, 20).
11. **The documents are in defendants' possession, custody or control.** Defendants attached and marked them, and their witness says he issued them (Q&A 20–22).
12. **The examination requires a qualified examiner.** The questions are technical: ink, writing sequence, paper and printing, and comparison of handwriting under Rule 132, Sec. 22. Rule 130 allows the opinion of a witness with the needed special knowledge.
13. **No prejudice.** The examination proposed is non-destructive first. Any sampling would occur only with separate leave. The documents stay under court-ordered custody throughout. Plaintiff bears the costs.

## IV. Proposed terms

14. **Production.** Defendants produce the originals of all receipts in Exhibit "5" before the Branch Clerk of Court on a date the Court sets. They also produce any receipt booklets, stubs or duplicates from which those receipts came, if in their possession, custody or control. 15. **Examiner.** The originals are examined by the PNP Forensic Group, Questioned Document Examination Division, Camp BGen Rafael T. Crame, Quezon City, on a request or order of this Court. In the alternative, they are examined by a private questioned-documents examiner whom the Court approves after both parties are heard on qualifications.
16. **Scope** (Annex "A"). Non-destructive examination: magnification, ultraviolet and infrared examination, indentation analysis, comparison of paper and printing, and comparison of handwriting and signatures with standards admitted or proved genuine under Rule 132, Sec. 22.
17. **Sampling only with leave.** Removal of ink or paper samples, including solvent-extraction ink-age testing, will not take place without a separate motion, notice and hearing, and a specific order identifying the entries and the method.
17-A. **Separate request: leave to take micro-samples for ink-age analysis.** Plaintiff separately asks leave to have micro-samples (hollow-needle plugs about 0.5 mm in diameter, taken from ink lines away from signatures where possible) removed from up to ten (10) designated receipts, and from control entries on the same paper stock, for chemical ink analysis:
    - (a) ink comparison (thin-layer chromatography, ASTM E1422 / SWGDOC);
    - (b) ink identification against a reference library for first date of manufacture (ASTM E1789 / SWGDOC); and
    - (c) relative ink-age analysis by gas chromatography–mass spectrometry of volatile solvent components (2-phenoxyethanol), to determine whether the entries are consistent with their stated dates or were written within the period such methods can detect.

    The analysis would be done by a laboratory named by the Court after both parties are heard. Plaintiff proposes Riley, Welch, LaPorte & Associates (Frankenmuth, Michigan, USA) or Aginsky Forensic Document Dating Laboratory, Inc. (USA), or another qualified laboratory the Court approves [VERIFY availability and engagement terms before filing].

    Sampling is done in the presence of both counsel and the Branch Clerk, photographed before and after, with each plug's location mapped. Samples travel sealed by international courier with a chain-of-custody form signed at each hand-off. Defendants may have an expert observe, and may request split samples.

    **Urgency.** Published solvent-loss methods detect recent writing only within a limited window after the ink is placed on paper, reported in the range of months to about two years depending on the ink and method. If the entries were written recently, any delay in sampling permanently reduces or destroys the evidential value of this test. Plaintiff therefore asks that this request be resolved promptly and, if granted, that sampling be scheduled immediately after production.

## ANNEX "A" — Proposed scope of examination (questions for the examiner)

1. **Ink sameness:** Are the entries and the signature on each receipt in one ink? Which receipts share an ink formulation? Priority:
   - (a) the receipts signed "Von dela Fuente" (Def. Exh. 5 pp.2–3);
   - (b) the "Heirs of Mary W. Keesey Estate" receipts dated 2013–2017 (pp.23–33);
   - (c) Nos. 0222–0228 (pp.31–33).
2. **Relative ink age:** only upon further leave (¶17). Whether any entry was written within the period the chosen method can detect. Whether any ink formulation post-dates the date written.
3. **Alterations and sequence:** strike-throughs and overwriting on the dates (pp.16, 18, 20). Whether these entries were written at the same sitting as the rest of each receipt.
4. **Paper and printing:**
   - Printer imprint and authority numbers on the printed forms ("4C's Press-Daet", "Auth. No. 451-1998", "RD64-016-99", "25 Bks. (50x2) 0001-1250"), compared with the dates written on them.
   - Whether Nos. 0136 and 0217 come from the same print run.
   - Whether any generic form or paper is of later manufacture than its written date.
5. **Serial and booklet sequence:** whether the receipts were written in serial order. Whether indentations on any receipt correspond to the next receipt in the series. If the stubs or duplicates are produced, their payors and dates, especially Nos. 0137–0216.
6. **Handwriting and signatures:** comparison of the collector signatures (both forms) and the body entries with standards of Salvador Osum dela Fuente and with standards of Cesar M. de la Fuente. Whether one writer is responsible for the two signature forms.
7. **Heading and TIN:** whether the heading and TIN on the printed forms are part of the original printing, and are consistent across all forms.

## TIMING NOTE (internal; delete before filing)

- **The ink-age prayer loses value with every month of delay.** Jonathan's theory is that the entries were written near the 2026 production. Solvent-loss methods can show "written recently" only while the ink is still within its detectable window: months to about 2 years, depending on ink and method (INK_DATING_PLAN_2026-10-18 §3). If the receipts were written in, say, early 2026, the window may close during 2027.
- **This pulls against the rebuttal-surprise plan.** Filing prayer (c-1) now reveals the theory. Waiting for the defense's evidence may run out the clock. Recommendation for Barandon's decision: file the production motion with (c-1) as soon as the pre-trial order allows. The surprise value is in the results, not the request.
- **First confirm the inks are ballpoint.** GC-MS 2-PE methods suit ballpoint inks containing 2-phenoxyethanol, and are of limited use for many gel or felt inks. The non-destructive examination (¶16) should establish ink type before samples are taken.
