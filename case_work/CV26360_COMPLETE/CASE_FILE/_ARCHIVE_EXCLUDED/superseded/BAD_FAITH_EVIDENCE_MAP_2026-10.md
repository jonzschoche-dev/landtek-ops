## Position (read this first)

1. **The best bad-faith proof is already in the record and needs no new filing.** Exhibit B's memorandum of encumbrances (rows A1–A7) and the defense's own Exhibits 5 and 6 (rows B1–B5) show the whole push-through: Cesar's affidavit of loss two days before the deed; a court-ordered replacement duplicate; the price still being paid four months after a deed that "acknowledged" it; a June 2020 tax clearance taken under a title cancelled in 1999; and registration only in November 2021, after the revocation was published and four years after Cesar died. Barandon can use all of it on cross of Salvador dela Fuente, Princess and Erwin in Jan–Mar 2027.
2. **The single strongest new item is Donata King's Verified Answer of 9 Jul 2021** (row C1). It was received that day by the law office of the lawyer for Nelly Juntilla (Gloria's attorney-in-fact for this parcel) and Jomil Torralba (a 26-360 defendant). It attached the Bicol Post revocation notice and said Cesar "was stripped of" his authority "way back in 2005." That was 137 days before registration. Under *Duenas v. Metrobank* (row F1), good faith must last until registration. King is already a listed plaintiff witness (Exhibit L), so she can identify her own pleading by supplemental affidavit.
3. **The second is Gloria's own SPA of 15 Sep 2021 to Karen Gay A. Elevado** (row C6). It authorizes the transfer of "my property covered by … TCT No. T-52540" and "the re-issuance of ECAR." It is an RD certified copy, and its pages 2–3 are already our Exhibit E.
4. **Decision for Barandon before 4 Nov:** which of C1, C4, C6, C9 and C10 to bring in by supplemental judicial affidavit (Jonathan for the RD/RTC certified copies; King for her own Answer), and which to hold for cross. Everything in Part E stays out of the testimony pack.
5. **What the record does not support:** no forum ever found the Balanes in bad faith. The 2020 prosecutor's resolution that Jonathan remembers as finding that "they didn't know who the owner was" (row C2) actually *declined* to decide ownership, and it calls Gloria the "landowner." It is double-edged; use it only for the agency fact.

Row count: **76** (A 15 · B 16 · C 21 · D 13 · E 11 HELD). Part F is the legal frame, Part G the gaps, Part H the corrections.

## F. Legal frame for counsel (each point NEEDS-COUNSEL-VERIFICATION)

LandTek law library check, 12 Oct 2026. "In library" means the full text is in `legal_chunks` and the G.R. number appears in the stored text.

1. **Good faith must last until registration.** *Duenas v. Metropolitan Bank and Trust Co.*, G.R. No. 209463 (2022), **in library**: "The Court now holds that buyers of registered land must be continuing purchasers for value and in good faith until the registration of the conveyance. In the event a buyer of registered land who has yet to register the conveyance is made aware of any claim or interest of some other person in the property, or of any defect or restriction in the title of the seller or in his or her capacity to convey title, the buyer shall no longer be considered to be in good faith even if he or she subsequently registers the conveyance." Applied: A12 (publication Sept–Oct 2020), C1 (9 Jul 2021), A5 (registration 23 Nov 2021). PD 1529 §53, **in library**: "No voluntary instrument shall be registered by the Register of Deeds, unless the owner's duplicate certificate is presented with such instrument." That bears on who surrendered the replacement duplicate in 2021 (C4, C10).
2. **A buyer dealing with an agent must inquire at her peril.** *Bucton v. Go*, G.R. No. 188395 (2013), **in library**, quoting *San Pedro v. Ong*: "every person dealing with an agent is put upon inquiry, and must discover upon his peril the authority of the agent … If a person makes no inquiry, he is chargeable with knowledge of the agents authority." *Bucton* also faults buyers who lived in the same locality and could have contacted the owner. *Manalese v. Ferreras*, G.R. No. 254046 (2024), **in library**: reliance on the face of the title suffices only when "the seller is the registered owner of the land[;] … the latter is in possession thereof; and … the buyer was not aware of any claim." Here Cesar was neither the registered owner (A9) nor in possession (the Balanes were, as tenants, B7/B12). *Yoshizaki v. Joy Training Center*, G.R. No. 174978 (cited in our SJ), is **not in the library**: ingest and verify it before citing.
3. **A sitting public official's heightened diligence:** **no authority found in the library.** Do not argue it as a rule of law until counsel supplies a case. As a fact it still weighs (D6, B13).
4. **A tenant is not a possessor in the concept of owner.** Civil Code Art. 525, **in library**: possession is "either in the concept of owner, or in that of the holder of the thing or right to keep or enjoy it, the ownership pertaining to another person." Art. 1436, **in library**: "A lessee or a bailee is estopped from asserting title to the thing leased or received, as against the lessor or bailor." A builder in good faith must be "a possessor in concept of owner" (*Sarili v. Lagrosa*, G.R. No. 193517 (2014), **in library**). Applied: B1, B7, B12, D1.
5. **Revocation and third persons (the risk and the answer).** Art. 1921, **in library**: "If the agency has been entrusted for the purpose of contracting with specified persons, its revocation shall not prejudice the latter if they were not given notice thereof." **This is Ramos's best article.** If the court finds Gloria *inside* the Llamanzares class, the unnoticed 2005 revocation will not defeat her 2016 deed. So the class question decides both prongs, and our case must win it on the SPA's text and the receipts (B1–B3). Art. 1922, **in library**: "Notice of the revocation in a newspaper of general circulation is a sufficient warning to third persons." It applies to the 2021 registration (A12). Art. 1923, **in library**: "The appointment of a new agent for the same business or transaction revokes the previous agency from the day on which notice thereof was given to the former agent." This applies to C17, if receipt can ever be proven.
6. **Special power for sale; strict construction.** Art. 1878(5), **in library**: special powers are necessary "To enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted or acquired." Also in library: *Bautista-Spille v. Nicorp*, G.R. No. 214057 (2015); *Cosmic Lumber v. CA*, G.R. No. 114311 (1996); *Alcantara v. Nido*, G.R. No. 165133 (2010). Applied: C5 (the 1991 SPA has no sale power) and Exh. E ¶7 (class).
7. **Imputing an agent's knowledge to the principal** (Juntilla's notice as Gloria's notice, C1/C2): **no authority pulled.** This is the hinge of C1; counsel to supply it.
8. **Ratification by the agent / receipts in the estate's name** (B1, C11): Art. 1910 (principal's ratification) is the defense theory to anticipate. **Not re-verified this pass.**
