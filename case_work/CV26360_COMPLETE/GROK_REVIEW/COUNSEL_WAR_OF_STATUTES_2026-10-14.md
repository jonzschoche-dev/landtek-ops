## 1 · Genesis: why the theory is built statute-first

The plaintiff's theory is organized article by article, not case by case. The reason is the method this court has shown in the four orders it has issued in this case. All four are in the dossier at Annex AB.

| Order | Annex AB | What the text shows about method |
|---|---|---|
| 6 Feb 2026 | p. 1 | One recital, then "**Considering** the nature of the action, let it be known to the parties that this case is governed by the Amended Rules of Civil Procedure, the Revised Rules on Evidence, and the Rules on Electronic Filing." Then "WHEREFORE, let Summons … for them to file their Answer within THIRTY (30) CALENDAR DAYS from service." She names the governing rules before anyone asks, then disposes. |
| 11 Mar 2026 | p. 2 | "**Liberally applying the rules of procedure**, Atty. Ronald A. Ramos is given until tomorrow, March 12, 2026 …" and "**No further liberality shall be accorded.**" One indulgence, expressly limited. |
| 12 Mar 2026 | p. 3 | She grants the extension "As prayed for." She reminds the defendants that the Answer "must be strictly compliant with the Rules of Civil Procedure, specifically the amendments thereto, as well as the Rules on Electronic Filing." Parties are named in full, with no adjectives. |
| 4 Sep 2026 | p. 4 | "**Noting** both counsels timely compliance," then cancellation, then WHEREFORE with ten dated settings, "final and intransferable unless the ground for resetting is allowed under the rules." |

**What the four orders show:**

1. **Rule first.** Each order opens by naming the governing rule or the procedural fact.
2. **"Considering" or "Noting," then disposition.** Facts appear only as far as the disposition needs them, and the WHEREFORE paragraph is operative and specific (dates written out; numbers in words and figures).
3. **One indulgence, and it is announced as the last** (11 Mar).
4. **No characterization of any party.** Counsel and parties are named by name and role; no order describes anyone's motives.
5. **No case law cited** in any of the four orders.

**Consequence for our theory.** On this record, the decision will most likely turn on which article's words fit the exhibit page in front of her. Each battleground below is therefore stated in a fixed order:

- the plain words of the provision;
- the exhibit page that meets those words;
- Ramos's best answer, taken from the T3 red team and the Answer;
- a grade on first principles, meaning whether the text and the exhibit alone carry the point without help from case law.

Cases appear only where a verified Supreme Court sentence decides a point the text leaves open.

**Scope limits observed:**

- method only, drawn from her written orders in this case;
- nothing about the judge personally or about any other case before her;
- no tactic outside the pleadings and the Rules;
- no characterization of any defendant.

### 2.2 The writing: Arts. 1874, 1877, 1878

- **Art. 1874:** "When a sale of a piece of land or any interest therein is through an agent, the authority of the latter shall be in writing; otherwise, the sale shall be void."
- **Art. 1878:** "Special powers of attorney are necessary in the following cases: … (5) To enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted or acquired either gratuitously or for a valuable consideration; … (12) To create or convey real rights over immovable property; … (15) Any other act of strict dominion."
- **Art. 1877:** "An agency couched in general terms comprises only acts of administration, even if the principal should state that he withholds no power or that the agent may execute such acts as he may consider appropriate, or even though the agency should authorize a general and unlimited management."
- **Exhibit.** Exh. E p. 2 ¶7 (CTC p. 3) authorizes Cesar to act "in favor of third persons in possession of CONTRACTS TO SELL executed by the previous administrator, MR. BEN LLAMANZARES and/or to those who have paid their down-payments or have made partial payments therefor to the latter … after prior consultation with us, and to sign and execute the necessary documents … as well as to receive the proceeds." The SPA was annotated on 21 Jun 1996 (Exh. B p. 3).
- **Ramos's best answer** (T3 R1):
  - The writing exists, and it gives power "to sign and execute … and receive the proceeds," which is a power to sell (Answer ¶39).
  - Our own JA (Q25, Q53, Exh. K p. 7) and the SJ motion said "only to negotiate."
- **Verified case, if one sentence is wanted:**
  - *Bautista-Spille v. NICORP*, G.R. No. 214057, 19 Oct 2015: "a power of attorney must be strictly construed, and courts will not infer or presume broad powers from deeds which do not sufficiently include property or subject under which the agent is to deal."
  - *Cosmic Lumber Corp. v. CA*, G.R. No. 114311, 29 Nov 1996: a power of attorney "must so express the powers of the agent in clear and unmistakable language," and a sale beyond it is "ipso jure … void."
- **Grade: STRONG**, provided we concede the power to sign and receive for the ¶7 class and fight only on the class and the consultation. The writing is on the page, and Art. 1874 requires that the authority for *this* sale be in it. Nothing in ¶7 reaches a buyer outside the class.

**[INFERRED from SPA purpose] Argument for counsel under Arts. 1878 ¶5 and 1898: "Why negotiate, if a contract to sell already existed?"**

1. Ramos will read "negotiate" in the SPA and in the 1996 annotation ("to sign, negotiate for the sale," Exh. B p. 3) as a free hand to find buyers and bargain a price.
2. The text points the other way. The persons in ¶7 already held Llamanzares contracts to sell, or had paid down payments on them. For them, the thing and the price had already been agreed (see Art. 1475, §2.6).
3. What remained to "negotiate" was completion: balances, documents and the deed of final sale. That is why ¶7 couples "negotiate" with "to sign and execute the necessary documents … as well as to receive the proceeds."
4. So read, the SPA is a mandate to **close out existing Llamanzares-era bargains**, after consulting the owners. It is not a mandate to strike new bargains with new buyers.
5. A 2016 sale to a person who held no contract to sell, at a price fixed in 2016 (₱250,000, Exh. H; receipts Def. Exh. 5 pp. 31–33), is a new contract. Art. 1878 ¶5 requires a special power for it, and Art. 1898 voids it if the buyer was aware of the limits.
6. **Caution:** this is an inference from purpose, not words of the SPA. Use it to explain ¶7, never as a substitute for it. It also helps cure our JA's "negotiate only" language: "negotiate" described the completion role, not an absence of power to sign.

### 2.3 Excess of authority: Art. 1898 against Arts. 1900 and 1902

- **Art. 1898:** "If the agent contracts in the name of the principal, exceeding the scope of his authority, and the principal does not ratify the contract, it shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal."
- **Art. 1900:** "So far as third persons are concerned, an act is deemed to have been performed within the scope of the agent's authority, if such act is within the terms of the power of attorney, as written, even if the agent has in fact exceeded the limits of his authority according to an understanding between the principal and the agent."
- **Art. 1902:** "A third person with whom the agent wishes to contract on behalf of the principal may require the presentation of the power of attorney, or the instructions as regards the agency. Private or secret orders and instructions of the principal do not prejudice third persons who have relied upon the power of attorney or instructions shown them."
- **PD 1529 §52:** registered instruments are "constructive notice to all persons from the time of such registering, filing or entering."
- **Exhibits:**
  - Exh. E p. 2 ¶7, the limits "as written."
  - Exh. B p. 3, the SPA entered 21 Jun 1996.
  - Answer ¶¶40, 43 (Annex A p. 3): the family paid "rent."
  - The earliest receipt in Def. Exh. 5 is dated 25 Oct 2003, to "Von dela Fuente" (Def. Exh. 5 p. 2).
  - No Llamanzares contract or receipt has been produced.
- **Ramos's best answer** (T3 R2, R4):
  - (a) Gloria was in the class: she paid ₱3,000 in the 1980s (Answer ¶40), and a 6 Jan 2005 receipt says "Down payment" (Def. Exh. 5 p. 3).
  - (b) "Prior consultation" is an understanding between principal and agent, which Art. 1900 disregards and Art. 1902 calls a private order.
  - (c) The 1996 Registry summary omits the class, so the "awareness" Art. 1898 requires is not shown.
- **Our reply:**
  - The class and the consultation are "the terms of the power of attorney, as written." Art. 1900 protects only acts within those terms, and Art. 1902 excuses only "private or secret" orders, which a registered instrument is not.
  - Under §52, the instrument itself, not the Registry's summary, is the notice.
  - ¶7's down payments are those paid "to the latter," meaning Llamanzares. The 2005 receipt is to Cesar's camp.
  - Verified support:
    - *San Pedro v. Ong*, G.R. No. 177598, 17 Oct 2008: "Every person dealing with an agent is put upon inquiry, and must discover upon his peril the authority of the agent."
    - *Spouses Yabut v. Nachbaur*, G.R. No. 243470, 12 Jan 2021: one "should have inquired with the registered owners as to the extent of the authority of the attorney-in-fact, especially when the property is co-owned by two persons."
    - Adverse case to meet: *Heirs of Sarili v. Lagrosa*, G.R. No. 193517, 15 Jan 2014: "mere inspection of the face of such public document already constitutes sufficient inquiry." Our answer is that the face includes ¶7.
- **Grades:**
  - **Class limb, STRONG.** The words "to the latter" and "CONTRACTS TO SELL executed by … LLAMANZARES" are on Exh. E p. 2. The defense pleads rent, not a contract.
  - **Consultation limb, MEDIUM.** The text is ours, but proof that no consultation took place must come from a principal, and Jonathan cannot supply it for events before 2025.
  - **Art. 1898 "aware," MEDIUM.** It rests on §52 constructive notice plus *San Pedro* and *Yabut*. She may require actual awareness, which needs the dated documents in §2.8.

### 2.5 Void or unenforceable: Art. 1874 against Arts. 1317 and 1403(1)

- **Art. 1403:** "The following contracts are unenforceable, unless they are ratified: (1) Those entered into in the name of another person by one who has been given no authority or legal representation, or who has acted beyond his powers."
- **Art. 1317 ¶2:** "A contract entered into in the name of another by one who has no authority or legal representation, or who has acted beyond his powers, shall be unenforceable, unless it is ratified, expressly or impliedly, by the person on whose behalf it has been executed, before it is revoked by the other contracting party."
- **Our texts:**
  - Art. 1874 ("otherwise, the sale shall be void").
  - Art. 1898 ("it shall be void if the party … is aware of the limits").
  - Art. 1409 (§2.7).
- **Ramos's best answer** (Answer ¶42; T3 R6): exceeding a *written* authority is "acted beyond his powers," which is the express case of Arts. 1317 and 1403(1). Art. 1874 speaks only of the absence of a writing, and here a writing exists.
- **Our reply:**
  - The special rule for land sold through an agent is Art. 1874, read with Art. 1898. Where the buyer is aware of the limits, the Code itself says "void," not "unenforceable."
  - *Cosmic Lumber* (G.R. No. 114311): a sale beyond the written power is "ipso jure … void."
  - *MCIAA v. Unchuan*, G.R. No. 182537, 1 Jun 2016: "a void contract produces no effect either against or in favor of anyone and cannot be ratified," and "laches will not set in against a void transaction, as in this case, where the agent did not have a special power of attorney to dispose of the lots co-owned by the other registered owners."
  - Do **not** cite *Alcantara v. Nido* (G.R. No. 165133) beyond Art. 1874's no-writing case (T1).
- **Grade: MEDIUM.** The text supports "void" only through Art. 1898's awareness condition. If she finds Gloria unaware, Art. 1403(1) applies and ratification (§2.4) becomes live. This is the hinge of the case.

### 2.6 Perfection of sale: Art. 1475

- **Art. 1475:** "The contract of sale is perfected at the moment there is a meeting of minds upon the thing which is the object of the contract and upon the price. From that moment, the parties may reciprocally demand performance, subject to the provisions of the law governing the form of contracts."
- **Exhibits:**
  - Exh. H: deed dated 29 Sep 2016, ₱250,000 "in hand paid."
  - Def. Exh. 5 pp. 31–33: receipts to Feb 2017, including No. 0219 for ₱40,000 and No. 0228 "Full payment."
  - Exh. B p. 6: registration on 23 Nov 2021.
- **Ramos's best answer:** minds met on the lot and the price, at the latest by the 1980s ₱3,000 payment or in 2016. The sale was perfected and performed.
- **Our reply:**
  - A meeting of minds binds the principal only through an agent's authority, and perfection is "subject to the provisions of the law governing the form of contracts," which include Art. 1874's writing rule.
  - The 2016 price-and-lot agreement is itself the "new bargain" described in §2.2. It shows the sale was not the completion of a Llamanzares contract.
- **Grade: STRONG** as a defensive point. Art. 1475 gives Ramos nothing that Art. 1874 does not take away, and the 2016 price helps our class argument.

### 2.7 Consequences of nullity: Arts. 1409(7) and 1410

- **Art. 1409:** "The following contracts are inexistent and void from the beginning: … (7) Those expressly prohibited or declared void by law. These contracts cannot be ratified. Neither can the right to set up the defense of illegality be waived."
- **Art. 1410:** "The action or defense for the declaration of the inexistence of a contract does not prescribe."
- **Ramos's best answer:** laches and estoppel, based on fifty years of possession, no objection, and no demand until 18 Aug 2025 (Answer ¶43; Exh. J).
- **Our reply:**
  - If the sale is void under Arts. 1874 and 1898, Art. 1409(7) bars ratification and Art. 1410 bars prescription (*Unchuan*, above).
  - Possession before the deed was as lessees (Answer ¶¶40, 43). *Esguerra v. Manantan*, G.R. No. 158328, 23 Feb 2007: "mere possession with a juridical title, such as by a usufructuary, a trustee, a lessee, an agent or a pledgee, not being in the concept of an owner, cannot ripen into ownership."
- **Grade: STRONG, but derivative.** It stands or falls with §2.5.

### 2.8 Revocation and notice: Arts. 1921 and 1922; PD 1529 §64

- **Art. 1921:** "If the agency has been entrusted for the purpose of contracting with specified persons, its revocation shall not prejudice the latter if they were not given notice thereof."
- **Art. 1922:** "If the agent had general powers, revocation of the agency does not prejudice third persons who acted in good faith and without knowledge of the revocation. Notice of the revocation in a newspaper of general circulation is a sufficient warning to third persons."
- **PD 1529 §64:** "Any person may, by power of attorney, convey or otherwise deal with registered land and the same shall be registered with the Register of Deeds of the province or city where the land lies. Any instrument revoking such power of attorney shall be registered in like manner."
- **Exhibits:**
  - Exh. F, 15 Aug 2005.
  - Exh. G and G-1, Bicol Post, 25 Sep, 2 Oct and 9 Oct 2020.
  - Exh. B: the revocation was **never annotated**.
- **Ramos's best answer** (T3 R5):
  - Exh. F does not name Cesar.
  - It was never registered, although §64 says it "shall be registered."
  - It was published four years after the deed.
  - *Central Surety & Insurance Co. v. Hodges*, G.R. No. L-28633, 30 Mar 1971 (En Banc): notice by newspaper "is a sufficient warning to third persons," and the principal there had published none.
- **Our reply, strictly in sequence:**
  - **First**, the sale was outside the agency, so Arts. 1921 and 1922 are never engaged. Gloria is not one of the "specified persons."
  - **Second**, *Republic v. Ciruelas*, G.R. No. 239505, 17 Feb 2021: §64 "does not state that the registration of an SPA is a prerequisite to its validity or conversely, its non-registration makes the agent's authority ineffective." Use it only for the proposition that the unregistered revocation is still valid. **Double edge:** Ramos can read it the other way.
  - **Third**, Art. 1922 concerns agents with "general powers," and Cesar's were special.
  - Never argue the 2020 publication as Art. 1922 notice of the 2016 sale. It goes only to good faith before the 23 Nov 2021 registration (§2.9).
- **Grade: STRONG if sequenced. MEDIUM on §64 alone.** The unregistered revocation is Ramos's fact, and *Central Surety* cuts both ways.

### 2.9 Good faith and its duration: Arts. 526, 527, 528; PD 1529 §§51, 53

- **Art. 526:** "He is deemed a possessor in good faith who is not aware that there exists in his title or mode of acquisition any flaw which invalidates it. He is deemed a possessor in bad faith who possesses in any case contrary to the foregoing."
- **Art. 527:** "Good faith is always presumed, and upon him who alleges bad faith on the part of a possessor rests the burden of proof."
- **Art. 528:** "Possession acquired in good faith does not lose this character except in the case and from the moment facts exist which show that the possessor is not unaware that he possesses the thing improperly or wrongfully."
- **PD 1529 §51:** "The act of registration shall be the operative act to convey or affect the land insofar as third persons are concerned."
- **PD 1529 §53** (only the clause relied on): the new certificate binds "in favor of every purchaser for value and in good faith." The "not aware" test comes from Art. 526, not §53 (T1).
- **Verified cases:**
  - *Heirs of Bucton v. Spouses Go*, G.R. No. 188395, 20 Nov 2013: "The burden of proving the status of a purchaser in good faith and for value lies upon one who asserts that status. This onus probandi cannot be discharged by mere invocation of the ordinary presumption of good faith." (Cite this quote to *Bucton* alone.)
  - *Duenas v. Metrobank*, G.R. No. 209463, 29 Nov 2022 (En Banc): "It is the party who claims to be an innocent purchaser for value who has the burden of proving such assertion, and it is not enough to invoke the ordinary presumption of good faith." Good faith runs "until registration of the sale on the title."
  - *Heirs of Gonzales v. Basas*, G.R. No. 206847, 15 Jun 2022: "Registration does not vest title; it is merely the evidence of such title."
- **Exhibits:**
  - Deed 29 Sep 2016; registration 23 Nov 2021 (Exh. B p. 6).
  - Cesar's affidavit of loss, dated 27 Sep 2016, two days before the deed (Exh. B p. 5).
  - Publication 2020 (Exh. G).
  - Elevado's certified copy of the SPA containing ¶7, 16 Aug 2021 (Exh. E footer).
  - [OFFER] items:
    - Gloria's SPA of 28 May 2020 to Nelly Juntilla, "to institute or file action regarding property in Purok 4, Barangay San Roque" (Annex M p. 1 [VERIFY body]);
    - King's Verified Answer with the Bicol Post notice, received by the Aquino Law Office on 9 Jul 2021 (Annex N pp. 1, 6);
    - Gloria's "my property," 15 Sep 2021 (Annex K p. 9).
- **Ramos's best answer** (T3 R11):
  - Art. 527 presumes good faith.
  - *Sarili*: inspecting the SPA's face is enough.
  - The title showed the SPA and no revocation.
  - Notice to Aquino, who was counsel in another case for Juntilla and Torralba, is not notice to Gloria.
- **Grade: MEDIUM.**
  - The burden rule (*Bucton*, *Duenas*) and the duration rule (*Duenas*; §51) are strong law.
  - Of the dated facts, the publication and the Exh. E footer are in. The 2020 SPA, King's pleading and "my property" are [OFFER], and imputing the Aquino notice to Gloria is an unresolved legal step (T6 J3).
  - **Caution:** *Vallido v. Pono*, G.R. No. 200173, may be cited only for its own sentence: "The burden of proving good faith lies with the second buyer … which is not discharged by simply invoking the ordinary presumption of good faith." Do not cite it for the "concurrence" line, which belongs to the CA.

### 2.11 Improvements: Arts. 448, 546 and 548 against Arts. 449–452, 546 ¶1 and 1678

- **Art. 448:** "The owner of the land on which anything has been built, sown or planted in good faith, shall have the right to appropriate as his own the works, sowing or planting, after payment of the indemnity provided for in articles 546 and 548, or to oblige the one who built or planted to pay the price of the land …"
- **Art. 449:** "He who builds, plants or sows in bad faith on the land of another, loses what is built, planted or sown without right to indemnity."
- **Art. 450:** "The owner of the land on which anything has been built, planted or sown in bad faith may demand the demolition of the work, or that the planting or sowing be removed, in order to replace things in their former condition at the expense of the person who built, planted or sowed; or he may compel the builder or planter to pay the price of the land, and the sower the proper rent."
- **Art. 451:** "In the cases of the two preceding articles, the landowner is entitled to damages from the builder, planter or sower."
- **Art. 452:** "The builder, planter or sower in bad faith is entitled to reimbursement for the necessary expenses of preservation of the land."
- **Art. 546:** "Necessary expenses shall be refunded to every possessor; but only the possessor in good faith may retain the thing until he has been reimbursed therefor. Useful expenses shall be refunded only to the possessor in good faith with the same right of retention …"
- **Art. 1678:** "If the lessee makes, in good faith, useful improvements which are suitable to the use for which the lease is intended, without altering the form or substance of the property leased, the lessor upon the termination of the lease shall pay the lessee one-half of the value of the improvements at that time. Should the lessor refuse to reimburse said amount, the lessee may remove the improvements …"
- **Exhibits:**
  - Answer ¶¶40, 43: the family was renting.
  - Def. Exh. 5 p. 1: the 2006 receipt.
  - No dated structure is in the record.
- **Ramos's best answer** (T3 R14):
  - *DepEd v. Casibang*, G.R. No. 192268, 27 Jan 2016: "Despite being a possessor by mere tolerance, the DepEd is considered a builder in good faith, since Cepeda permitted the construction."
  - *Macasaet v. Macasaet*, G.R. Nos. 154391-92, 30 Sep 2004.
  - The family built with the administrators' knowledge.
- **Our reply:**

### 2.12 Fruits, damages and fees: Art. 544 against Arts. 549, 2208, 2232 and 2234

- **Art. 544:** "A possessor in good faith is entitled to the fruits received before the possession is legally interrupted."
- **Art. 549:** "The possessor in bad faith shall reimburse the fruits received and those which the legitimate possessor could have received …"
- **Art. 2232:** "In contracts and quasi-contracts, the court may award exemplary damages if the defendant acted in a wanton, fraudulent, reckless, oppressive, or malevolent manner."
- **Art. 2234:** "… the plaintiff must show that he is entitled to moral, temperate or compensatory damages before the court may consider the question of whether or not exemplary damages should be awarded."
- **Art. 2208:** fees "cannot be recovered, except: (1) When exemplary damages are awarded; (2) When the defendant's act or omission has compelled the plaintiff to litigate with third persons or to incur expenses to protect his interest; … (5) Where the defendant acted in gross and evident bad faith in refusing to satisfy the plaintiff's plainly valid, just and demandable claim; … In all cases, the attorney's fees and expenses of litigation must be reasonable."
- **Exhibits:** Answer ¶27 (rooms rented after 2016); Exh. J (demand of 18 Aug 2025; receipt 22 Aug 2025 [VERIFY return card]).
- **Ramos's best answer:** Art. 544 lets a good-faith possessor keep the fruits. Exemplary damages require a finding in the words of Art. 2232, which this court does not make on impression.
- **Grades:**
  - **Fruits from the demand: MEDIUM**, on Answer ¶27 and Exh. J.
  - **Exemplary damages: WEAK** as now pleaded. No moral, temperate or compensatory award has been pleaded as a basis (Art. 2234; T6 J2), and the court's method does not reach adjectives.
  - **Fees under Art. 2208 ¶2: MEDIUM.**
