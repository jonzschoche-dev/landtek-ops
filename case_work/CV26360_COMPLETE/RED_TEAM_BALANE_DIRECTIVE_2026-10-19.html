Directive to counsel: how we keep the lot

From: the family (Efren and Gloria, abroad; Princess and Jomil; Erwin) · To: Atty. Ronald A. Ramos · Date: October 2026, after the plaintiff's first witness · Trial dates: theirs 4 Nov, 11 Nov, 2 Dec, 9 Dec 2026; ours 13 Jan to 10 Mar 2027, "final and intransferable."

1. Where we stand

The title is in Mother's name since 23 November 2021. We hold the original deed, the receipts, the Registry's certified copies, and the 1992 power the heirs themselves signed and annotated on the title in 1996. The plaintiff's son has testified and conceded he knows nothing of the years before 2025 except from documents and from his mother. The mother is in California, seventy-nine, and has not filed an affidavit. The next three plaintiff witnesses are neighbours. Their lawyer has filed nothing since April except what the court ordered. The summary judgment motion has no ruling. We do not need to win the law to win the case; we need the plaintiff to fail to prove it, and we need the title to stand until they run out of time and money.

2. What we have, in order of weight

  1. The title. A Torrens title is indefeasible after one year; their attack must be direct; they sued in the MTC. Keep the collateral-attack point alive in every pleading even if the court passes over it.
  2. The 1992 power, "read as a whole." Paragraph 7 says "negotiate for the sale"; paragraph 8 gives "any and every act … requisite, necessary or proper." The heirs annotated it themselves. They never annotated any revocation (PD 1529 §64 says a revocation "shall be registered in like manner"). Article 1900: an act is within the agent's authority "if such act is within the terms of the power of attorney as written." Article 1902: a third person may rely on the power shown. Article 1911: the principal is liable where he "allowed the latter to act as though he had full powers."
  3. Pattern of acceptance. The heirs let Cesar sell for years. Rosalina Hansol's sale (1999) produced TCT T-50192 without objection. Mother paid, Cesar's side received, nobody from the family in California ever wrote to us. Article 1403(1): an unauthorized contract is unenforceable, not void, and ratification "may be express or implied." Laches and estoppel in the alternative.
  4. Revocation and notice. Nothing was communicated to any of us before the 2016 sale (Answer ¶¶15, 19). The newspaper notice was in September and October 2020, four years after the deed, names only "TCT T-4497," not our title or our lot, and Article 1922 makes publication a warning "to third persons" from publication forward. Mother was in Canada and never saw it.
  5. Good faith and the official record. The Provincial Prosecutor, in 2020, twice wrote that Nelly acted for "the landowner" and that preventing construction was "a form of an exercise of a lawful right." The Registry registered the sale, the confirmation and the partition in 2021 without objection. Mother "checked the SPA, the Registry of Deeds records, tax records" (Answer ¶44[h]) and found nothing against the sale.
  6. Possession and improvements. We have been on this lot since 1975. The house, the roof, the rooms: Article 448 gives a builder in good faith the right to indemnity or to buy the land, with retention under Article 546 until paid.
  7. Their side's weaknesses as witnesses. The son has no personal knowledge. The mother is absent. Mrs. King was convicted of perjury by MTC Daet in 2023 (reversed on appeal) over this very dispute, is Nelly's and Jomil's adversary in two cases, and herself claims rights on the compound through a 2015 purchase from Ruby Ferrer. Francia de los Reyes was King's co-defendant and learned what she knows "kay Jonathan at sa mga dokumentong ipinakita niya." Shirley De Leon's father "bought" his lot the same way we did: from the administrators, not from California.

  3. Each plaintiff witness: object to any document not pre-marked; object to any question beyond the affidavit; move to strike opinion on what we "knew."
  4. 9 December, their offer: oppose Exhibits E and H if offered as the short Registry copies (pages missing); oppose G and G-1 for relevance to our title; note that no witness authenticated the 2005 revocation from personal knowledge.
  5. After they rest: demurrer to evidence. Ground: no competent proof that the buyer was outside paragraph 7, no proof of non-consultation from any principal, no proof of notice to the buyer before registration. If the demurrer is denied we present; the ruling is interlocutory.
  6. Patricia on rebuttal: oppose leave for a late affidavit; oppose videoconference unless the Supreme Court guidelines are met; if she testifies, cross on the 2023 affidavit of loss, on the 1991 and 1992 powers she signed, on the sales she never questioned, and on her absence from the Philippines since 1992.
  7. Non-joinder: plead that the agent's estate is an indispensable party to a case that voids his deed; keep it for appeal even if the court follows Rule 3 §11.
  8. Judgment against us: appeal to the RTC on the questions of law (collateral attack; Article 1900; Article 1403; Article 1922; indefeasibility); ask for execution to be stayed; the family stays in the house throughout.

  • No one speaks to Mrs. King, Shirley De Leon, Francia de los Reyes or the plaintiff's son. No messages, no posts, no barangay complaints. Anything said now becomes an exhibit.
  • No new construction, no new tenants, no fence moved. The lot stays exactly as it is in the 2020 plan.

  • Pay the real property tax on time, in Mother's name, and keep the receipts.
  • Settlement: none offered. If approached, the only terms are that the title stands and the heirs withdraw. We can afford to wait; they are paying a Manila firm and a Daet firm from California.
