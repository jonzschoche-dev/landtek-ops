Civil Case No. 26-360: the overwhelm plan

Prepared 14 October 2026, after Jonathan's direct testimony · internal, for counsel · not a pleading · nothing here is filed or served without counsel.

Why Ramos let Jonathan testify without a fight. His strongest move does not depend on cross. After we rest on 9 December he can ask for dismissal "on the ground that upon the facts and the law the plaintiff has shown no right to relief" (Rule 33, §1). If our proof of any element rests only on an attorney-in-fact who learned of the sale in late 2022, he will say that element is hearsay and unproved. If the court denies the demurrer, he loses nothing: "he shall have the right to present evidence." Under the 2019 amendments, a denial cannot be challenged before judgment [Rule 33 §2 as amended; counsel to confirm the official text].

So "overwhelm" means one thing. By 9 December, every element of our claim must be proved at least twice in our own formal offer, and never only through Jonathan. Ramos can then attack Jonathan all he likes and the case still stands. The tools are the defendants' own words, certified public records, and witnesses who saw things themselves.

Consultation must be proved, and not by hearsay. Non-consultation is part of our own claim, so it is ours to prove (Rule 131 §1: a party must present evidence "on the facts in issue necessary to establish" its claim). On cross, Ramos established that Jonathan was not present for the events before 2025 (as recorded in the consultation gap plan written earlier today). Anything Jonathan says about 1992 to 2016 is therefore not proof of those events. The buyer's burden on her own good faith (Bucton) is a backstop, not a substitute. Part 2A sets out what each piece of proof is good for.

Decision, 14 October: Patricia testifies on rebuttal, not in our case-in-chief. Three consequences follow.

  1. The case-in-chief must survive a demurrer without her. It rests on the class limit, proved by the certified SPA, the defendants' Answer and the request for admission. Consultation stays pleaded, but it is not proved first-hand until she testifies.
  2. She can only answer what they put in. Rebuttal is "rebutting evidence only, unless the court, for good reasons and in the furtherance of justice, permits them to adduce evidence upon their original case" (Rule 30 §5[f]). If no defense witness or exhibit claims the owners were consulted, approved, knew, or ratified, her denial has nothing to rebut. It then depends on that exception, from a judge who has written "No further liberality shall be accorded." So we must make sure the defense puts those claims into evidence. Part 2C lists the openings.
  3. Her filing date is a question for counsel. The Judicial Affidavit Rule requires affidavits "not later than five days before pre-trial" (§2[a]), and hers was not filed then. Counsel decides whether a rebuttal affidavit still needs the once-only leave for late filing (§10[a]). If it does, that leave stays reserved for her.

2B. Your point on T-52540, in the form that survives

The SPA names one property: the undivided 139,132 square meters under T-4497 (Exh. E ¶3). It never names T-52540, Lot 2-X-6-I-4-C, or any later lot. The 2016 deed does not name T-52540 either. The only papers that connect this sale to T-52540 are Cesar's own Deed of Confirmation of 28 March 2017, which no owner signed, and the Registry's registration stamp of 23 November 2021, made after his death. An agent cannot widen his own authority by confirming his own act; only the principal ratifies (Art. 1910). Read strictly, as Bautista-Spille requires ("courts will not infer or presume broad powers from deeds which do not sufficiently include property or subject under which the agent is to deal"), the power does not reach a sale described only by a later subdivision lot that the SPA never names.

How Ramos will answer it, and the reply. He will say T-52540 is part of the T-4497 parcel, that the Registry carried the SPA onto T-52540 in 1996 "to sign, negotiate for the sale of the parcel of land in this title" (Exh. B p. 3), and that the 2005 Era sale went through under it. Our reply: the Registry's carry-over is a clerical act; under PD 1529 §52 the instrument, not the Registry's summary, defines the power, and that is the same rule we rely on for the class limit. The Era sale came before the revocation. Use it alongside the class and the consultation, not instead of them. On its own, the point invites the one-sentence answer that a power over the whole covers a part. Two cautions: do not argue that the subdivisions creating T-52540 were themselves unauthorized, because T-52540 is the owners' own title and our Exhibit B; and if Ramos offers the 1991 Agcaoili SPA annotated on 10 October 2016 (Exh. B p. 5) as another source of authority, it fails: the 1991 SPA (Doc. No. 260, Page No. 53, Book No. I, Series of 1991, Makati, acknowledged before the Agcaoili notary) is in our own certified Registry file Ref. 2025001098, pp. 2 to 4, and in the Spec. Proc. 2156 record. It grants general control and supervision of the properties, payment of taxes, collection of money with power to sign receipts, and representation before the BIR, the Registry and DAR. It grants no power to sell. A power in general terms "comprises only acts of administration" (Art. 1877), and a sale needs a special power (Art. 1878[5]).

3. Four filings that do the overwhelming, all procedurally clean

  1. Request for admission (Rule 26), served by 21 October. Draft attached as Part 6 (Annex A of the memorandum pack of 15 October). Each matter is "deemed admitted unless," within the period stated (not less than fifteen days), the defendants file "a sworn statement either denying specifically" or explaining why they cannot. Every denial must be sworn, so Gloria, Efren and Princess commit under oath before their witnesses take the stand, and every false denial becomes impeachment. The rule also cuts the other way. A party who fails to serve a request on facts "within the personal knowledge" of the adverse party "shall not be permitted to present evidence on such facts" unless the court allows it for good cause (Rule 26 §5). Serving now protects our own proof.
