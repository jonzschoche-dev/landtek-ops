C2. Their Exhibit 6 (the certified deed): the BIR eCARs

Page 2 of their certified copy carries two eCARs for this one sale: one dated 19 June 2020 naming T-38838 as the title, and one dated 18 November 2021 naming T-52540. Taxes were paid on 15 June 2020. T-38838 is an earlier title over this same land: Lot 2-X-6-I, 32,448 sqm, issued 11 April 1996 in Mary Worrick Keesey's name, later subdivided into T-49061 (31,027 sqm) and then T-52540 (around 2000). I hold a certified copy of T-38838. So in 2020 the BIR cleared a 2016 sale under a title superseded some twenty years earlier, and the clearance had to be redone in 2021. T-38838 is cancelled: the paper title bears "CANCELLED" stamps referencing T.C.T. No. T-49060 and T.C.T. No. 40713, consistent with the request of 1 March 1999 (PE-226716-25-25) to segregate Lots 2-X-6-I-3 and 2-X-6-I-4 (Psd-05-019929), after the 1996 sales of small portions to Jose Pascual Jr., Roscoe Leaño and Cesar Ramirez. Yet the Registry's computerized record for T-38838 carries the October 2016 Affidavit of Loss, the 1991 SPA, the March 2017 court-ordered duplicate and my mother's 2023 affidavit, under the same entry numbers as on T-52540. So Cesar's 2016–2017 instruments were processed against a title cancelled some seventeen years earlier, and the 2020 eCAR followed suit. Taken together, it suggests the sale was papered without the current title in hand. Questions for their witnesses: why was a 2016 sale first processed in 2020, under a long-cancelled title, and who surrendered Cesar's 2017 replacement duplicate for registration in 2021, four years after his death?

C5. Building permits for the structures on the lot: the Building Official has refused to produce them

Nothing in our files shows any building or occupancy permit issued to the Balanes for the structures on Lot 2-X-6-I-4-C(-1), Brgy. San Roque (Brgy. 1). Engr. Erwin H. Balane is the Municipal Engineer and Building Official, and his office has refused my requests for permit records concerning Barangay 1, including a 2021 subdivision. (Those refusals became the ARTA complaints; I will not mention them on the stand.)

Why it matters: under the National Building Code a permit application must show the applicant's right over the land, a title in his name or the owner's written consent or lease. Whatever the Balanes submitted before 2016, if anything, is evidence of what they then acknowledged about ownership; if nothing was issued, their improvements went up without permits, which bears on any claim to be builders in good faith. And Erwin's own affidavit denies using his office in connection with the sale.

Since a further request from me will be refused and used to paint harassment, I suggest the court's process instead: a subpoena duces tecum (Rule 21) to the Office of the Building Official for all building and occupancy permits, applications and supporting documents for structures on the lot, in the names of Efren, Gloria, Erwin or Ferdinand Balane, or a certification of no record. Non-compliance is then a matter for the court, not for ARTA. On his cross, the question "does your office hold permits for your family's structures, and will you produce them?" asked in front of the judge is worth more than any FOI refusal. Sources outside the Balanes' control that show the same facts: the Bureau of Fire Protection (fire safety clearances issued for the structures), CANORECO (the documents on which electric service was connected), and the Barangay 1 clearance records for construction. The Provincial Assessor does not hold copies of the municipal tax declarations, so the declarations on the improvements would also have to come by subpoena to the Municipal Assessor. Your call on timing; it may be better served after my testimony.
