PART 1. THE DECISION THIS CASE TURNS ON

The court has to answer one question. Was Gloria H. Balane a purchaser in good faith and for value when she bought from an agent, Cesar M. de la Fuente, a 2,587-square-meter portion of land registered in the names of three sisters who were never consulted and never paid?

If she was not, the rest follows by statute. A sale of land by an agent outside the written authority is void (Civil Code, Articles 1874, 1878 paragraph 5 and 1898). A void contract cannot be ratified and the action to declare it void does not prescribe (Articles 1409 and 1410). Registration adds nothing to a void deed (Presidential Decree No. 1529, Sections 51 and 53). A builder in bad faith loses what she built without indemnity (Article 449). A possessor in bad faith returns the fruits (Article 549). Exemplary damages and attorney's fees follow a finding of bad faith (Articles 2208, 2229 and 2232).

The Supreme Court has settled who carries the burden. "The burden of proving the status of a purchaser in good faith and for value lies upon one who asserts that status. This onus probandi cannot be discharged by mere invocation of the ordinary presumption of good faith." (Heirs of Bucton v. Spouses Go, G.R. No. 188395, 20 November 2013.) The En Banc has settled when that good faith must exist. "Buyers of registered land must be continuing purchasers for value and in good faith until the registration of the conveyance." (Duenas v. Metropolitan Bank and Trust Co., G.R. No. 209463, 29 November 2022.)

Gloria Balane bought on 29 September 2016 and registered on 23 November 2021. Her good faith therefore has to survive five years, one month and twenty-five days. The record shows it did not survive the first day, and it shows what she and her household learned in every year after.

The forward target. A judgment that declares the deed void, cancels TCT No. 079-2021002126 and the tax declaration issued on it, restores possession to the registered owners, forfeits the improvements built after 29 September 2016, orders an accounting of fruits from the date of the demand, and awards exemplary damages and attorney's fees. The En Banc disposition in Duenas is the template: forfeiture of improvements without indemnity, exemplary damages, and attorney's fees because the owners "were compelled to litigate." Part 6 writes it out in the form this court uses.

The sentence that carries the case. Cesar sold to a buyer outside the class his authority allowed, without the consultation it required, and the buyer knew the owners, paid them rent for decades, never asked them, and finished the paperwork years after the agent was dead and his authority had been published as revoked.

PART 3. WHAT THE FACTS PROVE

Each element below is a rule the court must apply, stated by article or section, followed by the facts (by F-number) that satisfy it and the one ruling that decides it.

3.1 The sale was outside the written authority, so it is void

Rule. Article 1874: "When a sale of a piece of land or any interest therein is through an agent, the authority of the latter shall be in writing; otherwise, the sale shall be void." Article 1878, paragraph 5, requires a special power "to enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted." Article 1898: a contract made by an agent "exceeding the scope of his authority" which the principal does not ratify "shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal." A power of attorney "must be strictly construed, and courts will not infer or presume broad powers from deeds which do not sufficiently include property or subject under which the agent is to deal" (Bautista-Spille v. NICORP Management, G.R. No. 214057, 19 October 2015). Where the agent acts outside the power, "the sale ipso jure is consequently void" (Cosmic Lumber Corp. v. Court of Appeals, G.R. No. 114311, 29 November 1996).

Application. The written authority is F3. It permits a sale only to holders of Llamanzares contracts to sell or persons who paid Llamanzares, and only after prior consultation with the owners. Gloria Balane was a tenant (F1, F2, F8) whose earliest payment was to Cesar's side in 2003 (F5). No consultation was ever made (F10). The limits were on the title from 1996 (F4) and in the buyer's hands by August 2021 at the latest (F31). Article 1898 makes the contract void because the buyer is charged with the limits (PD 1529, Section 52).

Their own witness reads the clause the same way. Salvador's 2025 letter describes the owners' instruction as reaching only "the people in Mercedes who possesses CONTRACT OF SALE issued by Benjamin Llamanzares," and treats everyone else as a problem to be "appease[d]" with documents (F41). That is the class limit, stated by the man who collected the Balanes' money.

The property description: use it for specificity, not as a stand-alone "the SPA never covered this lot." The SPA describes only the undivided 139,132-square-meter T-4497 parcel (F3). Under Bautista-Spille a court "will not infer or presume broad powers from deeds which do not sufficiently include property or subject under which the agent is to deal," and a 2,587-square-meter cut from Lot 2-X-6-I-4-C is nowhere in the instrument. That supports strict reading of the class and the consultation clause. It should not be run as a separate nullity ground, because T-52540 descends from T-4497 on the face of Exhibit B, the Registry carried the SPA onto T-52540 in 1996 (F4), and the 2005 sale to Mariquita Era of another portion of the same lot was registered under it. The defense would answer all three in one sentence. The better use is in 4.1: the SPA they rely on and the revocation notice they say they could not connect to their land describe the property in exactly the same words.

Why this leads, and not "no power to sell." Paragraph 7 also lets Cesar "sign and execute the necessary documents" and "receive the proceeds." A court reading the whole clause will find a power to complete a sale for the class. Our affidavit's "limited to negotiation" wording (Q26, Q53) should be conceded on cross and the class limit held. The class limit needs no construction against the grant; it is the grant.

3.2 The buyer dealt with an agent and had to inquire at her peril

Rule. "Every person dealing with an agent is put upon inquiry, and must discover upon his peril the authority of the agent ... If a person makes no inquiry, he is chargeable with knowledge of the agent's authority, and his ignorance of that authority will not be any excuse" (San Pedro v. Ong, G.R. No. 177598, 17 October 2008). "In case the buyer does not deal with the registered owner of the real property, the law requires that a higher degree of prudence be exercised by the purchaser" (Bucton). "Despite the presence of an alleged SPA from the registered owners, respondent should have inquired with the registered owners as to the extent of the authority of the attorney-in-fact, especially when the property is co-owned" (Spouses Yabut v. Nachbaur, G.R. No. 243470, 12 January 2021). The presumption of good faith applies only when three conditions concur: the seller is the registered owner, the seller is in possession, and the buyer is unaware of any defect in the seller's capacity to convey; "absent one or two of the foregoing conditions, then the law itself puts the buyer on notice and obliges the latter to exercise a higher degree of diligence" (Duenas, restated in Chua v. Republic, G.R. No. 253305, 2 August 2023).

Application. The seller was not the registered owner. The seller was not in possession; the buyer was, as a tenant (F1, F16). Two days before the deed the seller swore the owner's duplicate was lost (F11), the warning sign the Court named in Vallido v. Pono, G.R. No. 200173, 15 April 2013 ("during the sale ... [the seller] did not have the owner's duplicate copy of the title"). The buyer knew who the owners were, paid them rent for decades (F8), held a tax declaration giving their California address (F24), and knew their Manila counsel was the family's contact (F10). She never asked them. In Bucton the Court found it "inconceivable" that buyers who personally knew the owners made no inquiry. That is this case.

3.3 Good faith had to last until 23 November 2021, and it did not

Rule. "In the event a buyer of registered land who has yet to register the conveyance is made aware of any claim or interest of some other person in the property, or of any defect or restriction in the title of the seller or in his or her capacity to convey title, the buyer shall no longer be considered to be in good faith even if he or she subsequently registers the conveyance" (Duenas, En Banc). PD 1529, Section 51: registration "shall be the operative act to convey or affect the land insofar as third persons are concerned."

Application. Between the deed and the registration the buyer's side learned, in order: that the owners' counsel had declared the agent's acts void (F21, through the lawyer who handled the papers); that a lawyer was needed to hold the ground (F22); that suits had to be filed and defended over the land (F23); that the agent was dead and the papers had to be moved by others, with penalties (F20, F25); that the Provincial Prosecutor would not call her the owner (F26); that the revocation was published in the local weekly (F27); that a court had issued warrants over the right to the land (F26); that a pleading served on her sister's own lawyer stated the revocation and attached the notice (F30); and that the SPA's own text, pulled from the Registry by her processor, confined the sale to a class she was not in (F31). 3.4 Revocation: the order of argument

Rule. Article 1921: "If the agency has been entrusted for the purpose of contracting with specified persons, its revocation shall not prejudice the latter if they were not given notice thereof." Article 1922: "If the agent had general powers, revocation of the agency does not prejudice third persons who acted in good faith and without knowledge of the revocation. Notice of the revocation in a newspaper of general circulation is a sufficient warning to third persons." The Court applied Article 1922 to an agent authorized to deal "not with a particular person, but with the public in general" (Central Surety & Insurance Co. v. Hodges, G.R. No. L-28633, 30 March 1971). PD 1529, Section 64, requires a revoking instrument to be registered, but the Court treats registration as a notice device, not a condition of validity (Republic v. Ciruelas, G.R. No. 239505, 17 February 2021).

Application. Lead with the class (3.1): if the buyer was never one of the "specified persons," the revocation is irrelevant to her, because the sale was never within the agency. Argue the 2020 publication (F27) only as a fact bearing on good faith before registration (3.3), never as an Article 1922 "sufficient warning," because Cesar's powers were special and class-limited, not general, and we cannot say both. Do not plead the non-annotation of the revocation as a defect in our own case; answer it with Section 52 and the 1996 annotation of the SPA itself (F4).

3.5 The tenant's possession was never possession as owner

Rule. Article 525: possession is "either in the concept of owner, or in that of the holder of the thing ... the ownership pertaining to another person." Article 540: "Only the possession acquired and enjoyed in the concept of owner can serve as a title for acquiring dominion." Article 1436: "A lessee ... is estopped from asserting title to the thing leased ... as against the lessor." Possession "with a juridical title, such as by a ... lessee ... cannot ripen into ownership" (Esguerra v. Manantan, G.R. No. 158328, 23 February 2007); "a person whose occupation of realty is by sheer tolerance of the owner is not a possessor in good faith" (National Housing Authority v. Manila Seedling Bank Foundation, G.R. No. 183543, 20 June 2016).

Application. "Occupancy since 1975" (Answer paragraph 44[a]) is tenancy by their own admission (F1, F2, F8). It adds nothing to the deed, it defeats the Article 448 "builder in good faith" claim (paragraph 49) for everything built while renting, and it is their own proof of actual knowledge of the owners. Use it for those three things. Do not use estoppel as a stand-alone bar to the deed; the buyer does not deny the owners' title, she claims to derive from it.

3.6 Void means imprescriptible, not ratifiable, and not cured by registration

Rule. Article 1409: void contracts "cannot be ratified." Article 1410: "The action or defense for the declaration of the inexistence of a contract does not prescribe." "A void contract produces no effect either against or in favor of anyone and cannot be ratified. Similarly, laches will not set in against a void transaction, as in this case, where the agent did not have a special power of attorney to dispose of the lots" (Mactan-Cebu International Airport Authority v. Unchuan, G.R. No. 182537, 1 June 2016). "One can sell only what he owns or is authorized to sell, and the buyer can acquire no more right than what the seller can transfer legally ... Registration does not vest title; it is merely the evidence of such title" (Heirs of Gonzales v. Spouses Basas, G.R. No. 206847, 15 June 2022).

Application. This answers the sixth defense (Article 1403[1], ratification), the seventh (estoppel and laches) and the premise of the eighth (the title). The estate receipts (F8) are payments to an agent whose authority was revoked; no owner received or saw a peso, which the plaintiff's representative will state under oath. The agent's own Deed of Confirmation (F19) is not the principals' ratification.

3.7 The consequences the court can order

Rule. Article 449: "He who builds, plants or sows in bad faith on the land of another, loses what is built, planted or sown without right to indemnity." Article 450: the owner may demand demolition at the builder's expense. Article 451: the owner "is entitled to damages from the builder." Article 549: "The possessor in bad faith shall reimburse the fruits received and those which the legitimate possessor could have received." Article 2232: exemplary damages where the defendant "acted in a wanton, fraudulent, reckless, oppressive, or malevolent manner." Article 2208, paragraphs 1, 2 and 5: attorney's fees where exemplary damages are awarded, where the defendant's act "compelled the plaintiff to litigate," or where the defendant "acted in gross and evident bad faith in refusing to satisfy the plaintiff's plainly valid, just and demandable claim." In Duenas the En Banc ordered that the bad-faith builder "forfeits what it has built ... without any right to be paid indemnity," awarded exemplary damages because the bad faith "resulted in the deprivation of petitioners' right to lawfully use and possess their property for a considerable number of years," and sustained attorney's fees because the owners "were compelled to litigate." In Heirs of Sarili v. Lagrosa, G.R. No. 193517, 15 January 2014, the Court remanded "for the proper application of Article 449 in relation to Articles 450, 451, 452 and the first paragraph of Article 546" to the house the bad-faith buyers had built.

Application. The improvements built after 29 September 2016 are forfeit. Fruits are owed at least from receipt of the demand (F34) and, on a finding of bad faith from the outset, from the deed. Exemplary damages rest on F11 to F19 and F28 to F33 taken together: a false receipt recital, a lost-title petition that hid the sale, a dead agent's name used for a 2020 survey certified as made with the owners' consent, a partition dated before its plan existed, and a sworn "my property" over land titled to others. Attorney's fees rest on the ignored demand (F34). Counsel to confirm the damages ceiling that applies in this court.

3.8 The replacement duplicate: evidence of bad faith, not a nullity claim here

Rule. PD 1529, Section 109, allows a new duplicate "after notice and due hearing." "A trial court does not acquire jurisdiction over a petition for the issuance of a new owner's duplicate certificate of title, if the original is in fact not lost" (Strait Times, Inc. v. Court of Appeals, G.R. No. 126673, 28 August 1998). But "the nullity of the reconstituted certificate does not by itself settle the issue of ownership" (same), and the Supreme Court cases reached that result through annulment petitions in the Court of Appeals, not collaterally in a first-level court.

Application. We cannot yet prove the duplicate was not lost. Use Special Proceeding No. 2156 for what Cesar said and hid (F13 to F17), not to void the order. The tenth defense (collateral attack) is thereby answered: we do not attack the order; we read it.

3.9 The families living on the lot: who the judgment reaches, and how possession is recovered

Rule. A judgment for possession binds persons who were not impleaded if each is "(a) a trespasser, squatter or agent of the defendant fraudulently occupying the property to frustrate the judgment; (b) a guest or occupant of the premises with the permission of the defendant; (c) a transferee pendente lite; (d) a sublessee; (e) a co-lessee or (f) a member of the family, relative or privy of the defendant" (Sunflower Neighborhood Association v. Court of Appeals, G.R. No. 136274, 3 September 2003, an ejectment case; counsel to confirm its application to the writ in this action). Republic Act No. 7279, Section 28, allows eviction and demolition "when there is a court order for eviction and demolition," and, where the occupants are underprivileged and homeless citizens, requires thirty days' notice, consultation, the presence of local officials, and relocation or financial assistance; Section 3(m) treats persons "who occupy lands without the express consent of the landowner" as professional squatters outside that protection, and Section 27 provides for their summary eviction.

PART 6. THE JUDGMENT WE ASK FOR

Written the way this court writes: the rule by section, "considering that," then an operative WHEREFORE with exact particulars. Counsel adapts the figures and the exhibit letters to the record as offered.

Before this court is a Complaint for Accion Reivindicatoria, Recovery of Possession, Declaration of Nullity of Deed of Absolute Sale, Cancellation of Transfer Certificate of Title No. 079-2021002126 and Declaration of Real Property ARP No. GR-2023-II-07-001-00256, Accounting, and Damages.

Article 1874 of the Civil Code provides that when a sale of a piece of land is through an agent, the authority of the latter shall be in writing, otherwise the sale shall be void. Article 1898 provides that a contract made by an agent exceeding the scope of his authority, and not ratified by the principal, is void if the other party is aware of the limits of the powers granted.

Considering that the Special Power of Attorney dated March 16, 1992 (Exhibit "E"), annotated on Transfer Certificate of Title No. T-52540 on June 21, 1996 (Exhibit "B"), authorized Cesar M. de la Fuente to sell only to third persons holding contracts to sell executed by the previous administrator or who had paid him down payments or partial payments, and only after prior consultation with the registered owners; considering that defendant Gloria H. Balane, by her own Answer, occupied the property as a lessee of the registered owners and produced no contract to sell and no payment to the previous administrator; considering that no consultation with the registered owners was made; and considering that under Section 52 of Presidential Decree No. 1529 the annotation of the said Special Power of Attorney was constructive notice of its limits to all persons from June 21, 1996, the Deed of Absolute Sale dated September 29, 2016 (Exhibit "H") was executed beyond the scope of the written authority and is void.

Considering further that a buyer of registered land must remain a purchaser in good faith until the registration of the conveyance; that the conveyance was registered only on November 23, 2021; and that before that date the defendants, through their attorney-in-fact and counsel, were made aware of the revocation of the agent's authority and of the registered owners' claim, the defendants were not purchasers in good faith.

1. Declaring the Deed of Absolute Sale dated September 29, 2016, executed by Cesar M. de la Fuente in favor of Gloria H. Balane, Doc. No. 304, Page No. 61, Book No. CXVI, series of 2016 of Notary Public Donald E. Asis, NULL AND VOID;
