1.3 What is common to all three

  • Language. The affidavits are in Filipino, which the Rule allows (Section 3: a judicial affidavit is prepared in the language known to the witness, with a translation only if that language is neither English nor Filipino). No translation is required. If the court wants an English copy for the record, counsel can supply one later; it is not a reason to delay.
  • The purposes paragraph. Each affidavit's "Purposes" is generic ("to prove the material allegations"). On direct, state the one purpose that matters for that witness in a sentence (Part 2 to Part 4 give it). Under the Rules, the purpose for which a witness's testimony is offered is stated when she is called. [COUNSEL]
  • Personal knowledge only. Each neighbour says what she saw, heard said to her, or did, with a year. Nothing about what the defendants knew or intended, nothing learned from Jonathan, nothing about the owners' dealings with Cesar.
  • No adjectives. No "land grabber," "fake," "illegal," "influential." The defendants are described by what they did and when.
  • Nothing outside the record through a neighbour. The exhibits were pre-marked at pre-trial. A paper not marked is not offered through a neighbour on direct. If cross opens a door, counsel may use re-direct with the court's leave; the paper is in counsel's folder, not the witness's hands.
