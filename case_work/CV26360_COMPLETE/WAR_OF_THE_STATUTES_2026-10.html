WAR OF THE STATUTES

Zschoche v. Balane, Civil Case No. 26-360, MTC Mercedes — the provisions each side will put in front of Judge Cu, and which words she will key in on

Prepared for counsel · 13 October 2026 · internal, not a pleading. Every provision below was read in the official text this week (lawphil.net copies of RA 386, PD 1529, RA 11576, RA 7279 and the Rules). Quoted words are exact. Where the text is paraphrased it says so.

Why this document exists. In seven signed writings this judge has cited no case. She cites the statute or rule by section and date, writes "Considering that," and disposes. The case will therefore be decided on which section's words fit the exhibit in front of her. This is the map of those collisions: for each battleground, the defendants' provision, ours, the exact words she will read, the exhibit that settles the fit, and the expected result on her method.

1. Jurisdiction and the "collateral attack" gate

Text Who cites it
BP 129 §33(3), as amended by RA 11576 (30 Jul 2021) First-level courts have "exclusive original jurisdiction in all civil actions which involve title to, or possession of, real property or any interest therein where the assessed value of the property or any interest therein does not exceed Four hundred thousand pesos (P400,000.00) exclusive on [sic] interest, damages of whatever kind, attorney's fees, litigation expenses and costs." Both; she will check it first
PD 1529 §48 "A certificate of title shall not be subject to collateral attack. It cannot be altered, modified, or canceled except in a direct proceeding in accordance with law." Defense (10th defense, ¶46)

What she keys in on. The words "direct proceeding." The Complaint's caption, which she herself recited in her 6 February 2026 order, prays for "Cancellation of Transfer Certificate of Title No. 079-2021002126." That is a direct proceeding against that certificate. §48 bars only attacks made in passing in some other case. The defense's §48 point has a real target only if we attack Special Proceeding No. 2156 (the replacement duplicate) or TCT 079-2021002127 (the owners' own title): we attack neither. The partition's nullity is pleaded as the ground for the cancellation prayed, not as a side-swipe at another title.

Our reply in one sentence. "This is the direct proceeding §48 requires; the only certificate attacked is the one named in the caption."

Expected result. Jurisdiction found: ARP No. GR-2023-II-07-001-00256 (Exh. D) states the current assessed value as ₱257,670.00 (market value ₱4,294,420 at a 6% assessment level, tax effectivity 2024; the "Previous Assessed Value" line shows ₱200,750), under the ₱400,000 threshold. §48 not engaged. On the plaintiff suing alone: Art. 487 ("Any one of the co-owners may bring an action in ejectment") "covers all kinds of action for the recovery of possession," including accion reivindicatoria, and "a co-owner may bring such an action, without the necessity of joining all the other co-owners as co-plaintiffs" (Catedrilla v. Lauron, G.R. No. 179011, 15 Apr 2013, quoting Tolentino), so Geraldine and Marcia are not necessary parties and relief may run in favor of all three.

2. The authority: what the agent could do, in writing

Text Who cites it
Art. 1874 "When a sale of a piece of land or any interest therein is through an agent, the authority of the latter shall be in writing; otherwise, the sale shall be void." Both. Defense: the SPA is the writing. Ours: the writing excludes her.
Art. 1878 ¶5 A special power of attorney is necessary "To enter into any contract by which the ownership of an immovable is transmitted or acquired either gratuitously or for a valuable consideration." Both
Art. 1877 "An agency couched in general terms comprises only acts of administration, even if the principal should state that he withholds no power or that the agent may execute such acts as he may consider appropriate, or even though the agency should authorize a general and unlimited management." Ours (against ¶8 of the SPA and against the 1991 and 2012 instruments)

What she keys in on. Art. 1874 says the authority must be in writing; she will read the writing. Paragraph 7 of Exh. E (Annex B, p. 3) is the writing: "in favor of third persons in possession of CONTRACTS TO SELL executed by the previous administrator, MR. BEN LLAMANZARES and/or to those who have paid their downpayments or have made partial payments therefor to the latter … after prior consultation with us." Two things in the writing limit the power: the class and the consultation. Nothing in the writing mentions partition or subdivision; paragraph 8's "any and every act … requisite, necessary or proper" is the general language Art. 1877 confines to administration.

The defense's reading. "Read as a whole, it authorizes negotiation, terms, documents and proceeds; that is a power to sell" (Answer ¶39). True, for the class.

Our reading. Concede the power to complete a sale; hold the class and the condition. The strict-construction rule (Bautista-Spille, G.R. 214057) is the only case worth one sentence here, and only if she wants one.

Expected result. She finds the authority limited to the class, after consultation, because the words are on the page.

3. Was the sale within the authority? Art. 1898 against Arts. 1900 and 1902

Text Who cites it
Art. 1898 "If the agent contracts in the name of the principal, exceeding the scope of his authority, and the principal does not ratify the contract, it shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal." Ours
Art. 1900 "So far as third persons are concerned, an act is deemed to have been performed within the scope of the agent's authority, if such act is within the terms of the power of attorney as written, even if the agent has in fact exceeded the limits of his authority according to an understanding between the principal and the agent." (second clause paraphrased from the text) Defense (apparent authority, ¶45)
Art. 1902 "A third person with whom the agent wishes to contract on behalf of the principal may require the presentation of the power of attorney, or the instructions as regards the agency. Private or secret orders and instructions of the principal do not prejudice third persons who have relied upon the power of attorney or instructions shown them." (first sentence paraphrased; second quoted) Defense
Art. 1911 "Even when the agent has exceeded his authority, the principal is solidarily liable with the agent if the former allowed the latter to act as though he had full powers." Defense (the owners "allowed Cesar to continue wielding" authority, ¶45)

What she keys in on. Art. 1900 protects acts "within the terms of the power of attorney as written." Art. 1902 protects reliance against "private or secret orders." Art. 1898 voids the contract where the buyer "is aware of the limits of the powers granted." The class and the consultation are not private orders; they are the written terms, and they were annotated on the title from 21 June 1996 (Exh. B, p. 3), which under PD 1529 §52 is "constructive notice to all persons." So Arts. 1900 and 1902 cut for us, and Art. 1898's condition ("aware of the limits") is met by the registry itself.

Art. 1911's trap. It makes the principal liable where the principal "allowed" the agent to act as though with full powers. The defense will say the owners let Cesar sell for years (the 1999 Hansol and 2005 Era sales). Answer: those sales were before the 2005 revocation and, for Hansol, under the 1991 SPA; "allowed" requires the owners' conduct, and from 15 August 2005 their conduct was a written revocation (Exh. F) and, in 2020, a published one (Exh. G). Art. 1911 also creates liability, not a valid transfer of land; it does not override Art. 1874's "shall be void."

Expected result. Sale outside the written terms; buyer charged with the limits; void under Arts. 1874 and 1898.

4. Void or merely unenforceable? Art. 1898 (with Art. 1874) against Art. 1403(1)

Caution (18 Oct 2026, second-chair review). Art. 1874 voids a sale made through an agent whose authority is not in writing. Here the authority is in writing (Exh. E), and the sale is outside its terms. The operative provision is therefore Art. 1898: a contract the agent makes "exceeding the scope of his authority" which the principal does not ratify "shall be void if the party with whom the agent contracted is aware of the limits of the powers granted by the principal." Awareness is supplied by the 1996 annotation (PD 1529 §52) and by the buyer's own pull of the full instrument in August 2021 (Def. Exh. 7). Art. 1874 is cited for the rule that the authority must be in writing, and for the reasoning in Cosmic Lumber and Unchuan that a sale outside the written power is a sale without written authority for that sale. Lead with Art. 1898.

Text Who cites it
Art. 1403(1) Unenforceable, unless ratified: "Those entered into in the name of another person by one who has been given no authority or legal representation, or who has acted beyond his powers." Defense (6th defense, ¶42: "not necessarily void ab initio … Ratification may be express or implied")
Art. 1910 "The principal must comply with all the obligations which the agent may have contracted within the scope of his authority. As for any obligation wherein the agent has exceeded his power, the principal is not bound except when he ratifies it expressly or tacitly." (second sentence paraphrased) Defense (tacit ratification by accepting payments)
Art. 1874 "… otherwise, the sale shall be void." Ours
Art. 1409 "The following contracts are inexistent and void from the beginning: … (7) Those expressly prohibited or declared void by law. These contracts cannot be ratified." Ours
Art. 1410 "The action or defense for the declaration of the inexistence of a contract does not prescribe." Ours

What she keys in on. The word "void" in Art. 1874 and the words "declared void by law … cannot be ratified" in Art. 1409(7). Art. 1403(1) is the general rule for unauthorized contracts; Art. 1874 is the special rule for land sold through an agent, and it uses "void," not "unenforceable." The Supreme Court says the same (Cosmic Lumber: "ipso jure … void"; Unchuan: "cannot be ratified … laches will not set in"), but she will reach it from the two articles alone.

The ratification facts. Payments were made to a revoked agent and his son (Exh. 5; Annex Z) and never reached any owner; the Deed of Confirmation (Annex G, p. 1) is the agent confirming himself. There is no act of the principals to ratify with, and Art. 1409 says a void sale cannot be ratified anyway.

Expected result. Void; imprescriptible; the laches and estoppel defenses (¶43) fall with it.

5. Revocation and notice: Arts. 1921 and 1922 against PD 1529 §64

Text Who cites it
Art. 1921 "If the agency has been entrusted for the purpose of contracting with specified persons, its revocation shall not prejudice the latter if they were not given notice thereof." Both. Defense: she is a "specified person" (Llamanzares-era buyer) never notified. Ours: she was never one.
Art. 1922 "If the agent had general powers, revocation of the agency does not prejudice third persons who acted in good faith and without knowledge of the revocation. Notice of the revocation in a newspaper of general circulation is a sufficient warning to third persons." Defense: publication only from 2020 forward (¶41). Ours: do not argue the publication as "sufficient warning"; Cesar's powers were special, not general.
PD 1529 §64 "Any person may, by power of attorney, convey or otherwise deal with registered land and the same shall be registered with the Register of Deeds … Any instrument revoking such power of attorney shall be registered in like manner." Defense (5th defense): the revocation was never registered.
PD 1529 §52 "Every conveyance, mortgage, lease, lien, attachment, order, judgment, instrument or entry affecting registered land shall, if registered, filed or entered in the office of the Register of Deeds … be constructive notice to all persons from the time of such registering, filing or entering." Ours: the SPA with its limits was entered in 1996.

What she keys in on. Art. 1921's words "specified persons." The SPA names them: Llamanzares contract holders and payors to Llamanzares. If the buyer is not one, Art. 1921 gives her nothing and the revocation is beside the point because the sale was never inside the agency. §64's "shall be registered" is a direction to register, not a condition of validity (Republic v. Ciruelas, G.R. 239505: registration under §64 is not "a prerequisite to its validity"); and §64 only matters for a sale within the power. §52 answers §64: what was registered, in 1996, was the SPA itself, limits included.

Two cases to cite sparingly, because they cut both ways. Central Surety v. Hodges applied Art. 1922 against the principal where the agent dealt "with the public in general"; Ramos will say Cesar, who sold to many buyers, had general powers. The answer is the SPA's own words: a named class, after consultation. Republic v. Ciruelas holds that §64 registration is not a prerequisite to an SPA's validity; Ramos will turn that into "nor is the non-registration of the revocation fatal to our reliance." The answer is §52: what charged her was the registered instrument with its limits, whatever was registered later.

The sequence to keep. First: outside the class, so revocation irrelevant. Second, in the alternative: the 2020 publication is a fact bearing on good faith before registration (section 6), not an Art. 1922 effect. Never both ways.

Expected result. Art. 1921 not engaged; §64 not a defense to a sale outside the power.

6. Good faith, and until when: Arts. 526 to 528 against PD 1529 §§51 and 53

Text Who cites it
Art. 527 "Good faith is always presumed, and upon him who alleges bad faith on the part of a possessor rests the burden of proof." Defense
Art. 526 "He is deemed a possessor in good faith who is not aware that there exists in his title or mode of acquisition any flaw which invalidates it. He is deemed a possessor in bad faith who possesses in any case contrary to the foregoing." Ours
Art. 528 "Possession acquired in good faith does not lose this character except in the case and from the moment facts exist which show that the possessor is not unaware that he possesses the thing improperly or wrongfully." Ours
PD 1529 §51 "The act of registration shall be the operative act to convey or affect the land insofar as third persons are concerned." Ours
PD 1529 §53 "In all cases of registration procured by fraud, the owner may pursue all his legal and equitable remedies against the parties to such fraud without prejudice, however, to the rights of any innocent holder for value of a certificate of title." And: "any subsequent registration procured by the presentation of a forged duplicate certificate of title, or a forged deed or other instrument, shall be null and void." Both. Defense: "innocent holder for value." Ours: "innocent" is defined by Art. 526, and the Supreme Court puts the burden of that status on the one who claims it (Bucton, G.R. 188395; Duenas, G.R. 209463, En Banc).

What she keys in on. Three words: "aware" (Art. 526), "from the moment facts exist" (Art. 528), and "operative act" (§51). The presumption in Art. 527 is for possessors; the "innocent holder" in §53 is read through Art. 526: a buyer "not aware that there exists in his title or mode of acquisition any flaw which invalidates it." Her mode of acquisition was a purchase from an agent of three absent owners she had paid rent to; the flaw was on the title since 1996 and in her hands by August 2021 (Elevado's certified copy of the SPA, Annex B, p. 1). Under §51 the conveyance affected the land only on 23 November 2021; Duenas reads §51 to require good faith to last until then. Between the deed and that date the facts "showing that the possessor is not unaware" are: her own 28 May 2020 SPA to sue over the land (their Annex "1"); the pleading received by her side's lawyer on 9 July 2021 with the published revocation attached (Annex N); and her sworn "my property" of 15 September 2021 (Annex K, p. 9).

Her habit that helps. She does not characterize. Art. 528 does not ask her to; it asks whether "facts exist." We put the dated facts on the table and let the article do the work.

Expected result. Not an innocent holder for value; §53's protection not available; bad faith at the latest from the dated notices and, on the inquiry rule, from the deed.

Art. 1878 ¶¶12, 15 A special power is necessary "(12) To create or convey real rights over immovable property" and "(15) Any other act of strict dominion." Ours

8. The improvements: Arts. 448 and 546 against Arts. 449 to 452, 546 ¶1 and 1678

Text Who cites it
Art. 448 "The owner of the land on which anything has been built, sown or planted in good faith, shall have the right to appropriate as his own the works … after payment of the indemnity provided for in Articles 546 and 548, or to oblige the one who built … to pay the price of the land …" (after the quoted opening, paraphrased) Defense (12th defense, ¶49)
Art. 546 "Necessary expenses shall be refunded to every possessor; but only the possessor in good faith may retain the thing until he has been reimbursed therefor. Useful expenses shall be refunded only to the possessor in good faith with the same right of retention …" Both
Art. 548 "Expenses for pure luxury or mere pleasure shall not be refunded to the possessor in good faith …" Defense
Art. 449 "He who builds, plants or sows in bad faith on the land of another, loses what is built, planted or sown without right to indemnity." Ours
Art. 450 "The owner of the land on which anything has been built, planted or sown in bad faith may demand the demolition of the work … at the expense of the person who built, planted or sowed …" Ours
Art. 451 "In the cases of the two preceding articles, the landowner is entitled to damages from the builder, planter or sower." Ours
Art. 452 "The builder, planter or sower in bad faith is entitled to reimbursement for the necessary expenses of preservation of the land." Ours (it fixes the only thing a bad-faith builder gets)
Art. 1678 A lessee who makes "in good faith, useful improvements which are suitable to the use for which the lease is intended" is entitled, on termination, to have the lessor "pay the lessee one-half of the value of the improvements at that time"; if the lessor refuses, "the lessee may remove the improvements." (paraphrased with quoted phrases) Ours, for everything built while renting (Parilla v. Pilar, G.R. 167680: Art. 448 "does not apply where one's only interest is that of a lessee under a rental contract")

What she keys in on. The words "in good faith" in Arts. 448 and 546, which send her back to Art. 526 (aware of a flaw) and Art. 528 (from the moment facts exist), and the word "lessee," which is in the defendants' own Answer (¶¶40, 43: "renting"). Three periods, three texts: lessee (Art. 1678) until 29 September 2016; buyer not in good faith (Arts. 449 to 452) after it; possessor on notice (Art. 528, 549) after the demand of 22 August 2025.

Their cases, her method. Macasaet and Casibang applied Art. 448 where the owner invited or permitted the building. She will not go to the cases; she will ask whether the owners consented. The exhibits say the owners' administrator rented to them, and that in 2006 they themselves wrote that the lot was "owned by the Heirs of Worrick Keesey" (Exh. 5, p. 1).

Expected result. Forfeiture of post-deed improvements (Art. 449); necessary expenses only, on receipts, no retention (Arts. 452, 546 ¶1); Art. 1678 option reserved to the owners for anything proved to predate the deed.

9. Fruits and damages: Art. 544 against Arts. 549, 2208, 2229, 2232

Text Who cites it
Art. 544 "A possessor in good faith is entitled to the fruits received before the possession is legally interrupted." Defense (keeps the rentals)
Art. 549 "The possessor in bad faith shall reimburse the fruits received and those which the legitimate possessor could have received …" Ours
Art. 2232 "In contracts and quasi-contracts, the court may award exemplary damages if the defendant acted in a wanton, fraudulent, reckless, oppressive, or malevolent manner." Ours
Art. 2229 Exemplary damages "are imposed, by way of example or correction for the public good, in addition to the moral, temperate, liquidated or compensatory damages." Ours
Art. 2208 ¶¶1, 2, 5 Attorney's fees may be recovered "(1) When exemplary damages are awarded; (2) When the defendant's act or omission has compelled the plaintiff to litigate … (5) Where the defendant acted in gross and evident bad faith in refusing to satisfy the plaintiff's plainly valid, just and demandable claim." Ours

What she keys in on. Art. 544's "good faith" (again Art. 526) and "legally interrupted"; Art. 2232's "fraudulent." She will not write "fraudulent" on an impression. She will write it if five dated documents leave no other word: the deed's "in hand paid" of 29 Sep 2016 against receipts to Feb 2017; the petition of 20 Oct 2016 by a revoked agent calling himself "Administrator," listing a title cancelled in 1999 and hiding the sale; the partition dated 15 Mar 2017 under a plan surveyed 8 Sep 2020; the engineer's certificate of 8 Sep 2020 that the survey was made "with the full knowledge and consent of the owner(s)" when the only agent was dead; the sworn "my property" of 15 Sep 2021 over land titled to three others. The Answer's own ¶27 ("certain rooms on the property were occasionally rented out after 2016") supplies the fruits; Exh. J supplies the interruption date (22 Aug 2025).

The predicate she will check first. Art. 2234: "While the amount of the exemplary damages need not be proved, the plaintiff must show that he is entitled to moral, temperate or compensatory damages before the court may consider the question of whether or not exemplary damages should be awarded." So the judgment must first award compensatory damages (the fruits, or reasonable compensation for use and occupation from 22 Aug 2025) or temperate damages ("when the court finds that some pecuniary loss has been suffered but its amount can not, from the nature of the case, be proved with certainty," Art. 2224; "reasonable under the circumstances," Art. 2225), and only then exemplary damages. A prayer that asks for exemplary damages with no compensatory or temperate award beside it fails on the text.

Expected result. Accounting of fruits from the demand (and, on a bad-faith-from-the-deed finding, from the deed), with a temperate or compensatory award as the Art. 2234 predicate; exemplary damages and fees if the five documents are in and tied to Art. 2232's words; the Duenas figures (₱200,000 / ₱100,000) as the template.
