If it was never resolved, I would like it resolved on paper. Under the Constitution a lower court must resolve a matter within three months of submission. Under Rule 35 as amended in 2020, even where the court does not render judgment on the whole case, it may ascertain "what material facts exist without substantial controversy," and "[t]he facts so ascertained shall be deemed established, and the trial shall be conducted on the controverted facts accordingly."

Why this matters now. The facts that decide this case are on documents the defendants themselves attached or do not dispute: the words of paragraph 7 of the 1992 power; its annotation on the title in 1996; the revocation of 2005; the deed dated 29 September 2016 reciting the price as paid, against their own receipts running to February 2017; the partition dated 2017 citing a survey made in 2020; registration in November 2021. An order that lists those facts as established, before the defendants present a single witness, fixes the trial on our ground. It also puts the court's reading of paragraph 7 on the record early. (The amended rule adds that the court's action on the motion itself is not reviewable by appeal or certiorari; the facts deemed established would stand in the judgment.)

What the draft asks for. First, a ruling on the pending motion. Second, in the alternative, that the court ascertain under Rule 35, Section 4 the facts without substantial controversy, listed in Annex "A", as established, and confining the trial to the three questions that are genuinely contested: whether Mrs. Balane paid Llamanzares before 1992, notice and good faith up to registration, and the improvements and damages.
