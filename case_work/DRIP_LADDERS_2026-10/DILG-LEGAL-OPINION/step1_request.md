Dear Sir or Madam,

Magandang araw po! I am a private citizen. I write to avail of the Department's service for the Provision of Legal Opinion on local governance administration and related concerns, which the Department's published service standards state is available to local officials, functionaries and citizens, and at no cost.

I have requested services that are listed in a local government's Citizen's Charter, and I would like to understand the law that governs how such requests are to be handled.

May I set out plainly what I am and am not asking. The three questions below are questions of law only, and they are of general application. I submit no question of fact. I submit no question that is pending before any court or other body. I do not ask the Department to pass upon the conduct of any official, to decide any controversy, or to apply its opinion to any particular local government unit. I name none. I ask only what the law requires.

**Question 1.** May a local government office impose, as a condition for acting upon a request for a service that is listed in its Citizen's Charter, a requirement that is not listed in that Citizen's Charter?

**Question 2.** Where a service listed in a Citizen's Charter carries a fee, does the assessment of that fee form part of the action that must be taken within the prescribed processing time? Or may an office defer assessing the fee indefinitely, while stating that the request is still being verified?

**Question 3.** Is the duty of a Secretary to the Sanggunian under Section 469(c)(5) of Republic Act No. 7160, to furnish, upon request of any interested party, certified copies of records of public character in his custody upon payment of the fees prescribed by ordinance, a ministerial duty? And does the duty to prescribe or to assess that fee arise upon the request being made?

Each question stands on its own. If the Department considers any one of them not to be a proper subject of an opinion, may I respectfully ask that the others still be answered.

This request is made without prejudice to any position I may take in any other matter.

So that I can follow the request properly, may I also ask for three small things:

1. The reference number assigned to this request;
2. The processing time that applies to it under the Department's Citizen's Charter; and
3. Whether the request will be resolved at the Regional Office or endorsed to the Central Office.

Maraming salamat po sa inyong tulong!

Respectfully yours,

## Why the letter looks like this

| Choice | Reason |
|---|---|
| **No LGU named** | Jonathan's instruction. It also removes the sub judice check, the referral-to-the-LGU route, and the delay of asking an LGU to comment. We get the clean legal standard and apply it ourselves where we control the application. |
| **No attachments** | Every document imports a fact, and a fact invites "we do not rule on questions of fact." A documentless letter is undeflectable on that ground. |

| **Without-prejudice line** | Nothing in the letter reads as conceding that the requirements questioned might be valid. |
| **No Sec. 31 pre-empt** | Raising a deflection nobody has made plants it. Held in reserve. |
| **Warm, short, plain** | A legal officer with a backlog answers the short clean query first. Three questions, no annexes, no history. |
