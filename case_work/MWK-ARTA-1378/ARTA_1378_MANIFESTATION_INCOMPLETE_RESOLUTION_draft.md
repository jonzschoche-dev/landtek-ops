# MANIFESTATION

COMPLAINANT, self-represented, respectfully submits this Manifestation to the Honorable Authority, and states:

**1.** Complainant received the Resolution dated **09 September 2026** by electronic mail on **15 September 2026**. He does not seek the reversal of any matter the Resolution passed upon, and this Manifestation is therefore **not a motion for reconsideration** within Rule IV, Section 8 of the 2023 ARTA Revised Rules of Procedure.

**2.** On **25 August 2026**, in CTN SL-2026-0209-1321, this Honorable Authority held, on this same Complainant's request to the Municipal Assessor of Mercedes "for certified true copies of various real property assessment records," that *"the **government service** sought by Zschoche may reasonably fall within the service denominated as 'Research/Verify History of Records of Tax Declaration' under the Citizen's Charter of the Municipality of Mercedes, Camarines Norte."* The Authority then applied the elements of Section 21(e) — the first of which is that *"the applicant transacted a government service"* — and measured the office's performance against the Charter's *"two (2) to three (3)-day processing period."*

**3.** The Resolution in the instant case, issued fifteen days later, concludes that a materially similar transaction with another office of the **same Municipality** — a request for the issuance of a certification from, and copies of, records in that office's custody — does not constitute a "government service" within the contemplation of R.A. No. 11032 at all.

**4.** The Resolution takes as material that the Municipal Engineering Office's own section of the Citizen's Charter does not list the transaction. Respectfully, the Charter is one instrument of one Municipality, published office by office. In the **Municipal Assessor's Office** section of the Charter of the same Municipality — the pages this Honorable Authority itself annexed in CTN SL-2026-0209-1321 (Annex "4" hereof) — the Municipality expressly lists and regulates *"Issuance of certified true/machine copy of assessment of real property (tax declaration)"* and *"Issuance of Certification of **No Improvement, No Property Holdings and Aggregate Landholdings**"* — the latter being, in substance, a certification that the office's records show no entry for a given property. It is classified there as a **simple**, government-to-citizen transaction, available to a declared owner or an authorized representative, with a published checklist, fee and processing period. Complainant notes for completeness that the Assessor pages annexed by the Authority in CTN SL-2026-0209-1321 are paginated 166 to 177, while the Municipal Engineering Office pages annexed to the instant Resolution are paginated 242 to 250; Complainant will furnish a certified copy of the current edition of the Charter upon its release by the Municipality.

**5.** Complainant's item A.1 of 24 January 2026 asked the Municipal Engineer, as Building Official, for the same species of act applied to the register he keeps: *"a certification stating that NO building permit exists for the subject structure."*

**6.** The Resolution leaves the captioned charge under **Section 21(b)** unaddressed. Its ISSUE is framed on Section 21(e) alone (p. 3), its FINDINGS discuss Section 21(e) alone (pp. 3–7), and Recommendation No. 1 cites Section 21(e) alone (p. 9), yet the caption and the concluding paragraph both carry Section 21(b) (pp. 1, 8). Rule IV, Section 7 of the 2023 ARTA Revised Rules requires that a Resolution contain, among others, the *"issues to be resolved, findings and recommendations."*

**7.** Unlike Section 21(e), the text of Section 21(b) contains no threshold dependency on the definition of a "government service." Section 21(e) penalizes the *"[f]ailure to render **government services** within the prescribed processing time …"* Section 21(b) penalizes the *"[i]mposition of additional requirements other than those listed in the Citizen's Charter."* Section 9(a)(2) confirms the point from the other direction: what may be required of a requesting party is *"limited to those enumerated in the Citizen's Charter."*

**8.** By demanding, on **05 February 2026**, a *"duly issued, authenticated or apostilled Special Power of Attorney"* as an absolute condition for the release of the documents, Respondent asserted administrative authority over the transaction. Respondent cannot impose an unlisted requirement to block a transaction while treating that same transaction as outside the Act. The Authority has, moreover, held that Complainant's standing *"proceeds from his own participation in the transaction"* and that an SPA *"is not indispensable"* (Resolution, p. 4), and has expressly declined to find *"that the SPA requirement was legally justified"* (Resolution, p. 7).

**9.** As reflected in the Authority's disposition in **CTN SL-2026-0209-1319**, where a public workflow is absent from an office's Citizen's Charter the Authority's practice is to endorse the matter to its Operations Group for the reengineering of that Charter, so that the service is captured — not to insulate the office. Any other approach would create the incentive for offices to omit their most contested record categories from their Charters in order to place them beyond review.
