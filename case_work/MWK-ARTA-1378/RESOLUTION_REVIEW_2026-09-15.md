## Bottom line

**On the law, the Resolution is substantially correct. On the record, it is sloppy. None of the errors would likely change the outcome.**
The deciding point is that a request for copies of existing records is not a "government service" under §4(f). That reading is
defensible on the statute's words, consistent with ARTA's rulings in 0690/0792, 1319 and 1321, and consistent with the Litigation
Division's 21 May 2026 clarification. Our best counter-argument is narrow: the Certificate of No Record item. It is not enough to carry
a merits appeal. The Resolution also gave us three things worth keeping: the SPA objection was rejected, ARTA expressly left open
whether the refusal was lawful, and the threat letter was referred to CSC Region V.

and proof-of-ownership papers others had submitted [doc 565]. That is records retrieval, not an application the office decides.
ARTA's "historical and voluminous" description fits those items. Only item A.1 looks like a chartered-type output, and even that
certificate is not in the MEO Charter.

## 3. Cracks on our side (learn from these; don't repeat them in CSC or OP)

1. **"No response / deemed denial" contradicts our own Annex C.** The SCA (¶¶5, 8, 10) pleads that the 15-working-day period lapsed
   "without a valid disposition". But Balane replied in writing on 5 Feb, 8 working days after receipt, stating his ground (no SPA).
   Under RA 6713 §5(a) that is a timely reply. The real grievance is the *content* of the reply (the condition and the threat), not silence.
2. **EO 2 s. 2016 is the wrong hook against an LGU.** EO 2 covers the Executive Branch; LGUs are only "encouraged" to follow it
   [VERIFY §2 official text; EO 2 is not in the law library]. No Mercedes FOI ordinance is in the corpus. The "15 working days under
   EO 2" and "§12 deemed denial" framing is therefore weak. The binding 15-working-day rule for LGU personnel is **RA 6713 §5(a)**.
3. **The Supplemental overstated the Charter.** ¶7 said each requested item was "expressly listed as a frontline service" in the MEO
   Charter. Annex G shows otherwise (§1 above). ARTA checked.
4. **Breadth.** The "entire history" requests (B, C) invited the "voluminous archival records" characterization.
5. **The threat was pleaded as RA 3019 / Grave Misconduct.** ARTA can't reach that, which is why it referred it. That referral was the right outcome.
