**I. THE AUTHORITY NO LONGER RESTS "GOVERNMENT SERVICE" ON CITIZENSHIP (Petition, Issue 4).**

The Resolution of 07 April 2026 disposed of Section 21(e) on a single ground: that Petitioner's request "does not fall under the definition of a government service considering that neither he nor Mrs. Patricia have the right to information both being American citizens" (Petition, Attachment on file; ARTA Resolution, p. 6).

The Resolution of 09 September 2026 decides the same Section 21(e) threshold, against the same respondent, **without any reference to citizenship**. It frames the question solely by the character of the request under Section 4(f). It recognizes that records "maintained by government offices, subject to limitations provided by law, may constitute public records and may be subject to disclosure in furtherance of the constitutional right of the people to information on matters of public concern" (Annex "P-1", p. 7). Read with the Authority's Response of 21 May 2026 in CTN SL-2026-0128-1210 (Annex "N-7" of the Second Manifestation, already on file), which placed the earlier citizenship discussion in the context of "the nature of the requests as framed," the ground on which the 07 April 2026 Resolution rests is **not one the Authority itself now applies**. Petitioner respectfully submits that the 07 April 2026 Resolution cannot stand on a rationale the issuing Authority has since set aside in the same controversy.

**II. THE AUTHORITY HAS DECLINED TO ENDORSE THE SPA CONDITION IT EARLIER CALLED "REASONABLE" (Petition, Issues 1 and 2).**

The 07 April 2026 Resolution excused the respondent's refusal to receive Petitioner's October 2025 request because "[i]t was reasonable for Engr. Balane to ask for proof of Zschoche's authority to transact on Mrs. Patricia's behalf" (ARTA Resolution of 07 April 2026, p. 4).

On the respondent's renewed demand for "a duly issued, authenticated or apostilled Special Power of Attorney," the 09 September 2026 Resolution holds two things:

- **(a)** Petitioner's standing "proceeds from his own participation in the transaction and the alleged acts committed against him, and not merely from his purported representation of the heirs," so that an SPA "is not indispensable" (Annex "P-1", p. 4).
- **(b)** Its closure "should not … be construed as a determination by ARTA that Respondent's refusal or failure to release the requested documents was proper, or that the SPA requirement was legally justified" (Annex "P-1", p. 7).

The Authority thus now expressly withholds the very validation of the SPA condition on which the 07 April 2026 findings under Sections 21(a) and 21(b) depend. The legality of that condition, imposed with no basis in any Citizen's Charter, statute or published rule, is the question the Petition asks this Office to decide.

**III. THE CERTIFICATION QUESTION RECURS, AND THE AUTHORITY'S STATED PREMISE IS CONTRADICTED BY THE RECORD (Petition, §V.A).**

The Petition submits that a request for building-permit verification and a **certification of no record** is a transaction within Section 4(f). The 09 September 2026 Resolution reaches the opposite result on 1378. But it does so on the stated premise that Petitioner "was not applying for the issuance of a building permit, occupancy permit, clearance, **certification**, or any other permit or authorization ordinarily processed by the Municipal Engineering Office" (Annex "P-1", pp. 6–7). The request itself, attached to that very Resolution, applied in terms for a certification (item A.1).

The same Resolution acknowledges that the office "is mandated to issue permits, clearances, and certifications as part of its standard functions." It also acknowledges that the omission of a service from the Citizen's Charter "does not, by itself, remove it from the ambit of a 'government service'" (Annex "P-1", p. 6). R.A. No. 11032 itself treats certification as an output of processing: "processing time" runs "to the issuance of certification or such similar documents" (Sec. 4(j)), and the law groups "license, clearance, permit, certification or authorization" together (Secs. 9(b)(1), 9(e), 10). The recurrence of this question across two dockets against the same office, now resolved on a premise the record contradicts, underscores the need for this Office's ruling on it.

For completeness: the respondent's letter of 21 October 2025 stated only that "there is no building permit issued recently pertaining to that particular area." It suggested that permits by "the previous building official" be checked elsewhere. It is not a certification of no record. As shown in the Petition (Attachments 5-A, 5-B), Petitioner did not receive it when sent and obtained it later. Petitioner's 24 January 2026 request followed it up precisely because it certified nothing.
