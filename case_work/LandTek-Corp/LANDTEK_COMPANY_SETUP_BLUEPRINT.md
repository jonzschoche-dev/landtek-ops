## 0. The one factor that drives everything: Jonathan is a U.S. citizen
Philippine foreign-ownership rules (Foreign Investments Act + Foreign Investment Negative List + Anti-Dummy
Law) govern how much of a PH operating company a foreigner may own and at what capital. This decides the
entire structure. Three paths:

| Path | Ownership | Capital reality `[confirm w/ counsel]` | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Foreign-owned opco (OPC/corp, Jonathan ≥40%)** | Jonathan | Domestic-market enterprise >40% foreign generally needs **US$200k** paid-in (or **US$100k** if advanced tech / employs ≥15 Filipinos) | clean control, but a real capital hurdle |
| **B. 60/40 domestic corp** | Filipino ≥60% (a *genuine* stakeholder — e.g. Kristyle) + Jonathan ≤40% | no foreign min-capital (~₱5k) | cheap, but needs a real Filipino equity partner; **Anti-Dummy risk if nominee** |
| **C. ★ IP offshore + lean Filipino opco (RECOMMENDED)** | **Jonathan owns the IP personally/offshore; PH opco is Filipino-compliant and *licenses* the platform** | opco stays cheap; Jonathan's value = the **license royalty**, not PH equity | matches your stated intent exactly: "run the tech, own all the IP" |

**Recommendation: Path C.** You keep the crown jewel (the platform/IP) outside the PH opco and outside the
estate fights; the operating company is a locally-compliant services entity that pays you to use the platform.
You capture value through **ownership of the IP + a license royalty**, not through PH equity you'd have to
over-capitalize. Confirm with counsel; B is the fallback if you'd rather hold opco equity directly.

## 0.5 Operator constraints (2026-09-16) + the hard limits
**Constraint:** neither Patricia nor Jonathan will own the opco or appear on its corporate paper; the estate
management agreement will be **non-public**.
**How it's satisfied (clean):** the opco is owned/officered by **genuine third-party Filipino principals**;
Jonathan is the **IP owner + licensor only** (a vendor to the opco, not an owner/officer); Patricia is a
**private client**; the management agreement is a confidential, unfiled contract.
**Two hard limits — do not cross:**
1. **Anti-Dummy.** The third-party owners must **genuinely own, control, and profit** from the opco. Jonathan
   may be the licensor/tech provider (open, legitimate) but **not the hidden beneficial owner**. Nominees
   fronting for his control = violation → voids the structure. Name the *real* principals.
2. **Real party in interest can't hide in litigation.** Demands may issue under LandTek and the agreement may
   stay private, **but any recovery/ejectment SUIT must name Patricia (co-owner) as plaintiff** (public court
   record); one cannot litigate anonymously. And "non-public" ≠ never-shown: LandTek's authority must be
   **producible** to a court or occupant who challenges its standing.

## 2. Entity type for the PH opco
- **OPC (One Person Corporation, RA 11232)** — single owner, limited liability. Clean **if** owner is
  foreign-eligible + capitalized (Path A). If Path C with a Filipino owner, a Filipino can hold the OPC.
- **Regular corporation** — needed for the 60/40 split (Path B) or multiple stockholders.
- **Sole proprietorship** — avoid (no limited liability; foreigner restrictions).
Decision hinges on §0. **Do not finalize entity type without the foreign-ownership ruling.**
