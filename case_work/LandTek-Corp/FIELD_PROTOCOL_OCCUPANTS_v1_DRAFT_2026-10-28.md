## 1. Purpose and the one-line doctrine

Turn occupied land into named, classified, documented occupants, then into payers, buyers, cleared land or
lawsuits, while every step asserts the owner's rights and none concedes anything.

**Doctrine:** identify first, classify at the desk, assert in every act, enforce only through a court or the
Building Official. [R: S1, S2, S6, S11]

## 1A. Opening posture: velvet glove, fact-finding only (Jonathan, 28 Oct 2026) [R: Jonathan's directive]

Jonathan, 28 Oct 2026: the first engagement is a fact-finding mission. Any aggressive move becomes chismis
and the community and barangays will close up and block us. So until Jonathan opens Phase 3 in writing:

- **The only goals are to listen, learn and be known as respectful.** Who lives where, since when, how they
  came to be there, what paper they hold, who they say sold or gave it. Relationships first; data second.
- **No demand, no offer, no letter, no deadline, no mention of lawyers, cases, ejectment, demolition, rent or
  payment.** The rep carries no demand letters at all.
- **No pressure questions.** Ask only open, friendly questions. Never ask anyone to "prove" anything.
  Capacity to pay and building permits are OBSERVED or pulled from records later, never asked at the door.
- **No names of adversaries.** Do not say de la Fuente, Balane, Baliza, Salvador, or any case number. If the
  occupant raises them, listen and note; do not respond on the merits.
- **Soft escort.** A barangay health worker or kagawad the household knows, not a uniformed tanod or police.
  No counsel in the field.
- **Small footprint.** Two people at most at a door. No vehicles parked in a row, no clipboards waved, no
  measuring tapes or survey gear on this pass.
- **What velvet does NOT mean.** The rep still never says "you can stay", "it's fine", "we accept your
  paper" or anything that sounds like consent (hard rule 5). Being kind is not conceding. A quiet,
  respectful visit by the owners' representative is itself evidence the owners are present and paying
  attention (anti-tolerance, §12).
- **Desk-side fact-finding is quiet too.** Records pulls (assessor roll, RD, barangay lists, health-worker
  household lists offered by San Roque) go through ordinary requests. Requests already in flight (e.g. the
  OBO request of 24 Jan 2026, doc 772) continue as they are; no new pressure letters to the LGU from the
  field program without Jonathan's go.
- **Exit from velvet** is Jonathan's call only, per barangay, in writing, after the desk has a complete roll
  for that barangay. Phases 3 to 5 below are written now so they are ready, not so they start.

## 2. Hard rules (no exceptions in the field)

1. **No self-help.** No demolition, padlocking, removal of belongings, cutting of utilities, fencing-in or
   physical ouster by anyone acting for LandTek. Removal only by court order or a Building Official order,
   executed by the authorities. [R: S6 caveat 4; S11 Altarejos]
2. **No threats, no promises.** The rep never says a house will be demolished, a person arrested, or that
   anyone may stay, buy or rent. Threats can expose the rep and the client to criminal and civil liability
   (Revised Penal Code, grave threats / coercion, in corpus) [C]. Promises can be argued as consent or a lease.
   [P]
3. **No classification in the field.** The rep records what is shown and said. The desk decides Track A, B
   or C. [R: S2, S10]
4. **No deal in the field.** No money accepted, no paper signed by the rep, no "arrangement". Pay-to-stay and
   all instruments are issued only by the desk through counsel on Jonathan's go. [R: S4; P for the cash rule]
5. **Nothing that reads as tolerance or recognition.** No "you can stay for now", no receipt for rent, no
   acceptance of a tax-dec copy "as valid". If asked, the rep uses the reservation statement (§6.3). [R: S1]
6. **Title holders are out of scope here.** Anyone holding a TCT or a deed (Track A) is handled under the
   separate `FIELD_PROTOCOL_TITLE_HOLDERS_v1_DRAFT_2026-10-28.md` (Jonathan, 28 Oct 2026). Kristyle visits
   house to house and learns the situation of each occupant, but NOT title holders (Jonathan, 28 Oct 2026:
   "no title holders"). Known title holders are on the desk's skip list; if she meets one unexpectedly, see
   §6.6. Never offer anyone anything. [R: S1, S2, S3]
7. **Barangay is an ally, not a client.** Share records and the map; give no legal advice; don't direct their
   cases. [R: S9]
8. **Safety beats data.** Any sign of hostility, alcohol, weapons or a crowd forming: stop, thank, leave,
   report. No record is worth an incident. [P]
9. **Every outward act is Jonathan-gated and counsel-executed.** [R: all sources]

## 6. Phase 1: Identify (the canvass)

### 6.1 At each structure
1. Escort introduces the rep. Rep shows the authorization letter and states purpose (script §6.2).
2. Read the short privacy notice (§8). Ask, kindly, if it is all right to write a few things down. If they
   would rather not: thank them warmly, note only the GPS pin and "visited, preferred not to talk", move on.
   Never push. A door that stays friendly today opens next month.
3. Fill one form per structure / household (§7). One record per structure even if occupants are related.
   Let them talk; fill the form from the conversation rather than reading questions off it.
4. Photos: the structure from the path, only if the household is comfortable with it. A document is
   photographed only if the occupant offers to show it; never ask for papers. Never take or keep an
   original. No photos of people or children. [P]
5. GPS pin at the door. No GPS = flag the record "no GPS", never estimate. [R: S10]
6. Barangay escort signs the certifier line (away from the household, not in front of them).
7. Thank them, and leave on a good note. No discussion of rights, outcomes or money.

### 6.2 Door script (English master; Tagalog and Bikol versions need a native-speaker translation and
Jonathan's approval before use) [P]

> "Magandang umaga po. I'm [name]. I'm helping the family of the Keesey heirs, the owners of the land here,
> get to know the people living on it. This is [escort], whom you know from the barangay. We're just
> visiting and listening, and updating the family's records with the barangay's help. Would it be all right
> if I asked how long you've been here and noted a few things down?"

Good open questions (pick, don't interrogate): How long has your family been here? How did you come to live
here? Did someone sell or give you the place, or do you rent? Who else lives nearby whom we should visit?
Is there anything you'd like the family to know?

If asked "Will we be evicted?" / "Can we buy?" / "Can we stay?" / "What will the owners do?":
> "Honestly, I don't know yet. The family asked me only to visit and listen first, so they understand
> everyone's situation. I'll pass on anything you'd like them to know."

Never add a promise ("nothing will happen to you") or a threat. Never say "lawyer".

### 6.3 If the occupant asks the rep to agree with their claim [R: S1 principle; P for the wording]
Velvet version (Phase 1). Kind, but it concedes nothing:
> "I'm only writing down what you've told me so the family understands. I'm not able to say yes or no to
> anything; that's for the family."
The formal reservation-of-rights wording (S1) belongs in Phase 3 letters, not at the door.

### 6.4 Do-not-say list [P]
"You'll be demolished." "You have to leave." "You can stay." "It's fine for now." "Your title is fake."
"Pay me / pay us." "The owners won't do anything." "Nothing will happen to you." "Lawyer", "case",
"court", "ejectment", "squatter", "illegal". Any comment on de la Fuente, Balane, Baliza, Salvador or a case.

### 6.5 Chismis control [P]
- Assume every word at a door reaches the whole barangay by evening. Say only what the rep would be happy to
  hear repeated.
- Same message at every door. No household is told more, or anything different, than the next.
- Visit the barangay hall first, every canvass day, so officials hear it from us before they hear it on the
  street.
- If a rumour starts (e.g. "the owners are evicting everyone"), the rep does not argue at doors. She reports
  it to the desk the same day; Jonathan decides whether a calm word through the Punong Barangay is needed.
- Nothing posted online, no group chats with residents, no social-media photos of the area.

### 6.6 Title holders: not visited [R: Jonathan 28 Oct 2026 ("Kristyle will visit house to house and
learn the situation of each occupant" ... "no title holders"); P for the wording]
The desk gives Kristyle a skip list each canvass day: every known title holder from the Track A protocol
(the 22 in `transferees_safe`, incl. Delfin Gaulit, and anyone added since). She does not knock there.
If a household not on the list says "we have a title" or "we bought it":
- Stay friendly. Do not ask to see the paper, who sold it, or the price.
- Accept nothing (ratification caution, Track A protocol §1.2).
- Say: "Thank you, I'll let the family know. I won't take more of your time." and leave.
- Note only: GPS pin, "says titled / says bought", name if offered, mood. Mark the record Track A.
- The desk moves the household to the Track A protocol and the skip list. No second visit.

## 8. Privacy (RA 10173, Data Privacy Act, in corpus) [R: adopted by Jonathan 28 Oct 2026][C]

- Purpose stated at the door, in plain words: "so the owners' family knows who lives on the land and can
  keep its records up to date." The written notice (kept by the desk, available on request) states the full
  purpose, estate administration including protecting the owners' rights. Counsel to confirm the short
  spoken version is enough at the door [C]. It must stay truthful; no claim that this is a barangay or
  government census.
- Collect only the form fields. No IDs copied, no phone photos of people's faces, no minors' names.
- Storage: field app (`field_survey_records`, `field_survey_photos`) or paper kept by the rep and handed to
  the desk within 48 hours. No copies on personal chat apps. No posting anywhere.
- Access: desk, Jonathan, counsel for the matter. Not the barangay (they get the map, not our records) unless
  Jonathan approves a specific record.
- Retention: for the life of the matter, then review. [C]
- Counsel to confirm the lawful basis (legitimate interest / establishment of legal claims) and whether a
  written notice must be left at each household.

## 9. Phase 2: Desk (after each canvass day)

1. Ingest the forms / sync the app. Every record keeps rep, date, GPS, photos (provenance). [R: S10]
2. Match each pin to the sub-lot (point-in-polygon on `map_parcels`, read-only). [R: S10]
3. Classify A / B / C / H (§4). Record the reason in one line.
4. **UDHA screen** (RA 7279) per Track B household [R: S1, S6]:
   - Capacity-to-pay indicators from the form (employment, business on site, vehicle, concrete house,
     renting rooms out). "Professional squatter" = occupies without consent and has sufficient income for
     legitimate housing (RA 7279 as quoted in Altarejos, S11).
   - Likely underprivileged and homeless → Track H for counsel; any later eviction follows §11.3 in full.
5. Records pull per Track B structure [R: S6 §3]: OBO certificate of no building permit (request in flight
   since 24 Jan 2026, doc 772); barangay certification of no owner consent; construction-dating proof.
6. Output per barangay: occupant roll, Track counts, list of Track H questions for Jonathan.

Never claim "no building permit" for a structure until the OBO certificate for THAT structure is in hand.
Permits were issued on void titles in the titled subdivision (doc 959). [R: S6 §0.1]

## 10. Field safety [R: adopted by Jonathan 28 Oct 2026]

- Never alone: rep + barangay escort minimum; buddy for any service of a demand.
- Daylight only, weekdays, agreed hours. Check in with the desk (or Jonathan) at start, midday, end.
- Agree an exit point and a no-go list with the barangay before starting (households with known disputes,
  the Baliza-linked area, any CV26360 party).
- Do not enter a house. Stay at the door or the gate.
- Stop rules: shouting, crowd forming, dogs loosed, alcohol, any weapon, any filming of the rep with threats.
  Say "Thank you, we'll go now," leave, write an incident note the same day.
- Incident note: what, who, when, where, witnesses. Desk logs it. Jonathan decides any follow-up (barangay
  blotter, PNP).
- Do not engage defendants or witnesses in Civil Case 26-360 (Balane and the transferees): record "titled,
  in suit" and leave. Anything they say goes to Barandon, not into an argument.

## 11. Phase 3 to 5: Instrument, clock, enforcement (all on Jonathan's go, through counsel)

### 11.1 Instrument per track
- Track A: not here; see the Track A protocol.
- Track B: pay-to-stay demand (`DRAFT_pay_to_stay_demand.md`) or demand+reservation to vacate
  (`DRAFT_squatter_demand_and_ejectment.md` Part A, with the lease option struck per §4). [R: S4, S5]
  Rate: must be set from a comp or zonal value, not the 0.5% estimate. [R: S4 marks it EST]
- Track C: LGU track (inverse condemnation / back-rental). [R: S1]

### 11.2 Service of a demand [R: S7 method]
- Personal service on every adult present. Each prints name, signs and dates the receiving copy.
- Refusal: write "refused, served by leaving copy", date, time, witness name; leave the copy.
- No one present: serve on the person found on the premises, or post. Rule 70 §2 allows "serving written
  notice of such demand upon the person found on the premises if no person be found thereon". [R: Rule 70 §2]
- Post one copy on the main door or gate; photograph it in place with the date visible.
- Keep one signed or annotated copy per recipient. Desk scans same day.
- Barangay escort or notary present where possible.

### 11.3 The clock (the drip) [R: S5, Rule 70; THE DRIP doctrine]
- Period runs from proof of receipt: 15 days for land, 5 days for buildings (Rule 70 §2). The letters use 15.
- Payers: logged to the co-ownership ledger (`v_mwk_reimbursement`); no receipt is issued as "rent".
- Refusers / silence: counsel files unlawful detainer within 1 year of the LAST demand; else accion
  publiciana. [R: S5]
- Barangay conciliation: RA 7160 §408(f) exempts parties who "actually reside in barangays of different
  cities or municipalities". Patricia resides in the US; exemption [C]. If counsel says conciliation is
  required, file at the Lupon and track the Certification to File Action as its own clock. [R: S8]

### 11.4 Enforcement
- Only after judgment, by the sheriff under a court order, or by a Building Official order with its own
  due process. LandTek's field role is limited to documenting. [R: S6, S11]
- Where the households are underprivileged and homeless, RA 7279 §28 is mandatory (in corpus): 30 days'
  notice; consultation on resettlement; LGU officials present; everyone taking part identified; weekdays,
  office hours, good weather; no heavy equipment except for permanent concrete structures; uniformed PNP in
  the first line; adequate relocation (LGU and NHA within 45 days of notice of final judgment, else financial
  assistance of 60 days' minimum wage). [R: RA 7279 §28, chunks 4829-4830]

## 12. Evidence and anti-tolerance log [R: S6 caveat 2; P for the log format]

The occupants' best defense is that the owners knew and did nothing (Art. 453). Every visit is evidence
against it. The desk keeps one log per parcel:
date · visit type (canvass / follow-up / demand / certification request) · rep · witness · what was recorded ·
link to photos and signed copies. The log feeds the drip and any complaint's annexes.
