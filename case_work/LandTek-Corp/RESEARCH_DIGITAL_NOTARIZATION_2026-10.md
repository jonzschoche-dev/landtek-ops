## Bottom line

Electronic notarization became usable in the Philippines yesterday, **19 October 2026**. The governing rule is **A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC, Rules on Electronic Notarization**, promulgated 4 February 2025. Its repealing clause **expressly repeals A.M. No. 20-07-04-SC**, the 2020 interim rules on remote notarization of paper documents, so those rules are dead. Three platforms are reported as Supreme Court-accredited facilities: NotarizeIT (UNAWA), Twala (Ohelio) and QLegal (Quanby Solutions).

Under **Remote Electronic Notarization (REN)**, Jonathan can swear to a PDF affidavit or acknowledge a letter from home by live video before any commissioned Electronic Notary Public, anywhere in the Philippines. The platform checks his location by geolocation, and VPNs are banned. A jurat is expressly supported: he signs in full view of the notary, then takes an oath. The rules give an e-notarized act the same validity, force and effect as a paper notarization.

Patricia cannot use REN from her US home. The rule's only overseas route requires her to be inside a Philippine embassy, consulate or honorary consul's office, and the Supreme Court is still coordinating with DFA on that. For her, US notarization plus apostille, or consular notarization, remains the path.

**For R2:** keep the courteous letter unsworn. Pair it with a short e-notarized **Affidavit of [facts/service]** by Jonathan, as Attorney-in-Fact, that attaches the letter and its proof of receipt. Use REN through one accredited platform once the commissioned notary, fee and output have been confirmed on a single test document.

## What's verified

All items accessed 20 Oct 2026.

1. **The rule and its date.** A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC, *Rules on Electronic Notarization*, was approved and promulgated by the Court en banc on **4 February 2025**, with the *Guidelines on the Accreditation of Electronic Notarization Facility Providers* attached. The PDF is the official SC upload, "Date Uploaded: 03/09/2025".
   - Source: https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/24-10-14-sc-rules-on-electronic-notarization/
   - PDF: https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/03/A.M.-No.-24-10-14-SC-Feb.-4-2025-published-3.9.25.pdf

2. **Effectivity clause.** Rule XII §9: the rules take effect "within 15 days following its complete publication" in the Official Gazette or two newspapers. Same PDF, p. 27.
   - The calendar effectivity date is **not settled**; see "What's not verified".

3. **The 2020 interim rules are repealed.** Rule XII §6: "A.M. No. 20-07-04-SC or the 2020 Interim Rules on Remote Notarization of Paper Documents is hereby repealed." Same PDF, p. 27.

4. **Scope, exclusions and legal effect.**
   - Rule I §2: the rules cover electronic documents only. Paper documents and instruments with handwritten signatures stay under the 2004 Rules on Notarial Practice (A.M. No. 02-8-13-SC).
   - Rule I §5: an act done under the rules has "the same validity, force, and effect" as a 2004-Rules act.
   - Excluded: notarial wills, deposition taking, and paper documents with wet signatures.
   - Rule I §4(d): documents must be PDF or PDF/A, and scanned paper documents count as electronic documents.
   - Rule I §3: the 2004 Rules apply suppletorily.

5. **Two modes.**
   - IEN, in-person (Rule II): physical presence before the notary.
   - REN, remote (Rule III): virtual appearance by synchronous videoconference counts as personal appearance. Pre-recorded video is "strictly prohibited". The principal must stay in camera view throughout.

6. **REN location rule.** Rule III §2(c): except under the limited extraterritorial rule, the notary "shall ascertain that all parties are present in the Philippines" by the platform's geolocation function and by requiring each party to disclose their actual physical location.
   - Rule VII §2(i): the platform must detect and disable VPNs.
   - Rule XII §3(e): using a VPN to change one's location is a punishable act.

7. **Overseas principals.** Rule IV §5 allows an act for a principal abroad only if the principal is "within the premises" of a Philippine embassy, consular office or honorary consul's office. The principal must pan the video to show this, and a designated embassy officer must confirm presence.
   - Rule VI §9: the notary must be in the Philippines.
   - Same PDF, pp. 10 and 14.

8. **Jurat, oath and acknowledgment by electronic means.**
   - Jurat (Rule IV §3): the notary requires the principal to sign with an electronic signature through the platform "in full view" of the notary, and to "take an oath before the ENP".
   - Oath/affirmation (§2) and acknowledgment (§1) also cover pre-signed electronic documents. The principal confirms the signature is theirs and avows truthfulness "under penalty of law".
   - Same PDF, pp. 8–9.

9. **Notary's territory.** Rule VI §9: an Electronic Notary Public may act "for principals located anywhere in the Philippines". This is unlike the court-territorial limit on paper notaries. Same PDF, p. 14.

10. **Competent evidence of identity.** Rule I §4(b) requires at least one government photo-and-signature ID: passport, driver's license, PRC, NBI, postal, voter's, SSS, GSIS, PhilHealth, senior citizen, OWWA, ACR/ICR, TIN ID, UMID, and others. PhilSys ID or the IBP ID also qualify.
    - Rule VII §2(b)–(d): the platform must run e-KYC, multi-factor authentication and liveness/anti-spoofing checks.
    - Rule VII §2(u): the platform must block notaries with **expired** commissions and principals with **expired** IDs.
    - Same PDF, pp. 1–2 and 16–18.

11. **Who can be an Electronic Notary Public.**
    - Rule VI: only a Bar member in good standing, MCLE and ULAS compliant, resident in the Philippines for at least 1 year, commissioned by the Electronic Notary Administrator after a virtual summary hearing.
    - A paper commission does **not** carry e-powers; separate commissions are needed (Rule XII §5).
    - Term (Rule VI §10): two years from 1 January of the commissioning year.
    - Directory (Rule V §3): a directory of commissioned notaries is posted on the SC website and updated on 15 February and 15 August.

12. **Records.**
    - Rule VIII: an Electronic Notarial Book is kept in the platform. It records the act, the identity evidence, the fee, a location statement and the IEN/REN mode, and the principal signs it electronically.
    - Rule IV §7: a PDF/A copy is auto-uploaded to the SC Central Notarial Database. During the transitional period it is emailed monthly to the Administrator instead.
    - Rule IV §8: the principal may record and download the audiovisual recording of the act.
    - Rule I §4(e) and Rule IX: the electronic seal carries a QR or bar code.

13. **Fees.** Rule XII §1: fees follow "any guide on notarial fees as may be issued by the Supreme Court", plus reasonable telecom and incidental charges. **No fee amount is stated in the rule.** Same PDF, pp. 25–26.

14. **Operational launch, 19 Oct 2026.**
    - The SC launched e-notarization nationwide on 19 Oct 2026.
    - Accredited facilities: **NotarizeIT by UNAWA, TWALA by Ohelio, QLegal by Quanby Solutions**.
    - Official hub: https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/enotarization/
    - Sources:
      - NewsBytes, 19 Oct 2026: https://newsbytes.ph/2026/10/19/sc-rolls-out-electronic-notarization-nationwide/
      - Radar PH, 19 Oct 2026: https://radar.ph/online-notarization-officially-begins-in-the-philippines-today/
    - The SC hub page itself returned 403 to our fetch, so this list comes from press reports. See "not verified".

15. **LRA and SEC acceptance circulars.** The SC signed Joint Memorandum Circulars with the LRA and SEC on **13 Oct 2026** on accepting e-notarized documents. The SC is still coordinating with DFA on overseas use.
    - Daily Tribune, 15 Oct 2026: https://tribune.net.ph/2026/10/15/sc-clears-way-for-electronically-notarized-land-business-documents
    - The SC press release URL exists but did not load for us: https://sc.judiciary.gov.ph/sc-strengthens-digital-transformation-through-enotarization-partnership-with-lra-and-sec/
    - **No equivalent circular was found for the DILG, BLGF, CSC or Ombudsman.**

16. **Principal flow, as reported.**
    - The principal uses a government ID plus a face scan.
    - A live session is held.
    - The platform checks the IP address; VPNs and proxies are barred.
    - The fee is paid within 24 hours.
    - The notarial certificate and seal are issued after payment.
    - About 100 ENPs had been commissioned as of 25 Sep 2026.
    - Source: Philstar Tech, 19 Oct 2026: https://philstartech.com/news/2026/10/19/20101/online-notarization-philippines/

17. **Ombudsman.** Under Administrative Order No. 07 (1990), Rule I §3, complaints "may be in any form, either verbal or in writing". For speedier disposition it is "preferable that it be in writing and under oath".
    - Rule III §3: an administrative case "may be initiated by a written complaint under oath", accompanied by witness affidavits.
    - Source: https://www.lawphil.net/courts/supreme/ao/ao_07_1990.html
    - Later amendments (e.g. A.O. No. 17) were not re-checked.

18. **CSC.** The 2025 RACCS is **CSC Resolution No. 2500357**, promulgated 30 Apr 2025, and amends the 2017 RACCS (Res. No. 1701077).
    - Source: https://batasnatin.com/laws/csc-policy-resolution-csc-resolution-no-2500357-2025-rules-on-administrative
    - This is a secondary host. The 4 Aug 2025 effectivity comes from commentary only.

    - Source: `case_work/CV26360_COMPLETE/CASE_FILE/07_WITNESS_PACKS/NEIGHBOUR_JA_CHECK.md`, and the CV26360 master index row 07-06.

## Risks

- **Validity challenges.** An opposing party or a receiving office may question:
  - the notary's e-commission,
  - accreditation of the facility,
  - whether the affiant was in the Philippines,
  - the electronic signature.

  **Mitigations:**
  - Choose a notary listed in the SC ENP directory.
  - Keep the platform's QR verification output and the downloaded session video.
  - Never use a VPN (a punishable act; the notary must log location).
- **Expired commission (the Exh. L/M/N problem).**
  - **Paper notary:** confirm the commission term covers the signing date. A stamp reading "valid until 12/31/2025" is exactly the defect we hit.
  - **E-notary:** platforms must block expired commissions (Rule VII §2(u)), and renewal lapses remove the notary from the directory (Rule VI §11). This is a structural improvement, but still check the certificate's "validity of the term" field (Rule X §2(f)).
- **Identity.**
  - The ID must be unexpired (Rule VII §2(u)).
  - The affiant's name must match the ID exactly.
  - When signing as Attorney-in-Fact, annex the SPA and its apostille so the representative capacity is on the record.
- **Content.** A jurat attests only that the affiant swore; it does not make the facts true. Every factual sentence must follow the exhibit test: no fact without an attachment, and nothing about anyone's state of mind. A false affidavit exposes Jonathan to perjury.
- **Agency friction.** E-notarization is one day old. Expect unfamiliar desk staff, and carry or attach the rule citation (A.M. No. 24-10-14-SC, Rule I §5).
- **Privacy.** Platforms collect biometrics and IDs and share them with the SC (Rule VII §2(m)). Minimise third-party personal data in affidavit annexes.
