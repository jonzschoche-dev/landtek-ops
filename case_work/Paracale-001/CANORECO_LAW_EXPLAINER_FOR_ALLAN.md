# The Law on Your CANORECO Line — Plain Explainer for Allan (concise)

**Matter:** Paracale-001. Client-facing — Jonathan sends; nothing goes to CANORECO from this.
**Legal basis verified:** Magna Carta for Residential Electricity Consumers, Art. 14 + ERC Guidelines Ch. IV — quoted in `CANORECO_LINE_TAPPING_INTAKE.md` §1a.

---

## Messenger version (5 short messages)

**1**
Allan, two separate issues here: the line you paid for, and the poles on your land. The law treats them differently.

**2**
The line: your payment was an ADVANCE, not a gift. The law requires CANORECO to pay it back — promissory note, yearly refund from the line's income, or coop shares.

**3**
The tapping: the coop IS allowed to connect others to your line. But the law counts every tapped customer's payments toward YOUR refund. So the winning question isn't "why did you tap my line" — it's "where is my refund?" One caution: this only works if your payment was for your own connection. Never let anyone call it a donation — then no refund applies.

**4**
The poles: private land needs a paid right-of-way agreement. No agreement = they owe you compensation. (Courts won't remove the poles — coops can expropriate — but they must pay.)

**5**
So the visit is records first, not confrontation: your contract, their record of your payments, the line map, the list of taps, any right-of-way papers. With those we compute what they owe. From you: any receipts, the year the line was built, when the tapping started, which titled land the poles are on.

## One-liner version
You didn't donate that line — you advanced it. CANORECO may tap others onto it, but every tap feeds a refund they owe you and never paid. The poles need a paid right-of-way they never got. Get the records first; the numbers do the accusing.
