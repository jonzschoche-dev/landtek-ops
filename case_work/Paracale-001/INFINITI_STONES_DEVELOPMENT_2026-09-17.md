# DEVELOPMENT — Infiniti Stones Resource, the personal Operating Agreement, and SMBC's ownership
*LandTek internal, 2026-09-17. Operator-stated by Jonathan today; nothing yet on paper in our hands. What follows is what these three facts do to Wave 1, and what we do about it.*

## The three facts
1. **SMBC owns everything** at Casalugan — the plant and what sits with it.
2. **Stephen's Operating Agreement with SMBC is in his personal name** — so it exists and is executed. We still have not seen it.
3. **Stephen's new mining company, Infiniti Stones Resource, is making a full contract with SMBC now** — in progress, not signed as far as we know.

## What they mean together
Taken separately these are unremarkable. Taken together they change who we would be lending to.

**Everything Stephen holds at Casalugan is contractual, not proprietary.** If SMBC owns everything, then Stephen owns nothing there. His 50% net-profit share is a claim against SMBC's net profits under a personal contract, not an asset he can pledge in the ordinary sense. That was already the position in the deal memo's verification note; Jonathan's confirmation settles it.

**A new company interposed now puts our security in play.** Our ₱5,000,000 was structured against three things: the equipment bought with the money, Stephen's 50% net-profit share, and his personal undertaking. A full contract between Infiniti Stones and SMBC can affect all three:
- If the personal Operating Agreement is **superseded or novated** into Infiniti Stones, the profit share we were relying on moves to a company we know nothing about — and a pledge of it by Stephen personally becomes worthless.
- If the equipment goes into a plant whose owner is SMBC and whose operator is now a third company, **retained title has to be expressly acknowledged by SMBC in writing**, with a right of removal, or we risk buying machines that become part of somebody else's plant.
- The **obligor changes**. Today the repayment obligation is Stephen's personally. After the new contract it may sensibly sit with Infiniti Stones — which would need its own covenant, and its own disclosure of who stands behind it.

**Nationality is a live question, not a formality.** Small-scale mining contracts are reserved to Filipinos and cannot be assigned or transferred. If Infiniti Stones is foreign-owned and its "full contract" reaches the mining rather than the processing, that is a problem for SMBC before it is a problem for us. If it reaches only the plant, the processing and the offtake, it is ordinary and lawful. **The scope of that contract is the single most important thing to read.**

**The timing matters.** It is being made *now*. Either we are at that table before it is signed, or we are a financier arriving after the terms are set by others. Our preference is neither: we hold our money until we can read it.

## What this is not
This is not evidence of bad faith. A contractor forming a company to hold an operating contract is normal and probably sensible for him. But it is the second time in two weeks that Stephen's plans for Casalugan have arrived after the fact — first the concept paper, now the company — and our standing rule already anticipated it: his execution is unproven, so control stays in our vehicles and his role is earned in stages.

## What we do
1. **Hold the ₱5,000,000.** No drawdown until the Infiniti Stones constitutive documents, the draft SMBC contract, and the personal Operating Agreement are all in hand. This is not a change of posture — it is gate 0, which was already in place.
2. **No equipment on site without SMBC's written acknowledgment** that our machines remain ours and may be removed. Since SMBC owns everything, that signature has to come from SMBC, not from Stephen or Infiniti Stones.
3. **Re-paper the financing when the counterparty is known.** Whoever ends up operating, the repayment obligation needs a named obligor, a named security, and a signature from the party that can actually give it.
4. **Accelerate Wave 2.** AVI Gold Processing is on Allan's own land under Allan's own permits, and nothing at Casalugan touches it. The more that is in motion at Jose Panganiban, the less any of this matters to us. **This development makes Wave 2 the priority, not Wave 1.**
5. **Keep the Minahang Bayan first.** Allan's declared area remains the position from which everything else is negotiated, whoever is operating at Casalugan.
6. **Ask, plainly.** Block B of the questions list is rewritten around this and goes to Stephen now.

## What changes in the model
Nothing structural. Wave 1 was always financing and equipment into someone else's plant; that is why it was never the foundation. The foundation is Allan's company, Allan's land, Allan's Minahang Bayan and Allan's plant, with PGC's capital behind them. Wave 1 is a good first revenue if it can be papered safely, and it is declined if it cannot.
