## URGENT MOTION FOR THE APPOINTMENT OF A SPECIAL ADMINISTRATOR

PETITIONER, through counsel, respectfully moves that, pending the hearing of the Petition and the issuance of letters of administration, he be appointed **Special Administrator** of the estates under Rule 80, and states:

### 1. The delay that Rule 80 contemplates is certain

1.1. Letters of administration cannot issue before the Petition is published once a week for three consecutive weeks and heard on notice to every heir and interested person (Section 3, Rule 79; Sections 3–4, Rule 76). The Petition names heirs in the Philippines and abroad (Brazil; ( others ? )) and a branch (the Heirs of Melvyn Inocalla) whose members are still being located (Petition ¶ 2.5). Opposition by the persons named in Petition ¶ 2.7 is expected. On any realistic view the questions on which regular letters depend will take months.

1.2. Section 1, Rule 80 authorizes the Court, "[w]hen there is delay in granting letters … by any cause," to appoint a special administrator "to take possession and charge of the estate of the deceased until the questions causing the delay are decided and executors or administrators appointed." The remedy is discretionary and its object is the preservation of the estate [*De Guzman v. Guadiz*, G.R. No. L-48585, 3 Mar 1980; *Heirs of Castillo v. Lacuata-Gabriel*, G.R. No. 162934, 11 Nov 2005; *Ocampo v. Ocampo*, G.R. No. 187879, 5 Jul 2010 ⟦verify⟧].

### 2. The estates are losing value every day the delay runs

2.1. **Unauthorized gold extraction** is being carried on upon the land formerly of Casper V. Inocalla, by his widow through a tenant and an outside financier, without the heir-majority approval that the judicially-approved partition of 28 January 1992 requires for any operating contract, and without any sharing of proceeds in which the estate's heirs are entitled to participate; the underground workings are reported to extend into adjoining family parcels ( which — estate res or an heir's own lot — to be fixed from the survey ? ) [O; Petition ¶ 8.3; Annex "K" ( ? )]. Ore removed cannot be restored.

2.2. **Adverse occupation** of about 25 hectares of family land at Paracale/Jose Panganiban claimed for the estates, subject to confirmation of the parcel (Petition ¶ 8.2), by persons claiming through the late Vicente Inocalla, Jr. and by his former caretakers asserting tenancy [O; Annex "J"], with the risk that continued, unchallenged possession is later pleaded against the estate and the heirs.

2.3. **The Manila apartment building** (TCT No. (44055) 002-2026001670, ten units) was **restored to the decedent's name on 16 April 2026** by the Register of Deeds of Manila under the alias writ in CC 13-131220 [V Annex "C"], yet remains physically held by the same claimants, who take its rents [O]. The unlawful-detainer action must be brought within one year of demand and by a party with authority to sue for the estate.

2.4. **A matured obligation to convey ~64 hectares to the estate's beneficiaries** (Casper Inocalla's Undertaking of 25 August 1985, condition of full payment to DBP now satisfied [O]) is unenforced and its obligors are the same persons conducting the mining in ¶ 2.1 [Petition ¶ 4.5].

2.5. **Estate-tax exposure** on two 1984–85 deaths is accruing and no one has authority to file for the estates [Petition ¶ 9.3].

### 3. A co-owner cannot do this; a special administrator can

3.1. The heirs hold the estate in co-ownership (Art. 1078, Civil Code). A co-owner may bring an action for the benefit of all (Art. 487), and four of them did so in Manila; but a co-owner cannot take possession of the whole for the estate, collect and account for all its fruits, register the 1992 partition, file for the estates with the BIR, or bind absent and contesting heirs. A special administrator, under the Court's control, "shall take possession and charge of the goods, chattels, rights, credits, and estate of the deceased and preserve the same for the executor or administrator afterwards appointed, and for that purpose may commence and maintain suits as administrator" (Section 2, Rule 80), while being barred from paying debts and from selling non-perishable property without leave — the custodial mandate that the facts call for and nothing more.

### 4. Petitioner is the proper appointee

4.1. Petitioner is a child of the decedents, a Philippine resident, nominated by every living sibling (Petition ¶ 7.1; Annexes "E"), already the heirs' attorney-in-fact over the very properties concerned (Annexes "F"), physically present on the estate, and the person who as attorney-in-fact assisted in the recovery of the Manila property and personally secured the Registry's certified copy of the reinstated title (Petition ¶ 7.3) [O as to his role in the case; V as to the certified copy]. The Rule 78 order of preference, though not binding in the choice of a special administrator [*Tan v. Gedorio, Jr.*, G.R. No. 166520, 14 Mar 2008 ⟦verify⟧], points to him.

4.2. Petitioner has disclosed in the Petition (¶ 7.4) the 2006 civil judgment in CC 98-88750 and the reasons it should not weigh against him; he repeats that disclosure here and **offers to post the bond of a special administrator in whatever amount the Court fixes** under Section 4, Rule 81, and to render accounts at such intervals as the Court directs.

### 5. Reliefs

WHEREFORE, petitioner prays that this Honorable Court, after such hearing as it may set on the shortest notice the Rules allow:

(a) **APPOINT ALLAN V. INOCALLA as Special Administrator** of the intestate estates of the spouses Vicente Inocalla, Sr. and Beatriz Villafria Inocalla, effective upon his oath and the filing of a bond in the amount the Court fixes;

(b) **AUTHORIZE** the Special Administrator, subject to the Court's supervision, to: (i) take possession and charge of all property of the estates, real and personal, wherever situated, including the building at 2531 G. Del Pilar St., Santa Ana, Manila; (ii) demand and collect the rents, fruits and income of estate property and deposit the same in an estate account to be opened with a bank designated by the Court; (iii) commence and maintain, in the name of the estates, actions to recover possession of estate property, to enjoin and to account for the extraction of minerals from estate land, and to enforce the Undertaking of 25 August 1985, and to continue and complete the execution of the judgment in Civil Case No. 13-131220; (iv) secure taxpayer identification numbers for the estates and file the returns required by law, and pay real property taxes on estate property from estate funds; (v) cause the annotation on the certificates of title of estate property of a notice of these proceedings and of his appointment; and (vi) engage counsel and such assistance as may be needed, with leave of Court;

(c) **DIRECT** the Special Administrator to file a **preliminary inventory** of the property taken into his charge within thirty (30) days of his appointment — without prejudice to the full inventory and appraisal under Section 1, Rule 83 within three months of regular letters — and to report to the Court every ( 90 ? ) days; and
