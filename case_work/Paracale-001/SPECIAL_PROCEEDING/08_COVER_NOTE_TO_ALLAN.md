## LANDTEK STRATEGY NOTE

**To:** Allan V. Inocalla
**From:** LandTek (Jonathan)
**Date:** 27 September 2026
**Re:** The petition to make you Special Administrator of your parents' estates — what we are doing, why, and what happens next

**This is LandTek's strategy. It is not legal advice. Everything in it is to be confirmed, corrected and refined by the lawyer you choose. Sign nothing until that lawyer has adopted the papers.**

### 1. What we are asking the court for

One thing: an order from the Regional Trial Court in Daet appointing you **Special Administrator** of the estates of your father and mother, so that for the first time since 1985 one person holds lawful authority, from a court, over the family property.

The petition also asks, in the ordinary course, for full letters of administration after a published hearing. But the order we are built to get first is the interim one.

### 2. Why "special administrator", and why we call it a bridge

Your parents died in 1984 and 1985. No court has ever appointed anyone to administer what they left. Every parcel has been held, fought over, mined or defended by whichever brother, sister or claimant was standing on it, in that person's own name. That is the gap we are closing.

Full administration takes months: notice has to be published for three weeks and served on more than thirty-five heirs here and abroad, and there will be opposition. A special administrator can be appointed **now**, under Rule 80, precisely because of that delay. The office is:

- **temporary** — it ends automatically the day regular letters issue;
- **custodial** — you take possession, collect income, protect the property, and sue for the estates; you cannot sell, mortgage, partition, distribute, or decide who is an heir;
- **supervised** — you post a bond the court fixes, deposit everything into estate accounts, file an inventory within thirty days and report monthly; anyone may complain to the court about anything you do.

We plead it that way on purpose. It is the least the court can give, it harms no heir, and it is very hard to oppose without saying you prefer the property to stay ownerless.

### 3. What is in the petition, and what is deliberately not

**In.** Two properties only: the 13.37-hectare parcel at Batobalani, Paracale under TCT T-2194, still in your father's name on the July 2024 certified copy; and the Vito Cruz building in Manila, restored to your mother's name on 16 April 2026. Your surviving siblings' powers of attorney to you. The 1992 partition, described as never carried into the titles. The reasons the court should act now (section 4 below).

**Out, on purpose.** Consents from your siblings (not needed for this order). The old Manila case from 1998 (not needed; it will be raised by the other side if at all, and there is an answer). The full list of family property — the four DBP lots, the mineral claims, the tax-declared parcels, the Milaor ricefield, the Labo lot. The mining, beyond one line. The person who claims to be Senen's child is **not named**; that claim is met at the hearing, where it has to be proved with a birth record or an adoption decree.

**Why so narrow.** The docket fee is assessed on the value the petition states, and the petition already promises to pay the difference when the inventory comes in. More importantly, every extra fact is a handle for the other side to grab. We give them nothing to grab but public records.

**One thing to understand clearly.** Narrow now does not mean hidden later. Within three months of your appointment you must file a sworn **inventory of everything** your parents left, including the property we did not plead and including property others claim. Leaving something out of the inventory is the one thing that can get a special administrator removed. The full list already exists in our file for that day.

### 4. Why the court should act now — the three grounds

The petition says the estates are losing now, not someday, and it says it from records only:

- **The building.** Won back by final judgment, title restored in April, and the estates still receive nothing because the people who claim through your late brother Vicente Jr. hold it and collect the rents. Only someone authorized to sue for the estates can start the case to recover it.
- **T-2194.** In your father's name, yet levied in 2014 for one heir's judgment debt; and a tenancy case by the Bombitas over part of it has run for ten years — Provincial Adjudicator, DARAB, Court of Appeals, now the Supreme Court — with your sister Senen litigating alone, in her own name. The estate that actually owns the parcel was never a party and has never been heard.
- **The killings.** Vicente Jr. was shot dead at his home in Sta. Rosa Sur on 11 September 2017. Senen was shot dead in her car at Batobalani on 12 February 2021. In September 2023 the NBI sent its investigation of Senen's death to the Provincial Prosecutor for murder against Eric Bombita, who the NBI says is married to Elena, one of the two people holding the building, and another man. The NBI's own words are that the case "emanates from a land dispute."

Then the argument: the cause of all of it is that nobody has had lawful authority over these estates for forty years. The court can end that today with one bonded custodian answerable to it. That decides nothing about who is an heir, who is a tenant, or who did what. It only means the estates stop being ownerless in fact.

**The line we hold.** The petition accuses no one of anything. It cites the police blotter, the death certificate and the NBI's transmittal and says in terms that guilt is for the prosecutor and the criminal courts. Do not go beyond that line in anything you say, sign or post. The moment the petition reads as an accusation, it becomes the fight instead of the bridge.

### 5. What happens after filing

1. **Filing and raffle** at the RTC in Daet. Docket fee paid on the assessed values of the two properties.
2. **The interim order.** Rule 80 requires no notice or hearing for this appointment. We ask the judge to sign on the papers or at the earliest date. A proposed order is in the binder for the judge to sign. Expect the judge to want to see you, and possibly to hear you briefly on your affidavit.
3. **Bond, oath, letters of special administration.** The court fixes the bond; a surety company issues it; you take the oath; the clerk issues your letters. Have a surety quotation in hand before the hearing so this takes days, not weeks.
4. **Day one after the order.** Open the two estate bank accounts. Annotate the order on both titles. Serve the written demand on the building's occupants as special administrator, with the order attached. Secure the estates' tax identification numbers. Start the inventory.
5. **The published hearing on letters** follows in the ordinary course. Expect opposition from Vicente III and Elena, possibly from the person claiming through Senen, possibly from the Bombitas. That hearing is where heirship is proved and where the other side gets its say. Your interim authority continues meanwhile.
6. **Inventory within thirty days** (preliminary) and **three months** (full). Monthly reports. Everything through the estate accounts, nothing through yours.

**Rough clock, if uncontested:** two to three weeks to gather the paper, a few days to sign, then the court's calendar. Six to ten weeks to an order is a fair estimate, not a promise.

### 6. What you need to do now

Paper, in this order. The binder has a tab sheet for each one saying exactly where it goes.

1. **PSA death certificates** of your father and your mother (Annexes A and A-1). Order online. These prove residence at death, the one fact that fixes the court.
2. **Your PSA birth certificate** (Annex E).
3. **Senen's PSA death certificate**, and the death certificates of Francisco, Cipriana, Casper and Melvyn (behind Annex B).
4. **The schedule of heirs** (Annex B): every heir across the ten branches with name, age and address, including the surviving spouses of your late brothers and sisters. Sit with the family and fill it. Ask each living sibling whether they know of any debt of the estates; the petition says you inquired.
5. **Page 5 of the T-2194 certified copy** (Annex G-1). You hold the paper; scan the last page or pull a fresh copy at the Registry.
6. **The Daet trip:** assessed values of both properties from the assessors (Manila and Paracale), the certified copies of the 1992 partition, the approving Order and the Certificate of Finality from Branch 41 (Annex D), and the certified Court of Appeals decision of 19 October 2023 in Senen's case (Annex H).
7. **Ask the Provincial Prosecutor's Office in Daet** whether an information was ever filed on the NBI's September 2023 transmittal. Your affidavit currently says you do not know. If one was filed, we add the case number; if not, it stays as written.
8. **Read your affidavit line by line** before you swear it. It is written in your voice from what you have told us. Where it says something you did not see or do not know, strike it. Do not sign to anything you cannot say from the witness stand.

### 7. What you must not do while this is pending

- **No self-help.** Do not enter, fence, clear, or post guards on any parcel the Bombitas or Vicente III occupy. Do not attempt to take the building. Everything runs through the court once you are appointed, and through counsel before.
- **No confrontation** with the Bombitas, Vicente III or Elena. Serve papers through counsel or a process server, never in person.
- **No statements** about the killings, in person or online. The petition says what the records say and no more. Anything you add becomes evidence against the estates' neutrality.
- **No deals on T-2194** — no mining agreement, no lease, no permit application in the estates' name — until the order issues and, for anything beyond custody, until the hearing has settled whose share it is.
- **Keep money apart.** From the day you are appointed, not one peso of estate income touches your own account.

### 8. How T-2194 and the mining fit

The order puts the **titled parcel** in your custody for the estates. It does not decide who ultimately gets it. The 1992 partition awarded that parcel to Senen, and a person who was substituted for her in the tenancy case claims it "by succession." Your family says Senen left no child. That is decided at the hearing on letters, on paper: a birth record naming Senen as parent or an adoption decree makes an heir; nothing else does.

A mining permit is a separate matter for the Mines and Geosciences Bureau and the Provincial Mining Regulatory Board. No letters of administration grant one. What the order gives you is standing: the recognised custodian of the parcel, able to deal with the agencies, the tenancy case and any operator in the estates' name. The 2008 DAR order excluding T-2194 and the adjoining lots from agrarian coverage as mineral land is in the file and will matter at that stage.
