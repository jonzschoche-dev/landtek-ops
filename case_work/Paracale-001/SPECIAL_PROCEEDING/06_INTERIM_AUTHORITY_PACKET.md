## VERIFIED PETITION FOR THE SETTLEMENT OF THE INTESTATE ESTATES OF THE SPOUSES VICENTE INOCALLA, SR. AND BEATRIZ VILLAFRIA INOCALLA, WITH URGENT PRAYER FOR THE APPOINTMENT OF A SPECIAL ADMINISTRATOR

PETITIONER respectfully states:

### I. WHAT IS ASKED

1. Petitioner asks this Honorable Court to take cognizance of the settlement of the intestate estates of his late parents under Rules 73 and 79, and — **because the estates are being stripped of income and substance now, while the notice and hearing on regular administration run their course** — to appoint him **Special Administrator under Section 1, Rule 80, at once and pending the issuance of letters**, with the custodial powers set out in the prayer. The special administration is sought **as a bridge, and only as a bridge**: a temporary, custodial, court-supervised holding of the estates between today and the day this Court appoints a regular administrator after full notice and hearing. It decides nothing about who that administrator will be, disposes of nothing, distributes nothing, and ends by operation of the Rule the moment letters issue.

### II. THE DECEDENTS, INTERESTED PERSONS AND VENUE

2. **VICENTE INOCALLA, SR.** died on **26 November 1984**, and his wife **BEATRIZ VILLAFRIA INOCALLA** on **12 August 1985**, both residents of **Paracale, Camarines Norte**, at the time of death, which is also the address of record of the registered owner on the title described in ¶ 6(b) (Annexes "A", "A-1" and "C"). Neither left a will. No settlement, judicial or extrajudicial, of either estate has ever been made, save the partial judicial partition in ¶ 6.

3. The spouses were married before the Family Code and their property relations were governed by the conjugal partnership of gains under the Civil Code. Petitioner requests the appropriate liquidation and settlement under Section 2, Rule 73, with the interests, liabilities, inventories and accounts of the two estates separately identified. Administration is sought only over property and rights remaining subject to settlement, with due regard to the final partition judgment described in paragraph 6.

4. The decedents were survived by ten legitimate children: **Allan** (petitioner), **Jesus**, **Marilou** and **Herbert**, who are living; and **Melvyn**, **Francisco** (died 2 July 2002), **Vicente, Jr.** (died 11 September 2017), **Senen** (died 2021, without issue), **Cipriana** and **Casper**, who are deceased. The persons asserting rights through deceased children are identified in the **Schedule of Heirs and Creditors, Annex "B"**, subject to proof of their respective interests. Annex "B" states the heirs' names, ages and residences and the known creditors' names and residences, as required by Section 2, Rule 79. The only creditor known to petitioner after inquiry of his surviving siblings is the Republic of the Philippines, through the Bureau of Internal Revenue, for estate tax on both estates in an amount not yet determined.

5. **Vicente de Leon Inocalla III** and **Elena de Leon Inocalla**, of 2531 G. Del Pilar Street, Barangay 759, Zone 82, Santa Ana, City of Manila, assert interests through the late Vicente, Jr. They are included for notice as interested persons without a determination in this application of their asserted heirship. ### III. PROPERTY AND JURISDICTION

6. The property and rights requiring settlement include: (a) agricultural and mineral land in **Paracale and Jose Panganiban, Camarines Norte, registered in the decedents' names**, including TCT Nos. T-3897, T-5656 and T-2194 and tax-declared parcels, subject to the effect of the prior partition (Annex "G"); (b) the **four-storey, ten-unit apartment building at 2531 G. The probable value of the estates exceeds the jurisdictional amount of this Court and cannot be stated with precision until the certified titles and current assessments in Annex "G" are complete; petitioner pays the docket fee on the assessed values there stated and undertakes to pay any deficiency upon the inventory.

### IV. WHY A SPECIAL ADMINISTRATOR IS NEEDED NOW

7. **The Manila building yields nothing to the estates, although the estates have already won it back.** Title was restored to the decedent on 16 April 2026 by final judgment (Annex "C"). The building has nonetheless remained in the possession of the persons named in ¶ 5, who claim under the cancelled title, and its rents are not received by the estates. The action to recover possession must be brought within one year of demand, and only by a party with authority to sue for the estates.

8. **Minerals are being extracted from the family's mineral land without authority from the heirs.** The 1992 judgment (Annex "D") permits operation of the family's mineral claims only with the approval of a majority of the heirs and with proceeds shared among all of them. Extraction is presently being carried on without either. Ore removed cannot be restored.

9. **Portions of the family land at Paracale and Jose Panganiban are in the possession of persons holding no title, lease or permission from the heirs.** Their possession hardens against the estates with every year it goes unchallenged.

9-A. The facts in ¶¶ 7 to 9 are attested in petitioner's affidavit, filed herewith with its supporting records (Annex "H"). Petitioner pleads them no further than the appointment requires; the identity and claims of the persons concerned are for the actions the special administrator will bring, and for the hearing.

10. Letters of administration cannot issue before publication for three consecutive weeks and a hearing on notice to some forty heirs on three continents (Section 3, Rule 79; Sections 3–4, Rule 76), and opposition from the persons in ¶ 5 is expected. That is the "delay … by any cause" of Section 1, Rule 80. The appointment lies in the Court's sound discretion; its object is the preservation of the estate until it can pass into the hands of a person fully authorized to administer it, and the order of preference in Section 6, Rule 78 guides but does not bind the choice of a special administrator (*De Guzman v. Guadiz*, G.R. No. L-48585, 3 March 1980; *Tan v. Gedorio, Jr.*, G.R. No. 166520, 14 March 2008). A co-owner cannot do what is needed: take possession of the whole for the estates, collect and account for all fruits, sue for the estates, register the 1992 awards, or file with the Bureau of Internal Revenue for the estates. A special administrator can (Section 2, Rule 80), and can do nothing more.

### IV-A. THE APPOINTMENT IS A BRIDGE, NOT A JUDGMENT

10-A. **It is temporary by the terms of the Rule.** A special administrator holds the estate only "until the questions causing the delay are decided and executors or administrators appointed" (Section 1, Rule 80), and "when letters testamentary or of administration are granted … the powers of the special administrator shall cease, and he shall forthwith deliver to the executor or administrator the goods, chattels, money, and estate of the deceased in his hands" (Section 3, Rule 80). The office ends automatically. No order is needed to end it, and no heir need move to end it.

10-B. **It is custodial by the terms of the Rule.** The special administrator takes possession and preserves; he may sue to protect the estate; he may sell only perishables and only as the Court orders; he may not pay the decedents' debts unless the Court so orders (Section 2, Rule 80). He cannot partition, cannot distribute, cannot convey, and cannot decide who is an heir. Every share stands exactly where it stands today. The claims of the persons in ¶ 5 to a share through the late Vicente, Jr. are neither admitted nor diminished by the appointment; they remain for the hearing on notice, where those persons may oppose regular letters and be heard in full.

10-C. **It is supervised.** Everything the special administrator collects goes into estate accounts opened under the Court's order, the two estates' collections kept separately; he acts under bond fixed by the Court; he reports monthly; and any interested person may move against any act of his at any time.

10-D. **It is therefore the least relief that stops the loss, and it harms no one.** The rents now leaving the Manila building, the ore now leaving the ground at Paracale, and the possession now hardening against the estates are lost to **every** heir alike — to those who support this petition and to those who will oppose it. Preserving those assets under the Court's hand until the Court decides who administers them is in the interest of all, and against the interest of none but the persons presently taking them. That is what the bridge is for, and that is all it is for.

### V. PETITIONER IS THE PROPER APPOINTEE

11. Petitioner is a son of the decedents (Annex "E") and therefore himself within the class of **next of kin preferred by Section 6, Rule 78**; of legal age; a resident of Purok 4, Barangay Capacuan, Paracale, Camarines Norte; of sound mind; and never convicted of any offense involving moral turpitude (Section 1, Rule 78). Since 2024 he has held the special powers of attorney of his surviving siblings, Jesus, Marilou and Herbert, over the family's properties (Annex "F"); he assisted, as their attorney-in-fact, in the execution that restored the Manila building to the estate, and personally obtained the Registry's certified copy of the restored title (Annex "C"). He is on the ground, already entrusted by the heirs with these very properties, and able to act at once.

12. Petitioner will post the bond of a special administrator under Section 4, Rule 81 in whatever amount the Court fixes, will file an inventory within thirty days of appointment, and will account at such intervals as the Court directs.

INOCALLA as SPECIAL ADMINISTRATOR** of the intestate estates of the spouses Vicente Inocalla, Sr. and Beatriz Villafria Inocalla, upon his oath and the filing of a bond in the amount the Court fixes, with authority, subject to the Court's supervision, to: (i) take possession and charge of all property of the estates wherever situated, including the building at 2531 G.
