11. Letters of administration cannot issue before publication for three consecutive weeks and a hearing on notice to some forty heirs on three continents (Section 3, Rule 79; Sections 3–4, Rule 76), and opposition from the persons in ¶ 5 is expected. That is the "delay … by any cause" of Section 1, Rule 80. The appointment lies in the Court's sound discretion; its object is the preservation of the estate until it can pass into the hands of a person fully authorized to administer it, and the order of preference in Section 6, Rule 78 guides but does not bind the choice of a special administrator. ⟦*De Guzman v. Guadiz*, G.R. No. L-48585, 3 March 1980; *Tan v. Gedorio, Jr.*, G.R. No. 166520, 14 March 2008 — verify and pull exact quotations before filing.⟧ A co-owner cannot do what is needed: take possession of the whole for the estates, collect and account for all fruits, sue for the estates, register the 1992 awards, or file with the Bureau of Internal Revenue for the estates. A special administrator can (Section 2, Rule 80), and can do nothing more.

### IV-A. THE APPOINTMENT IS A BRIDGE, NOT A JUDGMENT

11-A. **It is temporary by the terms of the Rule.** A special administrator holds the estate only "until the questions causing the delay are decided and executors or administrators appointed" (Section 1, Rule 80), and "when letters testamentary or of administration are granted … the powers of the special administrator shall cease, and he shall forthwith deliver to the executor or administrator the goods, chattels, money, and estate of the deceased in his hands" (Section 3, Rule 80). The office ends automatically. No order is needed to end it, and no heir need move to end it.

11-B. **It is custodial by the terms of the Rule.** The special administrator takes possession and preserves; he may sue to protect the estate; he may sell only perishables and only as the Court orders; he may not pay the decedents' debts unless the Court so orders (Section 2, Rule 80). He cannot partition, cannot distribute, cannot convey, and cannot decide who is an heir. Every share stands exactly where it stands today. The claims of the persons in ¶ 5 to a share through the late Vicente, Jr. are neither admitted nor diminished by the appointment; they remain for the hearing on notice, where those persons may oppose regular letters and be heard in full.

11-C. **It is supervised.** Everything the special administrator collects goes into an estate account opened under the Court's order; he acts under bond fixed by the Court; he reports every ninety days; and any interested person may move against any act of his at any time.

11-D. **It is therefore the least relief that stops the loss, and it harms no one.** The rents now leaving the Manila building, the ore now leaving the ground at Paracale, and the possession now hardening against the estates are lost to **every** heir alike — to those who support this petition and to those who will oppose it. Preserving those assets under the Court's hand until the Court decides who administers them is in the interest of all, and against the interest of none but the persons presently taking them. That is what the bridge is for, and that is all it is for.
