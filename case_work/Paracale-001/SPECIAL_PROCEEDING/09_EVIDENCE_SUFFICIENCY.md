## 5. The "already partitioned" objection, and the answer

The strongest legal objection is not evidentiary: *the estate was judicially partitioned in 1992; both properties were adjudicated; there is nothing to administer; the remedy is to execute Civil Case 5625, not to appoint an administrator.* The answer, all of it from the record:

1. **The partition was partial.** By its own terms it covered listed parcels and "omitted" items; it did not liquidate the conjugal partnership, did not pay the estate tax, and did not reach T-3424, T-4185 or the tax-declared parcels except as listed (Rule 73 §2; Annex D).
2. **No award was ever registered.** Both titles stand in the decedents' names in 2024 and 2026 [V]. No certificate can move without an estate-tax clearance, which was never obtained.
3. **The judgment can no longer be executed.** A judicially approved compromise is immediately final and executory (Art. 2037 CC). Execution by motion lapsed in 1997 and by independent action in 2002 (Rule 39 §6). What remains is a final adjudication of shares that can only be given effect through a settlement proceeding and a project of partition in conformity with it. This petition says it proceeds "with due regard to the final partition judgment" (¶ 3) — rev. 11 says so expressly in ¶ 6.
4. **The awardees are dead in part.** Senen (T-2194) died in 2021; six of the ten children have died. Their awards now pass through their own estates, which is exactly what an administration proceeding sorts out and a Rule 39 execution cannot.
5. **Rule 80 is about preservation in the meantime.** Whatever the ultimate distribution, the property is being lost now, to everyone.

Counsel's call recorded: whether to plead point 3 in the petition (rev. 11 does, in one clause) or hold it for the opposition.

## 5-A. Venue double-check (2026-09-29, Jonathan: "let's double check the correct venue")

**Layer 1 — the province (Rule 73 §1).** The petition for letters is filed in the RTC "of the province in which [the decedent] resides at the time of his death." "Resides" means actual, physical habitation, not legal domicile (*Fule v. Court of Appeals*, G.R. No. L-40502, 29 Nov 1976; *Garcia-Quiazon v. Belen*, G.R. No. 189121, 31 Jul 2013). A death certificate's recital of residence is evidence of it but does not bind the court. Venue under Rule 73 §1 is not jurisdictional; it is waived if not raised in time, but an oppositor may raise it. **Evidence for Paracale:** T-2194 (1958): "resident of the Municipality of Paracale" [V]; Manila title: address "Paracale, Camarines Norte, Phils." [V]; the heirs brought their own partition suit in RTC Camarines Norte (CC 5625/5626, 1991) [V]; Allan's own statements: the family farm and houses at Capacuan/Batobalani, the Manila building "built by my parents from our original family house" so that each child would have a unit [O]. **Evidence for Manila:** the same statement — the Manila building was the family's Manila home; Corazon (Vicente Jr.'s wife) lived there from about 1980; Beatriz died in August 1985 (the 88750 decision does not say where). **Neither certificate is in hand. Both parents died of cancer; where they were living in 1984–85, and where each died, decides this.** If the certificates put them in Manila, the correct court is RTC Manila, not Daet, and the whole petition re-plates to Manila (the Manila building is there; T-2194 is administered from there under Rule 73 §1 regardless). Ask Allan; get the PSA certificates; if Manila, stop and re-plan before filing.

**Layer 2 — the station within Camarines Norte.** RTC Camarines Norte sits at Daet (Branches 38, 39, 40, 41 and Family Court 5) and at Labo (Branch 64, Hillcrest Subd., Brgy. Bulhao, Labo; in operation by at least May 2000 per the Supreme Court's audit resolution A.M. No. 02-9-580-RTC). Paracale and Jose Panganiban are the western towns of the province, in the same legislative district as Labo, Capalonga and Santa Elena. **The official OCA "Updated Territorial Jurisdiction (27 Nov 2024)" list could not be fetched from this environment (judiciary domains blocked), and the secondary sources found online are inconsistent** — one directory lists Paracale and Jose Panganiban under a Daet branch, another lists them under Branch 64 Labo. The 1992 partition was filed in Branch 41 Daet, which proves nothing for today if Branch 64 was carved out afterward. **Action before filing: one call to the Office of the Clerk of Court, RTC Camarines Norte, Daet (or Branch 64, +63 920 958 0025, rtc2lab064@judiciary.gov.ph) — "Which station takes a special proceeding for a decedent who resided in Paracale?"** If Labo: the caption's station line (job file `court[3]`) and the two "Daet, Camarines Norte" lines in the petition's signature block and the proposed Order change to Labo; nothing else in the petition changes. Filing at the wrong station is not fatal (same court), but the OCC will re-route it, and the interim order waits for that. **Jonathan (2026-09-29): a case filed with the RTC is allocated internally to the proper station, Labo or Daet.** Correct in law — it is one court, and the assignment to a branch is administrative, not venue. The practical wrinkle: Daet is a multi-branch station with an Office of the Clerk of Court that raffles; Labo is a single-branch station (Branch 64 only), where a case within its territory is filed directly with that branch. If Paracale is Labo's, a petition lodged at Daet is transmitted to Labo and only then acted on; if Paracale is Daet's, a petition lodged at Labo comes back. Either way the only cost is days, but days are what the interim order is about. Hence the one call before filing, and the station line typed accordingly.

**Layer 4 — reach of the Daet appointment over the Manila building (Jonathan, 2026-09-29: "will the admin appointment in Daet also have jurisdiction over property in Manila?").** Yes. The settlement court's jurisdiction is over the estate as one whole, in rem, wherever in the Philippines the property lies; the venue rule of Rule 73 §1 picks the one court that takes the entire estate and excludes every other court from settling it. Letters of special administration issued at Daet are valid nationwide: the special administrator takes possession of the Manila building, has the order and letters annotated by the Registry of Deeds of Manila on TCT (44055) 002-2026001670 (prayer (a)(v)), collects the rents into the estate account, and sues in the estates' name. Two limits, neither a problem: (i) the ejectment of the occupants is a separate Rule 70 action filed where the property is, the Metropolitan Trial Court of Manila, brought by the special administrator for the estates (Rule 80 §2 authorises suits); the Daet court does not itself try possession against them; (ii) the execution of the 13-131220 judgment stays with RTC Manila, Branch 15, which issued it; the special administrator steps into the plaintiffs' shoes there, he does not move it to Daet. Conversely, if the province turns out to be Manila (Layer 1), an RTC Manila appointment reaches T-2194 in Paracale in exactly the same way.

**Layer 5 — split residence (Jonathan, 2026-09-29: "if one parent resided in Manila upon death and one in Paracale, how is venue decided?").** Venue under Rule 73 §1 is fixed per decedent: each estate belongs to the province where that spouse actually resided at death. But Rule 73 §2 supplies the bridge: "If both spouses have died, the conjugal partnership shall be liquidated in the testate or intestate proceedings of either." Both pleaded properties are conjugal on their face (T-2194 to "Vicente Inocalla, married to Beatriz Villafria", 1958; the Manila lot to "Beatriz V. Inocalla, married to Vicente Inocalla"), so the whole conjugal mass, the Manila building included, may be inventoried, administered and liquidated in the proceeding of whichever spouse's estate is opened. Hence: **open the estate of the spouse who resided in Paracale, in RTC Camarines Norte, and administer and liquidate the conjugal partnership there under §2; the special administrator appointed in that proceeding takes custody of all the conjugal property, in Manila and in Paracale alike.** The other spouse's own estate (that spouse's half after liquidation, plus what that spouse inherited from the first to die) is, in practice, settled in the same proceeding, because the heirs of both are the same ten children; if an oppositor insists that it be settled where that spouse resided, the answers are (i) §2, (ii) venue is waivable and not jurisdictional, (iii) identical heirs and a single mass make a second proceeding pointless, and (iv) Rule 73 §1's last sentence bars contesting the court's jurisdiction except on appeal or where want of jurisdiction appears on the record — which is why the petition must plead each parent's residence truthfully rather than assert Paracale for both. Two consequences for drafting: **(a) counsel's call whether to keep the joint caption "Estates of the Spouses" or to style the case as the estate of the Paracale spouse "with liquidation of the conjugal partnership with the late [other spouse]"; (b) the date order matters — Vicente died first (26 Nov 1984), Beatriz nine months later (12 Aug 1985); where Beatriz actually lived in those nine months decides her venue, and the Manila house was the family's Manila home.** Two clarifications of "residence": a stay in a Manila hospital or a child's Manila home for cancer treatment is not a change of residence; and a couple's residence is presumed common, so a genuine split is the exception and needs the certificates or a witness to establish it. If it turns out that *both* resided in Manila, the case re-plates to RTC Manila and the same §2 logic runs the other way.

**Layer 3 — the "court first taking cognizance" point.** No estate proceeding for either parent is pending anywhere; CC 5625/5626 was a Rule 69 partition among the heirs, terminated in 1992. It therefore neither bars a new special proceeding nor fixes it in Branch 41. Counsel may still ask that the new case be assigned to Branch 41 as a related case; that is a raffle matter, not venue.
