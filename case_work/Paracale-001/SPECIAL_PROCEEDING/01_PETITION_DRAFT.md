6.1. Judicial settlement, rather than the summary remedies of Rule 74, is required because: (a) the heirs are not all in agreement and one branch's succession is contested (¶ 2.7); (b) heirs reside abroad (Brazil, the United States, Canada) or are unlocated (the Heirs of Melvyn); (c) the estates hold rights of action that must be pursued by a party with authority to sue for the estate (¶¶ 4.5, 4.6, 4.8); (d) the estates have unliquidated tax obligations (¶ 9.3); and (e) the 1992 partition must be completed of record.

Individual co-owners have preserved what they could — the Manila judgment in ¶ 4.6 was pursued by four of them — but a co-owner's acts of preservation cannot substitute for administration: no co-owner may take possession of the whole for the estate, collect and account for its fruits, register the partition awards, liquidate the conjugal partnership, or bind the absent and the contesting heirs.

### VIII. GROUNDS FOR THE IMMEDIATE APPOINTMENT OF A SPECIAL ADMINISTRATOR

8.1. Under **Section 1, Rule 80**, "[w]hen there is delay in granting letters testamentary or of administration by any cause … the court may appoint a special administrator to take possession and charge of the estate of the deceased until the questions causing the delay are decided and executors or administrators appointed." Delay is inherent here: letters cannot issue until the petition is published for three successive weeks and heard on notice to some forty persons on three continents (Section 3, Rule 79), and opposition from the persons in ¶ 2.7 is expected. The appointment of a special administrator lies in the sound discretion of the court and its principal object is the **preservation of the estate** until it can pass into the hands of a person fully authorized to administer it [*De Guzman v. Guadiz*, G.R. No. L-48585, 3 Mar 1980; *Heirs of Castillo v. Lacuata-Gabriel*, G.R. No. 162934, 11 Nov 2005; *Tan v. Gedorio, Jr.*, G.R. No. 166520, 14 Mar 2008; *Ocampo v. Ocampo*, G.R. No. 187879, 5 Jul 2010 — ⟦verify each citation and pull the exact quotations⟧]. The order of preference in Section 6, Rule 78 is a guide but does not bind the court in choosing a special administrator [*Tan v. Gedorio*, supra ⟦verify⟧].
