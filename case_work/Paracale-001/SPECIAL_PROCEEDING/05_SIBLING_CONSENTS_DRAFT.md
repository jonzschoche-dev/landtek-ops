## WHY THESE EXIST, AND WHAT THEY MUST NOT BE

**The gap they close (packet gate G6).** Allan holds special powers of attorney from Jesus, Marilou and Herbert dated 2024 and 2025 [V docs 647/648/649/657]. Those prove the siblings trust him with the family's property. They are **not** consents to *this* petition, and they say nothing about who should administer the estates. Section 6, Rule 78 gives the appointment to the next of kin "or to such person as they may request," so the Court will look for the siblings' own, current word.

**The four things each form does.**
1. **Waives** that sibling's own preferential right to be appointed, in favour of Allan.
2. **Nominates** Allan for both offices — Special Administrator now, Regular Administrator on letters — so a second round of consents is not needed later.
3. **Attests, from the sibling's own knowledge, to the estate's present jeopardy** — Ace and Elena still holding the Vito Cruz building and taking its rents after the estate won the title back, the unapproved mining on Casper's land and the proceeds never shared, and the occupation of the Paracale and Jose Panganiban land. ⟦This is the addition of 2026-09-10 and it matters more than the rest. Rule 80 turns on dissipation, and until now the whole urgency case rested on Allan alone, operator-attested. These three siblings were the **plaintiffs in Civil Case No. 13-131220** — the Registry's annotation on the restored title names them — so their knowledge of Ace and Elena is first-hand, not hearsay. Jesus is on the ground at Batobalani where the mining is. Three sworn corroborations from heirs with no stake in flattering Allan is a different order of proof from one man's affidavit.⟧
4. **Records that the sibling was told about Civil Case No. 98-88750 before signing.** This is the paragraph that does the real work. The fitness defence in the petition (¶ 7.4) rests on the heirs' *informed* confidence in Allan. Without the disclosure clause, an oppositor says the consents were obtained by keeping the siblings in the dark, and the strongest asset in the petition turns into a liability. With it, the consents survive cross-examination.

**What they must never do — do not let this drift.**
- ⟦A consent to *administration* is not a renunciation of *inheritance*. Paragraph 6 of each form says so expressly and must stay. A signed instrument that reads as a repudiation of a share (Civil Code arts. 1041 ff.) would be a catastrophe: repudiation is irrevocable, retroactive, and carries tax consequences. If counsel edits these forms, paragraph 6 is the one that cannot be trimmed for brevity.⟧
- ⟦These are sworn statements of the signatory's own knowledge, not of ours. Every reason given in paragraph 5 is something the sibling personally knows — that they gave Allan a power of attorney, that he reported to them, that they still trust him. Nothing in these forms asks a sibling to swear to the contents of court records they have not read.⟧
- ⟦**Do not paper over a refusal.** Each sibling must actually be told what paragraph 5 says before signing. If any of them hesitates once they hear it, that hesitation is information counsel needs immediately — it bears on whether Allan is the right nominee at all, and the fallback (Jesus as co-administrator, dossier §7) exists for exactly that case. Do not obtain a signature by skipping the paragraph.⟧

**No court caption.** These are notarised instruments executed *before* the petition is filed, when there is no docket number yet. They are annexed to the petition, not filed on their own. ⟦Counsel may add a caption once a docket number exists, if their practice prefers it.⟧
