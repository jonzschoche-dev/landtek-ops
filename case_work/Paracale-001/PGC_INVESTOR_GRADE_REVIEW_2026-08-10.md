## A. The three deal-killers (fix first)

### 1. NI 43-101 — the governing standard for everything technical
Every scientific or technical statement a Canadian public issuer makes must comply with National Instrument 43-101. Diamcor's deck — even a mere conference PowerPoint — names its Qualified Person on page 2 ("The Qualified Person… is Mr. James P. Hawkins (B.Sc., P.Geo.)"). The PGC report has no QP.

- **Engage a Qualified Person.** A QP must belong to a recognized professional association (P.Geo./P.Eng. in Canada, or AusIMM, AIPG, etc.). A Philippine PRC geologist license alone does not qualify. Suggestion: have Manny Acena co-author with (or be peer-reviewed and signed off by) a QP, or check whether he is eligible to join a recognized association (AusIMM admits internationally).
- **The Benguet Corporation numbers are "historical estimates" under s.2.4** (Virginia 55,120 t @ 1.27 g/t; Manila 45,080 t @ 5.8 g/t; Vallejo 57,114 t @ 5.7 g/t). They may be disclosed, but only with the mandatory caveat block: source and date; relevance and reliability; key assumptions; whether the categories are CIM-consistent; the work required to verify/upgrade them; plus the two required statements — *"a qualified person has not done sufficient work to classify the historical estimate as a current mineral resource"* and *"the issuer is not treating the historical estimate as a current mineral resource."* The report already concedes "computation details were not available for verification" — turn that concession into the formal caveat block.
- **Delete the "initial indicated mineral resource" wording.** "Indicated" is a defined CIM category; applying it to an unverified 1980s-era estimate is precisely what OSC/BCSC mining-disclosure reviews flag.
- **Delete the "Estd Recoverable Au" column and the 658,024 g / 21,156 oz total.** Deriving recoverable metal from unverified historical estimates presents them as current and economic — misleading disclosure. The tonnage/grade rows can stay (with caveats); the recoverable-gold arithmetic cannot.
- **Ban the word "ore"** ("45,080 tonnes of ore", "Au ore"). "Ore" implies demonstrated economic viability (a mineral reserve). Use "mineralized material."
- **Phase I economics need a compliant wrapper.** Publishing production and revenue projections (₱37.6M/yr) with no supporting technical report is off-limits for an issuer. Two clean routes: (a) frame Phase I as **bulk sampling / test processing** — exactly Diamcor's "trial mining" framing, which lets them report carats and revenue without declared reserves; or (b) better, do the small amount of work that makes the tailings a *real* maiden resource — see B.1.

### 2. Tenure: stay out of the legal particulars — which means deleting the legal claims the draft currently makes
Direction from Jonathan (2026-08-10): the report should not get into legal particulars one way or the other; the veins will be explored with NIBDC's consent or otherwise. The good news is that this direction and Canadian-disclosure hygiene point the same way — the problem is that the **current draft volunteers legal particulars**, and wrong ones, which invites exactly the scrutiny we want to avoid:

- **Delete the FTAA highlight** ("may be 100% foreign-controlled") — it asserts a legal pathway (FTAA carries a US$50M minimum investment commitment) that doesn't match this project, and any investor's counsel will test it.
- **Delete the "four ways to acquire mineral tenements" primer and the taxation-regime list** — a legal treatise the report doesn't need, partly outdated, and every line is an invitation to cross-examine.
- **Delete the Deed of Assignment claim as currently worded** — asserting a specific instrument with specific legal effect *is* a legal particular, and an assignment of a pending application is only effective with MGB approval, so the claim as written over-promises.
- **Replace all of the above with one factual, claim-free paragraph:** PGC operates in the district through arrangements with the mineral-rights applicant and with local small-scale miners' associations; exploration access to the vein systems is secured through these relationships; documentation is available to qualified investors under NDA in the data room. Facts on the record (the corpus holds an NIBDC Affidavit of Consent supporting the Capacuan Minahang Bayan application) — no legal conclusions in either direction.
- **One expectation to keep set internally:** this light-touch treatment works for the current document and for private/exempt-market conversations. At actual listing or prospectus stage, Canadian regulators and the sponsor will require tenure substantiation and legal opinions — that's a later workstream, not something this report needs to argue now.
- Still worth a paragraph on the operating side (facts, not law): the processing layer's permits (Section C) and the gold-selling channel — **RA 11256** makes gold sales to Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas tax-exempt for small-scale miners/traders, a genuine selling point.

## B. What would most increase investor value (highest-leverage suggestions)

1. **Make the tailings a compliant maiden resource — the fastest 43-101 win available.** Tailings are flat, soft, and trivially sampled (auger/pit grid), so a few weeks of fieldwork + assays + a QP site visit yields a maiden NI 43-101 tailings resource and a simple PEA. That single step converts the document from promoter-grade to bankable, and a listing will require a 43-101 technical report on the qualifying property anyway.
2. **Commission metallurgical test work before publishing 80% recovery.** These are *already-cyanided* CIL tails — the remaining gold is disproportionately locked/refractory, and retreatment recoveries are typically far below fresh-ore recoveries. The 80% assumption is the single biggest technical risk in Phase I. Bottle-roll tests on representative composites (the report's own Suggestion #1 — multi-spot sampling — is the right instinct; extend it to metallurgy). Same for reagent costs: ₱25,000/mo of cyanide for 280 t/mo of leaching looks understated (typical NaCN consumption 0.5–2 kg/t → 140–560 kg/mo), and the report admits reagent prices came from small-scale-miner hearsay. Get plant-scale quotes.
3. **Complete the operating-cost model.** The ₱2.88M/mo "Gross Revenue Before Tax" is revenue minus labor and reagents only. Missing lines: **cost of acquiring the tailings** (the report itself lists "tails buying" as an activity), hauling, power/fuel, maintenance and spares, assay/refining charges, security, environmental compliance and tailings-residue disposal, the 4% excise tax on gold, surface-owner royalties, and permit fees. Add a sensitivity table (grade × recovery × gold price), payback, and a simple NPV/IRR. Also: a CIL plant staffed with encoders and a bookkeeper but **no metallurgist/plant chemist or cyanide-safety officer** will draw ESG flags — add the roles and a statement of alignment with the International Cyanide Management Code.
4. **Add the pages every Canadian deck has and this report lacks** (copy Diamcor's architecture directly):
   - Full **forward-looking-statements cautionary block** + "this does not constitute an offer of securities" (Diamcor p.2 is a usable template; PGC's current half-paragraph "DISSCLAIMER" is not sufficient — and is misspelled).
   - **Management & board bios with credentials** — the report names no officers or directors at all. Who runs PGC? Add Manny Acena's credentials, PRC license number, and independence/relationship to the company; add the Inocalla family's role and shareholding.
   - **Share structure & the raise:** issued/outstanding, fully diluted, insider %, how much is being raised, at what terms, and **use of proceeds** tied to milestones. An investment document that never says how much it's raising isn't one.
   - **Milestones timeline:** permits → plant commissioning → bulk processing revenue → tailings resource → drill program on Manila/Vallejo/Virginia → maiden vein resource.
   - **Risk factors section:** tenure/assignment approval, permitting, foreign-ownership structure, small-scale-miner and community relations, insurgency (the report discloses it — good instinct; move it here), cyanide/environmental, gold price, FX, single-asset concentration.
   - **The missing exhibits:** the text cites Figure 1, Figure 2, Attachment I (Benguet data) and Attachment II (Bulawan geo-assay map) — none are in the file. Add tenement map, geology/vein plan, plant photos/schematic (redraw the ASCII flow-sheet as a proper figure, credited to Engr. Roque Malipoy), and a data-room index.
5. **Cut and correct the country primer.** Sections II–III read as a dated country study and contain errors that torch credibility with anyone who checks: Marcos Jr. was elected **May 2022** (not 2024); the Philippines has **7,641 islands** (not 1,700) and **17 regions** (not 15); 2020 census population was **~109M** (not ~100M); Spanish is not "widely understood"; annual rainfall of "183.2 mm" is off by roughly 20× for a Type II climate (that's a monthly figure); and above all, **the Vancouver Stock Exchange has not existed since 1999** — the target is the **TSX Venture Exchange** (or CSE). Compress the whole thing to a half-page snapshot (Diamcor does location in one slide) and re-verify the fiscal-incentives section — the CREATE Act reworked the EO 226 incentive regime, so the 1987-era list needs updating by Philippine tax counsel.
6. **Lead with the district story — it's the best material in the report.** A gold district mined since the 12th century; Benguet's Sta. Rosa operation milling 670 tpd at ~7 g/t (≈100 kg gold/month, 1983–92); UPGMC at 500 tpd and 10 g/t; UNDP-confirmed prospectivity. Restructure so the executive summary opens with this plus the two-phase plan, the way Diamcor opens with "co-located with De Beers' $4.0B Venetia mine." Presently the strongest facts are buried under the geography primer. Third-party market context (the Bain-report role) should come from the **World Gold Council / LBMA**, cited with dates, rather than an unsourced "current gold price of PhP7,000 per gram."
7. **Version-control and date the document.** The file carries no author block, no date, no version. Add: "Prepared by M. Acena, [credentials], [date], Version N — supersedes [date]" — both for investors and because successive undated drafts are how numbers like 25,000 t / 200,000 t / 20,000 t end up coexisting.
8. **Full professional edit.** DISSCLAIMER, REGINAL GEOLOGY, DIADRAN, "westt", "Phase =2", "PHYSIGRAPHY", and the company's own acronym flipping between PGC and PCG throughout. One unit convention (g/t, not gpt/ppm mixed), one currency convention (₱ with US$ equivalents).

## D. Realistic path to a Canadian listing (recommendation)

A direct TSX-V listing today would fail: it requires a sponsor, a compliant NI 43-101 technical report on the qualifying property, audited financials, and defensible tenure. The sequence that actually works:

1. **Now (weeks, low cost):** fix the document per Section A; design and diagram the ownership structure with Philippine + BC counsel; confirm the APSA assignment's MGB status.
2. **Next (1–3 months, modest cost):** tailings survey + auger sampling + met test work + QP site visit → maiden NI 43-101 tailings resource + simple economics; secure the Mineral Processing Permit and ECC.
3. **Then:** raise privately in the exempt market (accredited investors) on the strengthened document to fund the plant and a first drill program on the veins.
4. **List** via TSX-V (IPO, or CPC/RTO — often faster for this size; CSE is the lighter-touch alternative) once there is a compliant resource and, ideally, tailings cash flow — at that point the story is Diamcor-shaped: "cash-flowing bulk processing + defined growth targets," which is exactly the profile that deck sells.
