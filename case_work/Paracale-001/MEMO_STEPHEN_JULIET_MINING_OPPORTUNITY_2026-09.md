## 1. The picture in one paragraph

We are building one business in three waves. **Wave 1** starts now inside the already-declared SMBC Minahang Bayan at Casalugan, around the already-built ₱29-million mercury-free plant that Stephen operates: activate the plant with the ₱5 million committed in principle from our side (a private investor, through us), add the LAB X gravity circuit, run a toll station and ore-supply agreements with SMBC's contract holders, and set up the gold-selling channel. **Wave 2** is Allan's new plant, **AVI Gold Processing**, on Allan's own titled lot at Brgy. Santa Rosa Sur, Jose Panganiban, with Allan's own Minahang Bayan at the same site — as Allan describes it today; the site and its filings are being confirmed. **Wave 3** is the exploration upside on NIBDC's 2,360-hectare tenement (EXPA-000250-V, endorsed for Exploration Permit issuance), which we propose to enter through an earn-in — and that is the asset a Canadian listing would be built on. Philippine law fixes the shape: **Filipinos hold the mining contracts and at least 60% of any mine once it converts to an MPSA; a foreign company may own a stand-alone processing plant (sited outside a Minahang Bayan processing zone), the financing, the ore trading and the exploration-stage tenement outright.** So the vehicle is a Filipino extraction layer (Allan) plus a foreign-eligible processing / finance / exploration layer — and that second layer is **Paracale Gold Company, the Canadian company already formed, in from Wave 1**, so that Canadian capital is already at the table before NIBDC is approached (§4A). Waves 1 and 2 do not depend on NIBDC: as we read the 2022 small-scale mining rules (text being obtained), a Minahang Bayan may be declared over an area under a pending Exploration Permit application, with the applicant notified.

### 2.2 LAB X — the gravity circuit at the MFPS
One full-size Wilfley/6-S-class shaking table, locally fabricated, on a rigid concrete pad inside or beside the MFPS, targeting 10 tonnes a day of gravity feed; table 2 is decided from measured recovery. Stephen is head engineer and designer; Allan supplies the crew. Planning range ₱177–306k for the table, mid-point ₱190–220k, all unquoted; pad, water, feed preparation and electrical excluded. The one gate before steel is cut is a gravity-recoverable-gold test on a 20–50 kg representative ore sample — Paracale gold is often sulfide-locked, and the test sets stroke, slope and riffle geometry. We also need SMBC's written consent and confirmation that added tables sit inside the MFPS permits.

### 2.3 Toll station and ore-supply agreements with SMBC contract holders
After the upgrade, equipment is allocated to a small-batch toll station, and we sign ore-supply / toll-processing agreements with individual SMBC contract holders, with production advances repaid in ore where useful. This is the only lawful way outside money participates in contract-holder output — offtake, advances, equipment and processing — and it works today because the SMBC area is already declared.

### 2.4 The gold-selling channel
All small-scale gold must be sold to the Bangko Sentral ng Pilipinas (RA 7076 §17). Sales to the BSP by registered small-scale miners and by BSP-accredited traders are exempt from income and excise tax (RA 11256). A BSP-accredited trader is therefore where doré becomes cash at the best price with no tax leakage, and it is a clean, auditable revenue line for the listing story. Allan's Filipino company is the natural first trader; whether a foreign-owned processing company can also be accredited is a question for counsel and the BSP.

### 2.5 Gracesen — retired
Allan's earlier plant at Capacuan (Gracesen) is obsolete and is not part of this plan. Its environmental record follows Allan as proponent, so the file is formally closed with EMB before the AVI Gold Processing application is lodged; nothing else carries forward.

## 3. Wave 2 — AVI Gold Processing and Allan's Minahang Bayan, Jose Panganiban

Allan's new plant, **AVI Gold Processing**, is to be built on Allan's own titled lot at Brgy. Santa Rosa Sur, Jose Panganiban, with Allan's Minahang Bayan at the same site — as Allan describes it today; the site, its title particulars and filing status are being confirmed. Stephen's assessment is that ore supply on the site is not the constraint — the plant sets the pace; any statement of tonnes or grade waits for a Qualified Person. Two decisions shape everything downstream:

- **Siting relative to the Minahang Bayan processing zone.** A mill inside the zone is licensed by the Provincial Mining Regulatory Board under the 2022 small-scale mining rules (DAO 2022-03 — we are obtaining the full text; the licensing route inside a processing zone is counsel question #1, not settled) and, conservatively, should be Filipino-owned. A stand-alone plant outside the zone is permitted by DENR as a Mineral Processing Permit holder — and a foreign-owned corporation is expressly eligible for that permit (RA 7942 §3). The second option is the cleanest asset to put under the future public company. We choose the site with counsel's answer in hand, before an ECC application fixes the location.
- **Circuit.** Gravity-only and mercury-free (planetGOLD-aligned) avoids the cyanide, hazardous-waste and air-permit stack; a cyanide circuit brings it back. Stephen's call as engineer, informed by the ore.

Permit stack to build for the new site: ECC (site-specific; a new application), the processing licence or permit as above, wastewater discharge permit, cyanide and hazardous-waste registrations if applicable, air permit-to-operate, municipal and barangay permits, and the land arrangement — if the plant company is foreign-owned it leases the lot from Allan (foreign investors may lease private land for up to 99 years under the 2025 amendment to the Investors' Lease Act); if Allan-owned, the lot is his own surface. The Minahang Bayan petition goes to the PMRB with the occupation-history evidence, registered-miner roll, Sanggunian endorsements and the NCIP certificate; the pending Capacuan petition is reconciled into it.

**One interaction to sequence, not race:** a Minahang Bayan declared inside a pending Exploration Permit area closes that ground to the permit unless the small-scale miners consent, with any royalty going to a community trust fund (RA 7942 §19(e)). Sta. Rosa Norte and Sur lie inside the EXPA-000250-V barangay list, so the Wave-2 siting and the Wave-3 tenement have to be planned together.

## 4A. Order of entry — the Canadians come in before NIBDC

The sequence is deliberate: **Canadian capital enters at Wave 1, NIBDC is approached only afterwards.** Concretely:

1. **Now — Paracale Gold Company (PGC), the Canadian company already formed, is the vehicle.** Juliet's group comes in at PGC level; PGC forms and owns the Philippine ProcessCo and FinanceCo (§5). The Wave-1 financing is written so it can be assigned to FinanceCo, and PGC's first capital goes into Wave 1 (plant activation, LAB X, toll station, trader) and Wave 2 (AVI Gold Processing). Housekeeping before money moves: PGC's jurisdiction and good standing, current shareholder register and officers, and a minute book that will stand up to a listing review.
2. **Wave 2 under the same vehicle.** If AVI Gold Processing is sited outside the Minahang Bayan processing zone, ProcessCo holds it under a Mineral Processing Permit and leases the lot from Allan; if inside the zone, Allan's Philippine company holds the mill and PGC participates by equipment, financing and tolling.
3. **Only then, NIBDC.** With a running plant, audited books and a working cooperative relationship, PGC — not the individuals — proposes the earn-in to NIBDC's board. The listing of PGC follows at Exploration Permit stage.

This order does three things: it gives the Canadian partners a real operating position before any tenement negotiation; it keeps the NIBDC approach a corporate proposal from a funded vehicle rather than a personal one; and it means nothing in Waves 1–2 waits on a third party's board.

## 4. Wave 3 — NIBDC and the exploration upside (entered after Waves 1–2 are running)

NIBDC's EXPA-000250-V (2,360.04 ha across Paracale, Jose Panganiban and Labo) was endorsed by MGB Region V for Exploration Permit issuance in July 2025; Central Office action since then is the one open question on the file. When we enter, Stephen's **Big Brother / Little Brother** framing is the coexistence chapter: a surveyed carve-out, an arm's-length coexistence MOA, ore offtake through our plants, and historical-workings data flowing to the resource model. The carve-out terms are sequence-dependent: once an Exploration Permit issues, a carve-out needs the holder's consent and is capped by the small-scale mining rules (25% of the area above 500 hectares, 20-hectare minimum, with a statutory royalty to the holder). Our proposed instrument is an **earn-in** by PGC: it funds the exploration program in exchange for a majority interest in a project company holding the permit; the same funding commitment serves as financial-capability evidence with MGB. No earn-in has been proposed to NIBDC yet. Any earn-in or assignment of the permit needs NIBDC board approval, a supermajority shareholder vote, and MGB approval of the transfer. Sequencing matters: Wave 3 is entered after Waves 1–2 are running.

## 5. The structure under Philippine law

| Rule | What it means for us |
|---|---|
| Exploration, development and utilization of natural resources: at most 40% foreign (Constitution Art. XII §2; 13th Negative List, EO 113 s.2026) | Once a tenement converts to an MPSA, the public company holds at most 40% of the holder. Exception: an FTAA with the President allows 100% foreign ownership for large-scale projects with a firm commitment of at least US$50 million |
| Small-scale mining: 0% foreign; contracts to registered Filipino miners or cooperatives; two-year renewable terms; not assignable or transferable (RA 7076) | The public company never owns a contract. Its participation is offtake, advances, equipment and processing — Waves 1 and 2 as designed |
| A foreign-owned corporation is a "qualified person" for an Exploration Permit, an FTAA and a Mineral Processing Permit (RA 7942 §3) | The public company can hold the exploration-stage tenement and a stand-alone plant outright |
| Anti-Dummy Law (CA 108); the Supreme Court's grandfather rule in *Narra Nickel* | Allan's 60% must be genuinely his — his own capital or contributed assets, dividends to him — and not financed by the public company. Clean papers at arm's length are what keep the structure standing |
| Gold to the BSP (RA 7076 §17); tax-exempt BSP sales (RA 11256) | The trader is the revenue point |
| A more-than-40%-foreign Philippine company serving the domestic market needs at least US$200,000 paid-in capital, or US$100,000 where it employs at least 15 Filipinos or uses advanced technology (Foreign Investments Act as amended) | Budget per subsidiary |
| Canada–Philippines tax convention: dividends 15% where the Canadian company holds at least 10% of the votes (25% otherwise), interest 15%, royalties 25% or the lowest rate the Philippines grants a third country | Active-business dividends reach the public company at 15% Philippine withholding and are generally exempt surplus in Canada; interest and royalties are taxable in Canada — Canadian tax counsel to confirm |
| Large-scale metallic mining fiscal regime (RA 12253, operative February 2026): margin-based royalty 1–5% outside mineral reservations, windfall profits tax above a 30% margin, project ring-fencing | Applies to Wave 3 at production, not to Waves 1–2 |

## 6. The Canadian path — for Juliet

**What the exchange actually tests (TSX Venture Policy 2.1, as at 31 March 2026, Tier 2 Mining, applying the Policy 1.1 definition of Significant Interest):** the issuer must hold a *Significant Interest* — at least 50% — in a Qualifying Property, or the right to earn one, and the Exchange expects control and direction over operations; at least C$100,000 of approved expenditures on the property in the prior 36 months (waivable where the Phase 1, or Phase 1+2 non-contingent, program exceeds C$400,000, or the property is a Tier 1 Property); a current NI 43-101 technical report recommending an initial-phase program of at least C$200,000; working capital for 12 months plus C$100,000 unallocated; a public float of 500,000 shares and 200 public board-lot holders. No net-tangible-asset test for Mining.

**The consequence that shapes the whole design:** a 40% MPSA stake does not meet the 50% test on its face. The compliant route is to list at **Exploration Permit stage**, where a foreign-owned company may hold the tenement and PGC's proposed earn-in would reach at least 50% of the project company, and to restructure to 40% plus processing and ore-purchase at MPSA conversion (or to an FTAA if the scale justifies it). The CSE, which has no Significant-Interest test, is the fallback.

**Route options:** a Capital Pool Company (Policy 2.4, rewritten 2021 — up to C$10M may now be raised, and the 24-month qualifying-transaction deadline is gone), a direct IPO by prospectus, or a reverse takeover of an existing shell. The sponsor requirement has been eliminated. Board and insiders (Policy 3.1): at least three directors, two independent; every director, officer, promoter and insider files a Personal Information Form and passes security screening — we will run our own background checks on every principal early and decide board composition well before filing. Standard package: audited IFRS financial statements (two to three years or since inception — the Philippine subsidiaries keep audit-grade books from day one), an independent Qualified Person's NI 43-101 report (the Benguet-era historical estimates on the Paracale veins can be disclosed only as historical estimates under s.2.4, never as resources), a Philippine title opinion on the permit, and escrow of principals' shares (36 months for Tier 2). US-resident investors will need the PFIC and Reg S / Rule 144A points handled.

**What we would ask of Juliet**
1. Your view on venue and route for PGC — TSXV via CPC versus direct IPO versus CSE — for a company whose qualifying property would be an exploration-stage permit in the Philippines with near-term processing cash flow.
2. Sourcing: a suitable CPC or clean shell, a short list of Qualified Persons with Philippine epithermal-gold experience, and introductions to BC securities counsel and an audit firm that works on Philippine subsidiaries.
3. An indicative timeline and budget from today to listing, with the pre-listing financing steps.
4. A read on the 50% Significant-Interest point against the proposed earn-in, and on the CSE alternative.
