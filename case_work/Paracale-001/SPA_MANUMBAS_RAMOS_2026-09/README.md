## 2. The authority problem — read this before signing anything

An SPA transfers only what the principal actually holds.

- **AVI lane — clean.** AVI Gold Processing Plant is Allan's registered business name, on Allan's own titled
  land. He is the applicant of record for the MPP/MPL, the ECC, the barangay and mayor's permits. He can appoint
  anyone he likes to carry those papers. **Instrument A is ready to sign as drafted.**
- **CSSMA lane — blocked as an SPA.** The applicant before PMRB/MGB is the *association*, not Allan. An
  association acts through its board and its officers, evidenced by a resolution and a secretary's certificate.
  A personal SPA from a non-officer confers nothing, and MGB-V — which has already once refused to treat a
  private MOA as valid consent (letter MTSS-2019-173, 14 Jun 2019) — is the wrong office to test that on.
  **Instrument B is the correct paper**, and it does not need Allan at all. Note also that Manumbas was himself
  the Acting President in 2021: if he still holds that office, he needs no grant from anyone, only proof of
  incumbency.
- **If Allan really is CSSMA's incumbent President** (the MPP memo asserts it; no document supports it),
  Instrument C is the form he would use — but only with Instrument B or an equivalent resolution attached.
  **Do not sign C on the strength of the assertion alone.**

## 6. Light route (Jonathan, 2026-09-17): plain signed authorization letter, no notarization

**Built:** `AUTHORIZATION_LETTER_MANUMBAS_RAMOS_2026-09.docx` / `.pdf` — one page, Allan's signature and
contact only, both lanes in one letter, specimen-signature lines for the two representatives. Instruction:
*light, no notarization — unless the law says otherwise.*

**Where the law does not require notarization (the ordinary case here).** An agency for administrative
errands — lodging applications, signing the receiving logbook, paying an assessed fee, collecting a certified
copy or a released permit — is an ordinary agency. The Civil Code requires no particular form for it
(Art. 1869: agency may be express or implied, oral or written), and nothing in RA 7942, RA 7076, their
implementing DAOs, or the MGB Citizen's Charter on record conditions a representative's standing on a
notarized instrument. A plain signed letter with IDs attached is legally sufficient for this scope.

**Where a notarized (or public) instrument IS required — keep Instrument A on the shelf for these.**

| Situation | Why |
|---|---|
| Any act of strict dominion over OCT P-1616 or any other property — sale, mortgage, lease over 1 year, donation, encumbrance, or binding Allan to a contract | Civil Code Art. 1878 requires a **special power** for these; Art. 1358 requires a **public instrument** for acts affecting real rights over immovables. *Expressly withheld in both drafts, so this should not arise.* |
| Anything presented to the **Registry of Deeds** for registration or annotation | PD 1529 registration practice: instruments must be notarized. *Requesting a certified true copy is not registration and does not need one.* |
| **BIR** transactions done by a representative (registration, returns, CAR-type matters) | BIR issuances routinely require a notarized SPA; assume notarized unless the RDO says otherwise |
| Affidavits, sworn applications, sworn undertakings, the DTI/MGB sworn forms | The oath itself is the notarial act — **Allan signs these personally**, no representative can |
| The **CSSMA** lane, in practice | Not a statute, but PMRB/MGB will look for the association's Secretary's Certificate, which is customarily notarized. The plain letter gets the runners in the door; it does not make Allan an officer |

**Practical read.** Send the light letter now. If a specific window refuses it — and a window can set its own
counter rule even where the law does not — notarize Instrument A for that office and carry on. Nothing is lost:
the pack is already drafted.
