**Two findings worth knowing before you go:**
1. **No Mayor's Permit needed to register with BIR.** Page 4 of this form lists the complete documentary
   requirements for a self-employed individual: a government-issued ID showing name, address and birthdate;
   the DTI Certificate (if with a business name); and either purchased BIR Printed Invoices or a final sample
   of your own. That is all. The ₱500 annual registration fee is gone (RA 11976, Ease of Paying Taxes) — the
   only fee is **₱30 loose documentary stamp** for the Certificate of Registration, plus the cost of the
   invoice booklets.
2. **If a representative files it, BIR wants a notarised SPA, not a letter** — page 4, Additional Documents:
   *"Special Power of Attorney (SPA) executed by the taxpayer-applicant indicating specific transaction/s
   [1 original for first time submission]"*, plus a photocopy of both IDs each bearing an original wet
   specimen signature. This is exactly the carve-out flagged in `../SPA_MANUMBAS_RAMOS_2026-09/README.md` §6.
   The light authorization letter will not clear this counter.

## Decisions

**Q5 — the 8% option (item 23). Recommendation: No.**
The 8% is charged on **gross sales** above ₱250,000, in place of both the graduated income tax and the
percentage tax. It is a good deal for a service earner with almost no costs. A gold processing plant is the
opposite case: it buys ore, pays power, labour and consumables, and its margin is a slice of a large gross
number. Under the graduated rates you deduct those costs first and are taxed on what is left; under 8% you are
taxed on the whole of what comes in. With ore purchases in the flow, graduated-with-deductions almost
certainly wins — and while the plant is pre-operational, 8% of nothing is the same either way. The election is
also not permanent: it can be made each year in the first-quarter return, so choosing "No" now costs nothing.
**Tick No unless Allan's accountant says otherwise.**

**Q6 — size classification (item 24).** Micro is under ₱3M expected annual gross sales. ₱3M is also the VAT
threshold: cross it and VAT registration becomes mandatory and the 8% option disappears. A 10-tonne-per-day
plant could pass ₱3M quickly once it actually runs, but it is pre-operational now and the classification can be
updated. **Micro** unless Allan expects real sales inside the first year.

**⚠ Address mismatch between the ID and the form — deal with this before going.**
Allan's driver's licence prints his address as **2531 G. DEL PILAR ST., SINGALONG, MALATE, MANILA, NCR FIRST
DISTRICT** — the family apartment building. The form declares **Purok 4, Capacuan, Paracale** as residence
(item 16) and **Santa Rosa Sur, Jose Panganiban** as business address (item 17). Page 4 of the form warns that
IDs *"should be readable, untampered and contain**s** consistent information with the documents submitted upon
application"*, so a Manila ID against a Camarines Norte residence is exactly the thing a reviewer stops on —
and at worst an officer tries to route the registration to a Manila district.

Two clean ways through, and the first is better:
- **Keep Capacuan in item 16 and carry a Barangay Certificate of Residency from Barangay Capacuan.** It is a
  same-day, low-cost document, it is the truth, and it keeps the form consistent with the Bombita affidavit,
  the SPA and the whole Paracale permit stack, all of which recite Capacuan. This is the recommendation.
- **Or declare the Manila address in item 16**, matching the licence exactly. Item 17 still governs the RDO
  for a business registration, so the district stays Camarines Norte either way — but the form would then
  disagree with the affidavits, which is the same problem moved somewhere worse.
Either way item 17 does not change: the business is at the plant site.
