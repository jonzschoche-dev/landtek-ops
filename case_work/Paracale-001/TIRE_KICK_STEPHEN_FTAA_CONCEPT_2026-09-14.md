# TIRE-KICK — Stephen's "Minahang Bayan → FTAA" concept paper (Casalugan)
*LandTek internal assessment, 2026-09-14. INTERNAL-HELD / UNDER NDA. For Jonathan. Not legal advice; the points marked [PV] need Philippine mining counsel. Source: `received/STEPHEN_CONCEPT_PAPER_MB_TO_FTAA_CASALUGAN_2026-09.md`.*

**One sentence:** The paper is technically literate and honestly caveated, but it proposes the wrong vehicle for this ground — an FTAA is a US$50-million, presidentially-approved, large-scale contract that requires a defined contract area and the small-scale miners' consent to be closed out of it, none of which Casalugan has or wants — and, as written, it would move the extraction layer to a 100%-foreign contractor, which is the opposite of the model in the two memos (Filipino 60/40 operating company, Canadians at 40%, community contracts untouched). Keep its technical pillars; park its vehicle for Wave 3 at genuine scale; and read it as a signal of where Stephen wants to sit.

---

## 1. What the paper gets right
- The law it cites is real and correctly stated at the headline level: Art. XII §2; RA 7942; DAO 2010-21 as the FTAA IRR; foreign-owned corporation as a "qualified person" for an FTAA; 81,000 ha / 1,000 meridional blocks onshore; 25 + 25 years; ≥US$50M for development [V, matches our own verification]; the mandatory-document list (EWP, DMPF, FS, ECC, EPEP, FMR/DP, SDMP, NCIP certification) is the standard set.
- It is candid that MB rights, SSM continuity, FPIC and LGU endorsement have to be dealt with, and its own disclaimer is proper.
- The technical pillars — multi-stage gravity ahead of leach, tight cyanidation control, CIL/CIP + electrowinning, closed-circuit water and reagents, cyanide detox to ICMC standard, engineered or dry-stack tailings, wastewater treatment, legacy-mercury protocols, public monitoring — are exactly the design brief the **AVI plant** should be written to, whatever the tenure. That is the reusable part.

## 2. Where the tires go flat

1. **There is no "upgrade" pathway from a Minahang Bayan to an FTAA.** A Minahang Bayan is a declared small-scale area under RA 7076; the contracts in it are personal to registered Filipino miners/cooperatives, non-assignable and non-transferable (RA 7076 §12). An FTAA is a *new* large-scale application over ground that must be open to it. Ground covered by small-scale miners is **closed** to mining applications unless the small-scale miners consent, with a royalty to a community trust fund (RA 7942 §19(e)). So the only mechanics are: (a) the SMBC members consent to the FTAA over their own area and their contracts are surrendered or expire out, or (b) the PMRB/DENR de-declares the Minahang Bayan. Both take the contracts *away* from the community. The paper's "tenure continuity … or surrender/conversion mechanics" (Stage 2) is that step, named softly. [V law; counsel to confirm the de-declaration route under DAO 2022-03 — PV]
2. **No contract area.** An FTAA needs a defined contract area with area clearance. The Casalugan Minahang Bayan is a small declared area (MB areas are tens of hectares; the concept paper never states the size). A US$50M large-scale project cannot sit on that; the paper's "and potentially contiguous community mining areas" is the tell. The contiguous ground in this district is largely under existing applications — NIBDC's EXPA-000250-V (2,360 ha) among them — and an FTAA application would be blocked or protested wherever it overlaps a prior application. [PV: whether Casalugan lies inside or outside the EXPA polygon — the MTES-2025-411 barangay list we hold does not name Casalugan, so probably outside; MGB-V area-status certification answers it]
3. **No resource, no scale.** FTAA is "large-scale" by definition and by investment threshold. Nothing in Casalugan is measured to any code; the paper's Phase 2 "resource definition to PMRC" is the work that would tell us whether large-scale is even plausible — and it belongs on a tenement, not on a Minahang Bayan. Our own memos are strict on this: no tonnes or grade until a Qualified Person says so.
4. **The economics of an FTAA are not "100% foreign equity."** The paper never mentions the **government share**: an FTAA carries a basic share plus an additional government share designed to bring the State's total take to a set proportion of net mining revenue after cost recovery (the 50% net-revenue framework of the IRR and DAO 2007-12), on top of taxes, and now RA 12253's margin-based royalty and windfall tax for large-scale metallic mining. "100% foreign" is 100% of the equity in a contractor that gives up a large slice of net revenue to the State. [PV: how RA 12253 (Feb 2026) now interacts with the FTAA additional-government-share regime — counsel/tax]
5. **The capital and time figures.** The ≥US$50M is right. The "on the order of ₱500 million paid-up prior to registration" is not something we have verified anywhere — cite it to the IRR section or drop it [PV]. The roadmap's 18–30 months to presidential approval is optimistic against the record: FTAAs are few (Didipio, Tampakan, and a handful), they are negotiated at DENR and executed by the President with notice to Congress, and each has been politically contentious for years. Plan on this being the slowest and most visible instrument in Philippine mining.
6. **The donor asset.** The MFPS was built with GEF money through UNIDO/UNEP and the Artisanal Gold Council **to formalise artisanal and small-scale mining**, and turned over to SMBC. Using it as the seed of a large-scale foreign contractor's project cuts against the purpose it was funded for; the turnover instrument almost certainly carries use, transfer and encumbrance conditions. We have never seen it — it is the same "plant title / authority to encumber" gap the gate flagged in the memo. [Obtain the turnover deed/MOA before anything else — PV]
7. **Social licence runs the other way.** planetGOLD's own model, the PMRB, the LGU endorsements and the barangay are all premised on *community* mining. Converting the district's showcase Minahang Bayan into an FTAA in which the cooperative becomes a minority/royalty holder is the story an opposition group writes for us. It also contradicts the goal we just put at the top of the memos.
8. **It moves Allan out of the extraction layer.** In our model Allan's company holds 60% of the operating company and every reserved right; Canadians hold 40% and the processing/finance/exploration layers. In an FTAA the contractor is foreign-owned; SMBC (not Allan's company) is the "community association"; Allan appears nowhere. Whoever the "prospective FTAA applicant" is — Stephen's investors, Johson, someone else — this paper is a proposal for *that* party to hold the ground. Read it as a statement of where Stephen would like the centre of gravity to be.

## 3. What to keep, what to park, what to ask

**Keep (now):** §7's technical pillars as the AVI plant's design brief and as the LAB X programme's target list; the environmental-controls list as the ECC/EPEP backbone for Wave 2; the cyanide-detox and dry-stack tailings points as the argument for gravity-first, leach-later at Jose Panganiban; the "public monitoring and reporting" commitment as part of the poster-child story.

**Park (Wave 3):** the FTAA as *one* end-state for the NIBDC tenement if a Qualified Person's resource ever justifies a ≥US$50M commitment — which is exactly how the memos already carry it ("or an FTAA at US$50M scale"). It is not a Wave-1 or Wave-2 instrument, and it is not a Casalugan instrument.

**Reject (politely):** any step that converts, surrenders or de-declares the Casalugan Minahang Bayan or SMBC's members' contracts; any structure in which the community's contracts become a royalty stream to a foreign contractor; anything filed in SMBC's or the LGU's name on the strength of this paper (Stage 1 "consultation convened by SMBC and the local government" is a filing in all but name).

**Ask Stephen (in this order, under the NDA):**
1. Who is the "prospective FTAA applicant" he has in mind, and is he acting for them?
2. The turnover deed/MOA for the MFPS — what it allows SMBC to do with the plant, and whether it may be contributed, encumbered or expanded by a third party.
3. The Casalugan Minahang Bayan's declared area (hectares, PMRB resolution number) and whether it sits inside or outside EXPA-000250-V.
4. Whether he has raised any of this with SMBC's board, the PMRB, or planetGOLD/AGC — and if so, what they said.
5. Whether he would be content to see §7 adopted as the AVI design brief and the FTAA idea held for the tenement only.
