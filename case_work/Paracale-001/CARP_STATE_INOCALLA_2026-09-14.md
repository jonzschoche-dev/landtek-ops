### Why the tribunals found a tenancy (what their side proved)

Four bodies of evidence carried the Bombitas' claim. We only know them from the draft's rebuttal, not from the decisions themselves.

1. **Tenancy by succession.** Mauro Sr. says his **father was installed as tenant by the Inocalla patriarch** and that he inherited the tenancy. He says he has kept up his obligations "religiously" for more than 30 years. His son Mauro Jr. is described only as a farm helper. The draft admits this is the core of the claim: Mauro Sr. "derived his rights from his father who was allegedly instituted by Inocalla's patriarch as tenant."
2. **The "Patotoo," a joint sworn statement by Liliosa A. Montalban and Rogelio Rigon** (Annex F). They say Mauro is **"tumatao (tenant)" of the Inocallas** on the San Rafael land. **The draft says this was corroborated by Vicente Inocalla Jr., Senen's own brother.** This is the most damaging item, because an Inocalla family member backed the tenancy.
3. **Receipts for coconut deliveries** (Annexes G to G-2), offered as proof of harvest sharing.
4. **A certification by the Barangay Agrarian Reform Committee (BARC) Chairman, 22 Jan 2015** (Annex H). It certifies Mauro Sr. as tenant for more than 30 years over Senen's coconut land.
5. **Implied tenancy** from long, open, continuous cultivation, which they said applies even without the owner's express consent.
6. **Senen's criminal cases were harassment.** Mauro Sr. called the illegal-logging, qualified-theft and grave-threats cases a "premeditated malicious" plan to push them off the land. The NBI report (doc 652) confirms Senen filed those cases "purposely to eject them."

The CA also rejected Senen's grounds for ending the tenancy: disrespect, unpaid rent, theft, and destruction of improvements.

### The legal basis they stand on (law, for counsel to confirm)

These are the statutory general rules. No DARAB or CA text has been read, so how the tribunals actually applied them is PENDING VERIFICATION.

| Rule | What it gives them | Where it can be attacked |
|---|---|---|
| **RA 3844 §4–5** (as amended by RA 6389). Share tenancy was abolished and converted to leasehold by operation of law. Leasehold can also be created orally or in writing, **expressly or impliedly**. | No written contract is needed. Long cultivation plus accepted harvest shares can prove an **implied** lease. | Implied tenancy still needs **all six elements** and **consent by someone with authority to lease** (owner, usufructuary, legal possessor, §6). Tolerance or a caretaker arrangement is not enough. |
| **Six elements** (jurisprudence): landowner and tenant; agricultural land; consent; agricultural production; personal cultivation; sharing of harvest. Proven by substantial evidence. | The Patotoo, Vicente Jr.'s corroboration, the BARC certificate and the receipts were offered on consent, sharing and cultivation. | Tenancy is never presumed, and the claimant carries the burden. BARC certifications and neighbors' statements are not binding on their own. **Who consented?** A sibling (Vicente Jr.) or an overseer binds the estate only with authority. |
| **RA 3844 §9, succession.** When the tenant dies, the lease continues with **one** successor who personally cultivates: spouse, then eldest descendant. The **lessor chooses** within one month. | This is Mauro Sr.'s route: he says his father was the tenant and he took over. | Did Vicente Sr. or the estate ever **choose** him? Was he the proper successor in the order of priority? Proof of **the father's** own installation by Vicente Sr. is the foundation, and the petition says it rests only on Mauro Sr.'s word. |
| **RA 3844 §10.** The lease is not ended by sale, transfer, or death of the landowner. Heirs and successors take the land subject to it. | Vicente Sr.'s death and the partition do not wipe out a validly created tenancy. | Only helps them if the tenancy was validly created in the first place. |
| **RA 3844 §7, security of tenure.** A tenant can be removed only for cause **with DARAB authorization**. | Ejectment or harassment suits cannot remove them. This is why Senen's criminal cases backfired as "harassment." | See §36 below. |
| **Coconut is an agricultural crop.** The RA 3844 lease covers agricultural land generally, not only rice and corn (that limit is PD 27). | Defeats the draft's "not rice or corn" argument. | — |
| **Mineral classification.** The 2008 DAR order excluded T-2194 and others from **CARP acquisition** as mineral land. | Their likely answer: a CARP exclusion does not by itself extinguish a leasehold on land actually farmed. | Our side: RA 6657 §3(c) excludes mineral land from "agricultural land." **But Lot 905 is not on the exclusion list.** Whether the exclusion reaches RA 3844 leasehold is a key **counsel question**. |

**Ways to remove them if the tenancy stands (RA 3844 §36; the lessor carries the burden under §37):**
1. **Conversion** of the land to non-agricultural use, with disturbance compensation. This is **the natural route for a mining/Minahang Bayan plan**, and it goes through DAR conversion approval.
2. Failure to comply with the lease or the law.
3. Using the land for a purpose other than agreed (e.g., **mining**).
4. Not following proven farm practices.
5. Substantial damage to the land or crops through the tenant's fault (e.g., **illegal tree-cutting**).
6. **Non-payment of rental.**
7. **Sub-leasing.**

Grounds 3, 5 and 6 match facts already alleged (mining, tree-cutting, no rental delivered). Senen pleaded them, but the CA found them insufficient on the record she built. An estate-led case would need independent proof: MGB/PNP mining records, the DENR tree-cutting case, and the absence of any rental receipts signed by an owner.

### Senen's side's answers (per the draft)

- **Not agricultural land.** The draft argues T-2194 was **excluded from CARP as mineral land** by the 14 Apr 2008 order (Track 1). It also says the Bombitas mine small-scale rather than farm.
- **The six tenancy requirements are not all met.**
  - Consent: "tumatao" means someone who mans or looks after a place, i.e. a caretaker, not a tenant.
  - Sharing: the receipts are buyer/hauler delivery slips that don't show the source lot or Senen's receipt of a share.
  - Personal cultivation: they are miners.
  - BARC chairmen have no power to certify tenancy.
- **Even if they are tenants, the tenancy should end.** The Patotoo itself says Mauro also farms land not owned by the Inocallas. The draft calls that a violation of RA 3844 §27(1).

### Cracks to weigh (analysis, not findings)

- ⚠ **The mineral exclusion may not cover most of the disputed land.** The 2008 order lists land by title (T-2194 is on it). **Lot 905, Pls-819-D, is not on the list**, and it is held by tax declaration. Lot 905 is where the ~5 ha dispute is; the T-2194 part is only ~2,000 sqm. Unless Lot 905 is inside one of the excluded titles, the mineral argument protects only a small piece. **Check Lot 905 against the excluded titles.**
- ⚠ **Coconut land is agricultural land.** The draft leans on "not primarily rice and corn." That test comes from PD 27. RA 3844 leasehold is not limited to rice and corn, so this argument is probably weak. **Question for counsel.**
- **Records that help our side:**
  - **1979 annotation (doc 1161, CARP Inocalla pp.):** Vicente Sr. executed a sworn statement, inscribed 16 Oct 1979 and marked "File under TCT No. T-2194." It says the land "is not tenanted; nor portion thereof is devoted to rice or corn cultivation." That predates the claimed 30-plus years. The OCR page it sits on appears to be a sibling title, so confirm on the certified copy of T-2194 that the entry is actually annotated there.
  - **20 May 2014 Paracale zoning certification (doc 1285):** issued for Senen's application for a small-scale gold processing plant on T-2194. This supports the mineral/industrial use of that title.
  - **The Minahang Bayan association's officer list (doc 1434 / Drive 1719544755864630.jpg)** names Joshua A. Bombita and Rogelio L. Bombita Jr. as officers. That shows Bombita family members in mining roles; it does not show Mauro Sr. mining.
- **The draft has errors that could hurt it if filed as written:**
  - Mauro is called "Bonifacio" and "Bautista" in places.
  - The prayer calls the DARAB decision "dated October 12, 2020," which is actually the resolution date.
  - "RA 3884" and "RA 8834" should be RA 3844.
  - It argues almost entirely about T-2194 even though Lot 905 is the main disputed area.
  - It cites an unrelated criminal case (G.R. 247661) on procedural standards.
- **Who owns the claim now: the estate [O, Jonathan 2026-09-14].** The land is still estate property, and **Radj Gymson Inocalla is not a legal heir.**
  - The corpus agrees: [INOCALLA_HEIR_ROSTER.md](INOCALLA_HEIR_ROSTER.md) §3 records that Senen **died without issue** and that Radj Gymson's basis is unknown.
  - So the petition's description of him as Senen's "sole heir and surviving child" is **unsupported**.
  - The consequence: since Senen's death (12 Feb 2021), the CA and SC proceedings have been run by a substitute who does not represent the owners. The estate and the real heirs were not parties.
  - Whether that makes the CA decision not binding on the estate, or open to attack for defective substitution (Rule 3 §16), is a **question for counsel**. A PSA death certificate of Senen plus proof that she had no children closes the factual side.
  - The tenancy claim itself runs against the estate. Mauro Sr. says the **patriarch, Vicente Sr.,** installed his father, which makes the estate the lessor he claims under. **This is an estate/special-administrator matter.**
- **If the CA decision became final** (SC petition never perfected, or denied), the Bombitas have a final ruling that they are tenants. Removing them would then likely take a new DARAB case on a ground for ending the tenancy, not an ejectment suit. **Question for counsel.**
