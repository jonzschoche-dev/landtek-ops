## 0. The answer in one paragraph

The five OP filings chase a screen, not a judgment. Under R.A. 11032 ARTA has no power to discipline anyone: §24 vests administrative jurisdiction over violations in the CSC or the Ombudsman, and ARTA's role under §17(d)–(e) is to investigate, **refer**, **file**, or **assist the complainant in filing**. ARTA's own Litigation Division said in writing (28 Apr 2026, Attachment 2 to the May petition) that its findings are "merely recommendatory". A "no prima facie / closure" resolution therefore decides only that ARTA will not refer; it bars nothing. The Office of the President can reverse that choice because ARTA is an attached agency under the President's control, but the OP cannot itself compel a municipal assessor or discipline a mayor: over LGUs the President has supervision, not control. So the OP is the right forum for **correcting ARTA's legal standards** and for **ordering ARTA to refer and to assist**, and the wrong forum for **getting the records released**. Getting the records is an Ombudsman §15(2) directive or a mandamus. On the merits, two of the five dockets are strong (1321 assessor; 1210 receipts), one is arguable (0690/0792 engineer, citizenship), two are weak as R.A. 11032 cases (0747 mayor's letter; 1212 Sanggunian). The basket should be pleaded with that ranking, not flat.

## 1. First principles — what the process actually is

| Layer | Who | What they can do | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Service duty | LGU office (assessor, engineer, treasurer, mayor, SB) | Accept, assess, act within the Citizen's Charter period; deny only in writing with grounds | R.A. 11032 §9(a)–(c) (legal_chunks 47–49) |
| Screen | ARTA (attached to OP) | "Initiate investigation … **refer** the same to the appropriate agency, or **file** cases"; "**assist complainants in filing** … with the CSC, the Ombudsman"; issue notices of warning | §17(c)(d)(e) (chunk 65) |
| Adjudicate | CSC (appointive officials) · Ombudsman (any official) | Hear and decide §21 violations; impose §22 penalties | §16, §24 (chunks 63, 75); RA 6770 §15 |
| Discipline elective municipal officials | Sangguniang Panlalawigan → appeal to OP | Administrative complaints vs mayor / vice mayor / councilors | LGC §61(b) (chunk 12) |
| Review ARTA | Office of the President (Exec. Sec.; DES for Legal Affairs) | Appeal under A.O. 22; control power to reverse/modify ARTA and direct it | Const. VII §17; E.O. 292 Bk III §1, §27; A.O. 22 |
| Compel an officer to act | Ombudsman | "Direct … any officer … to perform and expedite any act or duty required by law" | RA 6770 §15(2) (chunk 1964) |
| Compel a ministerial duty | RTC / CA | Mandamus (Rule 65) | Rules of Court |

**Consequences that follow from this map.**
1. An ARTA closure is not res judicata on anything. The same facts can go straight to the CSC (appointive: Abla, Balane, Macale, Remoto/Fortuno) or the Ombudsman (all respondents; elective officials only there or at the Sangguniang Panlalawigan). Nothing in the record shows this was done for 0747, 1210, 1212 or 1321 except through ARTA's own referrals (Balane → CSC; Teope/Torralba → Ombudsman; 1891 CART → CSC → Ombudsman).
2. The OP appeal is nonetheless worth keeping for the reason the May petition gave: the standards ARTA applied (citizenship, "government service" narrowing, charter omission as defense) will govern every future request against this LGU. That is a control-power question, and only the OP can answer it for ARTA.
3. The OP will process what it receives through A.O. 22 whether or not the pleading calls itself "supervisory". ARTA's transmittal notices say so, and A.O. 22 §17 puts appeals in the hands of the DES for Legal Affairs. A filing routed to PACE is a complaint, not an appeal.

## 2. The OP's powers in this circumstance — and their limits

**A. Over ARTA (control).**
- Const. art. VII §17 and E.O. 292 Book III §1: "The President shall have control of all the executive departments, bureaus, and offices." Control = "the power … to alter or modify or nullify or set aside what a subordinate officer had done … and to substitute the judgment of the former for that of the latter" — *Mondano v. Silvosa*, G.R. No. L-7708 (1955) *[verify]*. The President (through the Executive Secretary) may "go over, confirm, modify or reverse" a department/agency decision — *Lacson-Magallanes Co. v. Paño*, G.R. No. L-27811 (1967) *[verify]*.
- E.O. 292 Bk III §27(3),(5),(8): the Executive Secretary decides "matters not requiring personal presidential attention", exercises supervision over agencies under the OP, and assigns matters to the appropriate OP unit. Section 27(8) is the hook for the docketing/consolidation motion.
- A.O. 22 §10 and §13: the OP may require ARTA's comment, dismiss outright (no jurisdiction, patently without merit, insubstantial), or decide by memorandum decision. §17: the DES for Legal Affairs signs interlocutory orders and jurisdictional dismissals.
- **What the OP can order ARTA to do:** set aside a closure; remand with a stated legal standard; **direct referral** of a docket to the CSC/Ombudsman under §17(d); **direct ARTA to assist the complainant in filing** under §17(e); direct a §17(c) notice of warning; issue guidance that binds ARTA's regional offices on §4(a)/(f) and on citizenship.

**B. Over the Municipality of Mercedes (supervision only).**
- Const. art. X §4; E.O. 292 Bk III §18; LGC §25: general supervision. The President "may not … substitute [his] judgment" for the LGU's — *Mondano*; *Ganzon v. CA* *[verify]*. The OP cannot order the assessor to release records or the treasurer to issue receipts.
- The OP does sit as the appellate body over the Sangguniang Panlalawigan in administrative cases against municipal elective officials (LGC §61(b)), and may direct the DILG to investigate/monitor — the DILG track (MLGOO Guerrero, PD Relucio) is already open.

**C. Limits that cut against us.**
- Control does not override statutory procedures for disciplining civil-service personnel — *Ang-Angco v. Castillo*, G.R. No. L-17169 (1963) *[verify]*. The OP will not itself find Abla or Balane liable; it can only send them to the CSC/Ombudsman.
- A.O. 22 §8: non-payment of the fee and a defective memorandum are grounds for dismissal. None of the five filings paid. §1's 15-day period ran from each ARTA notice; the notices of appeal were timely, perfection was not.
- ARTA's findings being "recommendatory" helps the supervisory framing but also invites the OP to say there is nothing final to appeal — which is why the pleading must ask, in the alternative, for the control-power order rather than only an appellate reversal.

## 3. "The OP acting for the client" — what that can and cannot mean

The OP acts for the State, never for a party. What we can obtain is an **order to ARTA** to do the things §17 lets ARTA do *for* a complainant. Ranked by what the client actually needs:

| Client need | Best forum | What to ask | OP's role |
|---|---|---|---|
| Assessor records released (1321) | Ombudsman §15(2) directive; or mandamus (assessor's ministerial duty under LGC §472 and §§205/207: property is listed "in the name of the owner … or anyone having legal interest") | order to perform/expedite | indirect: direct ARTA to refer + assist |
| Official receipts for ₱125,000 paid under protest (1210) | Ombudsman / CSC; BLGF supervision (open) | §21(g) charge — the statute's own ground | reverse ARTA's "not a government service" |
| Accountability of Abla, Balane, Macale | CSC (via ARTA referral or direct) | §22 penalties | direct ARTA to refer (§17(d)) — the 30 Apr 2026 Balane endorsement is the precedent |
| Accountability of Mayor / VM / councilors | Ombudsman; Sangguniang Panlalawigan (LGC §61(b)) | administrative + RA 3019/6713 | none directly; OP hears appeal from SP |
| Stop the citizenship / "not a service" standards | OP over ARTA | supervisory declaration + remand | **this is the OP's job** |

So the omnibus should carry two tiers of relief: **Tier 1 (appellate):** set aside and remand each closure with the correct standard. **Tier 2 (control):** direct ARTA to refer the five dockets under §17(d) and to assist under §17(e). Tier 2 is what makes the OP "act for the client" in the only way it lawfully can.

**Cross-cutting: the citizenship point.** Right-to-information (Const. III §7; E.O. 2) is citizen-limited; ARTA and the Mayor leaned on that. But a registered co-owner's request for the assessment records of **her own property** is a proprietary transaction with the assessor (LGC §§205/207 list property "in the name of the owner … or anyone having legal interest"; §472 duties), not an FOI request, and R.A. 11032 §4 speaks of "applicants or requesting parties" without qualification. This is our strongest legal-standard argument and it is cleanest on 1321 and the treasury receipts; it is muddier on generic "inquiries" (0747/1212) where the LGU can say no chartered service was invoked.

**Bottom line for the pleading:** lead with 1321 and the 1210 receipts; carry 0690/0792 as the standards case; plead 0747 and 1212 only for consolidation and the citizenship/§4(f) standard, not for remand on their facts.
