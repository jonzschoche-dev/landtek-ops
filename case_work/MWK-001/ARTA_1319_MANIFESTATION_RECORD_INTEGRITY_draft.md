**1. Notice and nature.** Complainant received the Resolution dated 25 August 2026 by e-mail from the Litigation Division on **7 September 2026**. Complainant is aware that under Section 8, Rule IV of the 2023 ARTA Revised Rules of Procedure, Resolutions of the Authority are not subject to a motion for reconsideration, and **this Manifestation seeks none**. It asks only that the record of this case be complete and accurate as to (a) a pleading of record that the Resolution does not mention, (b) the counts that the Resolution did not pass upon, and (c) the reservation of matters outside the Authority's mandate, which the Resolution itself confirms it did not decide; and (d) that the Authority state in writing whether two readings of the Resolution, which the text of R.A. No. 11032 does not bear, are its position under the Act, so that Complainant may govern his remaining remedies accordingly.

**2. A pleading of record is absent from the procedural history.** On **29 June 2026**, within the period following the Notice of Submission for Resolution of 15 June 2026, Complainant filed by e-mail to the Litigation Division a **Manifestation** (*"Narrowing the issues submitted for resolution; reservation of the property and records issues"*), three (3) pages, signed by Complainant as Attorney-in-Fact, with the request that it be "include[d] … in the case file and forward[ed] to the Litigation Officer assigned to this file" (Annex "1"). **On the same day, the Litigation Division acknowledged in writing: "This is to acknowledge receipt of this email as well as the attachment"** (Annex "2"). **3. The counts submitted, and the count decided.** The Manifestation of 29 June 2026 identified, for the conduct already complained of, **two** service-delivery counts under Section 21: **(i)** failure to act, or to give any written notice of action, on the 5 December 2025 request — **§21(e), §9 and §21(d)**; and **(ii)** the imposition of a requirement and cost not in the Citizen's Charter — **§21(b) and (c)** — by conditioning the correction of the Department's own admitted records error (the cadastral map's "inadvertent" designation of Complainant's lot as Psd-229480 instead of Psd-221861, admitted in PENRO Letter No. LMS-25-550 of 26 November 2025 and reiterated in the Joint Counter-Affidavit) upon Complainant's engagement of a private Geodetic Engineer and recourse to litigation. The Authority's own **CART Indorsement Form of 13 February 2026** (Annex "A" to the Resolution; Annex "3" hereto) likewise marked, as the violations alleged, both *"Imposition of additional requirements other than those listed in the Citizen's Charter"* [§21(b)] and *"Failure to render government services within the prescribed processing time"* [§21(e)] *(confirmed on the PDF, p. 13: boxes (b) and (e) ticked)*. **4. The reservation stands, and the Resolution confirms it.** Paragraph 4 of the Manifestation of 29 June 2026 expressly reserved, and asked the Authority not to resolve, (a) the correctness or correction of the cadastral and survey records, reserved to the DENR Land Management Bureau and the proper DENR offices, and (b) the boundary extent of the titled land, the validity of OCT No. 2018000090, and ownership, reserved to the Regional Trial Court. No. 11032"* (p. 2); that whether Complainant *"may ultimately establish entitlement to access particular documents under other laws or administrative remedies is not the issue before this Authority"* (p. 9); and that the records *"do not establish that PENRO-Camarines Norte was required … to conclusively resolve such controversy"* concerning the titles, the cadastral survey and the Special Patent (p. 10). Complainant manifests, for the record, that **nothing in the Resolution decides, and nothing in this closure waives,** the reserved matters, and that Complainant proceeds with them before the offices and tribunals to which they belong.

**5. Matters the Resolution places on record, which Complainant notes without argument.** Without re-arguing anything: (a) Respondents' averment, as summarized at p. 5 of the Resolution, that the cadastral map *"inadvertently indicated"* Complainant's lot as Psd-229480 when its correct designation is Psd-221861; (b) Respondents' statement, as summarized at p. 4, that PENRO-Camarines Norte *"did not possess records or documents establishing the legal bases for those government occupancies"* of the property; and (c) the Authority's Recommendation No. 2 endorsing this case to its Operations Group *"for the reengineering of PENRO's Citizen's Charter."* Complainant notes these only so that the record is read as a whole.

**6. Two readings of the Resolution that the text of R.A. No. 11032 does not bear — request for written clarification.** Complainant does not believe the Authority intends either. But, read without qualification, the Resolution will be understood to hold the following, and Complainant asks the Authority to state in writing whether each is its position under the Act:

  (a) **That the absence of a processing period in an office's Citizen's Charter defeats a complaint under Section 21(e).** The Resolution finds "no sufficient evidence identifying the particular government service … much less the specific processing period applicable thereto" (p. 9), and in the same breath endorses PENRO's Citizen's Charter to the Operations Group "for the reengineering" thereof (Recommendation 2). The Act does not leave the period to the office. Section 6 requires every agency to set up a Citizen's Charter that details, for each service, *"(d) The maximum time to conclude the process."* Section 9(b) then fixes the ceiling by statute: applications or requests *"shall be acted upon by the assigned officer or employee within the prescribed processing time stated in the Citizen's Charter which shall not be longer than three (3) working days in the case of simple transactions and seven (7) working days in the case of complex transactions from the date the request and/or complete application or request was received."* The Resolution itself concedes that Complainant's requests, to the extent they sought *"documents, certifications, and information which a government office ordinarily issues,"* *"may partake of a government service"* (p. 7; likewise p. 10). For that conceded component, the Act supplies the period the Charter omits. Where an office has not stated a period — the very deficiency Recommendation 2 identifies — the statutory maximum is the period. If the omission of a Charter line instead extinguishes the complaint, the office that has not complied with Section 6 stands better than the office that has.

  (b) **That a request received at an office's official e-mail address is not "received" for purposes of Section 9(b) until the citizen proves how the office routed it internally.** The Resolution assumed that the 5 December 2025 e-mail reached penro.camnorte@yahoo.com — the address from which Respondents had written to Complainant the day before, within the same thread — and then held that "mere transmission of a communication to the general electronic mail address of an office, without evidence linking its receipt and subsequent non-action to the particular officials charged," does not suffice (p. 9). Section 9(b) counts the processing period *"from the date the request … was received."* It is receipt by the office that starts the period; to whom the office then assigns the request is the office's own act, known only to it. If receipt at the address an office uses to correspond with the public is not receipt under Section 9(b), no request sent by e-mail to any office can ground a Section 21(e) complaint, and the reckoning point the Act fixes becomes one the office controls.

Complainant asks for the Authority's written position on (a) and (b) not to reopen the Resolution but because those positions govern what a citizen must do the next time — and because, if the Authority's answer is that neither is what the Act provides, that answer belongs on this record.
