## 2. Wrong citations — replace before filing

**a) LGC §276 does not make assessment records public.** Section 276 is "Condonation or Reduction of Real
Property Tax and Interest" (crop failure, calamity). There is no public-inspection clause in LGC §§204-215.
Use instead:
- **§205(b)**, which puts the heirs squarely in the record: "The undivided real property of a deceased
  person may be listed, valued and assessed in the name of the estate or of the heirs and devisees … such
  heir, devisee, or co-owner shall be liable severally and proportionately for all obligations imposed by
  this Title". A person the Code makes severally liable for the tax has legal interest in the record.
- **§223**, notice of new or revised assessment within 30 days — correctly cited in the draft.
- **§252**, payment under protest — correctly cited, and it carries the receipt point: "There shall be
  annotated on the tax receipts the words 'paid under protest'."

**b) LGC §247 is not a receipt duty.** Section 247 is "Collection of Tax" and only makes the treasurer
responsible for collection. The express duty to issue official receipts in the LGC runs to the *barangay*
treasurer (§334, §395). For a municipal treasurer the duty comes from the Government Auditing Code
(P.D. 1445, in the library) and COA rules. Cite those, plus §252's annotation requirement.

**c) P.D. 1096 §207 does not require keeping permit and inspection records.** Section 207 makes the Building
Official responsible for enforcement and for issuing permits, and lets them enter and inspect. The
record-keeping duty is **§208**, and it is limited to "a permanent record and accurate account of all fees
and other charges". Neither section grants public access. The draft's sentence merges the two and overstates
both.

**d) The Constitution's information clause is textually against us.** Art. III §7 says access to official
records "shall be afforded **the citizen**". So ARTA had a textual hook. The answer is not to argue Art. III
§7 covers foreigners; it is to say this was never an information claim — it was a property owner's
transaction with the office that keeps the records of their own land, governed by the LGC and the Building
Code. Frame it that way or the argument loses on the text.

## 3. Unverified — do not cite until obtained

## 4. Factual corrections against the resolutions themselves

**1319 was not decided on volume or on a missing extension.** The draft's Tab V argument assumes ARTA
excused delay as "highly technical". It did not. It closed because it could not find a service or a period
at all: "Neither is there sufficient evidence identifying the particular government service allegedly left
unrendered and the prescribed processing period governing the same." It also relied on custody,
confidentiality, and personal attribution. A missing-extension argument only bites where a period existed,
which is 1321, not 1319.

Stronger points available in 1319: the §21(b) item was ticked on the referral form and never adjudicated;
and the closure orders PENRO's charter sent "for the reengineering" while the body contains no finding about
that charter.

## 6. The vehicle problem — the most important correction

The draft proposes consolidating all seven dockets into one appeal under AO 22. Four of them are far past
the 15-day window:

| Docket | Notice received | AO 22 window | Position now |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0690/0792 | 20 Apr 2026 | lapsed | already before the OP in the 5 May petition |
| 0747 | 26 May 2026 | lapsed | covered by the Second Manifestation (4 Jun) |
| 1210 | 15 May 2026 | lapsed | covered by the First Manifestation (29 May) |
| 1212 | 8 Jul 2026 | lapsed | covered by the Third Manifestation (23 Jul) |
| 1321 | 27 Aug 2026 | lapsed 11 Sep | filed 8 Sep; delivered to MRO 14 Sep |
| **1319** | **7 Sep 2026** | **22 Sep 2026** | **open** |
| **1378** | **15 Sep 2026** | **30 Sep 2026** | **open** |

Only 1319 and 1378 can still be taken up as ordinary appeals. The other five reach the President through
the route already in use: supervisory review under the President's control power, Art. VII §17, over an
agency attached to the Office of the President. Our AO 22 note in the library says exactly that: this
control power "is the foundation for supervisory review and corrective action over the resolutions of
agencies under the Office of the President, including ARTA, independently of an ordinary appeal under
A.O. 22."

Practical consequence: file as a **supplement to the existing OP docket 050526-MRO-234187** — the same
vehicle as the three manifestations and the 1321 petition — and, within their own windows, a **Notice of
Appeal for 1319 and 1378** with the P1,500 fee each. Do not restart a fresh consolidated appeal.

## 8. Review of revision 2 (received 16 Sep 2026)

Revision 2 adopts the vehicle split, drops the unsourced circular and the unverified case, and corrects the
1319 characterisation. Four problems remain; two are new.

**a) NEW ERROR — the payment figure. Revert to P75,000.00.** Revision 2 attaches P41,119 to docket 1210.
The 1210 record is explicit: "Remittances totaling Seventy-Five Thousand Pesos (Php 75,000.00) were made on
19 December 2025 (Confirmation Nos. VCHMU47RH and VYFLCEPVH). However, no official receipts annotated
'PAID UNDER PROTEST' have been issued" (doc 624), of which P50,000 was a supplement tendered 20 December
2025. The P41,119 is a different, later payment and appears only in the COA / BLGF / Ombudsman documents
(docs 1247, 2431, 5362). Putting it in an ARTA appeal misstates the docket and hands the respondent an easy
correction.

**b) NEW ERROR — 1321 did not find a violation.** Revision 2 says ARTA "found that a violation had occurred
regarding a ten-month processing delay" and then ordered no remedy. It found the opposite: "Accordingly,
while the requested government service was not fully rendered within the processing period indicated in the
Citizen's Charter, the fourth element of Section 21(e) has not been established", and it closed for want of
prima facie evidence. The accurate and still-devastating formulation is: ARTA found the service covered,
found the charter period blown by roughly ten months, excused the delay as "just cause" with no written
extension in evidence, and ordered nothing produced.

**c) The committee point is garbled.** "The committee's blanket refusal to act, with respondents simply
sitting on requests" loses the finding. The point is that the LGU's own Committee on Anti-Red Tape passed a
standing refusal — "the Local Government Unit (LGU) of Mercedes shall not release, provide, or act upon the
requested documents of Mr. Jonathan Zschoche unless and until he sufficiently establishes and submits proof
of his legal personality" (docs 712-717) — and that the respondents in 1210, 1212 and 1321 sat as members of
that committee, alongside Antonio B. Teope, whose structure is the subject of the 1378 request. It is a
conflict-of-interest point, not a delay point.

**d) The 1212 referral is misdescribed.** ARTA referred Coun. Torralba to the Ombudsman on R.A. 6713 and
Tony Teope on R.A. 3019 §3(i) (doc 1614). It did not refer "the 15-day rule".

**e) 1321's posture.** It is not merely a lapsed docket awaiting a supplement — its own OP petition was
filed 8 September and delivered to the Malacanang Records Office on 14 September (docs 8240, 14076). The
supplement should reference that pending petition and its consolidation prayer, not re-argue it from scratch.

**f) Appeal mechanics.** Revision 2 reads as though the memorandum is also due within 15 days. AO 22 §1 and
§3 fix 15 days for the Notice of Appeal and the fee. The memorandum period is not in our excerpt of AO 22 —
confirm it before calendaring.

**g) Tone.** "Absurdly", "shockingly", and "bad faith interpretation" attribute motive to named ARTA
lawyers. The inconsistencies are strong enough stated flatly, and a filing that argues motive invites a
fight about motive. Recommend neutral phrasing throughout.

**Still missing, and worth adding:**
- ARTA's own retreat in the letter of 21 May 2026 (doc 972), which is the cleanest answer to the citizenship
  holding because it comes from ARTA itself.
- 1378's concession against its own result: "the fact that the request is not expressly included in the
  Citizen's Charter does not, by itself, remove it from the ambit of a 'government service'".
- The §21(b) three-element test (docs 719, 1614), whose second element has no statutory basis.
- In 1319: the §21(b) item was ticked on the referral form and never adjudicated, and the charter was sent
  for "reengineering" with no finding in the body to support it.

**Calendar.** 1319's window closes **22 September 2026** — six days from today. If that docket is to be
appealed, the Notice of Appeal, proof of service and P1,500 fee must go this week. 1378 closes 30 September.
