I write on a narrower question than any of the foregoing, and one that your Office is uniquely placed to answer from records it already approves.

**The question.** Twenty-nine parcels are declared by the Municipality of Mercedes in the names of the Heirs of Mary Worrick Keesey, totalling 72,384 square metres, and the real property taxes on them have been paid. On twenty-seven of those twenty-nine declarations, the declared market value reproduces **exactly** as land area multiplied by the Municipality's own land rate of ₱970.00 per square metre — to the peso. That arithmetic indicates declarations carrying **land value only, with no improvement component**.

Structures nevertheless stand on several of these parcels. Attention is respectfully invited to the consequences under the Code:

**1. Improvement entries — Secs. 202, 203 and 205.** Section 202 makes it the duty of persons owning or administering real property *"including the improvements therein"* to declare; Section 203 obliges any person *"making any improvement on real property"* to file a sworn statement *"upon completion or occupancy of the improvement, whichever comes earlier"*; and Section 205 requires that property be *"listed, valued and assessed in the name of the owner or administrator, or anyone having legal interest."* Whether these structures appear on the roll at all, and in whose name, is determined by the Improvement block on the face of each declaration.

**2. Classification and actual use — Secs. 201, 217 and 218.** Appraisal follows actual use, and the assessment level for land turns on the class assigned — **residential 20%, commercial 50%** under Section 218(a). If any of these parcels carries a classification driven by a structure or an activity the registered owners did not authorise, the owners bear an assessment level, and therefore a tax, set by another party's use of their land.

**3. Permit transmittals — Sec. 210.** Section 210 requires the official issuing a building permit to transmit a copy *"within thirty (30) days of its issuance, to the assessor."* Whether your Office or the Municipal Assessor received any such transmittal for these parcels is the mechanical test of whether permits were issued at all.

**What is respectfully requested.** So that the request imposes the least possible work on your Office, the twenty-nine parcels are set out at **Annex "A"** in a table whose first four columns are already filled from the Municipality's own declarations.
