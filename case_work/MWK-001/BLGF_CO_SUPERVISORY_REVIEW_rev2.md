I write as Attorney-in-Fact for Patricia Keesey Zschoche, an heir of the assessed owner of the properties covered by ARP No. 001-00249, Mercedes, Camarines Norte. The Bureau exercises administrative and technical supervision over both the **local treasury** and the **local assessment** operations of that Municipality (Section 470, R.A. No. 7160; E.O. No. 127, §4).

My request is simple, and I will keep it concise. Every office below the Central Office has now been asked, in writing and with the documents attached, to examine the conduct itemized below — and every one has declined, deflected, or gone silent. **If the Bureau likewise declines, then by that silence it effectively endorses each item below as acceptable treasury and assessment practice in this Municipality.** I do not believe the Bureau endorses any of them. But the record needs to know, one way or the other — which is why this letter ends with a request for guidance either way.

**What, absent the Bureau's supervision, stands endorsed** — each item documented, dated, and already of record with Regional Office No. V:

  1. **Withholding a real property tax assessment for three months** (requested in writing 10 December 2025; released 7 March 2026) — through the discount period;
  2. Doing so **after written warning** (30 December 2025) that the office's silence was consuming the taxpayer's 10% discount;
  3. Releasing the assessment **only after a complaint was docketed** against the Mayor and Treasurer (ARTA CTN SL-2026-0128-1210, filed 27–28 January 2026);
  4. Answering the taxpayer's material written inquiries on that assessment **two weeks before the payment deadline** (first substantive reply 17 March 2026; deadline 31 March), the taxpayer paying from abroad;
  5. **Forfeiting the taxpayer's discount** for a delay the office itself caused;
  6. **Charging interest on the entire annual tax from January** (₱4,111.86) where Section 250 gives a quarterly-installment right and only the first-quarter installment was delinquent on the dates of payment — roughly **sixteen times** the ~₱257 a lawful computation yields;
  7. Resting that charge on an ordinance **cited three different ways in three letters** (No. 2-2004; No. 31-2015; No. 22-2024 §208) — a legal basis that changes with each letter;
  8. **Never producing the Section 249 publication and posting proof** on which the 2026 discount-forfeiture and delinquency framework depends;
  9. **Aggregating thirteen ARPs into a single charge** on one ARP, with no schedule furnished and no rule cited;
  10. Issuing official receipts marked **"paid under protest"** (O.R. Nos. 0434682–84, 7 July 2026) and then **deciding nothing within the sixty days** Section 252(a) commands — the period lapsed on or about 5 September 2026 without a ruling of any kind;
  11. Asserting to a supervising office that statements of account were **"always" furnished**, against a dated written record of demands and non-answers that shows otherwise;
  12. On the assessment side: **accepting a ₱300.00 research fee** (O.R. No. 7383466, 20 June 2025) for a Citizen's-Charter service published at **2–3 days** — and rendering **nothing in fifteen months**;
  13. Declaring under oath that the work **"cannot possibly be done"** — while the **Provincial Assessor's own office** had compiled, released, and confirmed in writing (26 January 2026) that the research was **"done"**;
  14. **Invoking the Provincial Assessor's Memorandum No. 2024-01 to withhold records**, when Section 5 of that very memorandum **exempts a party to a pending case**;
  15. A municipal assessor **sitting on the municipal committee that recommended closure of the complaint against her own office**;
  16. A Regional Office answering **none of the nineteen numbered questions** put to it (13 July 2026 request; 31 July 2026 disposition; 15 August resubmission acknowledged 17 August — nothing since);
  17. A Provincial Assessor **committing in writing (1 July 2026) to act** on the taxpayer's escalation, receiving the certified titles he requested the next day — and issuing **nothing in the two months since**;
  18. A Provincial Treasurer who, having required the Municipal Treasurer's explanation (17 July 2026), has **never issued any disposition to the taxpayer at all**.

**The request.** I ask the Central Office to: **(a)** rule on, or refer for authoritative ruling, the interest computation in item 6, requiring the certified ordinance provision (item 7) and the Section 249 proof (item 8); **(b)** review Regional Office No. V's non-response and direct written, itemized action on the pending 15 August resubmission; **(c)** note the Section 252 lapse and issue guidance to the Municipal Treasurer on the timely decision of protests, release of assessments, and response to taxpayer inquiries; **(d)** on the assessment side, cause the fifteen-month-old chartered request to be acted upon and the municipal–provincial records for Barangay 5 reconciled; and **(e)** furnish the reference number under which this request is recorded.

**And for our guidance moving forward:** should the Bureau decline to act on any item above, I respectfully ask that it **say so in writing, item by item** — so that the taxpayer may treat each declined item as the accepted standard of practice in this Municipality, and govern the remaining remedies accordingly. Silence will be read, reluctantly but reasonably, the same way.

All rights and remedies under R.A. No. 7160 and other laws are expressly reserved, including the appeal now proceeding from the Section 252 lapse; nothing herein concedes any charge or assertion. The complete documentary set is available on request; most of it is already in the Regional Office's record.

Supervision that can never issue guidance is not supervision. I ask the Central Office to issue some.
