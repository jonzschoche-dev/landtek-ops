## 1. The two stacked games

**Game A — war of attrition.** The player with the lower per-period waiting cost wins.
Every counterparty (LGU, provincial and regional supervisors, private occupants) waits
for free; the estate pays every month (time, money, Patricia's age). Absent a change in
payoffs, "delay" is not their tactic — it is their equilibrium.

**Game B — deflection (buck-passing).** Each supervisor chooses Act or Deflect.
Deflect costs ≈ 0 + P(escalation lands on me personally) × personal cost. Act costs
effort + political cost of crossing an LGU + precedent risk. Deflect dominates while
responsibility is diffuse. No coordination needed — the structure produces the circle.
The record now documents the closed circle in writing (ARTA → "supervisory authority";
DILG → ARTA; BLGF → CART; CART = the respondents; Governor referral → void).
**Structural, not (provably) coordinated — and that is better for us: cartels without an
enforcement mechanism break when one member's individual incentive flips.**

## §12 — CONSEQUENCE-FOR-THE-LIE DOCTRINE (operator, 2026-09-09)

The question "is there no consequence for lying directly to the DILG?" has an answer, and
the answer is a **discipline of the split**, not a new charge. Recorded verbatim in substance:

**What does NOT attach automatically.** Perjury (RPC Art. 183) needs an oath; an action-taken
letter to DILG is almost never sworn — do not lead with it unless the reply was notarized or
verified. "He lied, therefore jail" fails: prosecutors treat legal conclusions ("valid deed,"
"no further action appropriate") as argument, not false narration of fact. **"Valid" is the
weaker of the two flagged sentences.**

**What CAN attach.**
1. **Administrative dishonesty / conduct prejudicial / RA 6713** — easiest, most
   proportional, no oath required. "Actively devoted to their intended public road purpose"
   over a street under houses is a factual statement the ground contradicts. Forum for an
   elective mayor: Ombudsman / OP / Sanggunian by penalty sought; for the Secretary
   (appointive): simpler.
2. **Falsification, RPC Art. 171(4)** — untruthful statements in a narration of facts, in a
   public document, by a public officer taking advantage of office. The official 1 Sep report
   to DILG is in form a public document. **The live candidate is "actively devoted…", not
   "valid."** Prosecutor will demand: (a) the exact signed page; (b) proof the street was
   occupied when he signed; (c) knowledge — the 22 May 2025 letter + 3 Oct 2025 renewal carry
   notice; (d) that the sentence is fact, not opinion. Real, not quick — park as a referred
   predicate, not the main engine.
3. **RA 3019 §3(a)/§3(e)** — only via the causal chain: false report → Department treats
   file as answered → default continues → advantage/injury. Better as an Ombudsman narrative
   paragraph than a standalone information.
4. **Inside DILG** — the Department cannot convict the Mayor of lying; it can treat the
   1 Sep paper as non-performance of its own Indorsements and elevate. The departmental
   consequence is that **the lie fails as a discharge**. If DILG accepts it as a discharge,
   that is the Department's problem — which is what Annex A exists to make visible.

**The discipline.**
- Keep "misrepresentation of fact" for exactly two sentences: **"a valid Deed of Donation"**
  (→ DILG default column ONLY: failure to identify and produce + unsupported
  characterization; weak as a criminal lie, strong as default) and **"actively devoted to
  their intended public road purpose"** (→ the sentence that travels: false narration of a
  physical fact after 476 days' written notice; admin dishonesty / Art. 171(4)).
- **Never accuse Guerrero of lying.** He did not write those sentences. The cost to DILG is
  accepting them; the cost to the signatories is having signed them.
- If every bad legal position is criminalized, the Ombudsman files it as a land case and
  sits on it. One signed factual sentence the ground refutes is a charge an investigator
  understands in ten minutes.

**Next-filing shape ("what expensive looks like"):** a short, separate Ombudsman note (or a
reserved paragraph in an existing complaint) — attach the 1 Sep signed pages; the
22 May 2025 / 3 Oct 2025 notices of houses on Road Lot 6; a photograph or ocular that the
street still cannot be walked; quote ONLY the "actively devoted" sentence; charge dishonesty
+ conduct prejudicial in an official report to DILG, in the alternative Art. 171(4) on that
narration. Leave "valid deed" in the DILG default column. **The signature is what makes it
expensive; the work is to keep pointing at that signature on that sentence, not at the
whole war.**

**Compliance check — the 10 Sep Reiteration already honors this split:** it uses
"misrepresentations of fact" for the two sentences only, situates them "in an official
action-taken report to a supervising department" (the 171(4) frame without the charge),
never accuses Guerrero, and keeps "valid" in the default meter (Annex A row 1) while the
road-lot row carries CONTRADICTED BY THE GROUND ITSELF with the 476-day count. The
Ombudsman note above is a build-on-request item — RL-6 photo/ocular is its hard gate.

### §12.1 — Addendum (operator sharpening, 2026-09-09): the letterhead IS the oath

Operator's point: the Mayor is **deliberately lying to Guerrero on official letterhead.**
Confirmed against the embedded RPC (Act 3815, verbatim): Art. 171 — *"any public officer,
employee, or notary who, taking advantage of his official position, shall falsify a document
by committing any of the following acts: … 4. Making untruthful statements in a narration of
facts"* — penalty prisión mayor + fine. Three consequences of the frame:

1. **No oath needed.** Perjury's defect (no sworn statement) is irrelevant here: for a public
   officer, public faith attaches to the official document itself. The unsworn lie on
   municipal letterhead is not weak perjury — it is the distinct felony written for this
   actor and this paper.
2. **The Indorsement supplies the legal-obligation element.** 171(4) doctrine requires a
   legal obligation to disclose the truth of the facts narrated. The 1 Sep reply was not
   volunteered — it was **directed by the supervising department under LGC §25**. Guerrero's
   Indorsement created the duty of truthful narration; lying to Guerrero specifically is
   what perfects the offense. (Case-law citations for the elements: PENDING VERIFICATION —
   route through the jurisprudence-ingest lane before any filing quotes a G.R. number.)
3. **Deliberateness is documentary.** 476 days' written notice (22 May 2025 + 3 Oct 2025
   renewal) + his own Building Official's documentation forecloses error on the road-lot
   sentence. Deliberate distortion in an official document = **Serious Dishonesty** (not
   simple) on the admin side, plus conduct prejudicial — the lie targeted the supervision
   mechanism itself.

The §12 split is unchanged in application: the deliberateness case is complete-in-terms on
"actively devoted…" only; "valid Deed of Donation" keeps a legal-characterization defense
and stays in the DILG default column. Guerrero is the **victim** of the falsification in
this frame — which aligns DILG's institutional interest with the heirs'. All criminal
framing NEEDS-COUNSEL before any filing.

### §12.2 — Threshold check on the DILG lane (19 Oct 2026): §3(e) + Art. 208 + MC 2019-72

Operator asked whether the DILG side has crossed into R.A. 3019 §3(e) (gross inexcusable
negligence), paired with RPC Art. 208 and DILG MC 2019-72. Verified answer: **not against
MLGOO Guerrero, and the circular is the wrong issuance.**

1. **§3(e), verbatim (RA 3019, embedded):** *"Causing any undue injury to any party, including
   the Government, or giving any private party any unwarranted benefits, advantage or preference
   in the discharge of his official administrative or judicial functions through manifest
   partiality, evident bad faith or gross inexcusable negligence."* Two elements fail as to
   Guerrero: (a) **the mental element** — he issued three indorsements within days, withdrew the
   sub judice carve-out on correction, copied PD and RD, and transmitted the replies; "gross
   inexcusable negligence" means want of even slight care with conscious indifference, which 45
   days without a compliance finding does not reach, especially with the Province telling him in
   writing to allow "sufficient time"; (b) **causation/injury** — the injury (withheld records,
   blocked street, unproduced instrument) is caused by the LGU, not by his failure to grade the
   answer. Where §3(e) IS reached is the **Mayor** (manifest partiality / evident bad faith +
   written motive admission + unwarranted advantage to the LGU), and the cleaner inaction hook for
   the Assessor and Treasurer is **§3(f)** — *"Neglecting or refusing, after due demand or request,
   without sufficient justification, to act within a reasonable time on any matter pending before
   him"* — 486 days and a lapsed §252 period respectively.
2. **RPC Art. 208, verbatim:** *"…any public officer, or officer of the law, who, in dereliction
   of the duties of his office, shall **maliciously** refrain from instituting prosecution for the
   punishment of violators of the law, or shall tolerate the commission of offenses."* It requires
   (a) a duty to **institute prosecution** and (b) **malice**. An MLGOO has no prosecutorial duty.
   The officer who does hold that duty here is the **Mayor** under §444(b)(1)(x), who declined it
   in writing — Art. 208 is a possible alternative count against HIM, never against Guerrero.
3. **DILG MC 2019-72 — DO NOT CITE.** Verified against public sources: it is the **guidelines on
   accreditation of CSOs and selection of their representatives to local special bodies** (one
   DILG regional list ties the same number to a Bantay Korapsyon PMO). It has nothing to do with
   supervisory review of LGU acts. The correct hooks for review of local acts are statutory —
   LGC **§25** (general supervision) and **§§56-57** (review of ordinances/resolutions by the
   Sangguniang Panlalawigan). If an issuance is wanted, it must be pulled and read first.

**Strategic note:** Guerrero's indorsements are the instruments that prove the Mayor's default.
Charging the author of our best evidence converts him into an adverse party who must defend

## §14 - HOW TO WIN WITHOUT BEING DEFLECTED (28 Oct 2026)

Jonathan: "how do we win this one without them deflecting?"

### 14.1 Why deflection keeps working

Every forum used so far shares one property: **it owes us no ruling.** ARTA could close on
Sec. 4(f). The MLGOO could "facilitate the referral." The PD could allow "sufficient time."
The Province could refer the complaint back to the LGU complained of. The CSC could pass the
Mayor to the Ombudsman. None of those offices is obliged to decide anything, so deflection is
free for them and costs us only calendar. No letter fixes that, because the defect is in the
choice of forum, not in the drafting.

### 14.2 The test for a non-deflectable move

A move cannot be deflected when all four hold:

1. **The duty is ministerial and personal.** No judgment call to hide behind, and it belongs to
   one named officer rather than to "the LGU."
2. **The forum has exclusive jurisdiction over that person or that money.** There is nowhere to
   pass it to.
3. **The relief needs no third office's cooperation** to be effective.
4. **Silence itself creates the next right.** Inaction advances us instead of costing us, because
   a period lapses by operation of law and the next remedy ripens.

Supervision requests fail 1, 2 and 4. That is the whole story of the last fourteen months.

### 14.3 The moves in this matter that pass the test

|---|---|---|
| **Action B, mandamus under Rule 65** (`RTC_CONSOLIDATED_ACTION_outline_2026-09.md`) on the ministerial records duties | A court seized of a petition must rule. The duties are ministerial, the fee was paid, and the demand is documented | **Outlined, unfiled.** Counsel for this matter is OPEN |

### 14.4 The reframe that turns the deflections into assets

Mandamus requires proof that there is **no other plain, speedy and adequate remedy.** Fourteen
months of being passed between ARTA, the MLGOO, the Province and the Region is precisely that
proof, in writing, from the offices themselves. The deflection ledger is not a record of failure;
it is the exhaustion exhibit, and it is already assembled in `DILG_TRACK_REGISTER.md`, the
102-page pack, and the Schedule of Default.

**Consequence for the DILG lane:** keep it alive, cheaply, as the exhaustion and notice exhibit.
Stop expecting a remedy from it. It is also the Art. 27 and Sec. 3(f) "after due demand" predicate
against each officer personally.

### 14.5 Anti-deflection drafting rules (for the letters that remain)

1. One officer, one act, one period. Never "the LGU," never a bundle.
2. Ask for a document whose **absence is itself the answer** (the record book, the enactment
   number, the permit). A non-existent document cannot be referred anywhere.
3. Pre-close the referral scripts by stating what is not being asked (no title adjudication, no
   compensation, no criminal case).
4. Never ask a question that requires another office's input to answer.
5. Put the consequence in the statute, not in a threat. A lapsing period does the work.

### 14.6 The honest bottom line

The letters have done their job and cannot do more. What is blocking the win is not legal
analysis but **resourcing: who signs the court filings and who funds them.** Action B is the
highest leverage per peso and produces the records as its relief. The CSC complaints and the

parcel money and is fee-gated at roughly P150,000 to P200,000.

Court filings are the one place where counsel is genuinely required. Everything in the

## §16 - WHAT MANDATE THE DILG ACTUALLY FOLLOWS WHEN A MAYOR SIGNS SOMETHING UNTRUE

*(Added 29 Oct 2026 on Jonathan's question. Every provision below was pulled verbatim from the
embedded law library this session: `legal_chunks`, citations "RA 7160 (Local Government Code, full)",
"RA 7160 §§25,29 (general supervision over LGUs)", "RA 7160 §§60-67", "Act 3815 (Revised Penal Code,
full)". The stored corpus text drops the letter "s" as a scanning artefact; the wording below restores
it and is otherwise word for word. **Before any of these appear in an outward filing, re-pull the clean
text from lawphil.**)*

### 16.1 The short answer: supervision has no truth-finding power

**There is no DILG mandate that reaches the truth of a statement.** The Department's mandate over a
municipality is *supervision*, and supervision is defined by its own statute as a power to keep acts
inside their legal boundaries, not a power to decide what is true.

**Sec. 25(a) [V]:** "Consistent with the basic policy on local autonomy, the President shall exercise
general supervision over local government units **to ensure that their acts are within the scope of
their prescribed powers and functions.**"

That clause is the whole ceiling. An untrue sentence in a letter is not an act outside a prescribed
power; it is a false assertion of fact. Nothing in the supervision mandate authorises any supervising
officer to find it false. **This is why fourteen months of asking the MLGOO and the Provincial Director
has produced no finding: we have been asking officers to exercise a power the Code does not give them.**
Guerrero can direct and transmit. Relucio can record and refer. Neither can adjudicate.

### 16.2 The structural discovery: for a municipality, supervision runs through the PROVINCE

**Sec. 25(a), second paragraph [V]:** "The President shall exercise supervisory authority directly over
provinces, highly urbanized cities, and independent component cities; **through the province with
respect to component cities and municipalities**; and through the city and municipality with respect to
barangays."

**Sec. 29 [V]:** "**The province, through the governor, shall ensure that every component city and
municipality** within its territorial jurisdiction **acts within the scope of its prescribed powers and
functions.**"

**Consequence, and it is uncomfortable.** The officer on whom the Code places the supervisory duty over
Mercedes is **the Governor**, not the DILG. PD Relucio's habit of referring everything to the Governor,
logged five times in `RELUCIO_COMMUNICATION_PROFILE` and read there as her "pressure valve", is
**statutorily correct**. Her 13 March and 17 March 2026 letters to Acting Gov. Ascutia cite Secs. 29
and 31 by number. She has been telling us where the mandate sits since March and we read it as a dodge.

**This does not mean abandon the DILG lane.** It means being honest about what that lane can produce:
dated written directives, dated written non-compliance, and technical guidance. That is a *record*, and
the record is the predicate for the forum that can actually act. It is not itself a remedy.

### 16.3 The mandate that does reach a false signed statement: three forums, none of them the DILG

**(a) The Sangguniang Panlalawigan. The only body with original administrative jurisdiction.**

**Sec. 60 [V]:** "An elective local official may be disciplined, suspended, or removed from office on any
of the following grounds: ... **(c) Dishonesty, oppression, misconduct in office, gross negligence, or
dereliction of duty**."

**Sec. 61(b) [V]:** "A complaint against any elective official of **a municipality shall be filed before
the sangguniang panlalawigan** whose decision may be appealed to the Office of the President."

**Sec. 62(a) [V]:** within 7 days of filing, the sanggunian "shall require the respondent to submit his
verified answer within fifteen (15) days from receipt thereof, and commence the investigation of the
case within ten (10) days after receipt of such answer." **Sec. 62(c) [V]:** "no investigation shall be
held within ninety (90) days immediately prior to any local election."

**Sec. 63(a)(2) [V]:** preventive suspension may be imposed "**by the governor**, if the respondent is an
elective official of a component city or municipality", where "the evidence of guilt is strong", and no
single preventive suspension may exceed sixty (60) days.

So: **dishonesty is a named ground, the forum is provincial, the complaint must be verified, and the
clock it starts is 7 + 15 + 10 days rather than the open-ended silence we have been living in.**

**(b) The Office of the Ombudsman.** Already seized of this matter: CSC **OAC-L Letter No. 270, s. 2026**,
of 23 June 2026 forwarded the identical ARTA referral there expressly because the official involved is
elective. That is the same reasoning as Sec. 61(b), reached by a different agency.

**(c) The courts, for falsification.**

**Art. 171, Revised Penal Code [V]:** "The penalty of prision mayor and a fine not to exceed 5,000 pesos
shall be imposed upon any public officer, employee, or notary who, **taking advantage of his official
position, shall falsify a document** by committing any of the following acts: ... **4. Making untruthful
statements in a narration of facts**."

Elements to satisfy, per §12.1 of this file: official position taken advantage of; untruthful statements
in a narration of facts; **a legal obligation to disclose the truth of the facts narrated**; and
materiality. The third element is the hard one, and **the MLGOO's Indorsements of 25 and 26 August 2026
supply it**, because they directed him to state the action taken and to cite the instrument by name.
Art. 172 extends the same acts to private individuals, which is why the notarial and deed lanes matter.

### 16.4 What this changes, stated plainly

1. **Stop asking the DILG for a finding.** It cannot make one. Ask it only for what it can give:
   directives, dates, control numbers, and written confirmations that nothing has been received. The
   `RELUCIO_COMMUNICATION_PROFILE` §7 rule was right for the wrong reason: it is not just that she
   *will not*, it is that she *may not*.
2. **The provincial forum has been the destination all along.** The Sangguniang Panlalawigan under
   Secs. 60(c) and 61(b) is the only body that can hold the Mayor to a signed untruth, with the Governor
   holding the preventive-suspension power under Sec. 63(a)(2). Everything the DILG lane has produced is
   the evidence for that filing.
3. **The Schedule of Default is already the draft of that complaint.** Nine items, each one a duty, a
   directive, an answer and the documents that test the answer. What it lacks to become a Sec. 61(b)
   complaint is a verification, a respondent named as respondent, and the Sec. 60 ground pleaded as a
   ground. **It is closer than it looks.**
4. **Sec. 62(c) is a timing constraint to calendar.** No investigation within 90 days before any local
   election. The next applicable election date and the resulting cut-off are **UNKNOWN** and must be
   checked before any filing is timed. Do not guess it.

### 16.5 VERIFIED 29 Oct 2026: the DILG-as-OP-investigator question, answered

**Question:** does the DILG also investigate administrative complaints against elective municipal
officials as the investigating arm of the Office of the President? **Answer: NO, not for this Mayor.**
The mandate is real, but its coverage does not reach him. Sources read directly this session.

**A.O. No. 23, 17 December 1992** [V, lawphil], as amended by **A.O. No. 159, 25 November 1994** and
**A.O. No. 66, 1999**, is titled "PRESCRIBING THE RULES AND PROCEDURES ON THE INVESTIGATION OF
ADMINISTRATIVE DISCIPLINARY CASES AGAINST ELECTIVE LOCAL OFFICIALS OF PROVINCES, HIGHLY URBANIZED
CITIES, INDEPENDENT COMPONENT CITIES, COMPONENT CITIES, AND **CITIES AND MUNICIPALITIES IN
METROPOLITAN MANILA**."

Rule 1, Sec. 1 (Coverage) reaches three groups only: provincial governors and provincial board members;
mayors, vice-mayors and council members of highly urbanized, independent component and component cities;
and mayors, vice-mayors and council members of **cities and municipalities in Metropolitan Manila**.
Rule 1, Sec. 2: the **President, who may act through the Executive Secretary**, is the Disciplining
Authority. Rule 1, Sec. 3: the **Secretary of the Interior and Local Government is the Investigating
Authority**, and may form an investigating committee within the DILG. A.O. 159 amended only Secs. 2 and
3 of Rule 1 and **did not touch the coverage** [V]; A.O. 66 restated the Disciplining Authority and
allowed a Special Investigating Committee in lieu of the DILG Secretary, and **also did not expand
coverage** [V].

**Mercedes is a municipality of Camarines Norte. It is not a city and it is not in Metropolitan Manila.
Its Mayor falls outside A.O. 23 entirely.**

**Why the line is drawn there, which is worth understanding and not just memorising:** Metropolitan
Manila has no province, so Sec. 61(b)'s forum, the sangguniang panlalawigan, does not exist for its
municipalities. A.O. 23 reaches Metro Manila municipalities because nothing else can. Everywhere else
the provincial sanggunian exists and Sec. 61(b) governs. There is no gap and no overlap.

**Joson v. Torres**, G.R. No. 131255, 20 May 1998 [V, lawphil], confirms every step:

- Sec. 61(b): "A complaint against any elective official of a municipality shall be filed before the
  sangguniang panlalawigan"; a complaint against "an elective provincial or city official must be filed
  with the Office of the President."
- "The Secretary of the Interior and Local Government is the Investigating Authority," and "the power of
  the DILG to investigate administrative complaints is based on the alter-ego principle," which "is
  corollary to the control power of the President."
- And the sentence that settles it: **"The President remains the Disciplining Authority. What is
  delegated is the power to investigate, not the power to discipline."**

**Operational consequence for the Mayor:** there is no DILG mandate to investigate him, on the
President's behalf or on anyone's. Asking the DILG for a finding against him was never going to work,
by coverage as well as by Sec. 25(a). **Section 16.1 stands, and is now confirmed from two directions.**

### 16.7 What each forum can actually deliver, verified

**Sec. 66 [V]:** (a) "The investigation of the case shall be terminated within ninety (90) days from the
start thereof. Within thirty (30) days after the end of the investigation, the Office of the President
or the sanggunian concerned shall render a decision in writing." (b) "The penalty of suspension shall
not exceed the unexpired term of the respondent **or a period of six (6) months for every
administrative offense**." (c) removal as a result of an administrative investigation bars candidacy.

**Sec. 67 [V]:** decisions may be appealed within 30 days: (a) to the sangguniang panlalawigan from the
sangguniang bayan and component-city sanggunian; (b) **to the Office of the President from the
sangguniang panlalawigan**. "Decisions of the Office of the President shall be final and executory."

**Pablico v. Villapando**, G.R. No. 147870, 31 July 2002 [V]: the last paragraph of Sec. 60 means
**dismissal of an erring elective local official may be decreed only by a court of law**; the power to
remove is "lodged exclusively with the courts." The Court struck down Art. 124(b), Rule XIX of the
implementing rules insofar as it vested removal in the disciplining authority. Followed in G.R. No.
170626 (2008).

**So the Sangguniang Panlalawigan route tops out at suspension, up to six months per offence. It cannot
remove him.** Anyone who says otherwise is relying on the IRR provision the Supreme Court voided.

**Hagad v. Gozo-Dadole**, G.R. No. 108072, 12 December 1995 [V]: the Ombudsman **retains** administrative
disciplinary authority over elective local officials after the Local Government Code, **concurrent** with
the Office of the President. "There is nothing in the Local Government Code to indicate that it has
repealed" the Ombudsman Act provisions; the two statutes are to be harmonised, and repeals by
implication are disfavoured. On suspension: under the Ombudsman Act the Ombudsman may suspend for **up
to six months** where the evidence of guilt is strong and the charge involves dishonesty, oppression,
grave misconduct or the like, against **sixty days** under LGC Sec. 63, and the Ombudsman's power was
not displaced by the shorter period.

**Reading across all of it:** the Ombudsman is the stronger administrative forum, it is already seized of
this matter through CSC OAC-L Letter No. 270 of 23 June 2026, and its preventive-suspension power is six
times the Governor's. **That is the forum to feed, and it is already fed.**

## §17 - MAXIMIZING THE DILG: STOP ASKING FOR A VERDICT, ASK FOR WHAT IT MANUFACTURES

*(Added 29 Oct 2026 on Jonathan's reframe: "the subject matter is maximizing the work of the DILG even
if they cannot dismiss an official. Why are they dismissing the evidence and not commenting?" The
question is better than the one §16 answered. §16 established what the Department cannot do. This
section is about everything it can, and why we have not been asking for any of it.)*

### 17.1 Why they are not commenting. Three reasons, and all three are ours to fix

**1. We have been asking the one question the Department is not allowed to answer.** Every letter in
fourteen months asked, in substance, whether the LGU's answers were true, or asked the Department to
make the LGU perform. Sec. 25(a) gives a supervising officer exactly one question it may decide:
whether an act is **"within the scope of their prescribed powers and functions."** A truth question can
be declined forever, politely, and they have. **The scope question cannot be declined, because the Code
assigns it to them by name.**

**2. We have never once used a chartered service.** Supervision correspondence has no service standard,
so there is no clock on it and no violation in ignoring it. That is why the answers come back as
acknowledgments. **The Department does operate chartered services with published processing times, and
we have used none of them.** An acknowledgment is the correct output of the channel we chose.

**3. We asked for reasoning where only records exist, and for records where only reasoning exists.**
Three different outputs, three different channels, and we have been mixing them in one letter:

| What we want | The channel that produces it | What it has that correspondence does not |
|---|---|---|
| a document | FOI / records request | a statutory deadline (E.O. 2 s. 2016) |
| a legal conclusion | **request for a DILG legal opinion** | a Citizen's Charter processing time |
| a consequence | the Department's assessment instruments | money and a published result |

### 17.2 The reframe, in the exact words to use

**Do not write:** the Mayor's statement that the Municipality holds a valid Deed of Donation is untrue.

**Write:** *Is the Municipality's continued occupation of registered private land, in the absence of any
produced instrument of acquisition and of any expropriation record, within the scope of its prescribed
powers and functions under Sections 22(a)(4) and 19 of the Local Government Code?*

The second version cannot be sent to a court, to the Governor or to the LGU, because **Sec. 25(a) makes
it the supervising officer's own question.** And it does not require anyone to call the Mayor a liar:
the premise is only that **no instrument has been produced**, which is not in dispute and is admitted by
the LGU's own answer of 1 September 2026.

**Both answers help us.** If the Department says the occupation is within prescribed powers, it must
identify the power, and there is none that lets a municipality hold titled private land without an
instrument or an expropriation. If it says it is not, the supervision mandate is engaged on its own
terms and the file can no longer be parked. **There is no comfortable third answer, which is the same
structure that makes the CART Secretariat letter work.**

Apply the same conversion to every item in the Schedule. The question is never "did he lie." It is
always "was that act within the prescribed power, and if so which one."

### 17.4 Instrument two: make the MLGOO close his own file

The 4 September 2026 transmittal passed the LGU's replies along "for your information, guidance and
appropriate reference" and contained **no compliance assessment**. That is the whole gap in the
supervision lane.

Asking him to assess compliance is **not** asking him to find against the Mayor. It is asking him to
state whether his own directives of 25 and 26 August were complied with. He issued them, he set their
terms, and he is the only person who can say whether they were met.

**Argument, not citation, and it must be labelled as such:** a directive issued without any
determination of compliance is not supervision, it is correspondence. Sec. 25(a) gives the power to
ensure acts stay within prescribed powers, and an unassessed directive ensures nothing. **No provision
says in terms that he must assess. Do not pretend one does.** Put it to him as a request to close his
own file, which is how a careful officer will hear it.

### 17.5 Instrument three, the one with money attached: the Seal of Good Local Governance

**R.A. No. 11292, the Seal of Good Local Governance Act of 2019** [V], institutionalises the SGLG as an
award, incentive and recognition programme **run by the DILG**. Two features matter:

- **It is "all-in": an LGU must pass every performance area.** A single failed area disqualifies.
- **A passing municipality receives an incentive fund reported at ₱1.8 million** (₱2.3m cities,
  ₱4m provinces), and assessment includes document review **and onsite validation**.

Among the performance areas are **Business-friendliness and Competitiveness**, which is where R.A. 11032
and Citizen's Charter compliance sits, and **Financial Administration**, which is where fee assessment
and the Full Disclosure Policy sit. **Those are precisely the two areas our documented facts touch.**

**The ask is narrow and the Department cannot pass it anywhere.** Not "disqualify Mercedes", which is
not ours to ask. Only: *that the documented non-performance be placed before the assessment team for the
current cycle, for whatever weight the Department determines.* DILG owns the scoring, the criteria and
the validation. **The LGU cannot answer for it, the Governor cannot answer for it, and the Mayor
certainly cares about ₱1.8 million and an onsite validation team.**

**UNKNOWN and to be obtained before citing:** the current cycle's Memorandum Circular number, its exact
performance areas and indicators, its assessment calendar, and the current incentive amounts. The area
names and counts have changed between cycles (seven areas in 2019, later expanded to ten) and the
sources conflict across years. **Get the year's MC before putting any of this in a letter.**

### 17.6 Instrument four: convert every non-comment into a written refusal

**A non-comment is worth less to us than a refusal.** Silence can be explained away later as oversight,
backlog or a file in transit. A written "this Office cannot do X" is permanent, and it is the exhaustion
exhibit for the Sangguniang Panlalawigan and the Ombudsman.

**Drafting rule from here on: every letter to the Department ends with a numbered menu of specific acts
that are plainly within its power, and asks which of them the Office will do.** Not "please act." A list.
Three or four items, each one answerable with a yes, a no or a date. A yes is a commitment with a clock.
A no is an exhibit. **Only an open-ended ask can be met with an acknowledgment**, and we have been
writing open-ended asks for fourteen months.

### 17.7 The principle, in one line

**Stop asking the Department for a verdict. Ask it for the three things it is built to manufacture:
records, opinions and scores.** Each has a published clock, none requires it to call anyone a liar, and
all three are usable in the forums that can actually punish.

### 17.9 THE TRAPS IN THE LEGAL OPINION ASK, AND THE DESIGN THAT SURVIVES THEM

#### 17.9.1 The vise, which is the real trap

Two deflections point in opposite directions and between them they can kill any question:

- **Too much fact** and the answer is *"the matter is sub judice"* or *"this Office does not rule on
  questions of fact."*
- **Too little fact** and the answer is *"the query is hypothetical and academic."*

A question written to escape one lands in the other. **The escape is not a wording trick. It is to ask
about the meaning of a provision of general application, or about the Department's own supervisory
standard, and never about our property.** Interpretation of R.A. 7160 and its related statutes is the
service's ordinary work, and its published archive is full of exactly that. Ownership questions are
inherently fact-bound and cannot be made to fit.

#### 17.9.3 The questions to actually file. Superseding §17.3

Ranked by risk of an adverse answer, lowest first. **File A, B and C. Hold D.**

**A. The Citizen's Charter question. The cleanest question in the whole matter.**
> May a local government office impose, as a condition for acting upon a request for a service listed in
> its Citizen's Charter, a requirement that is not listed in that Citizen's Charter?

R.A. 11032 Sec. 21(b) answers it in its own words [V]: liability attaches for "**Imposition of additional
requirements other than those listed in the Citizen's Charter**." **Adverse-answer risk: near zero**,
because an adverse answer would contradict the text of the statute the Department helps administer. And
it is the all-heirs power of attorney, item 3 of the Schedule, 276 days old, with no legal source ever
given. **Note: Art. 487 of the Civil Code is deliberately NOT the hook here.** Its text is "Any one of
the co-owners may bring an action in ejectment" [V], which is about actions, not about records requests.
Leaning on it would overclaim and hand them a way in. Sec. 21(b) needs no help.

**B. The deferral question.**
> Where a service is listed in the Citizen's Charter and a fee is payable for it, does the assessment of
> that fee form part of the action required within the prescribed processing time, or may an office defer
> assessing the fee indefinitely while the request is stated to be under verification?

This is item 5, "being verified and consolidated", and item 7, the research fee paid in June 2025, with
no facts in the question. **Adverse-answer risk: low.** The only adverse route is that verification time
is itself discretionary, which Sec. 9(b)(1)'s "shall be acted upon ... within the prescribed processing
time" makes hard to reach.

**C. The ministerial question.**
> Is the duty of a Secretary to the Sanggunian under Sec. 469(c)(5) of R.A. 7160 to furnish, upon request
> of any interested party, certified copies of records of public character in his custody upon payment of
> prescribed fees a **ministerial** duty? And does the duty to prescribe or assess the fee arise upon the
> request?

**Adverse-answer risk: low.** "Shall furnish, upon request of **any interested party**" is about as
ministerial as statutory language gets. This is the question that kills doctrine B for every records item
at once, which is why it is worth the small risk. **And it answers deflection 6 by being deflection 6.**

**D. HELD. The supervisory-standard question, reframed and NOT filed with A to C.**
> In the exercise of general supervision under Sec. 25(a), what is a local government unit required to
> produce in order to show that its occupation of land registered in the name of private persons is
> within the scope of its prescribed powers and functions under Secs. 22(a)(4) and 19?

This is §17.3's Q1, rewritten so that it asks about **the Department's own supervisory standard** rather
than about anyone's ownership. It is the highest-value question in the matter and still the highest-risk.
**Hold it until A to C come back**, for three reasons: it is the one question that can still draw a sub
judice answer; an adverse answer on it would be quoted against us for years; and filing it with A to C
risks deflection 9 taking all four down together. **If it is ever filed, it goes in its own letter.**

#### 17.9.4 Deflection 10, the one that is not a deflection: an adverse opinion

**A DILG legal opinion is permanent and public.** They are published by LO reference number. If we ask a
question and the answer favours the LGU, we will have manufactured, at our own cost and over our own
signature, a document the other side can wave in every forum for the rest of this matter. **A deflection
costs us nothing. An adverse opinion costs us more than silence ever has.**

Three rules follow, and they are not negotiable:

1. **Never ask a question whose correct legal answer is not already near-certain.** We are not seeking
   guidance. We are seeking a document that states what we already believe the law to be.
2. **Prefer questions where even the adverse answer is useful.** A ruling that the all-heirs requirement
   is permissible would at least have to name its legal source, which fourteen months of asking the LGU
   has failed to produce. That is why A survives this test and D does not.
3. **Never ask about ownership, title, valuation, taking or compensation.** Those are for the courts and
   for Botor's lane, and an opinion against us there would be worse than useless.

#### 17.9.5 Filing design

- **Addressee:** the chartered service, by name, at **DILG Region V**, with a copy to the Central Office
  through the Office of the Secretary. Novel questions are endorsed upward by their own procedure, so
  nothing needs to be forced.
- **DO NOT NAME MERCEDES, and this is a real trade-off Jonathan should decide.** Naming it makes the
  opinion directly usable and names the LGU in a Department document. It also invites a sub judice check
  that will find Civil Case 26-360, invites referral to the LGU for comment, and invites delay.
  **Recommendation: do not name it in the first filing.** Get the clean legal standard, then apply it
  ourselves in the SP or Ombudsman papers, where we control the application.
- **Standing, in one line and no more:** a citizen who has requested services listed in a local
  government's Citizen's Charter. Enough to not be academic. No facts imported.
- **Menu close** (§17.6): the reference number assigned; the applicable processing time under the
  Citizen's Charter; and whether the query will be resolved regionally or endorsed to the Central Office.
  Three items, each answerable with a word.
- **Without prejudice:** one line that the request is made without prejudice to any position taken in any
  other proceeding, so nothing in it reads as a concession that the requirements questioned might be
  valid.
- **Assume the Mayor will read it.** The Department furnishes copies, and we have asked it to. Nothing
  about strategy, nothing about any other forum, nothing we would not want him reading. **Which is easy,
  because questions A to C do not mention him, his Municipality, or any property.**
- **Contact addresses: UNKNOWN.** The DILG key-officials page shows a Director and a Division Chief for
  the Legal and Legislative Liaison Service, but the listings conflict across pages and one of them is
  over a year old. **Do not address a named officer from an unverified listing.** Address the office, and
  confirm the current central e-mail and the Region V legal unit's address before transmitting.
