## PART I — THE MUNICIPALITY: COMPENSATION FOR USE AND OCCUPATION

**1. The ground.** The municipal compound stands on **Cadastral Lots 401, 403 and 405, Cad. 118-D — 8,706 square meters** — which the **DENR itself** projects as lying within **Lot 2-A, (LRA) Psd-221861, covered by TCT No. T-32911**, registered in the name of the Heirs of Mary Worrick Keesey (DENR letter LMS-25-550, 26 November 2025).

**2. The only instrument ever cited — and why it conveys nothing.** Since my first written demand of **15 May 2025** the Municipality has been asked to produce the title, deed, expropriation judgment, or instrument on which its occupation rests. Its reply of 21 May 2025 cited a **1953 donation accepted by Resolution No. 21** — and produced an **unsigned, unregistered photocopy**. The estate holds that instrument: a Deed of Donation of **12 July 1953**, acknowledged before the Justice of the Peace on **13 July 1953**, by Mary Worrick Keesey and by Manuel Garrido *as guardian of the minors Dolores, Concepcion and Helen Garrido*, of **8,951.22 square meters** — the exact area the Municipality now declares as its own — *"only for the purpose of the site of a market and municipal building and its premises."* On its face that instrument fails to convey the land, for five independent reasons:

  (a) **It is void as to the minors' shares.** *"Guardians and trustees cannot donate the property entrusted to them"* (Art. 736, Civil Code). The wards were co-owners pro indiviso; their guardian's donation of their shares is a nullity, not a defect.
  (b) **It could not convey a specific portion of undivided property.** The land was co-owned by three (the title stood in the names of Mary, Helen and Alice Worrick — one of whom was no party to the deed). A co-owner may dispose only of her ideal, undivided share (Art. 493); a specific 8,951.22-square-meter site was never carved out by partition, and no partition has ever adjudicated it to the Municipality.
  (c) **It was never registered.** No annotation of it appears on TCT No. T-4497 or on TCT No. T-32911, both issued to the heirs after the deed; an unregistered instrument operates at most as a contract between the parties and binds no one else (Sec. 51, P.D. No. 1529). The land remains registered — today — in the heirs' names.
  (d) **It is a modal donation the Municipality has not honored.** The gift was *"only for the purpose of the site of a market and municipal building and its premises."* A donation with a burden is governed by the law of contracts (Art. 733), and non-compliance with its condition is ground for revocation (Art. 764) or rescission (Art. 1191). The Municipality has instead declared the land as its own general property, exempted itself from tax on it, and treated the site as if held in fee.
  (e) **The Municipality itself has admitted it did not acquire the land.** *Sangguniang Bayan Resolution No. 76, series of 1996* — forty-three years after the deed — is the Municipality's own written acknowledgment that it had not acquired the land on which it was building. Its own governing body has said what this letter says.

**2-B. Notice of the action to void the donation.** The estate gives notice that it will seek, in the same action described at Part III, **a declaration that the Deed of Donation of 1953 is void and conveyed nothing** to the Municipality — void as to the wards' shares under Article 736, ineffective as to any specific portion under Article 493, unregistered and inoperative against the title — and, **in the alternative, its revocation and rescission for breach of its condition** (Arts. 733, 764, 1191), with reconveyance of whatever interest the Municipality claims under it and cancellation of every declaration resting on it. The compensation account at ¶4 runs in either event: a void donation never made the Municipality an owner, and a revoked one returns the land with its fruits.

**2-C. Damages for breach of the donation's condition.** Rescission for breach of a conditional obligation carries **damages** (Art. 1191, last clause; Art. 764 in relation to Art. 1170). The Municipality accepted a gift of a hectare of Poblacion land on one stated condition and has for decades treated the gift as unconditional ownership — declaring it, exempting it, and building on it as its own — while the donors' heirs bore the loss of the land and of every use of it. The estate demands, as damages for that breach, **not less than ₱3,000.00 per square meter on the donated hectare — not less than ₱30,000,000.00** — a fraction of prevailing value, and independent of the compensation and just-compensation accounts at ¶¶4 and 4-A.

## PART II — THE OFFICERS PERSONALLY: DAMAGES UNDER ARTICLE 27

**Article 27, Civil Code:** *"Any person suffering material or moral loss because a public servant or employee refuses or neglects, without just cause, to perform his official duty may file an action for damages and other relief against the latter."* Moral damages are recoverable in such cases (**Art. 2219(10)**); exemplary damages where the refusal is in bad faith (**Art. 2232**); attorney's fees and litigation expenses where the refusal compelled litigation (**Art. 2208**). These heads lie against the officer, **not the treasury.** And **Article 32** makes any public officer who directly or indirectly impairs the right against **deprivation of property without due process of law** (Art. 32(6)) liable for damages **whether or not he acted in good faith** — a liability the law imposes precisely so that officers cannot shelter behind their offices. Every officer below is demanded under Article 32 as well.

No. 7383466) for a Citizen's-Charter service published at two-to-three days: certified history and assessment records of the estate's parcels. **Fifteen months later, nothing has been rendered.** In the interval she (i) swore the work "cannot possibly be done" — after the **Provincial Assessor's own office had done it and released it**; (ii) withheld records under Memorandum No. 2024-01 **against that memorandum's own Section 5 exemption**; (iii) imposed an "all-heirs" authority requirement found in **no law, ordinance, or Charter**, against an apostilled SPA of record; and (iv) **sat on the municipal committee that recommended closing the complaint against her own office** (CART Resolution No. 6, s. 2026).
