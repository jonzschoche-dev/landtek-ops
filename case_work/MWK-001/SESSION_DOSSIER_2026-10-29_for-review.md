## 5. THE LEGAL FINDINGS MADE THIS SESSION, WITH SOURCES

All statutory text below was pulled from the project's embedded law library (`legal_chunks`) or read
directly from lawphil. **Caution for the reviewer: the library's stored statute text drops the letter
"s" as a scanning artefact.** Wording is restored here and is otherwise word for word, but **every quote
must be re-pulled from lawphil before it appears in any outward paper.**

### 5.1 Supervision has no truth-finding power

**R.A. 7160 Sec. 25(a) [V]:** "Consistent with the basic policy on local autonomy, the President shall
exercise general supervision over local government units **to ensure that their acts are within the scope
of their prescribed powers and functions.**"

**Consequence.** An untrue sentence in a letter is not an act outside a prescribed power; it is a false
assertion of fact. **No supervising officer is empowered to find it false.** Fourteen months of asking
the MLGOO and the Provincial Director for a finding asked them to exercise a power the Code does not give
them.

### 5.2 For a municipality, supervision runs through the province

**Sec. 25(a), second paragraph [V]:** supervisory authority is exercised directly over provinces and
highly urbanized and independent component cities, "**through the province with respect to component
cities and municipalities**".

**Sec. 29 [V]:** "**The province, through the governor, shall ensure that every component city and
municipality** within its territorial jurisdiction **acts within the scope of its prescribed powers and
functions.**"

**Consequence, and it is uncomfortable.** The officer on whom the Code places the supervisory duty over
Mercedes is **the Governor**. PD Relucio's standing habit of referring everything to the Governor, which
the profile had read as a pressure valve, is **statutorily correct**. Her letters of 13 and 17 March 2026
cite Secs. 29 and 31 by number.

### 5.3 Where a signed untruth can actually be reached

- **Sec. 60(c) [V]:** an elective local official may be disciplined, suspended or removed for
  "**Dishonesty, oppression, misconduct in office, gross negligence, or dereliction of duty**".
- **Sec. 61(b) [V]:** "A complaint against any elective official of **a municipality shall be filed
  before the sangguniang panlalawigan** whose decision may be appealed to the Office of the President."
- **Sec. 62(a) [V]:** answer required within 15 days of a requirement made within 7 days of filing,
  investigation commenced within 10 days of the answer. **Sec. 62(c) [V]:** no investigation within 90
  days immediately prior to any local election.
- **Sec. 63(a)(2) [V]:** preventive suspension of a municipal elective official is imposed **by the
  governor**, where the evidence of guilt is strong, capped at 60 days for any single suspension.
- **Sec. 66(a), (b), (c) [V]:** investigation terminated within 90 days of its start, decision within 30
  days after; **suspension not to exceed the unexpired term or six months for every administrative
  offense**; removal bars candidacy.
- **Sec. 67(b) [V]:** sanggunian panlalawigan decisions appealable to the Office of the President within
  30 days; OP decisions final and executory.
- **RPC Art. 171 [V]:** prision mayor and a fine on "any public officer, employee, or notary who, taking
  advantage of his official position, shall falsify a document" by, among other acts, "**Making
  untruthful statements in a narration of facts**". The hard element is a **legal obligation to disclose
  the truth** of the facts narrated. **ARGUMENT:** the MLGOO's Indorsements supply it, because they
  directed the Mayor to state the action taken and to cite the instrument by name.

### 5.4 The DILG-as-OP-investigator question, asked by Jonathan and answered

**Answer: NO, not for this Mayor.**

**A.O. No. 23 of 17 December 1992 [V, lawphil]**, as amended by **A.O. No. 159 of 25 November 1994 [V]**
and **A.O. No. 66 of 1999 [V]**, is the issuance that makes the DILG Secretary the President's
Investigating Authority. Rule 1 Sec. 1 coverage reaches three groups only: provincial governors and
provincial board members; mayors, vice-mayors and council members of highly urbanized, independent
component and component cities; and mayors, vice-mayors and council members of **cities and
municipalities in Metropolitan Manila**. Rule 1 Sec. 2: the President, acting through the Executive
Secretary, is the **Disciplining Authority**. Rule 1 Sec. 3: the DILG Secretary is the **Investigating
Authority**.

A.O. 159 amended only Rule 1 Secs. 2 and 3 and **did not touch the coverage** [V]. A.O. 66 restated the
Disciplining Authority and permitted a Special Investigating Committee in lieu of the DILG Secretary, and
**also did not expand coverage** [V].

**Mercedes is a municipality of Camarines Norte. Not a city, not in Metropolitan Manila. Its Mayor is
outside A.O. 23 entirely.**

**Why the line sits there, which the reviewer should test:** Metropolitan Manila has no province, so
Sec. 61(b)'s forum does not exist for its municipalities, and A.O. 23 reaches them because nothing else
can. Everywhere else the provincial sanggunian exists and Sec. 61(b) governs. **I believe there is no gap
and no overlap. This is my reading [J] and it is worth a second opinion.**

**Joson v. Torres, G.R. No. 131255, 20 May 1998 [V, lawphil]** confirms the framework and supplies the
governing sentence: "**The President remains the Disciplining Authority. What is delegated is the power
to investigate, not the power to discipline.**" The DILG's investigative power rests on the alter-ego
principle, "corollary to the control power of the President."

### 5.5 The lever this produced, which is HELD and not pulled

A.O. 23's **first** covered group is provincial governors and provincial board members. Sec. 29 puts the
duty to supervise Mercedes on the province through the governor. **So against the Governor, for failure to
supervise, the Office of the President is the Disciplining Authority and the DILG Secretary is the
Investigating Authority.**

**A.O. 23 Rule 3 Sec. 3 [V]:** a complaint is filed with the Records Office, Office of the President, or
for officials of LGUs **outside Metropolitan Manila, "through the concerned Regional Director of the
DILG"**, who must transmit it to the DILG Secretary **within 48 hours** and authenticate the supporting
documents, the Secretary forwarding to the Office of the President **within 48 hours**.

That is the same Regional Office this matter has been writing to since July. **A complaint lodged there
cannot be referred downward.** Held, for four reasons: the Province has not refused us and its PPDC wrote
to the Mayor on 23 June with the Governor's notation; the **Escober letter is still unread**, so
provincial and regional conduct must not be characterised; there is no documented provincial default, no
directive to the Governor and no dated non-compliance by him; and it is the least reversible move
available.

### 5.6 Ceilings, and a correction to my own earlier statement

**I had told Jonathan the provincial route could "discipline, suspend or remove". On removal that was
wrong.**

**Pablico v. Villapando, G.R. No. 147870, 31 July 2002 [V]:** the last paragraph of Sec. 60 means
dismissal of an erring elective local official may be decreed **only by a court of law**; the power to
remove is "lodged exclusively with the courts". The Court struck down Art. 124(b), Rule XIX of the
implementing rules insofar as it vested removal in the disciplining authority. Followed in G.R. No.
170626 (2008). **So the sangguniang panlalawigan route tops out at suspension, six months per offence
under Sec. 66(b).**

**Hagad v. Gozo-Dadole, G.R. No. 108072, 12 December 1995 [V]:** the Ombudsman **retains** administrative
disciplinary authority over elective local officials after the Local Government Code, **concurrent** with
the Office of the President; "there is nothing in the Local Government Code to indicate that it has
repealed" the Ombudsman Act provisions. Ombudsman preventive suspension runs to **six months** against
sixty days under LGC Sec. 63.

**Reading across:** the Ombudsman is the stronger administrative forum, and it is **already seized** of
this matter, because the Civil Service Commission forwarded the identical ARTA referral to it by **OAC-L
Letter No. 270, s. 2026, of 23 June 2026**, on the stated ground that the official involved is elective.

## 8. THE TRAP ANALYSIS, WHICH CHANGED THE FILING

Jonathan warned: *"there must be traps in the legal opinion ask as it is customary for them to deflect."*
He was right, and it changed the letter.

**The vise.** Too much fact draws "the matter is sub judice" or "this Office does not rule on questions of
fact". Too little draws "the query is hypothetical and academic". **A question written to escape one lands
in the other.** The escape is to ask about a provision of general application, never about our property.

**Ten deflections were mapped with the design answer to each.** Three worth noting for a reviewer:

- *"Opinions are for officials, not private parties."* Defeated by their own charter, which lists citizens
  as eligible clients. Quoted in the opening line.
- *"Go to the Provincial Legal Officer."* Misreads **Sec. 31 [V]**: "In the absence of a municipal legal
  officer, **the municipal government** may secure the opinion of the provincial legal officer ... on any
  legal question affecting the municipality." It is addressed to the municipal government, not to a
  citizen and not to the Department. **Held in reserve, deliberately not raised in the letter**, because
  raising a deflection nobody has made plants it. This reverses my own earlier written guidance.
- *"Local autonomy; that is within the LGU's discretion."* Defeated by making discretion the question
  itself. A question in the form "is this duty ministerial or discretionary" cannot be answered with "it
  is discretionary".

**Deflection 10, the one that is not a deflection, and the worst.** **An adverse opinion.** DILG opinions
are published by reference number and are permanent. An adverse one would be manufactured at our own
cost, over our own signature, and quotable against us in every forum for the rest of the matter. **A
deflection costs nothing; an adverse opinion costs more than silence ever has.** Rules adopted: never ask
a question whose correct answer is not already near-certain; prefer questions where even the adverse
answer is useful; **never ask about ownership, title, valuation, taking or compensation.**

**The question set was rebuilt.** My original question, whether a municipality may assert legal title
without producing an instrument, is **not filed**: closest to sub judice, inherently fact-bound, and the
highest adverse risk. It survives only as a reframed **held** question about the Department's own
supervisory standard, to be filed alone if ever.

**What was filed instead:**

1. May a local government office impose, as a condition for acting upon a request for a service listed in
   its Citizen's Charter, a requirement **not listed** in that Charter? **R.A. 11032 Sec. 21(b) [V]**
   answers it in its own words: liability for "Imposition of additional requirements other than those
   listed in the Citizen's Charter." **Adverse risk near zero.** This is the all-heirs power of attorney,
   Schedule item 3, 276 days old at 29 October.
2. Where a charter service carries a fee, does assessing the fee form part of the action due within the
   processing time, or may assessment be deferred indefinitely while the request is said to be under
   verification?
3. Is the **Sec. 469(c)(5)** duty to furnish certified copies upon payment of prescribed fees a
   **ministerial** duty, and does the duty to assess the fee arise upon the request?

**A correction I made to my own work:** I had hung question 1 on **Civil Code Art. 487**. Its actual text
is "**Any one of the co-owners may bring an action in ejectment**" [V], which is about actions, not about
records requests. Leaning on it would have overclaimed. Sec. 21(b) needs no help.

**Letter design.** No LGU named (Jonathan's instruction). **No attachments**, deliberately, because every
document imports a fact. One line of standing only. Their charter quoted in the opening. No
processing-time number quoted, since we verified Region 1's and not Region V's; the letter asks them to
state the applicable time, so their answer states their own clock. Severability sentence. Without-prejudice

novel questions upward unasked.

**A banned-term scan was run against the staged message body itself**, not against the working notes:

## 14. THE ONE-PARAGRAPH VERSION

Fourteen months of asking two DILG officers for a finding produced nothing, and this session established
why: Sec. 25(a) gives a supervising officer only the question whether an act is within the scope of its
prescribed powers, so no supervising officer may find a statement false; and A.O. 23 does not cover a
municipal mayor outside Metro Manila, so the Department has no investigating mandate over him even on the
President's behalf. The forums that can reach a signed untruth are the Sangguniang Panlalawigan under
Sec. 61(b), capped at suspension because *Pablico* reserves removal to the courts, and the Ombudsman,
which *Hagad* holds concurrent and which is already seized through CSC Letter 270. In the meantime the
Department can still be made to produce records, opinions and scores, none of which require it to judge
anyone, and the cheapest of these is a **free chartered legal opinion request open to citizens** whose
first question is answered by R.A. 11032 Sec. 21(b) in its own words and which would unlock three
Schedule items and roughly 1,100 days of default. Two letters went out on 29 October and two more are
staged. **The plan for the next three weeks is to wait, because two court dates fall inside the reply
window and the discipline of not writing more letters is the strategy.**
