# ARTA-1319 — Counterfactual: what the Resolution would have said had it included the 29-Jun Manifestation

> Purpose: red-team our own record. Had the Litigation Division taken up the 29 Jun 2026 Manifestation
> (doc 1238) and ruled on every count it identified — §21(e)/§9 with the delivery proof, §21(d), and
> §21(b)/(c) — what would a no-prima-facie Resolution have said, and would anything have changed?
> Method: the same division's own element tests and phrasing from its Zschoche resolutions —
> 0747 (doc 967: §21(b)/(d)/(e) v. Mayor Pajarillo), 1212 (doc 1614: §21(b)/(e) v. Sanggunian),
> and 1319 itself (doc 8160). Text in the boxed blocks is **modelled**, not quoted; the element tests
> and phrases inside them are lifted from those resolutions. Prepared 8 Sep 2026 (Mac desk).

## 0. Bottom line

Nothing changes. Written in the division's own idiom, each added count closes on the same two gates the
actual Resolution used — the §4(f) "government service" threshold and the "not the proper office /
confidential / referral is an affirmative response" due-cause finding — plus one further gate the
division applies to §21(b) and §21(d) that our Manifestation does not survive: **a requirement is
"imposed" only as a prerequisite to a chartered service, and a "disapproval" must exist before the
absence of written notice matters.** The Manifestation's genuine strengths (delivery proof; the
§6/§9(b) statutory ceiling; the admitted Psd-229480 error) are all answerable in that idiom without
reaching their merits. The counterfactual therefore confirms the recommendation: no merits appeal, and
the record-integrity Manifestation is the right-sized filing.

## 1. Count 1 — §21(e) / §9, with the 5-Dec delivery proof (Manifestation ¶2.1)

**What we put in:** the 4-Dec e-mails from penro.camnorte@yahoo.com; the 5-Dec reply in the same
thread; the 7-Nov inquiry answered on 10 and 26 Nov as proof the office treats such inquiries as
actionable; §9's three/seven/twenty-day maxima whatever the classification.

**Modelled finding (division idiom):**

> Even granting that the Manifestation of 29 June 2026 sufficiently establishes that Complainant's
> electronic mail of 05 December 2025 was transmitted to, and reached, the official electronic mail
> address of PENRO-Camarines Norte, the records still do not establish that the said communication was
> received, routed, or assigned to either Respondent for action. Section 21 imposes liability upon the
> person who performs or causes the performance of the prohibited act; it does not create liability for
> an office in the abstract. That Respondents answered an earlier communication of November 2025 shows
> that communications actually brought to their attention were acted upon — it does not show that the
> communication of 05 December 2025 was so brought. More fundamentally, the 05 December 2025 request
> sought certifications and explanations concerning historical boundaries, the methodology employed in
> the processing of the Special Patent, and the reconciliation of the 1997 cadastral survey with the
> senior title. These are not applications "for any privilege, right, reward, license, clearance, permit
> or authorization" acted upon in the ordinary course of business within the meaning of Section 4(f).
> The periods prescribed by Section 9 attach to government services so defined; they do not convert a
> demand for a technical and legal determination into a transaction with a fixed processing time.

**Would the delivery proof have moved them?** No. The actual Resolution already conceded arguendo that
the e-mail reached the address (p. 9) and still closed on attribution and §4(f). The Manifestation's
best evidence was, in effect, already assumed true and found insufficient.

**Where the modelled finding is weakest:** §9(b) reckons the period "from the date the request … was
received." The division has never explained why receipt at the office's own outbound address is not
receipt. It avoids the question by moving to §4(f). That is exactly why proposition (b) of our
record-integrity Manifestation is worth putting to them in writing — and why we should expect no answer.

## 2. §21(d) — failure to give written notice of disapproval (Manifestation ¶2.1)

**What we put in:** no response "of any kind" to the 5-Dec request; hence no written notice.

**Division's element test (0747, doc 967, p. 4):** (1) an application or request; (2) the responding
agency **disapproved** it; (3) no written notice of the denial was given. In 0747 the count failed
because the Mayor "did not disapprove" — he asked for an SPA first.

**Modelled finding:**

> Section 21(d) presupposes a disapproval. The records do not show that Respondents disapproved the
> request of 05 December 2025; on Complainant's own account, the request drew no action at all, which
> is the gravamen of the charge under Section 21(e), not Section 21(d). As to the communications
> Respondents admit having received, the letters of 16 September 2025 and 26 November 2025 are
> themselves written responses stating the office's position and the offices to which Complainant was
> referred. There being either no disapproval, or a disapproval accompanied by written notice, no prima
> facie violation of Section 21(d) is established.

**Assessment:** airtight in their idiom. Silence is a §21(e) fact, not a §21(d) fact, and the two
answered letters are written notices. This count adds nothing and we should not lean on it anywhere.

## 3. §21(b) and (c) — imposition of a requirement and cost not in the Charter (Manifestation ¶2.2)

**What we put in:** PENRO admitted the Psd-229480 error (LMS-25-550) and then conditioned correction of
its own error on Complainant hiring a private Geodetic Engineer and litigating — a requirement and a
cost not in the Charter.

**Division's element test (1212, doc 1614, pp. 3–4):** (1) an application or request; (2) documents
listed in the Citizen's Charter for that service; (3) the agency imposed requirements **additional to
those stated in the Charter**. And the 0747 gloss (doc 967, pp. 3–4): where the Charter "does not
include" the processing of the response at all, "there is no additional requirement other than those
listed in the Citizen's Charter"; a requirement is not "imposed" where the request was **received**
without it and it was only asked for in framing the response.

**Modelled finding:**

> To sustain a prima facie violation of Section 21(b), there must be an imposition of additional
> requirements other than those listed in the Citizen's Charter. The records do not identify a service
> in the Citizen's Charter of PENRO-Camarines Norte under which the correction of cadastral records upon
> a private landowner's request is processed, nor the documentary requirements listed therefor.
> Respondents' letter of 16 September 2025 did not refuse to receive Complainant's request for want of a
> relocation survey; the request was received and acted upon by written reply. The recommendation that
> Complainant engage a licensed Geodetic Engineer was, on Respondents' explanation, a technical remedy
> proposed in view of the office's own prior involvement in the processing of the Special Patent. It was
> not made a prerequisite to any service the office renders under its Charter. Neither is the
> engagement of a private surveyor a fee or charge collected by the office within the meaning of Section
> 21(c); the provision addresses costs exacted by the agency, not expenses a party may incur in
> pursuing a remedy elsewhere. That the correction of an admitted clerical inadvertence in a cadastral
> map might, as a matter of good administration, have been undertaken motu proprio is not a question
> under Section 21(b) or (c). The correctness of the cadastral records and the manner of their
> correction are, as Complainant himself reserves, matters for the proper offices of the Department.

**Assessment:** this is the count we most wanted decided and the one they would dispose of most
cleanly. Three separate exits, each already used elsewhere: (i) no Charter line for the service → no
"additional" requirement by definition (0747); (ii) the request was received without the requirement,
so it was not "imposed" as a prerequisite (0747); (iii) §21(c) reaches fees the agency collects, not
third-party costs. Exit (i) is the same Charter gap that defeated §21(e) — and the division treats the
gap as a shield for the agency, not as the agency's own §6 default. Our reservation (¶4) hands them the
closing sentence.

## 4. The reservation (Manifestation ¶4) and the "not a service" framing

Had they included the Manifestation, the reservation would have been **welcomed and used**: it lets
the division say, in our own words, that the substantive dispute belongs to LMB and the RTC — which
becomes the premise for holding that what remained before ARTA was not a §4(f) service. The actual
Resolution reached the same place without citing us. Net effect of inclusion: the closure reads as
agreed rather than imposed. That is not worse for the LMB/RTC tracks (the reservation still protects
them), but it is not better for the ARTA count.

## 5. The one argument the idiom cannot answer on the merits

The §6(d) / §9(b) point: the Charter **must** state "the maximum time to conclude the process" for each
service, and §9(b) caps the period by statute. Where the office has no Charter line, the statutory
maximum is the period. The division's only escape is the one it took: declare the request not a
§4(f) service at all, so no period attaches. That escape is available because the 5-Dec request
bundled a records/certification component with a technical-determination component. **Lesson:** a
future request to PENRO or the Regional Office must be a single-document, Charter-listed service
(certified copy of X; certification that Y exists / does not exist), fee tendered, with the Charter
line quoted in the request, and nothing else in the same letter. Then §4(f) is satisfied on the face
of the request, and the §6/§9(b) argument has nowhere to hide.
