## 1. The exhibit's point, in three sentences

On 27 May 2025 Jonathan Zschoche requested from the **Municipal** Assessor certified true copies of the estate's tax records (historical declarations, structural assessments, improvements, transaction logs, maps, payment records) — doc 732 (request reproduced within the ARTA complaint), doc 1046 ¶7 (Abla's own admission of the request's scope). Abla answered in writing on 16 June 2025 that the history "cannot possibly be done in just 15 working days" (doc 895), and a year later swore in her Counter-Affidavit that the transaction was "complex and voluminous," and that "immediate delivery [was] impossible without disrupting public service" (doc 1046 ¶¶7, 12). Meanwhile the **Provincial** Assessor's Office — the very office whose Memorandum 2024-01 Abla invoked to withhold records — had **already compiled, prepared, and released to the same requester substantially the same record classes**: the estate's transaction-history ledgers, the 1948/1950-era historical declarations, and the computerized Declarations of Real Property for Barangays 1, 3, and 5, and in writing on 26 January 2026 reported it had "done the research of all transactions" (quoted in docs 730, 2388, 6908).

The sworn "impossibility" is contradicted by an adjacent office's actual performance — on the same parcels, for the same requester, from records the two offices share (Abla's own filing admits "approval of our transaction are being done by the Provincial Assessors Office," doc 6908 [annexed Response to Complaint]).
