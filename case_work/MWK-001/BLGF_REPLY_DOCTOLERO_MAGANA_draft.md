Dear Director Doctolero and Provincial Assessor Magana:

Thank you for the Regional Office's candor. A clear position on the record, even an adverse one, is a courtesy — and it lets me answer both letters, briefly and with equal respect, in one.

**So far, supervision is failing — at every level asked to exercise it.** I send this letter to those who hold that supervision: the Bureau; the Provincial Governor, over his own Assessor and Treasurer; and the Civil Service Commission, which already holds the Authority's referral touching one of the same officers. The record of that failure should sit, once, before all of them.

Here is what the failure shelters — concretely. The heirs hold **TCT No. T-4497 and its derivatives** over an estate the Municipality occupies without a single instrument of conveyance; yet the Municipal Assessor will not certify even the estate's own assessment history — a two-to-three-day service, **paid under O.R. No. 7383466 and unrendered for fifteen months** — though the Provincial Assessor's office already compiled and released it. The records, once certified, would show what is now hidden:

  - **Lot 2-A, TCT No. T-32911** — the western portion along Doña Marciana Moreno Street, Barangay 5 — carried under **no tax declaration whatsoever**, though its chain traces to T-4497 (which bore **Tax Declaration No. 4166**), with **no Deed of Sale, Extrajudicial Settlement, or Partition** ever cancelling that declaration (231- and 46-sq-m items east of the street likewise omitted);
  - the **municipal-hall parcel declared and self-exempted in the Municipality's own name** (**ARP No. GR-2014-HH-07-005-00045**), listing the heirs as its boundary; and
  - a cadastral the **DENR itself corrected in writing** (letter **LMS-25-550, 26 November 2025**, correcting Psd-229480 to the true Psd-221861).

And while the records stay dark, fresh claims arrive: **Mr. Miguel Baliza — who serves in that same Assessor's office** — asserts a **one-hectare, ₱3.4-million "purchase"** of the estate from the late Cesar de la Fuente under a **power of attorney the heirs had revoked**, a payment de la Fuente's own family denies receiving. The withholding is not incidental to these irregularities. **It is their shelter.**

I asked your Offices one thing: to **direct** a subordinate to produce records she is mandated to keep, or to state, per item, the legal ground for withholding (29 June and 13 July 2026). The answer was that this Office is "not a reviewing authority" and "not in a position to determine" my authority. But I did not ask you to review, appeal, or overturn — I asked you to **supervise**. The answer is to a request I never made; the one I made is unanswered. The question and the answer sit side by side, in your words and mine, at **Annex "A."** I would ask any reader of this letter to look there first.

The companion matter tells the same story twice. The 31 July disposition denied my protest **on a file that arrived corrupted and could not be opened** — as the 15 August resubmission confirms — adopted the Treasurer's own account without reading the record, and answered **none of nineteen questions**. A protest ruled on without the file opened; an account adopted without the record read; nineteen questions answered by none — twice, across two subordinates and two months.

I set this down not to wound but to spare. When an office that **holds** supervision declines to use it on a documented record, the declination attaches — permanently — to each thing that record contains; and those things, read together, carry a name in the Civil Service rules I would far rather your Offices never wore. Keeping it off costs **one page each, within fifteen (15) working days**. From the **Region**, a directive to the Municipal Assessor to (i) **certify, in writing, what assessment records for the Barangay 5 parcels do or do not exist, and where**; (ii) **issue or restore the tax declaration for Lot 2-A (TCT No. T-32911) in the owners' names**; and (iii) **produce the improvements/structural-assessment records and tax-map extracts** — or state, per item, the legal ground for withholding. From the **Province**, the **Memorandum 2024-01 §5 clarification**, the **Barangay 1/Barangay 5 reconciliation** undertaken on 1 July, and the improvements and tax-map records its office holds. That moves every item from a schedule of neglect to a record of supervision that worked — and I would say so, gladly, in every forum.

I would rather compel nothing, and thank you instead. But candor requires me to be plain about what the fifteen days decide. The records at issue are **ministerial** — the Code commands the Municipal Assessor to keep and furnish them (Secs. 205 and 207; Sec. 472(b)(4)–(5)) — and **a ministerial duty a supervisor declines to compel, a court will.** Absent action within the fifteen (15) working days, I will seek a **writ of mandamus** to compel production; and your Offices' letters — the service refused below, the supervision declined above — will stand in that record as the proof that the administrative remedy was sought, exhausted, and denied. This same record goes concurrently to the Executive Director of the Bureau and is already before the Office of the President, the Civil Service Commission, and the Office of the Ombudsman. I say none of this as a threat — I do not deal in those — but so the fifteen days read as exactly what they are: the last, and I sincerely hope the welcome, chance for supervision to do willingly what a court would otherwise be asked to compel.

With respect, and in the sincere hope of a short and friendly reply,
