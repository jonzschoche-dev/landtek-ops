The land under the courtroom is, in short, **registered private land, and the DENR itself says so.** A certified copy of the title is Annex "A"; the DENR letter is Annex "B."

**2. The Municipality holds no instrument for it.** The Municipality's own Declaration of Real Property for Lots 401 and 403 calls the property "GOVT. SITE" and leaves the space for an OCT/TCT number **blank**. Its own **Sangguniang Bayan Resolution No. 76, series of 1996**, records the acquisition of the Keesey estate land as merely "proposed," to be completed within ninety days — which never happened. **3. The registered owners have not consented.** The owners have never sold, donated, leased, or otherwise conveyed this land to the Municipality or to the Republic, and have never consented to the use of any part of it as court premises. A Torrens title is imprescriptible; registered land is not acquired by possession, however long. The court is therefore sitting, today, on land the Municipality occupies without right and furnishes to the Judiciary on that footing.

**4. The court's permanent site stands on the same estate.** TCT No. T-4497, the mother title, covers Lot 2, (LRC) Psd-12802 — 139,132 square meters bounded on the northeast by the Pacific Ocean and on the southwest by the Daet–Mercedes Provincial Road — and rests on a **1912 survey**. For Lot 402-A the Municipality entered a **new tax declaration in its own name** on 18 September 2018 — "GOVT. SITE," OCT/TCT number **blank** — re-issued 17 May 2022 in the same terms. No patent, certificate of title, deed, or judgment for Lot 402-A appears in the estate's records, in the DENR's response, or on the Municipality's own declaration; so far as the registered owners can find, the court's permanent site is held on a tax declaration alone, entered on a 2018 survey laid over land titled since 1912 and **disregarding the historical boundaries** of that title. The estate's position is that the 1912 title runs eastward over the whole Lot 402 area; that a special patent, an instrument for disposing of **public** land, cannot issue over land already registered and conveys nothing where it does; and that a tax declaration is not an instrument of title at all. The mother title is Annex "A-1"; the Municipality's 2018 and 2022 declarations on Lot 402-A are Annexes "F" and "F-1."

## Downstream-effects analysis (internal — 3 Sep 2026)

**What OCA will mechanically do with it.** A letter like this does not get adjudicated; it gets *routed*. OCA's standard move is to refer it to the Executive Judge of RTC Daet and/or to the MTC Presiding Judge (Cu) "for comment," and to the Municipality for the instrument. So even though the letter is not addressed to Judge Cu, **she will read it, probably within 2–4 weeks of receipt, with an OCA cover asking her to explain the ground under her own courtroom.** Plan for that, not for a Manila-only readership.

### A. Civil Case 26-360 (the live north star)
1. **Perception of pressure.** Judge Cu receives an OCA referral saying the plaintiff's representative and witness has told the Supreme Court her courtroom is on his family's land without right. Best case she is scrupulously neutral; worst case it colors every credibility call on Jonathan's testimony (9 Sep, 7 Oct, 14 Oct, 4 Nov, 11 Nov). Cannot be un-sent.
2. **Inhibition / re-raffle.** Either side can move. Defendants (Atty. Ramos) get a free delay motion: "the plaintiff's witness has made the court's own premises a subject of his grievance." If granted, 26-360 goes to another MTC or a designated judge — months lost, and the 28 Aug calendar dies. **This is the single largest cost.**
3. **Venue.** If the court accepts the letter's premise it may move sittings; the 9 Sep hearing is *in* the compound. A venue change is a delay, not a win.
4. **Mediation leverage.** Mediation already failed (24 Aug). No effect either way.
5. **Timing mitigation.** Serving after the *last* plaintiff-witness date (11 Nov) removes A1–A3 from the testimony window entirely. Serving after 9 Sep but before 7 Oct only moves the risk. Serving before 9 Sep is the worst option.

### B. The LGU (Mayor Pajarillo, SB, Abla, Macale)
1. **"Sub judice" gift.** The MLGOO already tried to park the compound as sub judice by citing 26-360 (24 Aug); the estate corrected it (26 Aug). A letter to the Supreme Court hands DILG, BLGF, OP and the Mayor a *better* version of the same deflection: "the matter is now before the Supreme Court." Expect it in the next DILG/BLGF reply. Pre-empt by stating in the letter (already partly in ¶7) and in every parallel letter that this is an administrative notice to OCA, not a case, and adjudicates nothing.
2. **Forces the LGU to put its claimed instrument in writing to a judicial body.** The Mayor's 24 July comment to DILG asserts belief in "its own legal title." If OCA asks the Municipality for the instrument, the LGU must either produce the unsigned photocopy (and expose it to the Judiciary) or admit it has nothing. Either answer is evidence for the RTC recovery action. **This is the largest upside.**
3. **Expropriation trigger.** The clean way out for the LGU is LGC §19: SB ordinance + offer + deposit + expropriation. The letter pushes them toward it. That converts the fight from "get out" to "how much" — which is the mandate's actual money outcome (just compensation at fair value, LGU-self-anchored ₱970/sqm → ~₱8.4M on the compound alone) but it also *ends* the recovery-of-possession lever and the Art. 449–450 improvements lever. Decide now whether expropriation is a welcome outcome. (Spine §6 item 7 says yes: "realistic conversion is back-rent / just compensation / reconveyance.")
4. **Retaliation surface.** RPT assessment escalation on the estate's other parcels, permit obstruction, the "all-heirs" gambit renewed. Nothing new — these are already live — but expect intensity.

### C. The Judiciary as a stakeholder (new)
1. **Halls of Justice funding.** If the construction on 402-A is Judiciary-funded (SC building program / JUSIP-type), OCA has a direct institutional interest in clean title and will lean on the LGU to regularize — the best ally the estate could acquire. If it is LGU-funded, OCA's interest is thinner and the letter is mostly a notice of record. **The billboard photo decides which world we are in. Get it before deciding to send.**
2. **COA hook.** A Judiciary or LGU project on unacquired land is an audit finding. The letter creates a paper trail COA can pick up; a copy to COA (Camarines Norte) is a later, separate move.
