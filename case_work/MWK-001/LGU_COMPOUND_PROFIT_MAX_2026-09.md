## 4. The valuation-date trap `[NEEDS-COUNSEL — this is the ₱10M+ question]`

If the LGU (or a court) characterizes its 1996 entry as a **"taking" for public use**, jurisprudence values just compensation **at the time of taking** plus interest — a 1996 Mercedes price, not ₱1,660. That converts a ₱15M claim into a fraction.

Our framing must be, everywhere and consistently: **there was no taking.** The LGU's own 1996 resolution says it was going to *acquire* the land by donation/purchase within 90 days `[V doc 389]`; its 2025 reply claims a *donation* `[V]`; it never expropriated, never deposited, never offered. That is **possession pending a transaction that never closed = occupation by tolerance**, which is **rent-bearing at today's value** and terminable on demand — not eminent domain. Every letter, schedule, and pleading must use "use and occupation / reasonable compensation / regularize," never "taking / inverse condemnation," until counsel picks the theory. The demand letter already does this; the SC draft's ¶5 uses "taken by a patent" — fine for the patent lot, but do not import "taking" into the compound.

Counsel questions: (i) tolerance-rent theory vs. inverse-condemnation theory for a 30-year LGU occupation; (ii) whether accepting a §19 expropriation locks the valuation date to the *ordinance/complaint* date (good) or to 1996 (fatal); (iii) mora date — 15 May 2025 letter vs. service of the formal demand.
