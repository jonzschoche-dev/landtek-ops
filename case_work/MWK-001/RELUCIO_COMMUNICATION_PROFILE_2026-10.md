## 2. The repertoire: four moves, and only four

Everything this office has ever done falls into one of these:

1. **Acknowledge.** Promptly, usually by staff (ADA IV Aguilar, ADA VI Baleza).
2. **Refer sideways or upward**, almost always to the **Governor**, never to her own Regional Director
   or to Central Office. The file leaves the DILG chain rather than climbing it.
3. **Explain why the Department cannot reach the officer complained of.**
4. **Promise a future update**, which is where the 28 Oct conference sits.

**What she has never once done, in eleven months:** issued a directive to any named Mercedes officer,
set any deadline, made any finding, or recorded any non-compliance. The only directives in this entire
record are Guerrero's of 25 and 26 August, which she did not originate and has never enforced.

## 3. Her two stated doctrines, quoted, and where each one breaks

**Doctrine A, 30 March:** DILG issuances "are addressed directly to Local Chief Executives ... and not
to particular functionaries or offices within the LGU," because the LCE "exercise[s] both control and
supervision over their respective functionaries."

*This is her reason for never writing to Abla, Macale, Balane or Olaguera.* It is also, word for word,
**the premise of our own Sec. 444(b)(1)(x) case against the Mayor.** By her doctrine, every failure of
every functionary lands on the Local Chief Executive. Her letter supplies that element for us.

**Doctrine B, 11 May:** "pursuant to the principle of local autonomy, local government units and their
respective officials retain a certain degree of discretion."

*This is the core of every deflection since.* It is a correct statement of supervision as against
control, and it is unanswerable **where discretion actually exists**. It has no application at all to a
**ministerial** duty: furnishing a record on payment of the fee, assessing that fee, deciding a protest
within sixty days, keeping a record book. **The place to press is the line between the two**, and the
question that does it is: is this a matter of discretion, or a ministerial duty under the Code?

## 4. What the profile says about the "pretending" read

The record supports **procedurally correct and unwilling to enforce**, rather than theatre:

- She **verifies** before answering ("upon verification, this Office ... is neither privy to nor has
  been furnished a copy") and she **admits limits honestly** ("the undersigned cannot be certain").
- She **replies, and quickly**: 13 days to the 17 Mar letter, 7 days to the 26 Aug letter, 1 day to the
  27 Oct chaser. **She can never be charged under R.A. 6713 Sec. 5(a).** That defence is built in.
- She has twice put on her own letterhead facts that hurt the LGU and the Province: that no report of
  action was ever received, and that the Province referred the complaint to the very LGU complained of.

**Operational consequence:** she is not the enforcer. She is the **recorder**, and she is a reliable one.

## 5. Tells to watch

- **"We hope to have been of assistance to you."** Appears in the 30 Mar and 11 May letters. It is the
  closing she uses when the file is being treated as answered rather than pending. If it appears on the
  conference apprisal, the matter has been closed in her mind.
- **"We trust that the foregoing sufficiently clarifies the status of your requests."** The 28 Oct 2026
  variant of the same move [V, doc 20248, read 29 Oct]. **There are therefore two close-out formulas,
  not one**, and the second is easy to miss because it sounds like a status report. Treat any sentence
  beginning "We trust that the foregoing" as the file being marked answered. The reply to her 28 Oct
  letter (`../DRIP_LADDERS_2026-10/DILG-PD-RELUCIO/step2_reply_to_28Oct.md`) answers it with one
  neutral sentence keeping the matter open by consent rather than by argument.
- **Referral to the Governor** is her pressure valve. Expect it whenever a question cannot be answered
  with doctrine A or B.
- **Staff acknowledgment then silence** means the item is logged, not actioned.

## 7. How to write to her from now on

1. **Never ask her to direct a functionary.** Her own doctrine says she will not, and asking produces
   doctrine A in reply. Ask the Mayor's office for that, or ask the forum that has jurisdiction.
2. **Ask only for things inside her own file:** status, control numbers, copies, whether a matter is
   open or closed, and whether her Office has received a report. These are the questions she answers
   honestly, and each dated negative is evidence.
3. **Frame the hard question as discretion versus ministerial duty.** Doctrine B cannot survive it.
4. **Quote doctrine A back whenever the Mayor's responsibility for a subordinate office is in issue.**
   It is the Department's own written position.
5. **Keep the velvet.** She answers everything, she is careful, and she is the one official in this
   record who has twice written down something inconvenient to the LGU. That is worth protecting.
