## My concerns, plainly

1. **The trial may be fought too small.** This is not one disputed sale. The estate was carved up
   through **more than twenty transferees — some thirty-five to forty derivative titles issued out
   of our mother title T-4497** — by Cesar de la Fuente acting "as attorney-in-fact," under powers
   of attorney that never authorized any of it, at a fraction of the land's value, with the money
   never reaching us or the estate's taxes. **Seven of the actual deeds are already in our hands**
   and prove the mechanism — one of them a sale to **Gloria Balane's own sister** — and the
   Registry's own title records document the rest, deed by deed, pullable on request. My fear is
   that the trial gets executed as a routine one-parcel dispute and the judge never sees this —
   and this case is the door to recovering everything else, because it is the case that rules on
   the SPA every other bad title depends on.

2. **Our own filed papers have a soft spot the defense has already aimed at.** Our Summary
   Judgment motion and my own affidavit argue the SPA "only allowed negotiating, not selling."
   The defense answers by reading aloud the SPA's clause about "executing documents and receiving
   proceeds." I don't want to lose on our own wording when we don't have to: the stronger ground
   is that whatever selling the SPA allowed was **only to people holding contracts from the prior
   administrator, Ben Llamanzares — and Mrs. Balane swears in her own affidavit she was a
   ₱50-a-month renter with no such contract who never once contacted us.** And the SPA was revoked
   in 2005, eleven years before the sale. I need the trial argued on all of these together, not on
   the one argument they've already answered.

3. **The court has gone silent on two motions that decide the shape of this trial.** No written
   ruling ever issued on our Summary Judgment motion (filed 24 April), and none on the defense's
   motion to bring back Gloria's judicial affidavit, which the court **excluded** at pre-trial.
   The 28-Aug Order says nothing about either. If her affidavit stays out, the defense has no main
   witness statement; either way, I should not be walking into court on the 9th not knowing.

4. **I don't feel the case is being pushed proactively**, and with nine days left I can't fix that
   by worrying. I can fix it by getting you and Atty. Barandon in one conversation, with the
   record in front of you.

## The red flags across the record (one line each — all documented)

1. **Scale:** 20+ transferees, roughly **35–40 derivative titles** issued out of T-4497, all
   through one agent "as attorney-in-fact of the heirs."
2. **Two powers of attorney, and neither authorizes what was done:** the **1991 SPA has no sale
   clause at all** (administration only); the **1992 SPA** permits sales only to holders of
   Llamanzares Contracts-to-Sell, after consulting us — no buyer we have checked qualifies.
3. **Sales continued for eleven years after the authority was revoked** (revoked 15 Aug 2005;
   Balane's sale is 2016).
4. **The Balane deed was executed FIVE times across 2016** — different dates, different notaries,
   one version even mis-citing the SPA's notarial book number. A clean sale is papered once.
5. **Grossly below value:** Balane paid ₱96.6/sqm against the assessor's ₱2.5M market value on
   the same parcel (~10%); the other deeds in hand run ₱43–₱150/sqm.
6. **The money vanished:** the 1992 SPA's stated purpose was to pay the estate's back taxes; the
   estate still owed **₱775,202** in 2023, and no proceeds were ever remitted or accounted for.
7. **The buyers were our tenants:** Gloria swears she paid **₱50/month rent** to the owners' side
   and bought "rights" from a third party for ₱16,500 — renters converted into "owners."
8. **Family web:** one deed in hand is a sale to **Gloria's own sister** (TCT T-50192); Gloria's
   own affidavit names her sister and another buyer as fellow purchasers from Cesar.
9. **Unauthorized hand-off:** in 2012 Cesar purported to delegate the agency to his brother
   Salvador ("Von") — an agent cannot appoint a substitute without our authority — and Salvador
   is the one who signed the family's receipts (earliest **2003**, none naming Llamanzares).
10. **Titles issued four years after the agent died:** Cesar died **21 June 2017** (stated to the
    RTC by his own counsel); Balane's title issued **23 November 2021** — someone else ran the
    registration cascade of a dead man's deed.
11. **Self-ratification attempt:** a March 2017 "Deed of Confirmation" by Cesar tries to confirm
    his own sale — while admitting the land was registered to co-owner Geraldine Hoppe.
12. **The same name on both sides of the registry:** "**Romel C. Jacinto**" appears as
    instrumental witness on the Balane deed and the 2017 Confirmation — **and** as the OIC
    Register of Deeds who issued Balane's title in 2021. (Names read from the document faces;
    whether it is the same person must be confirmed before anything is alleged.)
13. **A sitting public official as buyer:** Gloria was Barangay Kagawad then town Konsehal
    (1997–2017) — spanning both the revocation and her purchase; the law holds her to higher
    diligence, and she admits she never once contacted the owners.
14. **Nothing was ever annotated on our mother title** to warn us — the RD itself wrote in 2020
    that no conveyances appear of record on T-4497 — which is how a 30-year dismemberment stayed
    invisible to owners abroad. Government parcels (the Municipal Hall block, the PNP station)
    were carved out the same era; those are separate matters, but the same hands appear.

None of this needs to be *argued* as conspiracy on 9 Sep. It needs to be **in the record** —
through the deeds, the titles, and cross-examination — so the court sees what kind of transaction
it is ruling on. That is concern #1.
