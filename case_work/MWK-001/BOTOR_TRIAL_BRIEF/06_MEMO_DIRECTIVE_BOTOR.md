## My concerns, plainly

1. **The trial may be fought too small.** The estate roster carries **twenty named transferees**
   — nineteen besides Mrs. Balane. The mother title T-4497's own certified face (a 13-page
   registrar's copy) records **roughly thirty-five registered transactions between 1937 and
   2010**; **seventeen first-generation derivative titles** are documented off it in the
   certified chain (fifteen with a by-number certified copy in our hands), plus the Lot 2-X-6
   sub-branch beneath them, where the Balane parcel sits. The pre-1990s layer ran through the
   prior administrator **Ben Llamanzares**; the layer in suit is **Cesar de la Fuente's**, and of
   his conveyances "as attorney-in-fact of the heirs," **seven are identified in our file — six
   by the deed of absolute sale itself** (Balane, his sale to her sister Hansol, Nacasa,
   Pascual Jr., Victa, Ramirez), the seventh (Vela) so far only by his own 2000 Affidavit of
   Confirmation. My fear is that this gets tried as a routine one-parcel dispute and the court
   never sees what kind of transaction it is ruling on — when this is the case that decides the
   power of attorney the whole de la Fuente layer depends on.

2. **Our own filed papers have a soft spot the defense has already aimed at.** Our Summary
   Judgment motion and my affidavit lead with "the SPA only allowed negotiating, not selling."
   The defense answers by reading the SPA's "sign and execute … receive the proceeds" clause
   "as a whole" (Answer ¶39). I don't want to lose on our own wording when the grounds can be
   argued **in the alternative**: read strictly, the SPA contains **no word of disposition
   anywhere** — sell, convey, alienate, dispose, hypothecate appear nowhere — and read their way,
   whatever sale power it carries is confined **in the same sentence** to buyers holding
   Contracts-to-Sell from the prior administrator, after prior consultation with us, **and Mrs.
   Balane swears in her own affidavit that she was a ₱50-a-month renter who bought "rights" from
   a third party and never once contacted us.** On the stand I will argue none of this: if
   confronted with the clause I concede it and return to the class point.

3. **We are six days out and blind on the docket.** We hold no record of any written ruling on
   our Summary Judgment motion (filed 24 April) or on the defense's motion to bring back Gloria's
   judicial affidavit, which the court **excluded** at pre-trial — and the 28-Aug Order is silent
   on both. But we have not actually run a records check, so we do not know what is in the
   docket. If the exclusion stands, the defense's principal witness statement is out (they still
   hold three others — Salvador dela Fuente, Princess Torralba, Engr. Erwin Balane), and their
   Gloria-in-Canada problem must surface as motion practice before their first date on 2
   December. Either way I should not take the stand not knowing.

4. **I need the case driven, not drifting** — and with six days left the way to fix that is you
   and Atty. Barandon in one conversation with the record in front of you.

## The red flags across the record (each traced to a document in our file)

1. **Two powers of attorney, and neither authorizes what was done with them.** The **1991 SPA
   has no sale clause at all** — administration only. The **1992 SPA**'s only operative verb is
   "to negotiate for the sale," restricted to the prior administrator's Contract-to-Sell holders,
   after consulting us. *(One reconciliation item: our two certified copies of the 1992 SPA render
   the prior administrator's name differently — the clean 2025 LRA copy reads Llamanzares, an
   older consularized copy reads otherwise; read both before building on the clause.)*
2. **The register itself shows the authorized class — and Mrs. Balane is not in it.** The 1970s
   "Kasunduan sa Pagbibilihan" contract-to-sell entries on T-4497's face are the prior
   administrator's buyer class the 1992 SPA refers to. She appears nowhere; by her own affidavit
   she entered as a tenant through Kenneth Asistin (₱16,500 for "rights"; ₱50/month rent to the
   owners' side). *(Whether any of the other de la Fuente buyers held such a contract is
   unproven either way — no roster exists in our file, and that diligence item is open.)*
3. **The Balane deed came eleven years after the authority was revoked.** Revoked 15 Aug 2005;
   her deed is September 2016 — and the machinery kept running after: the 2016–17 duplicate-title
   proceeding (flag 5), the March 2017 "Confirmation," the November 2021 registration. *(Most of
   the other documented sales predate 2005 — those fall on the SPA's limits, not on revocation.)*
4. **The Balane sale was papered five times.** Five versions of the same deed across 2016 — four
   different dates and one undated, one version mis-citing the SPA's notarial book number. A
   clean sale is papered once; we also still need to **elect the one operative deed and cite it
   consistently**, which our papers do not yet do.
5. **He went to court to get the paperwork.** September 2016: Cesar swears an Affidavit of Loss
   over the owner's duplicates of **seven** of our titles including T-52540; in **Spec. Proc.
   No. 2156 (RTC Br. 41, Daet)** the court orders new duplicates issued (30 Jan 2017, final
   13 Mar 2017). The petition ran on the **1991 administration-only SPA**, eleven years after
   revocation, three months before he died. That order is what made the 2016 deed registrable.
6. **Grossly below value — by her own tax declaration.** She paid **₱250,000** for 2,587 sqm
   (₱96.6/sqm) against the estate's assessor market value of ~₱970/sqm (₱2,509,390) — and her
   **own current tax declaration states the parcel's market value at ₱4,294,420**. The other
   priced deeds in hand run ₱43–₱150/sqm.
7. **The money is unaccounted for.** The 1992 SPA's stated purpose was raising money to pay the
   estate's back realty taxes; thirty years on, the estate was still carrying arrears — the 2023
   amnesty proposal put the reduced liability at ₱775,202. The Balane deed recites the agent
   received the ₱250,000; **Salvador swears it went to taxes — we dispute that, it is contested,
   and no accounting was ever rendered.** (An Art. 1891 accounting claim, not a voiding element.)
8. **The family web, proven by their own exhibit.** One deed in hand is Cesar's sale to
   **Gloria's own sister, Rosalina Hansol — procured from the Registry by defendant Erwin Balane
   himself.** It is the sharpest deed we hold: it cites the **1991 SPA, which contains no power
   to sell anything** — void under Art. 1874, no class analysis needed. Gloria's own affidavit
   names her sister and Cesar Ramirez as fellow buyers.
9. **The unauthorized hand-off — and Salvador's own letter.** In 2012 Cesar purported to delegate
   the agency to his brother Salvador ("Von") — an agent cannot appoint a substitute without the
   principal's authority — and Salvador is who signed the family's receipts (earliest **2003**;
   none names Llamanzares; none predates the 1992 SPA). **Salvador's own letter in our file
   admits the de la Fuentes' handling of the estate's affairs** — a written admission from a
   living actor in the chain, which is also where the accounting claim lands now that Cesar is
   deceased.
10. **Cesar died 21 June 2017 — the title issued 23 November 2021.** His own counsel told the
    court of his death; someone else ran a dead man's deed through registration four years later.
11. **The self-ratification.** The March 2017 "Deed of Confirmation" has Cesar confirming his own
    sale — while reciting that the land was **registered in the names of Geraldine K. Hoppe,
    Patricia K. Zschoche and Marcia Ellen Keesey**. His own confirmation names my mother as the
    registered owner of what he sold.
12. **The same name on both sides of the registry.** "**Romel C. Jacinto**" appears as
    instrumental witness on the Balane deed and the 2017 Confirmation — **and** as the OIC
    Register of Deeds who issued Balane's title in 2021. *(Names read from the document faces;
    whether it is one person must be confirmed before anything is said — and even confirmed, it
    is a cross-examination question for a Registry witness, asked as a fact, never an accusation.)*
13. **A sitting public official as buyer.** Gloria was Barangay Kagawad, then town Konsehal,
    1997–2017 — spanning the revocation and her purchase; the law holds her to higher diligence,
    and she admits she never once contacted the owners.
14. **The registry paradox that answers "why didn't you act sooner."** The de la Fuente sales
    were never carried onto the mother title — the RD itself wrote in 2020 that **no conveyances
    appear of record on T-4497** — which is how the dismemberment stayed invisible to owners
    abroad. Yet his *authority* instruments **were** registered: the 1992 SPA sits on T-4497's
    face, and the 1991 SPA was annotated by the RD onto seven derivative titles **including
    T-52540, the title in suit**. And the RD has since **certified in writing that the de la
    Fuente-era deeds themselves are not on file** — an affirmative "no record" certification we
    should be using as evidence, not treating as a gap. *(Patricia's adverse claim has been
    annotated on Balane's title since 26 May 2023.)*

None of this needs to be argued as conspiracy on the 9th. It needs to be **in the record** —
through the deeds, the titles, the register, and cross-examination — so the court sees what kind
of transaction it is ruling on. That is concern #1.
