## Golden rules

1. **Never argue "he couldn't sell at all."** The SPA authorized a sale — to Llamanzares
   Contract-to-Sell holders only. Concede the clause; stand on the class. (If shown your own JA
   Q53, concede the wording and restate the class limitation.)
2. **Never claim personal knowledge you don't have** (the 1992 signing; any 2005 notice to Cesar).
   You testify as Patricia's attorney-in-fact on the documents and family records — plus the
   personal knowledge you do have (you were in the Philippines in 1996 and met Cesar).
3. **Documents over memory.** "I won't guess at survey lots — the certified titles prove it."
4. **Concede small truths instantly and cheerfully** (he WAS the agent — limited; the receipts
   WERE signed — by Salvador; the typo IS there). Deny only the false part. That's what makes your
   "no" on the void sale bulletproof.
5. **Two sentences, then stop.** Silence is not your enemy. No "it appears / I believe / in my
   opinion." Law and pleadings → "that's for counsel."

## The three tells that can sink you

1. **The hedge** — delete "it appears / we believe."
2. **Answering the question you wish they asked** — answer THEIR question in one clean line first,
   then reframe if needed. Don't volunteer.
3. **Over-denial** — see golden rule 4.

## The campaign / "syndicate" question — DISCIPLINE ON THE STAND

You believe this is bigger than one sale. On the stand, that belief is expressed **only through
documents, only when a question opens the door**:
