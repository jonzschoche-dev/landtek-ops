### II. STATEMENT OF FACTS

*(Each paragraph cites the annex that proves it; the full documentary timeline is Annex "2", the Comparator Exhibit.)*

4. On **27 May 2025**, Complainant requested from Respondent certified true copies of the estate's real-property-tax records — historical tax declarations, property assessment records, transaction processing records, tax maps — for the registered lands of the Estate of Mary Worrick Keesey (TCT No. T-4497 and derivatives). The service is listed in the Municipality's **Citizen's Charter** with a processing period of **two (2) to three (3) days** for multi-record research. *(Annexes "2", "3"; the Charter periods as found in Annex "4".)*
5. By letter of **16 June 2025**, Respondent acknowledged the request, undertook in writing to "conduct the history of all the properties declared in the name of Mary Worrick from the oldest book we have (1948)," demanded a research fee — and asserted that the history "cannot possibly be done in just 15 working days." *(Annex "3".)*
6. On **20 June 2025**, Complainant paid the research fee of **₱300.00 under Official Receipt No. 7383466** — a fact Respondent's own pleadings confirm. *(Annex "4", ARTA Resolution recitals; Annex "6", Respondent's Counter-Affidavit ¶7.)*
7. **As of the date of this Complaint — more than fifteen (15) months after the request and the official receipt of payment — the requested service has not been rendered.** The only deliveries were two spreadsheet "advance copies" of land history for Barangays 1 and 3 (September 2025); no certification of history, no historical declarations, no assessment records, no tax maps were ever furnished. *(Annex "2", timeline rows; Annex "4".)*
8. Meanwhile, the **Office of the Provincial Assessor of Camarines Norte** — the office Respondent herself invokes for approvals and restrictions — actually **compiled and released to Complainant the very record classes at issue**: the 1948-book historical declaration of Mary Worrick-Kessey (Provincial Form No. 140); the complete **transaction-history ledgers for Barangays 5 and 1 (1950–2014)**; and computerized Declarations of Real Property for Barangays 1, 3 and 5 bearing the Provincial Assessors' own approval and certification blocks. *(Annex "5", specimen productions; Annex "2" §§3, 5.)*
9. Complainant told Respondent so, directly and in writing, on **1 October 2025**: "I was able to obtain these records from your predecessor Mr. Albos when he was in charge of the Provincial Assessor's Office." *(Annex "7".)* On **26 January 2026**, the Provincial Assessor's Office confirmed **in writing** that it had "Done the research of all transactions." *(As of record in Annexes "2" and "4".)*
10. Despite the foregoing — with the records demonstrably extant, compiled, and released by the adjacent office, and with written notice of that fact — Respondent executed a **Counter-Affidavit on 28 May 2026** swearing that the transaction was "complex and voluminous in nature," that its "sheer volume … constituted an objective physical … challenge," and that "immediate delivery [was] impossible without disrupting public service." *(Annex "6", ¶¶7, 12.)*
11. The same Counter-Affidavit contradicts itself: it swears the requested data "were ultimately provided and received" (¶15) while simultaneously invoking a resolution that the LGU "shall not release" the documents absent a Special Power of Attorney of all co-owners (¶13) — a requirement found in no law, ordinance, or Charter entry, and one this Complainant's apostilled SPA answers in any event. *(Annex "6"; Annex "1".)*
12. Respondent's conflict ran deeper than the withholding: she **sat as a member of the Municipality's Committee on Anti-Red Tape on 6 April 2026 when that body took up and recommended the closure of the very ARTA complaint filed against her** — CART Resolution No. 6, s. 2026, covering CTN SL-2026-0209-1321, records her among the officials present. An officer participating in the collegial disposition of the complaint against herself is a judge in her own cause. *(Annex "9".)*

13. On **25 August 2026**, ARTA recommended closure of Complainant's R.A. No. 11032 complaint, crediting Respondent's impossibility narrative as due cause — while itself finding that the elapsed period "would substantially exceed the two (2) to three (3)-day processing period indicated in the Citizen's Charter." *(Annex "4".)* That threshold disposition under R.A. No. 11032 neither adjudicated nor forecloses Respondent's **administrative liability** under the RACCS and R.A. No. 6713, which are this Commission's to determine.

### III. CAUSES OF ACTION

**A. SERIOUS DISHONESTY.** Dishonesty is the concealment or distortion of truth in a matter relevant to one's office. Respondent, in an official sworn pleading, asserted the impossibility of producing records which — as she had been told in writing months earlier, and as the Provincial Assessor's written confirmation and actual productions establish — had already been compiled, researched, and released by the office she herself credits with approving her transactions ("approval of our transaction are being done by the Provincial Assessors Office" — her own Response, Annex "4"). The distortion was employed **in relation to her official duties, to escape liability and to defeat a citizen's chartered request** — circumstances that qualify the dishonesty as serious. The internal contradiction of her affidavit (¶¶13 vs. 15) independently evidences the distortion.

**B. REFUSAL TO PERFORM OFFICIAL DUTY.** The duty is chartered, specific, and hers; the fee was officially received (O.R. No. 7383466); the service remains unrendered after fifteen months and counting. This is not delay but continuing non-performance — sustained through shifting justifications (complexity; then an extra-legal "all-heirs SPA" condition; then referral of blame to the Provincial Assessor whose office was in fact producing the records).

**C. CONDUCT PREJUDICIAL TO THE BEST INTEREST OF THE SERVICE.** Respondent's conduct — taking a fee for a service never rendered, asserting impossibility disproven by a sister office's performance, swearing to it, and **sitting on the municipal committee that recommended closure of the complaint against herself** — tarnishes the integrity of the assessment service, of the Municipality's Citizen's Charter, and of the very compliance machinery R.A. No. 11032 requires.

**D. R.A. No. 6713, §5(a), (c), (d).** Failure to act promptly on letters and requests within fifteen (15) working days; failure to process documents and complete action on matters within a reasonable time; failure to make public documents accessible to the public — each established by the timeline at Annex "2".

  2. After due proceedings, impose the penalties provided by the 2017 RACCS for the offenses proved;
