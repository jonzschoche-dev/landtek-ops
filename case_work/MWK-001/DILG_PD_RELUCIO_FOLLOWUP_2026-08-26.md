Dear Director Relucio:

I write to follow up on your Office's letter dated **17 March 2026**, which referred my outstanding issues concerning the Municipality of Mercedes **to the Office of the Governor**. I requested that referral under Section 29 of R.A. No. 7160, and I thanked your Office for making it. Five months have now passed, and I have received no advice of any action taken on it.

One clarification on how those issues are properly characterized, because mischaracterization has already cost months elsewhere. My claims do **not** invoke the constitutional right to information or E.O. No. 2, s. 2016, and they never have. They rest on statutes that are not citizenship-bound: **Section 469(c)(5) and (c)(7) of R.A. No. 7160**, which oblige the Sanggunian Secretary to furnish records of a public character to *"any interested party"* upon payment of fees; the entitlement of a **registered owner and taxpayer** to the records of her own property and her own payments; and **R.A. No. 11032**, which protects any applicant or requesting party. I respectfully ask that the referral be understood — and, if necessary, restated to the Office of the Governor — in those statutory terms. Recasting these claims as "FOI" requests and then dismissing them for lack of citizenship is a pattern that has already occurred once: ARTA's CTN SL-2025-1021-0747 resolution was decided on E.O. No. 2 grounds the complaint never invoked, and ARTA itself has since clarified in writing (21 May 2026) that its resolution *"should not be read as a determination"* of any property owner's rights to documents or records.

So that the record is current, allow me to update your Office concisely on what has happened since your referral:

**1.** ARTA has now disposed of the complaints and, in the same dispositive portions its closures are drawn from, issued **onward referrals**: Engr. Erwin H. Balane (Municipal Engineer) to the **Civil Service Commission RO-V** (R.A. 6713 §5(a)); Councilor Princess B. Torralba and Mr. Tony Teope (Assistant to the Municipal Mayor) to the **Office of the Ombudsman** (R.A. 6713; R.A. 3019 §3(i)); and the **entire Committee on Anti-Red Tape of Mercedes — chaired by Mayor Pajarillo himself** — to the CSC and to the DILG. By **OAC-L Letter No. 270, s. 2026 (23 June 2026)**, the CSC forwarded that last referral to the **Ombudsman**, expressly because the official involved is elective.

**2.** ARTA has likewise stated in writing (21 May and 21 July 2026, Litigation Division and the Deputy Director General for Legal) that the underlying matters — records, real-property-tax administration, and related services — are outside R.A. No. 11032 and are *"more properly addressed to the concerned local government unit, **the appropriate supervisory authority**, or other competent forum."*

**3.** On 31 July 2026, MLGOO Guerrero initially closed his action on my request for assistance by deferring the merits to ARTA, the Ombudsman, and the CSC. On **18 August 2026** I answered him with a documented request for supervision under Section 25, itemizing eight specific breaches of the Local Government Code (among them: the Sanggunian Secretary's continuing non-production of session records under §469(c) with fees tendered since 11 February 2026; a matter pending before the Sanggunian without any final action since May 2025; a standing "all-heirs SPA" condition found in no law, ordinance, or Citizen's Charter; and the LGU's written assertion of "title" to the compound it occupies — land the DENR's own records projection places within **TCT T-32911, registered to the Heirs of Mary Worrick Keesey**).

**4. To his credit, the MLGOO has now acted — and I acknowledge it.** By letter of 24 August 2026 and two **2nd Indorsements of 25 August 2026** (received-stamped by the addressees the same day), MLGOO Guerrero: (a) transmitted my 18 August letter **in full** to Mayor Pajarillo as a directive, requiring a written comment/action-taken report **within five (5) working days** on the ARTA-1891/CART referral, the legal and statutory basis of the "all-heirs SPA" condition, and §17/§21 road-lot compliance; (b) separately indorsed the records matters to the **Sangguniang Bayan through the Secretary to the Sanggunian**, requiring a written status report within the same period on the session records (with fee assessments), the §469(c)(6) record book, and the matters pending since May 2025; and (c) **formally transmitted the complete record to your Office** and furnished the Regional Director. Those five-working-day periods expire on or about **2 September 2026**, and the MLGOO has committed in writing to advise me of the LGU's submission.

One clarification is nonetheless necessary, so that a well-intentioned caution does not quietly become a fourth deferral. The MLGOO's letter of 24 August sets aside the Municipal Hall parcel item as *sub judice* by reference to Civil Case No. 26-360. **Respectfully, that premise is mistaken.** Civil Case No. 26-360 is an *accion reinvindicatoria* against **private defendants** concerning a **different derivative parcel** of the estate. Neither the municipal compound, nor TCT No. T-32911, nor the Municipality itself is a party to or subject of that case — or of any case. **No judicial proceeding anywhere is adjudicating the ground on which the Municipality stands.** The position is simply this: the heirs hold a registered Torrens title to that ground (TCT No. T-32911, as the DENR's own records projection confirms); the Municipality, in two years of requests, has produced **no title, deed, expropriation judgment, or instrument of any kind** — and holds **no proof of lawful occupancy** — yet asserted its "legal title" in writing to this very Department. My request has never asked any DILG office to adjudicate ownership. It asks the Municipality to **identify the document** on which its written assertion rests, or to **correct the assertion**. Producing a document — or admitting there is none — is a records and supervision matter squarely within Section 25. It adjudicates nothing, and no *sub judice* principle reaches it.

In plain terms: **complaints against the Mayor, the Vice Mayor's chamber, the Treasurer, the Assessor, and the Mayor's own Assistant were "evaluated" — and recommended for closure — by a committee chaired by the Mayor and populated by the respondents themselves.** ARTA's own Memorandum Circular No. 2023-08 leaves CART composition to the discretion of the head of agency; that discretion was exercised to constitute a body in which no disinterested evaluation of these complaints was possible. This is precisely the institutional defect ARTA placed before this Department by its Notice of Referral in CTN SL-2026-0423-1891 — a referral that remains unacted upon.

**6. The climate this has produced — and its cost.** I must also place on record, on my own account: reports have reached me that officials among the CART's membership — in particular **Mr. Jayson M. Lo (Municipal Administrator) and SB Member Jude Alven S. Hernandez** — have taken an active role in the intimidation of persons merely perceived to be associated with me. I cite one instance as it was reported to me: **Ms. Mary Antonette Ibasco**, a private citizen with no involvement or interest whatsoever in my matters, was reportedly subjected to improper treatment following her election in the municipal senior-citizens' (OSCA) organization, apparently on the perception of association alone. I understand Ms. Ibasco is known in the community, and your Office may well be in a position to inform itself of the incident directly, without relying on my account. Whether she or any other affected person elects to come forward is theirs to decide, and I say nothing here on their behalf — but that is precisely the point: **in the present climate, coming forward is a decision that carries a price, and so witnesses in Mercedes do not readily make it.** A supervisory authority should not need volunteers to notice a pattern its own record already shows. Meanwhile the cost compounds: lawful, tax-paying registered owners are deprived of the use of titled land occupied without instrument, without building permits, and without adherence to the Local Government Code — a continuing loss now measured in millions of pesos.

The picture, respectfully stated, is now this: the Department's **municipal** officer has done what his level permits — the directives are served, the five-working-day clocks run to about **2 September**. What happens next is no longer a municipal question. If the LGU produces the records and answers, supervision will have worked. If it defaults or deflects — as it has at every prior demand — the matter lands, by the MLGOO's own transmittal, **on your desk**, at the level where the Code actually lodges provincial oversight of a component municipality. And one link in the chain remains unaccounted for in either scenario: your Office's own referral of 17 March 2026 to the Office of the Governor, of which nothing has been heard in five months.

  (d) **Advise what provincial supervisory action your Office will take should the LGU default on, or deflect, the MLGOO's 2nd Indorsements** when the five-working-day periods expire on or about 2 September 2026 — so that the next step is already determined and the pattern of expiry-then-silence is not repeated at the provincial level; and
  (e) If your Office declines any of the foregoing, **state the specific legal basis** for declining.

I make this request mindful that your Office was the first to recognize, by its own referral, that these matters warrant the Governor's supervisory attention. All I ask is that the referral your Office initiated be carried to a result — or that I be told, plainly and in writing, why it will not be.
