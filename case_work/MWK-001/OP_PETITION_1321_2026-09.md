Petitioner Jonathan Paul Zschoche, Attorney-in-Fact for Patricia Keesey Zschoche, heir in the Estate of Mary Worrick Keesey, respectfully files this Petition for Supervisory Review over the Anti-Red Tape Authority's Resolution dated 25 August 2026 in CTN SL-2026-0209-1321, received by Petitioner on **27 August 2026**. A Notice of Appeal is filed simultaneously, in the alternative and without prejudice to the supervisory character of this Petition; should this Office deem A.O. No. 22 applicable, the prescribed fee will be paid upon notice. This filing is made within fifteen (15) days of receipt.

**This Petition is unlike a request to re-weigh a discretionary judgment. It presents a single, documentary point: the Resolution excused a fifteen-month non-performance of a chartered 2–3 day service by crediting the respondent official's claim that production of the records was impossible — a claim that the record itself disproves, because the Provincial Assessor of Camarines Norte produced the same classes of records, and confirmed in writing that the research was done, months before the respondent swore it could not be done.**

**I. THE UNDISPUTED FINDINGS ALREADY ESTABLISH THE VIOLATION.**

The Resolution itself finds: (a) the service — certified copies of real property tax records — is listed in the Municipality's Citizen's Charter with a processing period of **two (2) to three (3) days**; (b) Petitioner requested the records on **27 May 2025** and **paid the ₱300.00 research fee on 20 June 2025 (O.R. No. 7383466)** — facts confirmed in the respondent's own annexes; and (c) the elapsed period *"would substantially exceed the two (2) to three (3)-day processing period indicated in the Citizen's Charter."* The service remains unrendered to this day — **fifteen months after the request, and counting**. A chartered service, a fee officially received, and a delay the Authority itself describes as substantially exceeding the published period: Section 21(e) requires nothing more, unless "due cause" excuses the failure. Everything therefore turns on the due-cause finding — and that finding is false in fact.

**II. THE "DUE CAUSE" THE RESOLUTION CREDITED IS DISPROVEN BY THE RECORD.**

The respondent's justification, from her letter of 16 June 2025 to her sworn Counter-Affidavit, was consistent: the properties are *"complex in nature and cannot possibly be done in just 15 working days"*; the transaction was *"complex and voluminous"*; *"immediate delivery [was] impossible without disrupting public service."* The Resolution accepted this narrative. The record proves the opposite:

  1. **The records exist and were produced — by another office.** The **Provincial Assessor of Camarines Norte** released to Petitioner the very record classes at issue: the historical declarations (including the provincial-form Declaration of Real Property of Mary Worrick-Kessey itself), the complete **transaction-history ledgers for Barangays 5 and 1 covering 1950 to 2014**, and more than forty computerized Declarations of Real Property for Barangays 1, 3 and 5 — each bearing the Provincial Assessors' own certification blocks. *(Annex "B" — the Comparator Exhibit, itemized with dates; specimens at Annex "E".)*
  2. **The Province confirmed the work was done — in writing, before the respondent swore it was impossible.** On **26 January 2026**, the Provincial Assessor's office confirmed it had *"[d]one the research of all transactions."* The respondent's Counter-Affidavit swearing impossibility was executed in **May 2026** — months later.
  3. **The respondent knew.** Petitioner informed the respondent directly, in writing, on **10 December 2025**, that he had obtained the predecessor Provincial Assessor's compiled records. And the respondent's own ARTA filing concedes that *"approval of our transaction are being done by the Provincial Assessors Office"* — an admission that the records chain she called impossible runs through an office that had already done the work.

A "due cause" consisting of an impossibility claim, sworn after the impossibility had been publicly and privately disproven, is not due cause. It is the very conduct — the invention of justifications for non-service — that R.A. No. 11032 exists to sanction. An administrative resolution that rests on it rests on nothing.

**III. THE SUPERVISORY QUESTION.**

Petitioner does not ask this Office to substitute its judgment on a discretionary matter. The question presented is narrower and reviewable on the documents alone: **may ARTA close a Section 21(e) complaint on a factual premise (impossibility of production) that is contradicted by the production of the same records in the same period by another office of government?** If the answer is no — and it must be — the Resolution cannot stand. The consequences of letting it stand reach beyond this case: it signals to every service office that a sworn claim of complexity, however false, defeats the Charter.

**IV. RELIEF.** Petitioner respectfully prays that this Office:
  1. **SET ASIDE** the Resolution of 25 August 2026 in CTN SL-2026-0209-1321;
  2. **FIND prima facie evidence** of violation of Section 21(e) (and Section 21(a)) of R.A. No. 11032, or **REMAND** with instructions to evaluate the complaint against the documentary comparator;
  3. **REFER** the matter of the respondent's sworn justification, in light of the comparator, to the appropriate disciplinary authority; and
  4. **CONSOLIDATE** this Petition with Petitioner's petitions already pending before this Office (Refs. 050526-MRO-234187; 060426-MRO-240932), which present the same Authority's recurring threshold practice on the same municipality — and grant such other relief as is just.

Petitioner requests expedited treatment: the withheld records bear directly on the administration of titled estate property and on proceedings now before the DILG and other bodies.
