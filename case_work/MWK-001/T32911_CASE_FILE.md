## 0. Position in one paragraph

The heirs hold a clean, registered title to Lot 2-A: 8,706 sq m, one-third each to Geraldine,
Patricia and Marcia. The title carries no donation and no entry for the Municipality. DENR has
put in writing that Lot 2-A covers the ground of the municipal compound. **Against that, the
Municipality has only a claim.** No signed deed has ever been produced. No Resolution No. 21,
s. 1953 has ever been produced. The deed was never registered or annotated on T-4497 or T-32911.
(The LGU does say it holds tax declarations in its own name "believed" to date from 1966, per a
Municipal Assessor certification cited at the 2 Mar 2026 meeting. A tax declaration is not
title, and ours were never cancelled; the Provincial Assessor's history shows no 1966 change.)

What exists is **one unsigned, retyped copy** (docs 278 and 291 are two photos of it; doc 279
attaches a translation). No one signed it: every party, the witnesses and the notary appear
only as typed "(SGD)." It has no seal, no notarial-register entry, no registration stamp, and no
custodian's certification (scans checked 2026-09-14). By law, a donation of
land exists only in a public instrument with a proven acceptance (Art. 749) `[NC]`. The
Municipality, as the party claiming the donation, has to prove both.

In 1996 the Municipality's own Sanggunian described the land as "owned by Mary Worrick Keesey
Estate" and set out to acquire it (Res. 76-96). That is the Municipality admitting, 43 years
later, that it had nothing.

**Where this leaves the case:** the Municipality is an occupant of registered land with no
proven instrument. The live question is no longer "does the deed convey?" It is "can they
prove a deed exists at all?" That is proven or disproven by two public registers (§4, gaps 1–2).

## 5. Red-team (their best answers)

1. **"The original was lost, but the donation happened; here's the copy and 70 years of
   possession."** They can offer a copy only after proving execution and loss `[NC]`. The
   copy signed by no one, the missing resolution and an empty notarial register (gap 2)
   defeat that. **This is the fight; gaps 1–3 win it.**
2. **"We have possessed since 1953 as owner."** Registered land cannot be acquired by
   prescription or adverse possession (PD 1529 §47) `[NC]`. Their own Res. 76-96 concedes they
   did not hold as owner in 1996.
3. **"The heirs recognized the donation."** Check every heir-side letter for any phrase like
   "donated to the LGU in 1953." The Assessor already frames Lot 2-A that way `[V doc 750]`.
   Our drafts must say "**alleged** donation, never produced."
4. **"If not a donation, it was a public-use taking in 1953. Pay 1953 prices."** See
   GAME_THEORY_EXPROPRIATION §5. They cannot claim a donation and a taking at the same time.
   With no deed, entry was unlawful occupation, not a taking `[NC]`.
5. **"Your own petition values it at ₱5M."** Gap 9.

#### What makes the MTC strong
1. **The ownership defense can't win possession by itself.** In ejectment the court may look at
   ownership only to decide possession. An unsigned, unregistered 1953 copy against a Torrens title
   is our best matchup. The judgment binds only on possession, and the RTC claims stay open.
2. **Speed and leverage.** Summary procedure. A plaintiff's judgment is **immediately executory**
   unless the defendant appeals, posts a supersedeas bond **and deposits the monthly reasonable
   compensation** during the appeal. That pressure produces a lease or purchase. (Whether an LGU is
   exempt from the bond or fees: `[NC]`.)
3. **Money:** reasonable compensation for use and occupation (fair rental value), attorney's fees and
   costs, normally from the demand. The Municipality's own 2023 ₱1,660/sq m valuation is the anchor.

4. **Collecting money from an LGU.** Public funds generally can't be garnished; money judgments go

5. **Lot 402 stays out.** 402-B is a **patent** (OCT 2018000090, the Republic/PNP; the OSG appears),
