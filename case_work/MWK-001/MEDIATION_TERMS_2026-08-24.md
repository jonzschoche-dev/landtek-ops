3. **Surrender of owner's duplicates.** Within 10 days from the Judgment on Compromise, defendants deliver to the Branch Clerk of Court, for transmittal to the Register of Deeds of Camarines Norte, the owner's duplicate certificates of (a) TCT No. 079-2021002126 and (b) TCT No. 079-2021002127.

4. **Vehicle — Judgment on Compromise.** The parties submit this agreement for approval and rendition of judgment. The judgment shall itself direct the Register of Deeds to effect items 2 and 3, and shall provide that if any owner's duplicate is not surrendered within the period, the court may order its annulment and the issuance of a new certificate under Sec. 107, P.D. 1529, on plaintiff's motion, without need of a separate action.

5. **Possession.** Defendants and all persons claiming under them peacefully vacate and turn over the property within **___ days/months** from the Judgment on Compromise (see negotiation note). During that period: no new construction, no transfer of rights, no lease to third parties, and defendants keep the premises in good order. Defendants may remove movable improvements and personal effects; permanent improvements remain without compensation from either side.

6. **Plaintiff's waiver (conditioned on full performance).** Upon completion of items 2, 3 and 5, plaintiff waives, as to defendants, all claims for damages, accounting of fruits and rentals, attorney's fees, and costs prayed for in the Complaint, and will not execute any money claim against defendants in this case.

7. **Defendants' recourse preserved.** Nothing herein prejudices defendants' right to recover the consideration they paid (₱250,000 as they have stated) from the estate of Cesar M. de la Fuente or any other person. Plaintiff will not oppose such recovery and will, on written request, furnish copies of non-privileged documents in her possession relevant to it.

8. **Taxes, fees, RPT.** The parties jointly present the cancellation/reinstatement as the undoing of a void conveyance and not a taxable transfer. Registration fees, documentary requirements, and any BIR clearance or assessment for the cancellation/reissuance are for the account of **[defendants / shared equally — see note]**. Real-property tax on the property is for defendants' account up to the date of turnover and plaintiff's thereafter.

9. **Annotations.** Plaintiff causes cancellation of any notice of lis pendens or adverse claim she has caused to be annotated, upon completion of items 2 and 3.

10. **Scope of release.** The release in item 6 runs only between the parties to this case and covers only civil claims arising from the 2016 sale, the 2017 confirmation, and the 2021 registration of the property. It does not extend to any third party (including the estate of Cesar M. de la Fuente, Salvador dela Fuente, or any notary or registration intermediary), and nothing herein compromises any matter that by law is not subject to compromise (Civil Code, Arts. 2034–2035).

11. **Co-owners.** Plaintiff is one of three co-owners. Items 2–4 inure to all registered owners. Plaintiff's waiver in item 6 binds her own interest; she will endeavor in good faith to obtain the written conformity of her co-owners within 60 days, but this settlement is not conditioned on it.

## PART C — CASE SUMMARY FOR THE JDR JUDGE (hand-across; neutral tone; verified-grade facts only)

**Civil Case No. 26-360, MTC Mercedes — Patricia Keesey Zschoche (by AIF Jonathan P. Zschoche) v. Sps. Efren & Gloria H. Balane, et al.**
*Accion reivindicatoria; nullity of deed; cancellation of title; possession; accounting and damages. Motion for Summary Judgment submitted ~1 June 2026, pending.*

**Property.** Lot 2-X-6-I-4-C-1, Brgy. San Roque, Mercedes, 2,587 sqm — TCT No. 079-2021002126 (Gloria H. Balane, issued 23 Nov 2021), whose face recites its origin: *"T-52540 (TOTALLY CANCELLED) by virtue hereof."* T-52540 stood in the name of the Keesey co-owners.

**The transaction.** September 2016 Deed of Absolute Sale, ₱250,000, signed by Cesar M. de la Fuente "as attorney-in-fact" of the Keesey heirs, invoking a **Special Power of Attorney dated 16 March 1992** (defendants' own Exhibit 6; LRA-certified copy on record). Deed of Confirmation by the same agent, 28 March 2017. Registration effected 23 November 2021 — after the agent's death on 21 June 2017 (stated by his own counsel to RTC Br. 40 in CV 6839).

**Why plaintiff says the case is decided on the papers.**
1. *The March 1992 SPA is a special power of limited object.* Its operative clause (LRA-certified copy) authorizes the agent "to negotiate for the sale of the above-described real estate property **in favor of third persons in possession of CONTRACTS TO SELL executed by the previous administrator, MR. BEN LLAMANZARES** and/or to those who have paid their down-payments or have made partial payments therefor **to the latter** … **after prior consultation with us**" — and its own recital states the purpose: to raise funds to settle the estate's unpaid real-estate taxes. Under Civil Code Arts. 1877–1878 a power involving an immovable is express and confined to its special object; a general "any and every act" clause is read down to that object (*Bautista-Spille v. Nicorp*, G.R. No. 214057, 19 Oct 2015).
2. *Mrs. Balane fails every limit — by her own account.* Her Judicial Affidavit states she entered the land by buying "rights and possession" from Kenneth Asistin Jr. for ₱16,500; paid ₱50/month rent to the owners' side; holds no Llamanzares Contract to Sell; and had no communication with any Keesey owner before the sale — so there was also no "prior consultation." Defendants' earliest receipt (their Exhibit 5) is dated 20 Oct 2003 — eleven years after the SPA — and none of their receipts names Ben Llamanzares.
3. *Consequence.* A sale beyond the agent's mandate conveys nothing; good faith cannot cure it (*nemo dat quod non habet*). The good-faith defense goes only to equities on possession and improvements — not to title.
4. *Independent ground.* The agent's authority was withdrawn by the heirs' SPA of August 2005 in favor of Ida M. Buenaventura (disowning any other SPA), published 2020, annotated 2021. One dealing with an agent must inquire into the authority (*Yoshizaki v. Joy Training Center*); Mrs. Balane admits she did not.

**Defendants' strongest facts, fairly stated.** Long occupancy (claimed since 1975); payments to the agent; taxes and improvements; no annotation of revocation before 2016. Plaintiff's position: these bear on *how* and *when* possession is turned over and on damages — and plaintiff's proposal gives them full weight — but they do not reach title.

**What plaintiff seeks by judgment.** Cancellation of TCT 079-2021002126; reinstatement of T-52540; return of the owner's duplicate of TCT 079-2021002127 (remainder lot, in the heirs' name, held by defendants); possession; accounting and damages.

**What plaintiff offers in settlement (detail in Part A).** The same title relief, by Judgment on Compromise — in exchange for **waiver of all damages, accounting, rentals, fees and costs**, a reasonable possession period, no claim to improvements, and **no opposition** to defendants recovering their ₱250,000 from the estate of Cesar M. de la Fuente. Plaintiff is one of three co-owners and says so openly; the title relief benefits all owners, the waiver is hers.
