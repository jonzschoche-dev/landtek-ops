# DPWH letter — what it rests on, and the three things that can break it

> Internal working note, 16 September 2026 (Mac desk). LandTek work product. Not a legal opinion; LandTek is not a law firm.
> Instruments: `DPWH_NBC_ENFORCEMENT_REQUEST_2026-09.md` (main) · `MEO_205_ENFORCEMENT_REQUEST_2026-09_draft.md` (predicate).

## 1. Why it is not styled an "appeal"

P.D. 1096 **§307** is the Code's only appeal to the Secretary, and it is narrow — verbatim from the law
library (source `dpwh.gov.ph/DPWH/files/nbc/PD.pdf`, legal_chunks 4579):

> "Within fifteen (15) days from the date of receipt of advice of the **non-issuance, suspension or
> revocation of permits**, the **applicant/permittee** may file an appeal with the Secretary…"

We are neither applicant nor permittee, and no advice of non-issuance has issued to us. Filing a §307
"appeal" invites a one-line dismissal for want of an appealable order. The letter therefore invokes
**§§201, 203 and 205** — the Secretary's own administration of the Code and his supervision and
control over the Building Official he designates. Same destination, a door that is actually open.

## 2. The three things that can break it

### (a) The predicate is missing — fix before serving
Per `BASKET_OF_SERVICES_STRATEGY_2026-09-16.md`, the **§205/§207 enforcement duty has never been
formally invoked in writing** — it is listed there as "Unused, and the strongest duty we have." A
supervising department asked to supervise a duty nobody has yet demanded will refer it back down.
Hence the companion letter, to be served first or same-day, with the receiving copy enclosed to DPWH
as Annex "G". **Serving the DPWH letter alone is the weak version of this move.**

### (b) The addressee of the predicate is a CV 26-360 defendant
`DEMAND_CLOCK_LEDGER_2026-09.md` line 76 holds any instrument to Engr. Balane pending **Atty.
Barandon's** clearance, because he is a defendant in Civil Case No. 26-360 (operator-verified
2026-09-14, entity 3060 — *not* Gloria). That hold governs the companion letter outright. It arguably
reaches the DPWH letter too, since he is the officer complained of. **Counsel clears both, or neither
goes.** The DPWH letter's ¶9 discloses the litigation rather than hiding it — that is the safer posture
either way, but it is not a substitute for asking Barandon.

### (c) "Unpermitted structures" is true of one structure, not of the estate
This is the one that could actually cost us. `T4497_IMPROVEMENTS_LEVER.md` §0.1 and §5.5 are explicit:
the corpus shows permits **were** issued for the titled BCTUE subdivision (doc 959 pleads officials
"knowingly allowed the issuance of building permits… based on these void titles"). A blanket
"no structures on this estate are permitted" claim is **wrong there and impeachable**. Worse,
§5.6: the corpus holds **zero proven structure inventory** — no photograph, no building tax-dec entry,
no OBO record in hand.

So the letter splits the claim three ways in ¶7, and that split is deliberate:
- **Teope structure, Brgy 1** — no permit, *admitted in writing by the Building Official himself*. This is the one hard fact.
- **Other private/informal structures** — we assert only that *nothing has ever been produced* for them, and that only his register can say. That is true and unimpeachable.
- **Titled adverse claimants** — permits may exist; the live question is whether **§302(2)** could have been satisfied, since the certified TCT is in the heirs' names and no lease exists.

Do not let anyone flatten ¶7 back into "all the structures are illegal." It would hand them the one
contradiction that matters (the campaign's own discipline rule III.1).

**§309 is the sleeper.** Jonathan's instinct about certificates of occupancy is legally sharper than the
permit point: a structure never permitted cannot have passed the final inspection and Certificate of
Completion that §§308–309 make the precondition of a Certificate of Occupancy. So the occupancy
violation is **continuing**, every day, independent of when the building went up — and §207 gives the
Building Official express power to order occupancy discontinued. That defuses the "it's been there for
years" answer, which is otherwise their best one.

## 5. Where this sits in the campaign

It is the **DPWH lever** already identified at `OP_LEVERS_BEYOND_SUPERVISION_2026-09-14.md` §A row 3 and
§E item 5 — but pointed at *enforcement* rather than at the 0690/0792 permit **records**. That is the
better aim: records requests in this cluster have been closed five times on the ARTA §4(f)
"not a government service" wall (`project-arta-cluster-positioned-to-lose`). **Enforcement of the
Building Code is a duty, not a service.** No §4(f) wall stands in front of it, and no citizenship
question reaches it either — which is why the letter says, in its own second paragraph, that it asks
for no record and no information. Keep it that way.
