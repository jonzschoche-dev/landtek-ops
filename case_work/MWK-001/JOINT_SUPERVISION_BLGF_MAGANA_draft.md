Dear Executive Director Agcaoili:

I write to the Central Office because **every layer of supervision below it has now either defaulted or declined in writing**. The Municipal Assessor of Mercedes has left a paid, chartered records service unrendered for fifteen months (Annex "A", items A-1 to A-7). The **Provincial Assessor** — whose duty under **Section 472(b)(1), R.A. No. 7160** is to *"ensure that all laws and policies governing the appraisal and assessment of real properties for taxation purposes are properly executed"*, and whose own **Memorandum No. 2024-01** governing record release is proof that the municipal assessors' practices are within his reach — committed in writing on 1 July 2026 to act, received the certified titles he requested, and has issued **nothing in the two months since** (items A-8 to A-9). And on **24 August 2026** (released 3 September), the **Regional Office itself declined in writing** "to issue a directive compelling the Municipal Assessor of Mercedes to release or certify the requested records" (Annex "I") — the same Regional Office that had invoked the Bureau's supervision under **E.O. No. 127**, by citation, in its letter of 31 July 2026. The authority was invoked to decline relief, and then declined when asked to grant it. That leaves this Office: the Bureau's administrative and technical supervision over local assessment operations is now the only supervision left standing. Beneath both duties sit **Sections 200 and 201 of the Code**: the province *"primarily responsible for the proper, efficient and effective administration of the real property tax"*; the **Department of Finance** the maker of the rules for *"the classification, appraisal, and assessment of real property."*

The conduct is itemized, in depth and with its instruments, at **Annex "A"**. In one sentence: for **fifteen months**, the Municipal Assessor has withheld a chartered 2–3 day records service for which she officially received payment — justifying the withholding **under oath** on grounds of impossibility that the **Provincial Assessor's own office disproved by actually performing the work**, and under a **Provincial Assessor's memorandum whose own text does not permit it**.

The Regional declination deserves a word, because its own terms decide this request. It rests on a single ground: that the Municipality, "through its CART," required proof of legal personality, and the Region "is not in a position to determine" the requester's authority. Consider what that adopts: **the condition is the "all-heirs" requirement found in no law, ordinance, or Citizen's Charter; the body imposing it is the CART that ARTA has referred to the Civil Service Commission and, through it, to the Ombudsman — a committee on which the Municipal Assessor sat while it disposed of the complaint against her**. A supervising office that defers to the supervised office's extra-legal condition has not exercised supervision; it has surrendered it. And the authority the Region says it cannot determine is determined already: by an **apostilled Special Power of Attorney of record**, by the **Provincial Assessor's own Memorandum No. 2024-01, Section 5** (exempting a party to a pending case), and by a **court of law** entertaining the same authority in active litigation. Meanwhile the declination concedes the decisive fact: it is *"not... a finding that the records do not exist"* and the records "may still be requested... upon compliance." By the Region's own letter, fifteen months of blockade now rest on a single documentary question that three instruments of record have already answered.

And the stakes are larger than one unrendered service. **The records in the Municipal Assessor's charge are not merely withheld — the withheld record itself carries documented irregularities, and while it stays dark, the property remains prey.** The estate's registered lands have for years been worked over by a pattern of transactions that functions as a syndicate whether or not it is organized as one: conveyances executed under a **special power of attorney revoked years before**; a claimed one-hectare purchase for ₱3.4 million — asserted by a person **inside the Assessor's own office** — under that same revoked authority, with the attorney-in-fact's own family denying the payment was ever received; assessment records resting on a **highly erroneous cadastral survey** — errors acknowledged in writing by the DENR itself, down to a mislabeled lot designation its own letter of 26 November 2025 had to correct — laid over a **senior, 1912-surveyed Torrens title** that no cadastral can defeat; structures standing on titled estate land **without permit or declaration**, including by municipal officials; and the Municipality itself declaring and self-exempting parcels within the estate's registered title while never declaring its own improvements. The certified histories, assessment records, FAAS, and tax maps this letter seeks are precisely the documents that expose and correct that pattern — **which is the most economical explanation of why, fifteen months and a paid fee later, they have not been produced.** I do not ask the Region to adjudicate any of it. I ask for the records that would let the proper forums do so — and I note that withholding them is the pattern's best protection.

My request is short. **Supervise — or say in writing that you will not.** Because the alternative must be stated plainly: if the conduct at Annex "A" draws no supervisory response from the Central Office — after the municipal layer committed it, the provincial layer slept on it, and the Regional Office declined it in writing — then the Bureau will have effectively **endorsed that conduct as its own national standard** of assessment practice: every proposition at Annex "A", adopted at the top. Silence does not merely tolerate the conduct — it adopts it.

I do not believe the Regional Office intends that adoption — and I note, so that the stakes are plain, that the conduct at Annex "A" is already the subject of proceedings before the **Office of the President**, the **Civil Service Commission's referral chain**, and the **Office of the Ombudsman's** intake in related dockets. Whatever this Office does — or declines to do — with this letter **becomes part of those records**. A copy of this letter goes to the **Civil Service Commission's Assistant Commissioner for Legal**, who already holds the Authority's referral touching this same officer's conduct as a member of the Mercedes Committee on Anti-Red Tape — so that the disciplinary record and the supervisory record may read each other. **WHAT IS REQUESTED** — so that there can be no doubt about what compliance looks like, I ask the Central Office, within **fifteen (15) days of receipt**, to **review and set aside the Regional Office's declination of 24 August 2026** (Annex "I") and to issue **two written directives and one written response**:

  **1. A directive to the Municipal Assessor of Mercedes** to render, within a definite period the directive states, the service paid for on 20 June 2025 — or to state in writing the specific legal ground for each item withheld.

  **2. A directive to (or through) the Provincial Assessor of Camarines Norte** to: (i) clarify in writing the application of his Memorandum No. 2024-01, including its Section 5 pending-case exemption; (ii) complete the Barangay 5 municipal–provincial reconciliation his office undertook on 1 July 2026; and (iii) furnish the improvements records and tax-map extracts requested 29 June and 13 July 2026, with lawful fees assessed for payment.

  **3. A written response to the undersigned** stating the action taken on each item — and, **as to any item this Office declines, saying so in writing, item by item**, so the record reflects exactly which conduct at Annex "A" carries the Bureau's endorsement.

One last thing must be said without decoration. **With this letter, notice to the Bureau, at every level, is complete** — and, by the copies furnished, to every office above and below it in the chain. The duties are statutory, the conduct is documented, and the proof is attached. From this day forward, in the proceedings already seized of these matters, the question will no longer be only what the Municipal Assessor did — it will be **who, holding the legal power and written notice to correct it, chose to let it stand.** The law does not confine accountability to the officer who commits a breach; it reaches the officers whose duty was to check it — administratively, and civilly under **Article 27 of the Civil Code**, which holds personally liable any public servant who "refuses or neglects, without just cause, to perform his official duty." The undersigned would far prefer to record this Office on the other side of that question — as the supervision that worked.

## ANNEX "A" — SCHEDULE OF THE CONDUCT WHICH, ABSENT SUPERVISION, STANDS ENDORSED
*(Municipal Assessor's Office, Mercedes, Camarines Norte. Each item states, first, the proposition your offices adopt by declining to supervise — then the documented facts. Quotations verbatim.)*

**A-1. What silence endorses:** *a municipal assessor may take a citizen's money for a two-to-three-day chartered service and render nothing for fifteen months — and counting.*
The facts: written request 27 May 2025; Citizen's Charter period **2–3 days**; research fee **₱300.00 officially received 20 June 2025 (O.R. No. 7383466)**; as of this letter, no certification of history, no certified declarations, no assessment records, no tax maps — only two informal spreadsheet "advance copies" (September 2025). ARTA itself found the elapsed period "would substantially exceed the two (2) to three (3)-day processing period" (Resolution, 25 August 2026, CTN SL-2026-0209-1321).

**A-2. What silence endorses:** *an office may undertake a service in writing and defeat it in the same letter by declaring it impossible.*
The facts: the Assessor's letter of 16 June 2025 undertook to "conduct the history of all the properties declared in the name of Mary Worrick from the oldest book we have (1948)" — and in the same letter asserted the history "cannot possibly be done in just 15 working days."

**A-3. What silence endorses:** *an official may swear to an impossibility she has been told, in writing, is false — and the oath will be credited over the proof.*
The facts: the Provincial Assessor's own office **compiled and released to the requester the very record classes at issue** — the 1948-book historical declaration (Provincial Form No. 140), the Barangay 5 and Barangay 1 transaction-history ledgers (1950–2014), and the computerized Declarations of Real Property for Barangays 1, 3 and 5, **each bearing the Provincial Assessor's own approval or certification blocks** — and confirmed in writing on **26 January 2026** that it had "[d]one the research of all transactions." The requester told the Municipal Assessor so in writing on **1 October 2025**. Her Counter-Affidavit swearing that "immediate delivery [was] impossible" was executed **28 May 2026** — months after all of it.

**A-4. What silence endorses:** *an office may cite a superior office's issuance to justify precisely what that issuance does not permit.*
The facts: the withholding is justified under **Provincial Assessor Memorandum No. 2024-01**, whose **Section 5 exempts "any party to a pending case"** — which the requester at all material times was — and which **excepts tax maps entirely**. Her own ARTA filing concedes the dependency: "approval of our transaction are being done by the Provincial Assessors Office."

**A-5. What silence endorses:** *an office may invent a documentary requirement found in no law, no ordinance, and no Citizen's Charter — and enforce it as though it were law.*
The facts: estate services conditioned on a Special Power of Attorney "signed by all the heirs" (Councilor Ong letter, 26 January 2026; restated over the Mayor's signature, 24 July 2026; Counter-Affidavit ¶13) — contrary to Article 487 of the Civil Code, and answered in any event by the requester's apostilled SPA from a registered co-owner heir.

**A-6. What silence endorses:** *an official may swear both that the records were delivered and that they are being withheld — in the same affidavit.*
The facts: Counter-Affidavit ¶15 swears the requested data "were ultimately provided and received"; ¶13 of the same affidavit invokes a resolution that the LGU "shall not release" them. Both were sworn on 28 May 2026.

**A-7. What silence endorses:** *an official may sit in judgment of the complaint against herself.*
The facts: on **6 April 2026** the Assessor sat as a member of the Municipality's Committee on Anti-Red Tape when it took up and recommended closure of **the very complaint filed against her** — CART Resolution No. 6, s. 2026 (CTN SL-2026-0209-1321), her name among those recorded present.

**A-8. What silence endorses:** *a provincial verification may begin, take the citizen's certified titles — and simply never conclude.*
The facts: the Provincial Assessor's office committed on **1 July 2026** that "all your requested relief will be replied with," and requested the certified titles and survey plan; the requester transmitted them the next day. Two months on: no reconciliation of the Barangay 5 records, no reply of any kind.

**A-9. What silence endorses:** *entire record classes may be denied by every office at once — including the classes excepted from every restriction any office has cited.*
The facts: the **improvements/structural assessment records** and the **tax maps** — requested of the Municipal Assessor 27 May 2025 and of the Provincial Assessor 29 June and 13 July 2026 — have never been produced by any office; tax maps sit outside even Memorandum 2024-01's restriction, and the Provincial Assessor's 1 July 2026 letter confirms his office maintains "the tax mapping records."

**A-10. What silence endorses:** *a supervisory review may answer none of the questions put to it — and still be called supervision.*
The facts: the Formal Protest and Supervisory Request of 13 July 2026 put nineteen numbered questions and record requests to this Regional Office; the disposition of 31 July 2026 answered **none**; the complete resubmission of 15 August 2026 was acknowledged 17 August ("referred to the concerned personnel") and nothing has issued since. The Region has now answered twice: first by answering nothing, then by declining in writing (Annex "I"; item A-13). **The Central Office is the supervision that remains.**

**A-13 (the newest entry). What silence endorses:** *a regional supervisory office may decline to supervise by adopting the supervised municipality's extra-legal condition, declaring itself "not in a position" to read an apostilled instrument of record, and directing the citizen to "the appropriate office or tribunal" without naming one.*
The facts: BLGF Regional Office V letter of 24 August 2026, released 3 September 2026 (Annex "I") — declining "at this time" to issue any directive; resting on the CART's legal-personality condition; conceding the action is "not... a finding that the records do not exist."

**A-11. What silence endorses:** *an assessment record may carry documented irregularities — and the office charged with it may keep it dark rather than correct it.*
The facts: the assessment records for the estate's barangays rest on a **highly erroneous cadastral** — the DENR's own letter LMS-25-550 (26 November 2025) had to correct a mislabeled lot designation (Psd-229480 for the correct **Psd-221861**) and acknowledged the discrepancies in the cadastral records; a request for interim suspension of transactions **pending cadastral correction** is of record with the DENR; the municipal and provincial histories for Barangay 5 diverge (the Municipal Assessor's 17 September 2025 position — that the Brgy 5 history is a single 1950s donation — against the Provincial ledger showing the full transaction history); the Municipality's own improvements on estate land stand **undeclared**, while the Municipality declared and **self-exempted** a parcel within the estate's registered title.

**A-12. What silence endorses:** *while the records stay dark, claims against the property may keep coming — including from inside the records office itself.*
The facts: the estate's conveyance history includes instruments executed under a **special power of attorney revoked years before their execution**; among the claims raised to the estate is a **one-hectare purchase for ₱3.4 million asserted by a person serving in the Assessor's own office**, under that same revoked authority, with the recipient family denying payment was received — raised to the Municipal Mayor in writing on 1 October 2025 and never resolved. The withheld transaction and history records are the very documents by which such claims are tested. *(The estate's title litigation is before the courts; nothing here asks any office to adjudicate it.)*
