# GAME THEORY — WHAT HAPPENS IF THE LGU EXPROPRIATES

**MWK-001 · built 2026-09-07 · INTERNAL — not for service**
Companion to `GAME_THEORY_LIABILITY_2026-09.md` (the supervision/liability game). This one
prices the branch that game does not cover: **the Municipality stops defending the donation
and takes the land the lawful way.**

**Status of the board when this was written (operator, 7 Sep): the DILG path is the ONLY live
track. NOTHING ELSE HAS BEEN SERVED.** No demand has gone to the Mayor or to any officer; the
whole suite in `DEMAND_CLOCK_LEDGER_2026-09.md` is unserved and every clock in it is unstarted.
What the LGU has actually felt for the last three weeks is **supervision and nothing else** —
the 18 Aug letter, the 25/26 Aug indorsements, the 26 Aug clarification, the 1 Sep replies.

That matters more than it looks. It means (a) the estate still holds first-mover choice on
every instrument; (b) the LGU has never been given a *price*, so nothing has yet pushed it
toward the expropriation exit; and (c) **no document of ours has yet asserted a date of taking**
— which, per §5, is the one thing we must not give away by accident. See §7, which is the part
that changes what goes out.

---

## 1. The answer in one paragraph

Expropriation is **not their attack — it is their escape hatch**, and on the compound parcel
it is probably close to the estate's realistic best outcome *if the price is set right*. It
costs them the donation claim permanently (you cannot expropriate what you already own), it
converts an uncertain title fight into a valuation fight the estate is better positioned to
win, and it does not erase the COA/Ombudsman exposure for money already spent — it documents
it. **But it carries one clause that can gut the entire LGU-recovery thesis: §19's final
proviso pays fair market value *at the time of the taking*.** If they establish an early
taking date, the parcel's principal falls to roughly **₱86** in 1953 money, and no interest
rate rescues that. The whole game is the taking date — and the currently-drafted demands
would hand it to them in our own handwriting.

---

## 2. The statute, verbatim (RA 7160 §19 — embedded law library)

> *"A local government unit may, through its chief executive and **acting pursuant to an
> ordinance**, exercise the power of eminent domain for public use, or purpose or welfare for
> the benefit of the poor and the landless, upon payment of just compensation … Provided,
> however, That the power of eminent domain **may not be exercised unless a valid and definite
> offer has been previously made to the owner, and such offer was not accepted**: Provided,
> further, That the local government unit **may immediately take possession** of the property
> upon the filing of the expropriation proceedings and upon making a deposit with the proper
> court of **at least fifteen percent (15%) of the fair market value of the property based on
> the current tax declaration** …: Provided, finally, That **the amount to be paid … shall be
> determined by the proper court, based on the fair market value at the time of the taking of
> the property.**"*

**Four gates they must pass, in order:** (1) an **ordinance** — not a resolution `[HV: the
ordinance-not-resolution rule is settled doctrine but the controlling case is NOT in our
corpus; verify before counsel relies]`; (2) a **valid and definite offer** to the owner,
refused; (3) **filing** + **15% deposit** on the current tax declaration → immediate
possession; (4) **court-determined** just compensation at time-of-taking value.

## 3. What it costs them — the admissions they cannot take back

| # | The moment | What it concedes | Where it lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The offer letter** (gate 2) | You cannot make a "valid and definite offer" to buy what you already own. The offer is, by itself, a written admission of the estate's ownership — **before** any filing. | Kills the 1 Sep "valid Deed of Donation" position and the 24 Jul "legal title" claim, permanently and voluntarily |
| 2 | **The ordinance** (gate 1) | Requires the Sanggunian to deliberate and enact **on the public record** — the same body that has produced nothing since May 2025 and whose minutes are "being verified" | Creates the very record Olaguera has been withholding; the §469(c)/§52(e) file gets its documents from their own necessity |
| 3 | **The appropriation** (gate 3–4) | Money must be appropriated to pay for land — which is a written statement that the land was never theirs to build on | The ₱2.88M Jimmy P. | 4 | **The pleading itself** | An expropriation complaint names the defendant as **owner**. It is a judicial admission. | Travels to every other track: RPT protest, the Assessor records fight, the T-4497 recovery spine |

**The asymmetry:** every gate they must pass produces a document the estate has spent fifteen
months failing to obtain by asking.

## 4. What it costs us — honestly

1. **The land is gone.** It becomes a money claim. Sea-front Poblacion land, permanently
   converted to a receivable. If the estate's mandate prizes the *parcel*, this is the loss.
2. **Arts. 449–451 evaporate on that parcel.** No forfeiture of their improvements, no
   demolition leverage, no bad-faith-builder claim going forward. **Expropriation is their
   cheapest exit from exactly the exposure the demand suite is built to create.**
3. **Cheap immediate possession.** The 15% deposit is computed on **their own current tax
   declaration** — on the compound ARP (MV ₱8,682,470) that is **₱1,302,370.50**. For about
   ₱1.3M they convert unlawful occupation into lawful possession, on day one, and the meter
   stops.
4. **Practical mootness of a defence we may already have lost.** Where public structures have
   been erected and public use has attached, courts commonly refuse to order recovery of
   possession and confine the owner to just compensation `[HV — doctrine not in corpus;
   counsel must confirm]`. If that is right, expropriation does not take away an option the
   estate realistically had. It formalizes the outcome a court would likely reach anyway.
5. **Scope.** They would take the compound footprint only — leaving the PNP parcel, the sports
   complex, Teope, and the informal settlers exactly where they are, while banking the
   precedent that the estate settles for money.

## 5. The taking-date trap — this is the whole game

§19 pays **"fair market value at the time of the taking."** Run the numbers on the same
8,951.22 sqm the LGU declares:

| Reckoning date | Basis | Principal |
|---|---|---|
| **1953** (the donation deed's date) | T-111's own recital: 26.9312 ha assessed at ₱2,600 → pro-rata | **≈ ₱86** |
| ...with 73 years of 6% simple interest | | ≈ ₱465 |
| ...with 73 years compounded at 6% | | ≈ ₱6,080 |
| **Today**, at the LGU's own ARP ₱970/sqm (8,706 sqm) | doc 152 | **₱8,444,820** |
| **Today**, at Jonathan's stated prevailing ₱10,000/sqm | `[ESTIMATE — appraisal TO-OBTAIN]` | **₱87,060,000** |

**Read that table twice.** The working files have assumed that an early taking date lowers the
principal but that "the interest is where the money is." **On these figures that assumption is
wrong by three orders of magnitude.** Interest on ₱86 is nothing. If the LGU establishes a
1953 or even a 1996 taking, the entire ₱25–29M LGU-recovery envelope in
`REVENUE_ACTIVATION_MAP.md` (O1) collapses. This is the single largest unpriced risk on the
board, and it is not flagged as such in `T4497_OFFENSIVE_REVENUE_FRONTS.md` §2.1, which
treats the taking-date rule as a discount rather than as an existential threat.

**Why the estate is not defenceless on it — three arguments, all NEEDS-COUNSEL:**
- **A donation is not a taking.** 1953 was a *voluntary conveyance*, however defective. If it
  conveyed, there is nothing to expropriate. If it did not, entry was **unlawful** — and an
  unlawful entry is not the "taking" §19 contemplates for valuation purposes `[HV]`.
- **Their own record dates it later.** Res. 76-96 (13 Mar 1996) says acquisition was still
  *"proposed"* — the LGU's own admission that as of 1996 it had not taken by any lawful means.
- **Different structures, different dates.** The Municipal Hall, the Health Center, the
  fisheries building and the current works entered at different times. There may be no single
  taking date at all — and the **fisheries construction happening right now** is a taking
  dated *2026*, on any theory.

**And here is the trap that runs the other way.** Their two defences are **mutually
exclusive**:

> *"We own it by donation"* ⟂ *"We took it in 1953, so pay us 1953 prices."*

They cannot hold both. Every step toward the cheap valuation destroys the ownership claim, and
every step defending the donation forfeits the cheap valuation. **Forcing them to pick, in
writing, early, is the highest-value move available on this parcel.** The Guerrero reply's
item 2 — demand that they identify the deed — is already that move; it just has not been sent.

## 7. What this changes about what goes out — the actionable part

Nothing has been served. So these are still free to fix:

1. **STOP asserting an early taking date in our own instruments.** The drafted
   `DEMAND_LGU_COMPENSATION_DAMAGES_2026-09.md` pleads back rent from **1996** across a full
   30 years. That pleading is, in their hands, our own written admission of a 1996 taking — the
   exhibit that sets the §19 valuation date against us. **The fix is not to drop the back-rent
   claim; it is to plead it in the alternative and expressly without conceding any date of
   taking**, with the position reserved that no lawful taking has occurred at all and that any
   valuation date is 2026. Counsel must draft that reservation before service. **This is the
   single most important line in this document.**
2. **Get the appraisal on record BEFORE any offer or filing** — with a clear head about what it
   does and does not do. **An appraisal does not bind them and does not set the price.** Just
   compensation in an expropriation is determined **by the court**, on the report of
   commissioners appointed under Rule 67; the LGU will bring its own appraiser, the tax
   declaration, and the BIR zonal value, and the court weighs all of it `[HV — Rule 67 two-stage
   procedure and the judicial-determination rule are not in our corpus; verify before counsel
   relies]`. What the appraisal actually buys is three things, each real:
   (a) at **gate 2** it lets the estate refuse a lowball offer *with evidence*, which is how an
       offer fails to be "valid and definite";
   (b) it is the estate's primary exhibit **before the commissioners**; and
   (c) it anchors any negotiated purchase (branch E5), where there is no court at all.
   **Its value is entirely contingent on winning the date fight in §5** — a 2026 appraisal is
   near-worthless if the court reckons the taking at 1996. The date argument is upstream of the
   appraisal, not downstream. Specify a **PRC-accredited appraiser**, sales-comparison method
   with documented comparables, and the capacity to value **as of alternative dates**. Companion
   moves, both cheap: pull the **BIR zonal valuation** for the barangay (objective, free, often
   the practical floor), and pull **registered comparable sales** in Poblacion from the RD —
   actual registered deeds outweigh any appraiser's opinion.
   **Caution on raising the ARP (item 3 below):** an owner's own declared value is treated as an
   admission against interest, so a low declaration hurts at valuation — but raising it also
   raises the estate's RPT liability, and the RPT is currently paid under protest. Trade-off;
   decide deliberately, not as a side-effect.
3. **Consider correcting the ARP upward.** The RPT protest track and the assessment-records
   fight have been run as defence. They are also **valuation infrastructure**: a higher declared
   value raises their deposit and their floor. Worth a deliberate decision rather than a
   side-effect.
4. **Press Arts. 449–451 with eyes open.** It is real leverage — and it is precisely the
   pressure that makes expropriation attractive to them. That may be exactly what we want, but
   it should be a **chosen** herding move with a price already set (see 2), not an accident.
5. **Send the Guerrero reply first — and it is the only thing that needs to go this week.**
   The DILG path is the one track already running, the one the LGU is already answering, and
   the one instrument that forces gate-2 logic while giving nothing away: *identify the deed.*
   Whatever they answer narrows them. It asserts no date of taking, names no price, starts no
   clock the estate must then honour, and cannot be used against us on valuation. Everything
   in the demand suite can wait behind the appraisal (item 2) and the taking-date reservation
   (item 1); the Guerrero reply cannot be improved by waiting.
6. **If they dress it as socialized housing** under §19's "welfare of the poor and landless"
   limb, RA 7279 (in the embedded library) imposes an order of priority that puts government
   land first and private land last — a real defence. Park it; do not raise it unprompted.

---

## 8. The recommendation

**Do not fear expropriation and do not invite it.** The correct posture is a written
non-objection to the Municipality *regularizing its occupation by the lawful means the Code
provides* — which is true, costs nothing, reads as reasonable to every supervisor on copy, and
is expensive for them to accept because accepting it means abandoning the donation. Say nothing
about price until the appraisal exists.

The parcel was probably always going to end as money rather than land. The estate's job is to
make sure that when it does, the money is reckoned in **2026 pesos** and not in 1953 ones — and
right now, the drafts sitting unserved would help them argue the opposite.
