IV. GROUNDS — THE STATUTORY DUTIES INVOKED

I quote the statute rather than paraphrase it, so that no one can later say the duty was unclear.

Section 25(a), R.A. No. 7160. The President's general supervision over local governments

is exercised "through the province with respect to component cities and municipalities".

Section 29. "*The province, through the governor, shall ensure that every component city

and municipality within its territorial jurisdiction acts within the scope of its prescribed powers and functions.*"

Section 465(b)(2)(i). The governor shall "*Ensure that the acts of the component cities and

municipalities of the province and of its officials and employees are within the scope of their prescribed powers, duties and functions*"; and, (iii), "Issue such executive orders for the faithful and appropriate enforcement and execution of laws and ordinances."

Section 472(b). The provincial assessor shall "*(1) Ensure that all laws and policies

governing the appraisal and assessment of real properties for taxation purposes are properly executed*"; "(5) Prepare, install and maintain a system of tax mapping …"; "(6) Conduct frequent physical surveys to verify and determine whether all real properties within the province are properly listed in the assessment rolls"; "(9) Issue, upon request of any interested party, certified copies of assessment records of real property and all other records relative to its assessment, upon payment of a service charge or fee to the treasurer"; and "(12) In the case of the provincial assessor, exercise technical supervision and visitorial functions over all component city and municipal assessor, coordinate with component city or municipal assessors in the conduct of tax mapping operations and all other assessment activities, and provide all forms of assistance therefor."

Section 470(d)(6). "*In the case of the provincial treasurer, exercise technical supervision

over all treasury offices of component cities and municipalities.*"

Section 200. The province is "*primarily responsible for the proper, efficient and

effective administration of the real property tax.*"

Section 61(b). "*A complaint against any elective official of a municipality shall be

filed before the sangguniang panlalawigan whose decision may be appealed to the Office of the President.*" The Province is not merely a supervisor of Mercedes; it is the forum that disciplines its Mayor.

R.A. No. 6713, Section 5(a). "*All public officials and employees shall, within fifteen

(15) working days from receipt thereof, respond to letters, telegrams or other means of communications sent by the public. The reply must contain the action taken on the request.*"

R.A. No. 3019, Section 3(f). It is a corrupt practice for a public officer to be

"Neglecting or refusing, after due demand or request, without sufficient justification, to act within a reasonable time on any matter pending before him … for the purpose of favoring his own interest or giving undue advantage in favor of or discriminating against any other interested party."

Civil Code, Article 27. "*Any person suffering material or moral loss because a public

servant or employee refuses or neglects, without just cause, to perform his official duty may file an action for damages and other relief against the latter, without prejudice to any disciplinary administrative action that may be taken.*"

"Local autonomy" is not an answer to any of these. Autonomy is the Municipality's freedom to act within its powers. Sections 29 and 465 exist precisely for the case where it does not. A supervisor who invokes the supervised unit's autonomy to excuse the supervised unit's breach has not deferred; he has abdicated.

V. GROUNDS — REFERRAL IS NOT SUPERVISION

The record now documents a closed circle in writing. ARTA referred to the DILG and the CSC. DILG referred to the Province. The Province referred to the Municipality. The Municipality routed the matter to its Committee on Anti-Red Tape — chaired by the Mayor, with the Municipal Assessor sitting on it — which resolved (CART Resolution No. 6, s. 2026) that the LGU "shall not release, provide, or act upon the requested documents … unless and until he sufficiently establishes and submits proof of his legal personality or authority."" The complaint about the committee was then referred, by the Province, to the committee's chairman.

No one in that circle has to coordinate for it to work. Each office need only decline, and the file returns to the officials complained of. That is why this petition is written the way it is: a further endorsement that returns the file to Mercedes without a written directive, a period for compliance, and a stated consequence for default will be treated as a refusal to supervise after due demand, and will be bound as an exhibit with that title.

VII. NOTICE — WHAT FOLLOWS IF THE PETITION IS NOT ACTED UPON

I set this out so that no one can later say the consequence was not stated.

  1. The clock is statutory. Section 5(a) of R.A. 6713 runs fifteen working days from receipt of this petition, independently of the ten days above. A reply that "contains no action taken" is not a reply under the statute.
  2. This is the due demand. From receipt, the elements of Section 3(f) of R.A. 3019 are measured against each Office's response. Every month of further non-action after this notice re-completes them. The demand is made of each of you in your official capacity and is notice to each of you personally.
  3. Personal liability. Article 27 of the Civil Code lies against the public servant, not the treasury. It runs from refusal after demand. The offices below you have already been put on that notice; this petition extends it upward.
  4. The Province will be asked to judge what it declined to supervise. A verified complaint against the municipal elective officials named in Part III lies before the Sangguniang Panlalawigan under Section 61(b). If it is filed, the Province's own record of referral without a directive will be the first exhibit. Section 60(c) of the Code makes "gross negligence, or dereliction of duty" a ground on which "an elective local official may be disciplined, suspended, or removed from office", and Section 61(a) sends a complaint against a provincial elective official to the Office of the President. Appointive provincial officials answer to the Civil Service Commission. All answer to the Office of the Ombudsman.
  5. The record is already before the President's Office. A petition for supervisory review of the Anti-Red Tape Authority's Resolutions of 7 April 2026 in CTN SL-2025-1008-0690 and CTN SL-2025-1104-0792, concerning this Municipality, has been pending before the Office of the President since May 2026 (Transmittal Ref. 050526-MRO-234187, with two manifestations since), and a further petition is filed there this month. The same conduct is in the Civil Service Commission's referral chain and the Ombudsman's intake, with the Bureau of Local Government Finance and the Department of the Interior and Local Government on copy. This petition, and each Office's response or non-response to it, will be filed in that proceeding as a supplemental manifestation, under the name of the office concerned. Silence after this notice is not neutral. It is a position, and it will be read as one by the Office that supervises the Province.
  6. The question changes. Until now the question in every forum has been what the Municipality's officers did. From this petition forward it is who, holding the statutory power and written notice to correct it, chose to let it stand. I would far rather record your Offices on the other side of that question.
