# Legal Consistency in the ARTA Resolutions — the Mercedes Cluster and R.A. No. 11032

**Final working version, 16 September 2026.** Argument blueprint for (i) a supplement to the pending
petition for supervisory review before the Office of the President and (ii) two appeals under
Administrative Order No. 22, s. 2011. Draft for counsel. Nothing here has been filed.

Every quotation is drawn from the corpus documents cited and has been machine-checked against the source

## Executive overview

Republic Act No. 11032, the Ease of Doing Business and Efficient Government Service Delivery Act of 2018,
imposes fixed processing deadlines, requires every agency to publish its services in a Citizen's Charter,
and makes officers liable for failing to render a service within the prescribed period. Section 3 extends
the Act to "all government offices and agencies including local government units (LGUs)".

Seven resolutions issued by the Anti-Red Tape Authority on complaints against officials of Mercedes,
Camarines Norte and PENRO Camarines Norte, decided between April and September 2026, apply that statute
inconsistently. The same class of transaction — a registered co-owner's representative asking a municipal
office for records of the family's own titled property — was held to be a covered government service in one
docket and outside the Act entirely in four others. In three early dockets the Authority barred the claim on
the complainant's foreign nationality, a ground it has since stopped using and has itself declined to defend.

The procedural vehicle must be split. Only two dockets remain within the fifteen-day appeal period under
Administrative Order No. 22. The rest are already before the Office of the President, or belong there, under
the President's power of control over an agency attached to that Office.

## Part I — The statutory framework

### A. "Government service" under Section 4(f)

Section 4(f) defines government service as "the process or transaction between applicants or requesting
parties and government offices or agencies involving applications for any privilege, right, reward, license,
clearance, permit or authorization, concession, or for any modification, renewal or extension of the
enumerated applications or requests which are acted upon in the ordinary course of business of the agency or
office concerned."

The Authority read this narrowly in most of the cluster, holding that requests for existing tax records,
building permits and official receipts are not applications for a privilege or permit. The difficulty is
that it did not read it that way in every docket, and it has never explained the difference.

### B. Processing periods and the single written extension

Section 9(b)(1) fixes the periods: three working days for simple transactions, seven for complex, and no
more than twenty for highly technical applications. An extension is available, but on strict terms:

> "The maximum time prescribed above may be extended only once for the same number of days, which shall be
> indicated in the Citizen's Charter. Prior to the lapse of the processing time, the office or agency
> concerned shall notify the applicant or requesting party in writing of the reason for the extension and
> final date of release of the government service/s requested. Such written notification shall be signed by
> the applicant or requesting party to serve as proof of notice."

An agency cannot extend a period silently, verbally, or after the fact. This is the cluster's strongest
procedural point.

### C. R.A. 11032 and R.A. 6713 run in parallel

Several resolutions treated the matter as a Code of Conduct question — the fifteen-working-day duty to
answer letters under R.A. 6713 §5(a) — and referred it elsewhere. That is not an alternative to R.A. 11032.
Section 23 of R.A. 11032 provides that a finding of administrative liability under the Act "shall not be a
bar to the filing of criminal, civil or other related charges under existing laws arising from the same act
or omission". The duties coexist; neither displaces the other.

## Part II — The seven dockets

### Tab I — CTN SL-2025-1008-0690 and SL-2025-1104-0792, Municipal Engineer Balane (7 Apr 2026, doc 719)

The complainant's letter of inquiry was refused at the counter for want of a Special Power of Attorney, then
re-submitted with one. The Authority found the refusal justified: "there was due cause when Engr. Balane's
refused to accept Zschoche's letter … It was reasonable for Engr. Balane to ask for proof of Zschoche's
authority to transact on Mrs. Patricia's behalf."

On Section 21(b) it held that a charter that omits the service cannot generate an unlisted requirement:
"since there are no documents listed in the Citizen's Charter, there can be no imposition of additional
documents other those listed in the Citizen's Charter." On Section 21(e) it applied a nationality bar:
"Zschoche's request does notfall under the definition ofagovernment service considering that neitherhe nor
Mrs. Patricia have the rightto information both being American citizens" [sic — word spacing as it appears
in the scanned copy]. The docket was closed and referred to the Civil Service Commission Regional Office V on
R.A. 6713 §5(a).

### Tab II — CTN SL-2025-1021-0747, Mayor Pajarillo (29 Apr 2026, doc 967)

The Mayor's office required a Special Power of Attorney executed by all heirs before answering. The
resolution reproduces the municipality's 2022 Citizen's Charter entry for the Mayor's Office —
**"Receiving Routing and Responding Incoming Communications and other Correspondence"**, classified
"Simple", three minutes — and then holds: "only the receipt of the correspondence is covered under the
Citizen's Charter while the processing of the Municipal Government's response does not appear therein."

The word "Responding" appears in the title of the service the Authority was reading. The same nationality
holding as Tab I follows, and the docket was closed without referral.

### Tab III — CTN SL-2026-0128-1210, Mayor Pajarillo and Treasurer Macale (13 May 2026, doc 624)

The complaint concerned unanswered real property tax inquiries and unissued receipts. On the record,
"Remittances totaling Seventy-Five Thousand Pesos (Php 75,000.00) were made on 19 December 2025
(Confirmation Nos. VCHMU47RH and VYFLCEPVH). However, no official receipts annotated 'PAID UNDER PROTEST'
have been issued", a figure that includes a ₱50,000 supplement tendered on 20 December 2025.

The Authority classified the communications as "requests for information and clarification of tax records"
outside Section 4(f), and raised the nationality bar to constitutional footing: "The 1987 Constitution
provides that access to official records and to documents are afforded only to citizens."

### Tab III-A — the Authority's clarification (21 May 2026, doc 972)

Asked for the legal basis of that holding, the Litigation Division maintained the closure but withdrew its
reach. The constitutional and Executive Order No. 2 discussion, it wrote, "was not intended to conclusively
determine the full extent of any property owner's rights or remedies under the Local Government Code, nor to
foreclose the complainant from pursuing appropriate relief before the proper office, agency, or tribunal",
and the resolution "should not be read as a determination that the Municipal Treasurer's Office may lawfully
refuse, withhold, or deny any document, receipt, or record to which a taxpayer or registered property owner
may otherwise be entitled under applicable law."

The three specific legal questions put to the Authority were not answered.

### Tab IV — CTN SL-2026-0128-1212, Vice Mayor Yapyuco and Councillors Ong and Torralba (1 Jun 2026, doc 1614)

Inquiries about an accepted deed of donation and the naming of donated road lots drew a demand for an SPA
signed by all heirs. The Authority applied a three-element test for Section 21(b) — "(1) There is an
application or request; (2) There are documents listed in the Citizen's Charter; and (3) The responding
agency imposed additional requirements other than those stated in the Citizen's Charter" — and closed on the
second element. It referred Councillor Torralba to the Office of the Ombudsman on R.A. 6713 and Tony Teope
on Section 3(i) of R.A. 3019.

By Order of 3 August 2026 (doc 4227) the Authority declined to explain its Section 4(f) reasoning — "the
question on whether 'road-naming' is considered a government service has not been discussed in the
Resolution and will not be discussed any further" — and closed the docket to further pleadings.

### Tab V — CTN SL-2026-0209-1319, PENRO Fortuno and Engr. Remoto (25 Aug 2026, doc 8160)

The Authority conceded that part of the request was covered — "such requests may partake of a government
service within the contemplation of R.A. No. 11032" — but closed because it could identify neither a service
nor a period: "Neither is there sufficient evidence identifying the particular government service allegedly
left unrendered and the prescribed processing period governing the same."

Two features sit oddly with that result. The Section 21(b) count was ticked on the referral form that began
the case and was never adjudicated. And the closure is paired with an order endorsing the case to the
Authority's Operations Group "for the reengineering of PENRO's Citizen's Charter", although the body of the
resolution makes no finding about that charter.

### Tab VI — CTN SL-2026-0209-1321, Municipal Assessor Abla (25 Aug 2026, docs 6908 and 6907)

Here the Authority held the opposite of Tabs I to IV on the threshold question. The request for historical
assessment records "may reasonably fall within the service denominated as 'Research/Verify History of
Records of Tax Declaration' under the Citizen's Charter of the Municipality of Mercedes, Camarines Norte",
and the first two elements of Section 21(e) were "undisputed". The charter period is quoted: thirty minutes
to one hour for simple research, "two (2) to three (3) days" for multiple records.

The delay was established on the face of the resolution:

> "There is likewise no dispute that the entirety of the documents requested by Zschoche was not furnished
> within the foregoing processing period. In fact, approximately ten (10) months had elapsed from the filing
> of his request in May 2025 until the filing of his Sworn Complaint-Affidavit in March 2026. Ordinarily,
> such period would substantially exceed the two (2) to three (3)-day processing period indicated in the
> Citizen's Charter for research involving multiple records."

The Authority nonetheless excused the delay for "just cause" — the volume and archival age of the records —
and concluded that "while the requested government service was not fully rendered within the processing
period indicated in the Citizen's Charter, the fourth element of Section 21(e) has not been established."

Two consequences follow. First, **no violation was found**: the closure rests on the absence of the fourth
element, not on a finding of liability. Second, and decisively, the excuse was accepted although no written
extension was ever issued. The resolution says so: "While Zschoche disputes the legal effect of the verbal
discussions and maintains that no formal written extension conforming to R.A. No. 11032 was issued, such
circumstance does not, under the particular facts of this case, negate the existence of just cause for the
delay."

The service was covered and admittedly unrendered, and nothing was ordered produced.

### Tab VII — CTN SL-2026-0218-1378, Municipal Engineer Balane (9 Sep 2026, doc 14205)

Fifteen days after Tab VI, and prepared by the same handling lawyer under the same signing chain, the
Authority returned to the narrow reading: "a request to inspect or obtain copies of previously issued
building permits and related records is materially distinct from an application for the issuance, approval,
or processing of such permits in the first instance."

Three findings in this resolution are useful and should be pressed:

- It rejected the authority objection: "an SPA authorizing Zschoche to represent the heirs of Patricia
  Keesey Zschoche is not indispensable to his filing of the present complaint", because "it was Zschoche
  himself who personally transacted with the Office of the Municipal Engineer".
- It refused to bless the refusal: the finding "should not be construed as a determination by ARTA that
  Respondent's refusal or failure to release the requested documents was proper, or that the SPA requirement
  was legally justified."
- It conceded the principle that defeats its own result: "the fact that the request is not expressly
  included in the Citizen's Charter does not, by itself, remove it from the ambit of a 'government service'."

The letter of 5 February 2026, in which the respondent wrote of deportation and prison time, was referred to
the Civil Service Commission Regional Office V.

## Part IV — Property rights, correctly sourced

**This was never a Freedom of Information claim.** Article III, Section 7 of the Constitution affords access
to official records "to the citizen", so an argument framed on that clause fails on its text. The correct
frame is that a registered co-owner, through an attorney-in-fact, dealt with the offices that hold the
records of the family's own titled land.

**Foreign heirs lawfully hold that land.** Article XII, Section 7: "Save in cases of hereditary succession,
no private lands shall be transferred or conveyed except to individuals, corporations, or associations
qualified to acquire or hold lands of the public domain."

**The Local Government Code puts the heirs in the record and makes them liable.** Section 205(b): "The
undivided real property of a deceased person may be listed, valued and assessed in the name of the estate or
of the heirs and devisees … Provided, however, That such heir, devisee, or co-owner shall be liable
severally and proportionately for all obligations imposed by this Title and the payment of the real property
tax with respect to the undivided property." A person made severally liable for the tax has legal interest
in the assessment record.

**Due process depends on the paper.** Section 223 requires written notice of a new or revised assessment
within thirty days. Section 252 makes payment under protest a precondition to challenging an assessment and
requires that "There shall be annotated on the tax receipts the words 'paid under protest'." Withholding the
receipt therefore blocks the remedy.

**The receipt duty.** Section 247 of the Code addresses collection, not receipts; the duty to issue an
official receipt for collections rests on the Government Auditing Code (P.D. 1445) and Commission on Audit
rules, and on Sections 334 and 395 for barangay treasurers.

**Building records.** Section 207 of P.D. 1096 makes the Building Official responsible for enforcing the
Code and "charged with the duties of issuing building permits"; Section 208 requires "a permanent record and
accurate account of all fees and other charges". Neither section grants public access, so the argument must
rest on the owner's interest in the regulatory record of their own land, not on a general right of access.

## Part VI — The arguments

### First — the threshold was decided two ways on the same facts

Tab VI holds a municipal records request is a charter service with a fixed period. Tab VII, fifteen days
later, from the same handling lawyer and the same signing chain, holds a municipal records request is not a
government service at all. Neither resolution acknowledges the other. Tab V occupies a third position, that
part of the request is covered but no service or period can be identified.

Tab VII supplies the principle that resolves it: a charter omission "does not, by itself, remove it from the
ambit of a 'government service'". Applied consistently, the Mercedes records requests are covered
transactions and the periods in the municipality's own charter govern.

The subsidiary reading in Tabs I, II and IV — that a charter omission defeats a Section 21(b) charge because
element two requires "documents listed in the Citizen's Charter" — adds an element the statute does not
contain and rewards the office that publishes least. Tab II is the clearest illustration: a service titled
"Receiving Routing and Responding" was read as covering receipt alone.

### Second — nationality is not a threshold under R.A. 11032

Tabs I, II and III turned on the complainant's and the principal's American citizenship. No later resolution
uses that ground. The Authority itself has since said the discussion was not meant to determine a property
owner's rights or to foreclose relief, and that the resolution is not to be read as permitting the Treasurer
to withhold any record a registered owner is otherwise entitled to (doc 972). Three closures rest on a
ground the Authority has abandoned in substance but never reversed on the record.

The transactions were property-owner transactions. Article XII, Section 7 permits the heirs to hold the
land; Sections 205(b), 223 and 252 of the Local Government Code give the owner the interest, the notice and
the receipt on which every later remedy depends.

### Third — "just cause" cannot substitute for the written extension

Tab VI is the cleanest procedural breach in the cluster. The service was covered. The charter period was two
to three days. Roughly ten months passed. The Authority excused the delay on volume and archival difficulty
while recording that no written extension conforming to the Act was ever issued, and it closed the docket
without directing that the records be produced or that a written denial issue.

Section 9(b)(1) allows one extension, in writing, before the period lapses, signed by the applicant as proof
of notice. An excuse constructed after the fact from the size of the request is not that. If volume alone
suffices, the three-seven-twenty rule ceases to bind any office willing to call a request voluminous.

### Fourth — the municipal committee decided the matter in its own favour

The standing refusal in the six committee resolutions, passed by a body whose members include four of the
respondents and the occupant of one of the structures in question, is the conduct the Act exists to reach.
The Authority accepted those closures without examining who voted for them.

## Relief to seek

1. Set aside the closures in the dockets under review and remand for determination on a consistent reading
   of Section 4(f), or in the alternative resolve the threshold question for the whole cluster.
2. Direct the Municipality of Mercedes to release the requested records, or to issue a written denial
   stating the ground and the officer denying it, as Section 9(c) requires.
3. Declare that the absence of a service from a Citizen's Charter is a compliance failure of the office, not
   a defence available to it.
4. Declare that an extension of a processing period is effective only on the written notice required by
   Section 9(b)(1).
5. Refer the composition and conduct of the Mercedes Committee on Anti-Red Tape to the appropriate
   disciplinary authority.
6. Consolidate these matters with the proceeding pending under Transmittal 050526-MRO-234187.
