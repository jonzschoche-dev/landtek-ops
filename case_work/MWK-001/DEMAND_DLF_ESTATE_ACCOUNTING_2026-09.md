Dear Mr. de la Fuente:

I write as Attorney-in-Fact for Patricia Keesey Zschoche, registered co-owner heir of the Estate of Mary Worrick Keesey, under an apostilled Special Power of Attorney. Mrs. Zschoche is one of the three heirs who signed the 1992 Special Power of Attorney in favor of your late father, Cesar M. de la Fuente. This demand is made for her and for the benefit of the co-ownership.

**1. The agency your father accepted.** By a Special Power of Attorney executed in March 1992 at Los Angeles, California, authenticated before Consul Ramon C. Munda and registered with the Registry of Deeds of Camarines Norte, Geraldine K. Hoppe, Patricia K. Zschoche and Marcia Ellen Keesey appointed Cesar M. de la Fuente their attorney-in-fact for one stated purpose: to raise money **to settle the estate's unpaid real estate taxes**. His authority was **to negotiate for the sale** of the property covered by Transfer Certificate of Title No. T-4497 **only** "in favor of third persons in possession of Contracts to Sell executed by the previous administrator, Mr. Ben Llamanzares and/or to those who have paid their down-payments or have made partial payments therefor to the latter," and only "after prior consultation with us." He was authorized "to receive the proceeds of the aforementioned sale **in our collective behalf**." Every peso he received under that instrument was the heirs' money, held for them.

**2. The agency ended; the duty to account did not.** The heirs revoked the Special Power of Attorney effective **15 August 2005**; the revocation was registered and was published in the Bicol Post on 25 September, 2 October and 9 October 2020. Your father passed away on **21 June 2017**, as his own counsel, Atty. Elaine Gay R. Belen, informed the Regional Trial Court, Branch 40, Daet in Civil Case No. 6839. Under **Article 1919 of the Civil Code** the agency was extinguished by the revocation, and in any event by his death. Under **Article 1891**, every agent is bound to render an account of his transactions and to **deliver to the principal whatever he may have received by virtue of the agency, even though it may not be owing to the principal**. That obligation was never performed in your father's lifetime. It did not die with him: under **Articles 774, 776 and 1311 of the Civil Code**, the obligations of the deceased pass to his estate and heirs, to the extent of the inheritance. It is also, by law, an obligation that carries interest: under **Article 1896**, the agent owes interest on the sums he applied to his own use from the day he did so, and on those he still owes after the extinguishment of the agency.

**3. What was received and never accounted for.** Signing as "Attorney-in-Fact of the Heirs of Mary W. Keesey," your father executed deeds of absolute sale over portions of the estate's land at Barangay San Roque, Mercedes, each reciting that the purchase price was **paid to him in hand and receipt acknowledged**. Those in the heirs' possession alone recite the following:

| Deed of Absolute Sale, 6 April 2002 | Delia H. Nacasa | ₱91,600.00 |

These are the instruments the heirs have been able to obtain. They are not the whole. The Registry of Deeds has certified that other instruments of the same period annotated on the estate's titles are no longer on file, and the annotations themselves record further "deeds of sale" and "confirmations of sale" executed in the heirs' name in the 1990s and 2000s. **Not one peso of any of these proceeds was ever remitted, reported or accounted for to Mrs. Zschoche or her co-heirs.** Nor was the stated purpose of the agency served: the estate's real property taxes were left unpaid, and the heirs are today paying, from their own funds, the very delinquency the agency existed to settle. That includes the sale to the Balanes made eleven years **after** the authority was revoked, and its "confirmation" signed by your father in March 2017, months before his death, on which sale a Torrens title was issued in November 2021, more than four years after he was gone.

**4. Your own part.** By an instrument dated 20 February 2012 your father purported to delegate his powers to you. The Special Power of Attorney gave him no power to appoint a substitute, and the heirs were never consulted or informed. Under **Articles 1892 and 1893 of the Civil Code** an agent who appoints a substitute without such power is answerable for the substitute's acts, and **the principal may proceed directly against the substitute**. The buyers' receipts for money paid on account of the estate's land were signed by **you**, as "Von dela Fuente," and not by your father. In your Judicial Affidavit in Civil Case No. 26-360 (Municipal Trial Court, Mercedes), sworn in April 2026, you state that you acted as your father's assistant in these transactions and that the sale proceeds were used to pay the estate's real property taxes and estate taxes. If that is so, it is a matter of official receipts, and it is exactly what an accounting shows. If it is not so, the money is owed. Either way, the accounting is due, and you are the person who has sworn he knows where the money went.

**5. THE DEMAND.** Within **fifteen (15) days** from receipt of this letter, you are demanded, for yourself and for the estate and heirs of Cesar M. de la Fuente, to deliver to the undersigned, in writing:

  (a) **A full and true accounting** of every sum of money and every thing of value received by Cesar M. de la Fuente, by you, or by any person acting for either of you, by virtue of or under color of the 1992 Special Power of Attorney or the 2012 delegation, from 1991 to date, stating for each transaction the instrument, the date, the buyer or payor, the amount received, and the disposition of the amount, with a schedule of all "contracts to sell," deeds of sale, confirmations of sale and receipts issued over any land of the estate.
  (b) **The supporting documents** for that accounting, in particular every official receipt for any real property tax, estate tax or other payment you claim was made for the estate's account from those proceeds, and copies of every receipt issued to any buyer.
  (c) **Turnover of all sums not shown, by such receipts, to have been applied for the estate's benefit** — in no case less than the **₱446,300.00** recited in the four instruments identified in paragraph 3, together with legal interest at six percent (6%) per annum from the respective dates of receipt, or at the least from receipt of this demand.
  (d) **Delivery of the estate's papers** in the possession of your family: any owner's duplicate certificate of title, tax declarations, tax receipts, the Llamanzares contracts to sell and payment records, subdivision plans and survey returns, and all correspondence with the Registry of Deeds, the Municipal Assessor, the Municipal Treasurer and the Bureau of Internal Revenue concerning the estate's land. These belong to the principals, not to the agent.
  (e) **Disclosure** of the names and addresses of all heirs of Cesar M. de la Fuente, and whether any proceeding for the settlement of his estate has been opened, and if so, where.
  (f) **A written undertaking** that neither you nor any member of your family will, from this date, execute, sign, deliver, "confirm," or receive money on any instrument, or hold yourselves out in any manner, as representing the Heirs of Mary Worrick Keesey or any of them.

**6. What follows if you do not.** Should the accounting and turnover not be made within the period stated, the heirs will pursue, without further notice, an action for **accounting, turnover of proceeds with interest, and damages** against the estate and heirs of Cesar M. de la Fuente and against you personally, in the Regional Trial Court of Camarines Norte, and will present the same claim in any proceeding for the settlement of his estate. The estate and heirs of Cesar M. de la Fuente are already identified as necessary parties by the defendants' own pleading in Civil Case No. 26-360. This demand fixes the date from which you and the estate are in default.
