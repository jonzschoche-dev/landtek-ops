## Legal footing (as pleaded in the letter)
- **Art. 1891** accounting + delivery "even though it may not be owing to the principal" — the core.
- **Art. 1896** interest on sums applied to own use / still owed after extinguishment.
- **Art. 1919** extinguishment (revocation 2005; death 2017).
- **Arts. 774 / 776 / 1311** transmission of the obligation to the estate/heirs, to the extent of the inheritance.
- **Arts. 1892 / 1893** — the SPA gave no power to appoint a substitute → Cesar answerable for Salvador's acts AND
  the principal may sue the substitute directly. *Correction to the internal dossier:* Art. 1892 makes a
  substitute's acts void only where the principal **prohibited** substitution; our SPA is silent, so the letter
  does NOT say the delegation is "void" — it uses the 1892/1893 liability + direct-action route, which is the
  accurate one.
- **Art. 1155** — this written extrajudicial demand interrupts prescription of the accounting claim (Art. 1144
  10-yr on a written contract is the exposure; the 1991–2004 receipts are the ones at risk — counsel to assess
  whether the accounting obligation is treated as running from demand/refusal, which is the better view for a
  fiduciary who never rendered an account).
