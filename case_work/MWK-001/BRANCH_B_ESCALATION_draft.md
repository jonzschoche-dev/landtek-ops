**What this default is — and is not.** Until 25 August, the record showed a municipality ignoring a citizen. Since 25 August, it shows a municipality **ignoring a written directive of the Department of the Interior and Local Government itself**, served through its own municipal officer, on received-stamped instruments, within a stated period. The question is no longer whether my requests have merit — the Department's own indorsements adopted them item by item. The question is whether a component municipality may disregard the Department, and what the Department does when it is disregarded.

I note also what the defaulted items have in common: each is one the LGU **cannot answer without admitting something**. The records either exist (and must be produced) or do not (breaching §§469(c)(1)/52(e)). The SPA condition has no legal basis to cite. The CART referral has no action to report. Silence is not an oversight; it is the only answer that concedes nothing — and it is itself an answer.

**The requests.** My letter of 26 August 2026, item (d), asked your Office to advise, in advance of this very moment, what provincial supervisory action would follow an LGU default on the MLGOO's indorsements. That moment has arrived. I therefore respectfully request that your Office, within **ten (10) days**:

  (a) **Issue, at provincial level, a directive to the Mayor and the Sanggunian** to comply with the MLGOO's 2nd Indorsements within a definite period — the Code's supervision, exercised from the level the LGU cannot outrank;
  (b) **Report the default to the Regional Director and the Office of the Governor** — the latter completing, at last, the referral your Office itself initiated on 17 March 2026 — with your Office's recommendation for action under Sections 25 and 29 of R.A. No. 7160;

  (d) If your Office will take none of these actions, **state the specific legal basis** — so that the administrative record is complete for the remedies that follow it.

On that last point I will be plain, because the time for implication has passed: with the Department's own directives now defied, the administrative supervisory route is [near] exhaustion. The remaining remedies — **mandamus** on the ministerial records duties, the **Office of the Ombudsman** (where ARTA and the CSC have already referred these officials, the Mayor included, per OAC-L Letter No. 270, s. 2026), and the **disciplinary jurisdiction of the Sangguniang Panlalawigan** over elective municipal officials under Section 61(b) — do not require the Department's leave. I would prefer, and the law would prefer, that supervision work first. That preference now rests with your Office.
