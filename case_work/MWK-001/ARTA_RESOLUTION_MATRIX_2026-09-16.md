## B. The central contradiction: is a records request a "government service"?

This single question decides every docket, and ARTA answered it four different ways.

| Docket | Answer | Words used |
|---|---|---|
| 0690/0792 (doc 719) | No — and no right at all, on nationality | "neither he nor Mrs. Patricia have the right to information both being American citizens" |
| 0747 (doc 967) | No — same sentence | "Zschoche's request does not fall under the definition of a government service considering that neither he nor Mrs, Patricia have the right to information both being American citizens" [sic] |
| 1210 (doc 624) | No — raised to constitutional footing | "The 1987 Constitution provides that access to official records and to documents are afforded only to citizens" |
| 1212 (doc 1614) | No — but on the nature of the request only | "Zschoche's inquiries do not involve an application for any privilege, right, reward, license, clearance, permit or authorization, or concession" |
| 1319 (doc 8160) | Partly yes | "such requests may partake of a government service within the contemplation of R.A. No. 11032" |
| **1321 (docs 6908/6907)** | **Yes — squarely covered** | "ARTA finds that the government service sought by Zschoche may reasonably fall within the service denominated as 'Research/Verify History of Records of Tax Declaration' under the Citizen's Charter"; the first two elements of §21(e) are "undisputed" |
| 1378 (doc 14205) | No — categorically | "a request to inspect or obtain copies of previously issued building permits and related records is materially distinct from an application for the issuance, approval, or processing of such permits" |

**The 1321/1378 pair is the sharpest.** Both were prepared by the same handling lawyer, Atty. Lance
Christopher D. Padilla, and signed by the same chain. They are fifteen days apart. In 1321 a municipal
records request is a charter service with a 2-3 day clock that was blown by roughly ten months; in 1378 a
municipal records request is not a government service at all, so no clock can exist. Neither resolution
mentions the other.

1321 goes further and finds the delay proven:

> "There is likewise no dispute that the entirety of the documents requested by Zschoche was not furnished
> within the foregoing processing period. In fact, approximately ten (10) months had elapsed from the filing
> of his request in May 2025 until the filing of his Sworn Complaint-Affidavit in March 2026. Ordinarily,
> such period would substantially exceed the two (2) to three (3)-day processing period indicated in the
> Citizen's Charter for research involving multiple records."

It then excuses the delay for "just cause" (volume, archival age, manpower) and closes the case — without
directing that the records be produced or that a written denial issue.

## C. Other inconsistencies

### C1. The SPA demand: lawful in April, irrelevant in August, unnecessary in September

- **0690/0792 (doc 719):** "there was due cause when Engr. Balane's refused to accept Zschoche's letter …
  It was reasonable for Engr. Balane to ask for proof of Zschoche's authority to transact on Mrs. Patricia's
  behalf."
- **0747 (doc 967):** the demand for an all-heirs SPA is not an added requirement because "the SPA was not
  imposed as a prerequisite in receiving the Letter of Inquiry, it was only required by Hon. Pajarillo in
  providing a specific response".
- **1321 (doc 6908):** the question is deferred and never returned to — "Whatever issue may have
  subsequently arisen regarding the authority of Zschoche … pertains to the continued processing of an
  already accepted request". The complainant's allegation that a tripartite SPA was demanded in December
  2025 is resolved nowhere in that resolution.
- **1378 (doc 14205):** "an SPA authorizing Zschoche to represent the heirs of Patricia Keesey Zschoche is
  not indispensable to his filing of the present complaint", because "it was Zschoche himself who personally
  transacted with the Office of the Municipal Engineer".

Same office, same complainant, same running demand. Note also that 1321 records the charter's own
requirement as "an authorization letter **or** Special Power of Attorney" — one representative's authority,
not all owners'.

### C2. The citizenship bar — asserted three times, then abandoned, never reversed

- Present in **0690/0792 (719)**, **0747 (967)** and **1210 (624)**, where it is the operative ground.
- Absent from **1212 (1614)**, the **1212 Order (4227)**, **1319 (8160)** and **1321 (6908/6907)** — the
  phrases "American citizen" and "afforded only to citizens" appear nowhere in those resolutions' own text.
- In **1378 (14205)** the phrase "American citizen" appears only inside the quoted threat letter, not in
  ARTA's findings.
- **ARTA's letter of 21 May 2026 (doc 972)** walks it back without disturbing the closure: the
  constitutional and E.O. 2 discussion "was not intended to conclusively determine the full extent of any
  property owner's rights or remedies under the Local Government Code, nor to foreclose the complainant from
  pursuing appropriate relief", and the resolution "should not be read as a determination that the Municipal
  Treasurer's Office may lawfully refuse, withhold, or deny any document, receipt, or record to which a
  taxpayer or registered property owner may otherwise be entitled under applicable law."

Three closures rest on a ground the Authority has since declined to defend. The same letter left the
complainant's three direct legal questions unanswered.

### C3. Charter silence cuts both ways — and a charter entry is read out of the charter

- **Silence as a shield for the office** under §21(b): "since there are no documents listed in the Citizen's
  Charter, there can be no imposition of additional documents other those listed in the Citizen's Charter"
  (0690/0792, doc 719); same in 1212 (doc 1614) and the 1212 Order (doc 4227).
- **Silence as proof the service is not covered** under §4(f) in 1378 (doc 14205): "the absence of
  Zschoche's particular request from the services enumerated under the Office of the Municipal Engineer
  supports the conclusion that the requested disclosure and reproduction of the subject records are not
  transactions ordinarily processed by that office". 1378 concedes the tension itself — "the fact that the
  request is not expressly included in the Citizen's Charter does not, by itself, remove it from the ambit
  of a 'government service'" — and then rests on it anyway.
- **A listed entry read as half of itself:** 0747 (doc 967) reproduces the Mayor's Office charter service
  **"Receiving Routing and Responding Incoming Communications and other Correspondence"**, classified
  "Simple", 3 minutes total — then holds "only the receipt of the correspondence is covered under the
  Citizen's Charter while the processing of the Municipal Government's response does not appear therein."
  The word "Responding" is in the service's own title.
- **The opposite approach in 1321:** there the charter entry is used to bring the request inside the Act and
  to supply the 2-3 day period.

### C4. A §21(b) element the statute does not contain

0690/0792 (doc 719) and 1212 (doc 1614) apply: "(1) There is an application or request; (2) There are
documents listed in the Citizen's Charter; and (3) The responding agency imposed additional requirements
other than those stated in the Citizen's Charter."

Element (2) turns a charter omission into a complete defence. R.A. 11032 §21(b) has no such element. 1321
and 1378 decide without it. 1319 never reaches §21(b) at all, though the referral form that started it
carries that box ticked.

### C5. "Due cause" and "just cause" used interchangeably in the same resolution

1321 quotes the statute as "without due cause", states the element as "without just cause", reverts to "due
cause", then holds on "just cause". The statutory phrase is "without due cause".

### C6. A missing written extension is excused

R.A. 11032 §9(b)(1) allows one extension, in writing, with the applicant's signature as proof of notice.
1321 records that none issued and excuses it: "While Zschoche disputes the legal effect of the verbal
discussions and maintains that no formal written extension conforming to R.A. No. 11032 was issued, such
circumstance does not, under the particular facts of this case, negate the existence of just cause for the
delay." The respondent's own defence, as recited, assumed the opposite: extensions are available "provided
that the applicant is duly notified".

### C7. Attribution: the office received it, but no officer did

1319 (doc 8160): "The mere transmission of a communication to the general electronic mail address of an
office, without evidence linking its receipt and subsequent non-action to the particular officials charged,
does not by itself establish prima facie individual liability."

R.A. 11032 §8 places primary responsibility on the head of office, and provides that "All transactions and
processes are deemed to have been made with the permission or clearance from the highest authority having
jurisdiction over the government office or agency concerned." No resolution reconciles the two.

## D. What this adds up to

Across seven dockets the Authority has held, on materially identical facts, that the same kind of records
request is and is not a government service; that the same SPA demand is and is not lawful; and that charter
silence both protects the office and defeats the citizen. Three closures rest on a nationality ground the
Authority itself has since declined to defend, and the one docket where it accepted coverage (1321) found
the delay proven and still ordered nothing.

The strongest single pairing for review is **1321 against 1378** — same drafter, same signing chain, fifteen
days apart, opposite thresholds on the same question.
