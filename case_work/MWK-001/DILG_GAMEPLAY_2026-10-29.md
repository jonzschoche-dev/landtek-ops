## 4. The single outcome worth more than the rest

**An answer to question 1 of row 36.** May a local government office impose, as a condition for acting on
a charter-listed service, a requirement that is not in the Charter.

R.A. 11032 Sec. 21(b) answers it in its own words, so the answer is near-certain. And it converts
immediately:

- **Item 3 of the Schedule**, the all-heirs power of attorney, becomes unlawful rather than merely
  unsourced. At 276 days on 29 Oct, 298 on 20 Nov.
- **Item 7**, the Assessor, loses its main justification, because the same requirement is what the
  Assessor relied on. 496 days on 29 Oct, 518 on 20 Nov.
- **Item 5**, the session records, loses it too. 260 days, 282 on 20 Nov.
- And it arrives as a **Department document with a reference number**, usable in the provincial forum, in
  the Ombudsman lane, and in court, without the Department ever being asked to judge anyone.

**One free document unlocks three items and roughly 1,100 days of default.** Nothing else on the board
has that ratio, which is why row 36 should go out even though it is the newest idea here.

## 5. The four composite scenarios

**A. Everything goes quiet. Probability ~30%, the single most likely world.**
No substantive reply from anyone by 20 Nov. **Our move: exactly three things and nothing else.** The
19 Nov Schedule edition, the 20 Nov short note to Relucio on her own promise, and the 23 Nov chasers on
rows 33, 34 and 36. **No new fronts. No new offices. No new theories.** Quiet is not failure here: it
produces four dated negatives from four offices in one month, where before we had one office saying
nothing.

**B. One or two acknowledge and refer. Probability ~35%.**
Most likely shape: the Secretariat says follow up with Region V, ARTA notes it, Relucio promises again.
**Our move: the FOI request on 12 Nov**, aimed at the national office, naming the Escober letter. The
referral answers are the predicate for it, because they confirm the file exists and is theirs.

**C. The legal opinion lands. Probability ~25%.**
**Our move: stop writing letters to the DILG and start drafting.** The opinion goes into the Section 61(b)
papers as the legal standard, and **we** do the applying. Never ask the Department to apply its own
opinion. This is also the moment to decide on question D, the supervisory-standard question, in its own
letter.

**D. Something real happens. Probability ~10%.**
ARTA monitors, or a conference produces a date, or a control number appears with a substantive answer.
**Our move: slow down.** A cooperating officer who has just moved is worth more than any pressure we
could add, and the fastest way to lose them is to answer performance with a new demand.

## 6. The discipline list. What we do NOT do before 20 November

1. **No third letter to the DILG chain.** The Secretariat, ARTA, Region V and the Region V legal unit have
   all heard from us inside two weeks. Two letters then silence reads as a record. A third reads as
   pestering and invites the easy answer that everything is being looked at.
2. **No fourth demand to Relucio for a date certain.** Three have gone out. Row 35 deliberately makes the
   only date in it **ours**.
3. **No new office.** Not the Governor, not COA, not DILG Central as a separate front, not 8888.
4. **Do not file against the Governor.** The A.O. 23 lever is real and it is held, for the four reasons at
   §16.6: the Province has not refused us, the Escober letter is unread, there is no documented provincial
   default, and it is the least reversible move available.
5. **Do not merge question D into row 36.** One sub judice answer would take all four questions down.
6. **Do not print anything.** No print authorization exists. It is per job and it does not carry.
7. **Do not add any name to row 36 in any later edition.** Re-run the banned-term scan every time.

## 8. The three signals that would change the strategy

1. **Any document, from any office, that makes a finding or sets a date.** None exists in fourteen months.
   The first one moves us from record-building to enforcement.
2. **The Escober letter.** It is the instruction that sent the referral from the Region to the Province.
   If it told them to resolve it, the Province defaulted. If it told them to transmit it, the Region did.
   **Until it is read, do not characterise what the Regional Director instructed.**
3. **An adverse legal opinion.** If question 1 comes back the wrong way, the Citizen's Charter theory is
   damaged in writing and permanently. The risk is low and it is the reason question D is held, but it is
   the one way this month ends worse than it started, and it should be named rather than hidden.

## 9. The honest summary

**We have moved from one lane with no clock to four offices with two clocks, in nine days, at no cost and
without accusing anyone.** Four of the five most likely outcomes are useful to us and the fifth is
silence, which is also useful because it is now dated and multiplied across four offices.

**The highest-value item on the board is also the cheapest and the newest: a free legal opinion request
that mentions nobody.** The biggest risk is not any of their moves. It is us filling three quiet weeks
with new letters while two court dates run, and thereby turning a disciplined record into noise.

**The plan for the next three weeks is mostly to wait, and the waiting is the plan.**
