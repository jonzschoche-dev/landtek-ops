**3. What the Municipality has told the DILG — and what it is doing.** On **26 August 2026**

That directive therefore stands substantially unanswered before three levels of the
Department. **And yet construction proceeds.** A local government that has been formally
directed by its supervising department to substantiate its claim to a parcel does not, while
that directive is outstanding, put new permanent works into the ground on it. The
Municipality is representing one posture to the Department and taking another on the land.

**4. The purpose limitation — the instrument cannot reach a fisheries structure.** Whichever
deed of donation the Municipality eventually names, **every donation instrument concerning
this estate is modal on its face** — it grants for one stated purpose and no other:

  (a) the deed of **12 July 1953**, notarized **13 July 1953**, conveys *"only for the purpose

  (b) the deed of **14 April 1979** conveys *"only for the purpose of constructing roads for
      the Municipality of Mercedes."*

**A fisheries building is not a market. It is not a municipal building. It is not a road.**
Taken at its very highest — assuming without conceding that any of these instruments was
validly executed, accepted and registered, none of which the record shows — the paper the
Municipality invokes **does not authorize the use now being made of the land, and still less
new construction for that use.** A donation for a stated purpose confers no right to a
different one; the excess is simply occupation of another's registered land, and it exposes
the donation itself to revocation for non-compliance with its condition (Arts. 733, 764 and
1191, Civil Code).

I note further that the Sangguniang Bayan's own **Resolution No. 76-96 of 13 March 1996**
records that the Municipal Hall, the Municipal Health Center and the **Bureau of Fisheries**
were then *"presently occupying"* the estate's land and that acquisition was still merely
*"proposed."* That is the Municipality's own record of its own position.

**5. The Local Government Code obligations engaged.** These are stated as matters of
compliance and supervision. Ownership itself is reserved to the proper forum and is not
raised here.

  **5.1 §22(a)(4) and §19 — the only two lawful roads to land.** The Code gives a local
  government exactly two ways to hold land it wants: **acquire it by instrument** (§22[a][4])
  or **expropriate it**, which "may not be exercised unless a valid and definite offer has
  been previously made to the owner … and upon payment of just compensation" (§19). For this
  parcel the record contains **neither** — no registered conveyance, no expropriation, no
  offer, no compensation. Building on it now does not cure that; it enlarges it.

  **5.2 §17 — delivery of basic services and facilities.** The infrastructure facilities the
  Code makes an LGU responsible for are to be built on land the LGU has the right to build
  on. A fisheries facility erected on registered private land, without the owners' consent
  and outside the purpose of any instrument the LGU can produce, is not a lawful discharge
  of §17 — it is a liability created against the local treasury.

  **5.3 §305(a) and §335 — the fiscal limits.** *"No money shall be paid out of the local
  treasury except in pursuance of an appropriations ordinance or law"* (§305[a]); *"No public
  money or property shall be appropriated or applied for religious or private purposes"*
  (§335). If public funds are being disbursed for these works, the appropriation ordinance,
  the contract, the notice of award and the disbursement vouchers exist and are public
  records. **They are demanded below.** Public money committed to permanent improvements on
  land the LGU cannot show it acquired is precisely the exposure the Commission on Audit
  exists to examine, and the officers who approve and certify such disbursements answer for
  them personally.

  **5.4 §444(b)(1)(x) — the Local Chief Executive's duty.** The Mayor must *"ensure that all
  executive officials and employees of the municipality faithfully discharge their duties and
  functions as provided by law and this Code."* Permitting, funding, or allowing construction
  to proceed on this parcel while the DILG's directive to substantiate the LGU's claim is
  outstanding is the opposite of that duty.

  **5.6 P.D. No. 1096 (National Building Code) — the permit.** No structure may be erected,
  added to, or altered without a building permit issued by the Building Official. Any

## A. Why the LGC frame, and where it stops

The DILG cannot adjudicate title and has said so; the estate has agreed to that boundary
from the start (18 Aug letter, 26 Aug clarification). So nothing in the outward letter asks
the Department to decide who owns the land. What the Department **can** do is exactly what
the letter asks: require a supervised LGU to say, on the record, under what instrument,
under what permit, and under what appropriation it is putting permanent works into the
ground. Every count above is a compliance count, not a property count. That is what keeps it
inside §25 and out of the ARTA-1319 trap.
